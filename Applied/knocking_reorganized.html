2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Knocking & Ignition Delay

  • SI Auto-Ignition Delay Relation:
    $${\boxed{\tau_{id} \propto \frac{1}{P^n \cdot e^{-E / (R \cdot T)}}}}$$
  • $\tau_{id}$: ignition delay period of end-gas $\text{ms}$
  • $P$: end-gas pressure $\text{Pa}$
  • $T$: end-gas absolute temperature $\text{K}$

  • $E$: activation energy $\text{J/mol}$

  • CI Total Ignition Delay:

    $${\boxed{\tau_{id} = \tau_{\text{physical}} + \tau_{\text{chemical}}}}$$

  • $\tau_{\text{physical}}$: atomization, heating, vaporization, and mixing time $\text{ms}$
  • $\tau_{\text{chemical}}$: pre-flame chemical reactions time $\text{ms}$

2. Fuel Rating Systems

  • Octane Number Definition:
    $${\boxed{\text{ON} = \% \text{ by volume of Iso-octane in a blend with n-Heptane matching test fuel knock intensity}}}$$
  • Iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane): $\text{ON} = 100$

  • n-Heptane: $\text{ON} = 0$

  • Anti-Knock Index (AKI):

    $${\boxed{\text{AKI} = \frac{\text{RON} + \text{MON}}{2}}}$$

  • $\text{RON}$: Research Octane Number (mild operating conditions)
  • $\text{MON}$: Motor Octane Number (severe operating conditions)

  • Sensitivity $= \text{RON} - \text{MON} > 0$

  • Cetane Number Definition:

    $${\boxed{\text{CN} = \% \text{ by volume of n-Cetane in a blend with } \alpha\text{-methyl-naphthalene matching ignition delay}}}$$

  • n-Cetane (n-hexadecane): $\text{CN} = 100$

  • $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene: $\text{CN} = 0$ (or Heptamethylnonane: $\text{CN} = 15$)

  • Hydrocarbon Detonation Tendency Order (Increasing SI Knock):

    $${\boxed{\text{Aromatics} < \text{Naphthenes} < \text{Olefins} < \text{Paraffins}}}$$

3. Air-Fuel Ratios & Stoichiometry

  • Stoichiometric Air-Fuel Ratio (Mass Basis):
    $${\boxed{\text{AFR}{\text{stoich}} = \frac{m{\text{air, theoretical}}}{m_{\text{fuel}}}}}$$
  • Gasoline / Petrol: $\approx 14.7:1 \text{ to } 15:1$
  • Methanol $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$: $6.5:1$

  • Diesel: $\approx 14.5:1$ (operates lean at $18:1 \text{ to } 70:1$)

  • Equivalence Ratio $\phi$:

    $${\boxed{\phi = \frac{\text{AFR}{\text{stoich}}}{\text{AFR}{\text{actual}}}}}$$

  • $\phi > 1 \implies$ Rich mixture; $\phi < 1 \implies$ Lean mixture; $\phi = 1 \implies$ Stoichiometric

4. Supercharging Relations

  • Boost Density Ratio:
    $${\boxed{\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) \left(\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right)}}$$

  • $\rho_1, \rho_2$: intake air density before and after supercharger $\text{kg/m}^3$

  • Approximate Power Ratio:

    $${\boxed{\frac{\text{Power}{\text{supercharged}}}{\text{Power}{\text{naturally aspirated}}} \approx \frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1}}}$$

5. Combustion Chamber Geometry

  • Flame Travel Duration (SI Engine):
    $${\boxed{t_{\text{combustion}} \propto \frac{L_{\text{flame}}}{S_L + u'}}}$$
  • $L_{\text{flame}}$: distance from spark plug to farthest end-gas $\text{m}$
  • $S_L$: laminar flame speed $\text{m/s}$
  • $u'$: turbulence intensity $\text{m/s}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Detonation (SI Knocking): Abnormal combustion where unburned end-gas auto-ignites spontaneously ahead of the advancing spark-initiated flame front, producing violent pressure shock waves $1000-2000 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Pre-Ignition: Ignition of the air-fuel mixture by a hot spot (spark plug tip, carbon deposit) BEFORE the electric spark occurs. Distinct from detonation (which occurs AFTER spark).
  • Highest Useful Compression Ratio (HUCR): The maximum compression ratio at which an engine can operate without knocking on a given fuel under specified conditions.
  • Diesel Knock: Violent pressure rise $dP/d\theta > 4-5 \text{ bar/}^\circ\text{CA}$ at the START of combustion in CI engines caused by excessive accumulation of fuel during a long ignition delay period.
  • Iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane): Highly branched paraffin hydrocarbon assigned an Octane Number of 100 due to superior knock resistance.
  • n-Heptane: Straight-chain paraffin hydrocarbon assigned an Octane Number of 0 due to extreme knock tendency.
  • n-Cetane (hexadecane): Straight-chain paraffin hydrocarbon assigned a Cetane Number of 100 due to short ignition delay and excellent auto-ignition quality.
  • Open Chamber (Direct Injection - DI): CI combustion chamber where fuel is injected directly into a single cavity in the piston crown. High fuel economy.
  • Divided Chamber (Indirect Injection - IDI): CI combustion chamber split into a primary pre-combustion / swirl chamber and a main cylinder cavity. High turbulence, quieter running, uses glow plugs.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Normal SI Flame Speed: $20 \text{ to } 30 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Detonation Shock Wave Velocity: $1000 \text{ to } 2000 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Gasoline Stoichiometric AFR: $14.7:1 \text{ or } 15:1$.
  • Methanol Stoichiometric AFR: $6.5:1$.
  • Commercial Diesel Cetane Number: $40 \text{ to } 55$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 SI Engine Knocking (Detonation vs Pre-Ignition)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Detonation Mechanism:
  • Spark plug ignites flame front, which advances at $20-30 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Advancing flame compresses and heats the unburned end-gas.
  • If end-gas temperature exceeds auto-ignition temperature before flame arrives, end-gas auto-ignites explosively, generating shock waves at $1000-2000 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Detonation vs Pre-Ignition:
  • Detonation: Occurs AFTER the spark plug fires.
  • Pre-Ignition: Occurs BEFORE the spark plug fires (ignited by hot-spot carbon deposit or overheated exhaust valve).
  • Factors Increasing SI Knocking:
  • Higher compression ratio $r \uparrow$.
  • Advanced spark timing.
  • High intake pressure / supercharging.
  • High intake air / cooling water temperature $T_{\text{coolant}} \uparrow$.
  • Lean air-fuel mixture.
  • Low Octane fuel.
  • Long flame travel distance $L_{\text{flame}}$.
💡 Key Concept: SI Knocking Definition: Detonation occurs NEAR THE END of combustion AFTER the spark plug fires, caused by end-gas auto-ignition.

[!TRAP] Spark Timing Rule for SI Knock: ADVANCING spark timing INCREASES knocking! To ELIMINATE knocking, spark timing MUST BE RETARDED.

2. Decision Tree
SI Knocking Diagnostic Check?
├─ Did combustion start BEFORE spark plug fired? → PRE-IGNITION (Hot-spot ignition)
└─ Did auto-ignition explode end-gas AFTER spark plug fired? → DETONATION / KNOCKING
   ├─ Cause: High compression ratio, advanced spark, low octane fuel, high inlet temp
   └─ Remedy: Retard spark timing, lower compression ratio, use HIGHER Octane fuel
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Detonation happens after spark; pre-ignition happens before spark.
  • [ ] Normal flame speed $20-30 \text{ m/s}$; detonation wave $1000-2000 \text{ m/s}$.
  • [ ] Advancing spark increases knock; retarding spark reduces knock.
  • [ ] HUCR = Max compression ratio without knock.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Differentiate between Detonation and Pre-ignition in a Spark-Ignition engine. - Solution: - Detonation: Spontaneous auto-ignition of the unburned end-gas after the spark plug has fired, caused by high end-gas temperature and pressure. - Pre-Ignition: Ignition of the air-fuel mixture by a hot spot (e.g., overheated spark plug electrode or carbon deposit) before the electrical spark occurs. - Key Insight: Timing relative to spark event (Detonation = After spark, Pre-ignition = Before spark).

3.2 CI Engine Knocking (Diesel Knock)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Diesel Knock Mechanism:
  • Fuel is injected into hot compressed air near TDC.
  • Ignition Delay Period $\tau_{id}$: Time interval between start of injection and start of auto-ignition. $\tau_{id} = \tau_{\text{physical}} + \tau_{\text{chemical}}$.
  • If $\tau_{id}$ is too long, a LARGE QUANTITY of fuel accumulates in the cylinder.
  • Upon ignition, all accumulated fuel burns simultaneously, producing an extremely rapid pressure rise $dP/d\theta > 4-5 \text{ bar/}^\circ\text{CA}$ and severe metallic knock.
  • Occurs at START of Combustion: Unlike SI knock (which occurs near the end of combustion), CI knock occurs at the BEGINNING of combustion!
  • Factors REDUCING Diesel Knocking:
  • HIGHER Compression Ratio $r \uparrow$: Increases compressed air temperature, shortening ignition delay.
  • HIGHER Inlet Air Temperature / Pressure: Shortens delay.
  • HIGHER Cetane Fuel: Chemical structure auto-ignites faster.
  • Higher Engine Turbulence / Swirl: Improves physical mixing $\tau_{\text{physical}} \downarrow$.
💡 Key Concept: Opposite Knock Trends (SI vs CI): - Compression Ratio $r \uparrow \implies$ SI Knock INCREASES, but CI Knock DECREASES! - Supercharging $\implies$ SI Knock INCREASES, but CI Knock DECREASES!

[!TRAP] Ignition Delay Effect: Long ignition delay INCREASES diesel knock! Short ignition delay REDUCES diesel knock!

2. Decision Tree
Diesel Knock Analysis?
├─ Is ignition delay tau_id LONG? → Excessive fuel accumulation → Severe Diesel Knock at combustion start
└─ Remedies to Reduce Diesel Knock:
   ├─ Increase compression ratio r (Raises compressed air temp)
   ├─ Increase inlet air pressure / supercharge
   └─ Use HIGHER Cetane fuel (Shortens chemical delay)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Diesel knock occurs at START of combustion.
  • [ ] Long ignition delay $\implies$ High diesel knock.
  • [ ] Higher compression ratio $r$ REDUCES diesel knock.
  • [ ] Higher Cetane number REDUCES diesel knock.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Explain why increasing the compression ratio affects knocking in SI and CI engines in opposite ways. - Solution: - SI Engine: Increasing $r$ raises end-gas pressure and temperature, shortening its auto-ignition delay and causing end-gas auto-ignition $\implies$ Knocking INCREASES. - CI Engine: Increasing $r$ raises compressed air temperature at TDC, shortening the ignition delay period so less fuel accumulates before ignition $\implies$ Knocking DECREASES. - Key Insight: $r \uparrow \implies \text{SI Knock } \uparrow$, $\text{CI Knock } \downarrow$.

3.3 Fuel Rating Systems (Octane vs Cetane)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Octane Number (SI Fuel Rating):
  • Measures knock resistance in petrol engines.
  • Iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane): $\text{ON} = 100$ (high knock resistance).
  • n-Heptane: $\text{ON} = 0$ (severe knock tendency).
  • Anti-Knock Index $\text{AKI} = \frac{\text{RON} + \text{MON}}{2}$.
  • Knock resistance by hydrocarbon structure: Aromatics > Naphthenes > Olefins > Paraffins.
  • Anti-knock additives: Tetraethyl Lead (TEL), MTBE, Ethanol.
  • Cetane Number (CI Fuel Rating):
  • Measures ignition readiness (short ignition delay) in diesel engines.
  • n-Cetane (hexadecane): $\text{CN} = 100$ (short ignition delay).
  • $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene: $\text{CN} = 0$ (long ignition delay) [or Heptamethylnonane $\text{CN} = 15$].
  • Ignition accelerators: Amyl nitrate, ethyl nitrate.
  • Inverse Relationship: High Octane fuel has LOW Cetane number; High Cetane fuel has LOW Octane number!
💡 Key Concept: Reference Fuel Pair Memory: - Octane Scale: Iso-octane $100$, n-Heptane $0$. - Cetane Scale: n-Cetane $100$, $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $0$.

[!TRAP] ON vs CN Ignition Quality: High Octane fuel RESISTS auto-ignition (slow ignition). High Cetane fuel AUTO-IGNITES EASILY (fast ignition). They are diametrically opposite!

2. Decision Tree
Fuel Rating Identification?
├─ Is fuel for Spark-Ignition (Petrol) engine? → OCTANE NUMBER (Iso-octane 100, n-Heptane 0)
│  └─ Goal: High ON resists detonation at high compression ratio
└─ Is fuel for Compression-Ignition (Diesel) engine? → CETANE NUMBER (n-Cetane 100, alpha-methyl-naphthalene 0)
   └─ Goal: High CN shortens ignition delay for smooth combustion
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Iso-octane $= 100$, n-Heptane $= 0$.
  • [ ] n-Cetane $= 100$, $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $= 0$.
  • [ ] $\text{AKI} = (\text{RON} + \text{MON})/2$.
  • [ ] Aromatics < Naphthenes < Olefins < Paraffins (increasing SI knock).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A petrol fuel sample matches the knock intensity of a blend containing $87\%$ Iso-octane and $13\%$ n-Heptane by volume in a CFR test engine. State its Octane Number. - Solution: - By definition, the Octane Number equals the percentage by volume of Iso-octane in a matching blend with n-Heptane. - Iso-octane percentage $= 87\% \implies \text{Octane Number } \text{ON} = 87$. - Key Insight: $\text{ON} = 87$.

3.4 Air-Fuel Ratios & Stoichiometry

1. Concept Pointers
  • Stoichiometric (Chemically Correct) Ratio:
  • Exact theoretical mass of air required for complete combustion of $1 \text{ kg}$ of fuel.
  • Petrol (Gasoline $\text{C}8\text{H}{18}$): $\approx 14.7:1 \text{ or } 15:1$ by mass.
  • Methanol $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$: $6.5:1$ by mass.
  • Diesel: $\approx 14.5:1$ by mass.
  • SI Engine Operating Ranges:
  • Cold Starting: $3:1 - 5:1$ (Very Rich, controlled by Choke).
  • Idling: $12:1 - 12.5:1$ (Rich for stability).
  • Maximum Power: $12.5:1 - 13.5:1$ (Slightly Rich).
  • Cruising / Economy: $16:1 - 16.5:1$ (Lean).
  • CI Engine Operating Range: Operates extremely LEAN $18:1 \text{ to } 70:1$ because load is controlled by fuel injection quantity (Quality Governing).
💡 Key Concept: Methanol Stoichiometric AFR: Methanol $\text{CH}_3\text{OH}$ requires only $6.5 \text{ kg}$ of air per $\text{kg}$ of fuel because its molecule already contains oxygen!
2. Decision Tree
Air-Fuel Mixture Requirement Check?
├─ Cold Starting (SI) → 3:1 to 5:1 (Extremely Rich)
├─ Maximum Power (SI) → 12.5:1 to 13.5:1 (Slightly Rich)
├─ Cruising Economy (SI) → 16:1 to 16.5:1 (Lean)
└─ CI Engine Range → 18:1 to 70:1 (Quality governed, always lean)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Petrol stoichiometric AFR $= 14.7:1$.
  • [ ] Methanol stoichiometric AFR $= 6.5:1$.
  • [ ] Cruising $\implies$ Lean mixture $16:1$.
  • [ ] CI engines always run lean $18-70:1$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Calculate the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio by mass for Methanol $\text{CH}3\text{OH}$. Air contains $23.2\%$ $\text{O}_2$ by mass. - Solution: - Reaction: $2 \text{CH}_3\text{OH} + 3 \text{O}_2 \to 2 \text{CO}_2 + 4 \text{H}_2\text{O}$ - Molar masses: $\text{CH}_3\text{OH} = 32 \text{ g/mol}$, $\text{O}_2 = 32 \text{ g/mol}$. - $2 \times 32 = 64 \text{ kg}$ methanol requires $3 \times 32 = 96 \text{ kg}$ $\text{O}_2$. - Air mass required $= \frac{96}{0.232} = 413.79 \text{ kg}$ air. - $\text{AFR}{\text{stoich}} = \frac{413.79}{64} = 6.465:1 \approx 6.5:1$. - Key Insight: Methanol stoichiometric $\text{AFR} = 6.5:1$.

3.5 Supercharging & Turbocharging Principles

1. Concept Pointers
  • Supercharging: Increasing intake charge mass density entering the cylinder above ambient density.
  • Density Ratio:
    $${\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) \left(\frac{T_1}{T_2}\right)}$$
  • Effect on SI vs CI Engines:
  • SI Engine: Supercharging raises charge temperature and pressure $\implies$ SHORTENS end-gas ignition delay $\implies$ INCREASES KNOCKING TENDENCY! Boost is limited by fuel Octane rating.
  • CI Engine: Supercharging raises compressed air temperature $\implies$ SHORTENS ignition delay $\implies$ DECREASES DIESEL KNOCK! Highly preferred.
  • Supercharger Types:
  • Positive Displacement: Roots (lobe), Vane, Screw.
  • Dynamic: Centrifugal.
  • Reciprocating compressors are NOT used as superchargers (high pressure, low flow rate, heavy).
  • Turbocharging: Centrifugal compressor driven by an EXHAUST GAS TURBINE mounted on a common shaft. Uses waste exhaust energy.
💡 Key Concept: Supercharging Suitability: - CI (Diesel) Engine $\implies$ Supercharging REDUCES knock, improves combustion, raises power! - SI (Petrol) Engine $\implies$ Supercharging INCREASES knock, requires intercooling & high Octane fuel!
2. Decision Tree
Supercharging Engine Effect Check?
├─ CI Engine → Supercharging REDUCES ignition delay → REDUCES DIESEL KNOCK (Highly beneficial)
└─ SI Engine → Supercharging raises end-gas temp → INCREASES DETONATION (Limited boost)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Boost density ratio $\frac{\rho_2}{\rho_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_1} \frac{T_1}{T_2}$.
  • [ ] Turbocharger driven by exhaust turbine.
  • [ ] Reciprocating compressors not used as superchargers.
  • [ ] CI supercharging reduces knock; SI supercharging increases knock.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Why are reciprocating compressors not used as engine superchargers? - Solution: - Reciprocating compressors deliver low volume flow rates at high pressure ratios, whereas supercharging requires a large, continuous volume flow of air at moderate pressure ratios $1.3-2.5 \text{ bar}$. - Reciprocating compressors are also bulky, heavy, and have high mechanical friction losses. - Key Insight: Superchargers require high continuous volume flow $\implies$ Rotary / Dynamic compressors used.

3.6 Combustion Chamber Design for SI & CI Engines

1. Concept Pointers
  • SI Engine Chamber Design:
  • Objectives: Short flame travel distance $L_{\text{flame}}$, compact volume with low surface-to-volume ratio $A/V$, central spark plug, high turbulence (squish/swirl), no uncooled hot spots.
  • Progression:
    1. T-Head: Valves on opposite sides. Longest flame path, high knock tendency (Obsolete).
    2. L-Head (Side Valve): Valves on same side in block. Compact, but limited compression ratio and breathing.
    3. I-Head (Overhead Valve - OHV): Valves in cylinder head. Excellent breathing, central plug location.
    4. F-Head: One valve in head, one in block.
    5. Modern Pent-Roof / Hemispherical: 4 valves per cylinder, central spark plug $\implies$ Shortest flame path, highest knock resistance.
  • CI Engine Chamber Design:
  • Objectives: High air utilization, controlled mixing rate, short ignition delay, smooth pressure rise.
  • Open Chambers (Direct Injection - DI):
    • Fuel injected directly into piston crown cavity (Shallow, Hemispherical, Toroidal, Cylindrical).
    • Requires high injection pressure $200-1000+ \text{ bar}$. Highest fuel economy.
  • Divided Chambers (Indirect Injection - IDI):
    • Primary combustion in pre-combustion chamber or Ricardo Swirl chamber, connecting to main cylinder via a narrow throat.
    • Strong ordered swirl allows lower injection pressure. Quiet operation. Uses Glow Plugs for cold starting.
💡 Key Concept: Chamber Classification: - SI Chambers $\implies$ T-Head, L-Head, I-Head (OHV), F-Head, Pent-Roof. - CI Chambers $\implies$ Direct Injection (Open cavity in piston) vs Indirect Injection (Swirl / Pre-combustion chamber).
2. Decision Tree
Combustion Chamber Type Identification?
├─ Spark-Ignition Engine Chambers:
│  ├─ T-Head → Valves opposite sides (Worst knock)
│  ├─ L-Head → Side valves in block
│  └─ I-Head / Pent-Roof → Overhead valves, central plug (Best knock resistance)
└─ Compression-Ignition Engine Chambers:
   ├─ Direct Injection (DI) → Open cavity in piston (High efficiency)
   └─ Indirect Injection (IDI) → Swirl / Pre-chamber + Glow plug (Quiet running)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] T-head has worst knock resistance.
  • [ ] Pent-roof / Hemispherical has central plug $\implies$ lowest knock.
  • [ ] CI DI $= \text{Open chamber in piston crown}$.
  • [ ] CI IDI $= \text{Swirl / Pre-combustion chamber + Glow plug}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Compare Direct Injection (DI) and Indirect Injection (IDI) combustion chambers in Diesel engines. - Solution: - Direct Injection (DI): Fuel injected directly into an open chamber in the piston crown. Uses multi-hole high-pressure injectors. Higher thermal efficiency and lower fuel consumption. - Indirect Injection (IDI): Fuel injected into a separate pre-combustion or swirl chamber. Uses single-hole pintle injectors at lower pressure. Smoother, quieter running, but higher heat losses and requires glow plugs for cold starting. - Key Insight: DI = Open chamber (High efficiency); IDI = Swirl chamber + Glow plug.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is detonation in an SI engine and when does it occur?
A: Detonation is the spontaneous auto-ignition of unburned end-gas ahead of the spark-initiated flame front, occurring near the END of combustion after the spark plug has fired.

Q2: Differentiate between Detonation and Pre-ignition.
A: Detonation occurs AFTER the spark plug fires due to end-gas auto-ignition. Pre-ignition occurs BEFORE the spark plug fires due to ignition from a hot spot.

Q3: Define Highest Useful Compression Ratio (HUCR).
A: HUCR is the maximum compression ratio at which an SI engine can operate without knocking on a specific fuel under standard test conditions.

Q4: What causes Diesel Knock in a CI engine and when does it occur?
A: Diesel knock is caused by a long ignition delay that allows a large mass of fuel to accumulate in the cylinder, which explodes simultaneously at the START of combustion.

Q5: How does increasing compression ratio affect knocking in SI vs CI engines?
A: Increasing compression ratio INCREASES knocking in SI engines, but DECREASES knocking in CI engines (by shortening ignition delay).

Q6: Define Octane Number and state its primary reference fuels.
A: Octane Number measures knock resistance in SI fuels. Iso-octane is assigned $\text{ON} = 100$ and n-Heptane is assigned $\text{ON} = 0$.

Q7: Define Cetane Number and state its primary reference fuels.
A: Cetane Number measures ignition quality (short ignition delay) in CI fuels. n-Cetane (hexadecane) is assigned $\text{CN} = 100$ and $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene is assigned $\text{CN} = 0$.

Q8: What is Anti-Knock Index (AKI)?
A: $\text{AKI} = \frac{\text{RON} + \text{MON}}{2}$, the average of Research Octane Number and Motor Octane Number.

Q9: State the stoichiometric air-fuel ratio for Gasoline and Methanol.
A: Gasoline $\approx 14.7:1 \text{ or } 15:1$; Methanol $= 6.5:1$.

Q10: Why does supercharging increase knocking in SI engines but reduce knocking in CI engines?
A: Supercharging raises intake temperature and pressure. In SI engines, this heats the end-gas and promotes detonation. In CI engines, this shortens ignition delay, smoothing combustion.

Q11: Why are reciprocating compressors not used as superchargers?
A: Because superchargers require continuous high volume flow at moderate pressure ratios, whereas reciprocating compressors deliver low volume flow at high pressure ratios and are heavy.

Q12: How is the compressor of a turbocharger driven?
A: By a gas turbine driven by engine exhaust gases mounted on the same shaft.

Q13: Why is a central spark plug location preferred in modern SI engine combustion chambers?
A: A central spark plug minimizes the flame travel distance to all end-gas regions, completing combustion faster and reducing knocking tendency.

Q14: Differentiate between Open and Divided combustion chambers in Diesel engines.
A: Open chambers (DI) inject fuel directly into a cavity in the piston crown (higher efficiency). Divided chambers (IDI) inject fuel into a separate swirl/pre-combustion chamber (quieter running, uses glow plugs).

Q15: Rank the hydrocarbon families in order of INCREASING detonation tendency in SI engines.
A: Aromatics < Naphthenes < Olefins < Paraffins (Aromatics resist knock best; straight-chain Paraffins knock most).

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • SI Knocking: Occurs AFTER spark near end of combustion. Fix by retarding spark, lowering $r$, higher Octane.
  • CI Knocking: Occurs at START of combustion due to LONG ignition delay. Fix by higher $r$, higher inlet temp, higher Cetane.
  • Octane Reference Fuels: Iso-octane ($100$, high knock resistance), n-Heptane ($0$, high knock tendency).
  • Cetane Reference Fuels: n-Cetane ($100$, short delay), $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene ($0$, long delay).
  • Stoichiometry: Gasoline $\approx 15:1$, Methanol $= 6.5:1$.
  • Supercharging: SI Knock INCREASES, CI Knock DECREASES. Turbo driven by EXHAUST TURBINE.
  • SI Chambers: T-head (worst knock) $\to$ L-head $\to$ I-head OHV $\to$ Pent-roof (central plug, best knock resistance).
  • CI Chambers: Direct Injection (DI, open cavity in piston) vs Indirect Injection (IDI, swirl chamber + glow plug).
