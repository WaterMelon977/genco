💡 Golden Revision Rules & High-Yield Exam Traps

  • Compression Intercooling: Perfect intercooling pressure ratio $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$. Work per stage is equal.
  • Cycle Efficiency Rules: At same compression ratio $r \implies \eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$. At same peak pressure $P_{\text{max}} \implies \eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.
  • Camshaft Kinematics: In a 4-stroke engine, camshaft speed $N_{\text{cam}} = N_{\text{crank}}/2$. In a 2-stroke engine, $N_{\text{cam}} = N_{\text{crank}}$.
  • Engine Governing: SI (Petrol) engines use Quantity Governing (throttle controls mixture volume). CI (Diesel) engines use Quality Governing (controls fuel injected into excess air).
  • Opposite Knock Trends: Higher compression ratio $r \uparrow$ or Supercharging INCREASES knock in SI engines, but DECREASES knock in CI engines!
  • Reference Fuels: Octane rating (Iso-octane = 100, n-Heptane = 0). Cetane rating (n-Cetane = 100, $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene = 0). High Octane fuels have LOW Cetane rating!
  • Stoichiometric AFR: Gasoline = 14.7:1, Methanol = 6.5:1. CI engines always operate lean (18:1–70:1).
  • Lubrication Regimes: Hydrostatic lubrication gives the LOWEST starting friction at zero speed ($N=0$). Mist lubrication is used exclusively in 2-stroke petrol engines. Dry-sump systems require at least 2 pumps (scavenge + pressure).
  • Ignition Systems: Magneto ignition requires NO BATTERY and produces stronger spark at high engine speeds.
