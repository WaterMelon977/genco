$${\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}}}$$
 $\text{bar}$ or $\text{Pa}$ Two-stage compressor with perfect intercooling for minimum work.

$${\boxed{r = \left(\frac{P_{\text{discharge}}}{P_{\text{suction}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}}}$$
 Dimensionless Pressure ratio per stage for equal stage work and perfect intercooling.

$${\boxed{P_k = P_1 \cdot r^k}}$$
 $\text{bar}$ or $\text{Pa}$ Pressure after $k$-th compression stage in multi-stage system.

$${\boxed{W_{\text{min}} = 2 \cdot \frac{n}{n-1} m R T_1 \left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{2n}} - 1\right]}}$$
 $\text{J}$ or $\text{kJ}$ Minimum total work with equal stage ratios and $T_{\text{intercooler}} = T_1$.
Slip Factor $\sigma$ (Centrifugal)
$${\boxed{\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}'}{u_2}}}$$
 Dimensionless $V_{w2}'$ = actual exit whirl velocity, $u_2$ = impeller tip speed. $\sigma < 1$.
Euler Work / Specific Power $W_{\text{Euler}}$
$${\boxed{W_{\text{Euler}} = u_2 V_{w2} = \sigma u_2^2}}$$
 $\text{J/kg}$ Ideal specific work input per kg for radial exit vanes $V_{w2} = u_2$.
Degree of Reaction $R$ (Axial)
$${\boxed{R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{h_3 - h_1}}}$$
 Dimensionless Fraction of total stage static enthalpy rise occurring in rotor blades.
Blade Loading Coefficient $\psi$
$${\boxed{\psi = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{u^2}}}$$
 Dimensionless $\Delta h_{\text{stage}}$ = specific work input, $u$ = mean blade speed. $\psi \propto 1/u^2$.

$${\boxed{\Omega = \frac{W_{\text{actual}}}{W_{\text{theoretical}}}}}$$
 Dimensionless Accounts for non-uniform axial velocity profile $0.98 \to 0.85$.
Roots Blower Pressure Ratio $P_2/P_1$
$${\boxed{W_{\text{Roots}} = (P_2 - P_1) V_s = P_1 V_s \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1} - 1\right)}}$$
 $\text{J}$ Irreversible backflow work formula for un-cooled Roots blower.

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Positive-Displacement Compressor: A machine where gas is drawn into a confined space and compressed by mechanically reducing the boundary volume (e.g., Reciprocating, Roots blower, Screw, Vane, Scroll).
  • Dynamic Compressor: A machine where rotating impellers or blades continuously impart kinetic velocity to the gas, which is subsequently converted into static pressure in diffusers (e.g., Centrifugal, Axial-flow).
  • Clearance Ratio $C$: The ratio of clearance volume $V_c$ to swept volume $V_s$ $C = V_c / V_s$. Clearance accommodates valves but reduces free air delivery.
  • Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$: The ratio of actual free air volume drawn per stroke at suction conditions to the swept stroke volume $V_s$.
  • Isothermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$: The benchmark performance ratio comparing ideal minimum isothermal work to actual indicated compression work.
  • Perfect Intercooling: The cooling process in a multi-stage compressor where the gas temperature leaving the intercooler is reduced back to the initial inlet temperature $T_1$.
  • Slip Factor $\sigma$: The ratio of actual exit whirl velocity $V_{w2}'$ to ideal tip speed $u_2$ in centrifugal impellers, caused by inertia fluid circulation between impeller vanes.
  • Pre-Whirl: The intentional rotation imparted to incoming air via inlet guide vanes to reduce relative inlet velocity $V_{r1}$ below Mach 1 and reduce aerodynamic losses.
  • Degree of Reaction $R$: The ratio of static enthalpy (or static pressure) rise in the rotor to the total enthalpy rise across the complete compressor stage.
  • Stalling: Local boundary layer separation of airflow from the suction surface of axial compressor blades caused by an excessively high positive angle of incidence.
  • Surging: Global, transient, high-amplitude instability characterized by periodic flow reversal across the entire compressor system.
  • Work-Done Factor $\Omega$: A correction factor $0.98 \text{ to } 0.85$ that reduces theoretical stage work to account for boundary layer thickening along casing walls in multi-stage axial compressors.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Gas Constant of Air $R_{\text{air}}$: $0.287 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K} = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • Adiabatic Index of Air $\gamma$: $1.40$.
  • Typical Single-Stage Centrifugal Pressure Ratio: $4.0 \text{ to } 5.0$.
  • Typical Single-Stage Axial Pressure Ratio: $1.15 \text{ to } 1.30$.

3.1 Classification of Positive-Displacement vs Dynamic Compressors

1. Concept Pointers
  • Positive-Displacement Category:
  • Traps a discrete volume of gas and compresses it mechanically.
  • Reciprocating: Piston-cylinder with spring-loaded automatic valves. Ideal for high pressure ratios and low-to-medium mass flow rates.
  • Rotary Positive-Displacement: Roots blower, sliding-vane, lobe, screw, scroll. Continuous/near-continuous delivery, no suction/delivery valves.
  • Roots Blower: Consists of two two-lobe or three-lobe rotors mesh-driven without internal compression. Compression takes place by backflow from the receiver!
  • Dynamic Category:
  • Continuous energy transfer from high-speed rotating blades to gas.
  • Centrifugal: Radial flow. Air enters axially at eye, turns $90^\circ$, and leaves radially. Higher pressure ratio per stage $4 \text{ to } 5$.
  • Axial-Flow: Axial flow. Air enters and leaves parallel to shaft axis. Lower pressure ratio per stage $1.15 \text{ to } 1.30$, but extremely high mass flow rate per unit frontal area.
  • Selection Criteria:
  • Aircraft Jet Engines: Axial-flow compressor preferred due to low frontal aerodynamic drag and high mass flow rate.
  • High Pressure Industrial / Refrigeration (up to 100 TR): Reciprocating compressor preferred.
  • Large Refrigeration Plants: Centrifugal compressor preferred.
💡 Key Concept: Roots Blower Classification Trap: A Roots blower is a ROTARY POSITIVE-DISPLACEMENT machine, NOT a dynamic machine! Compression occurs by backflow of high-pressure air from receiver when delivery port opens.

[!TRAP] Aircraft Compressor Selection Trap: Axial-flow compressors are selected for aircraft jet engines because of low frontal area and high mass flow rate per unit area, NOT because they have a higher pressure ratio per stage (Centrifugal has $4 \times$ higher pressure ratio per stage!).

  • [ ] Dynamic: Centrifugal, Axial-flow.
  • [ ] Roots blower is positive-displacement (rotary).
  • [ ] Axial preferred for aircraft (low frontal area, high mass flow).
  • [ ] Centrifugal pressure ratio per stage $4 - 5$ > Axial $1.15 - 1.3$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Compressor Selection for Aircraft Propulsion - Problem: Which type of compressor is universally selected for commercial aircraft jet engines, and what primary architectural advantage dictates this choice? - Solution: 1. Identify aircraft compressor requirements: High mass flow rate of air combined with minimal aerodynamic drag (small frontal cross-sectional area). 2. Compare Centrifugal vs Axial-Flow: - Centrifugal compressor has a large radial diameter (impeller + diffuser + volute), creating high frontal drag. - Axial-flow compressor allows straight-through flow with a compact frontal area and handles very large mass flow rates across multiple inline stages. 3. Answer: Axial-Flow Compressor, chosen primarily for small frontal area and high mass-flow capacity. - Key Insight: Always associate Axial Compressor $\implies$ Aircraft jet engines due to small frontal area.

Worked Example 2: Positive-Displacement vs Dynamic Characteristics - Problem: Categorize the following compressors as Positive-Displacement or Dynamic: (a) Roots Blower, (b) Centrifugal Compressor, (c) Screw Compressor, (d) Axial Compressor. - Solution: 1. Roots Blower $\implies$ Positive-Displacement (Rotary). 2. Centrifugal Compressor $\implies$ Dynamic (Roto-dynamic). 3. Screw Compressor $\implies$ Positive-Displacement (Rotary). 4. Axial Compressor $\implies$ Dynamic (Roto-dynamic). - Key Insight: Roots blower and screw compressors are positive-displacement machines despite having rotary motion.

3.2 Reciprocating Compressors: Work, Efficiencies & Clearance

1. Concept Pointers
  • Thermodynamic Compression Work:
  • Isothermal Compression $n = 1$: $W_{\text{iso}} = m R T_1 \ln(P_2/P_1)$. Gives the MINIMUM possible work input!
  • Polytropic Compression $1 < n < \gamma$: $W_{\text{poly}} = \frac{n}{n-1} m R T_1 \left[(P_2/P_1)^{\frac{n-1}{n}} - 1\right]$.
  • Isentropic Compression $n = \gamma = 1.4$: $W_{\text{isen}} = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} m R T_1 \left[(P_2/P_1)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1\right]$. Gives the MAXIMUM work input!
  • Isothermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$:
  • Benchmark efficiency comparing ideal minimum isothermal work to actual indicated work:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{W_{\text{iso}}}{W_{\text{actual}}}}}$$
  • Clearance Volume $V_c$ & Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$:
  • Clearance volume $V_c$ is mandatory to prevent piston-cylinder head collision and accommodate automatic spring valves.
  • Gas trapped in clearance volume expands from $P_2$ to $P_1$ during suction stroke before fresh air can enter, reducing effective suction volume $V_{\text{suction}}$.
  • Volumetric efficiency:
    $${\boxed{\eta_v = \frac{V_{\text{suction}}}{V_s} = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}}}$$
  • Effect of Clearance on Work per kg: Clearance volume REDUCES volumetric efficiency and mass delivered per stroke, but DOES NOT affect indicated work done per kg of air delivered!
💡 Key Concept: Clearance Volume Independence: Clearance volume $V_c$ reduces free air delivery and volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$, but does NOT alter the indicated work done per kg of air delivered! The energy stored in clearance gas during compression is recovered during its expansion stroke.

[!TRAP] Volumetric Efficiency Trend: Volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$ DECREASES when clearance ratio $C$ INCREASES or pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$ INCREASES. If $P_2/P_1$ becomes too high, $\eta_v \to 0$ and no fresh air is drawn!

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Reciprocating Compressor Thermodynamic Analysis
│
├── Determine Compression Process Type:
│   ├── Isothermal (n = 1)   ──► MINIMUM WORK!  W_iso = m * R * T_1 * ln(P_2/P_1)
│   ├── Polytropic (1 < n < gamma) ──► W = [n/(n-1)] * m * R * T_1 * [ (P_2/P_1)^((n-1)/n) - 1 ]
│   └── Isentropic (n = gamma)     ──► MAXIMUM WORK! W_isen = [gamma/(gamma-1)] * m * R * T_1 * [ ... ]
│
└── Evaluate Volumetric Efficiency:
    └── eta_v = 1 + C - C * (P_2 / P_1)^(1/n)
        ├── If Pressure Ratio P_2/P_1 Increases ──► eta_v DECREASES
        └── If Clearance Ratio C Increases       ──► eta_v DECREASES
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Minimum work occurs during isothermal compression $n = 1$.
  • [ ] Isothermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}} = W_{\text{iso}} / W_{\text{actual}}$.
  • [ ] Volumetric efficiency $\eta_v = 1 + C - C(P_2/P_1)^{1/n}$.
  • [ ] $\eta_v$ falls as $C \uparrow$ or $P_2/P_1 \uparrow$.
  • [ ] Clearance volume does NOT affect work per kg of air.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Volumetric Efficiency Calculation - Problem: A single-stage reciprocating air compressor has a clearance ratio $C = 0.05$ $5\%$ and compresses air from $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ to $P_2 = 6 \text{ bar}$. The expansion index $n = 1.30$. Calculate the volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$. - Solution: 1. Substitute values into volumetric efficiency formula:

$${\eta_v = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = 1 + 0.05 - 0.05 \left(\frac{6}{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{1.30}}}$$
2. Compute pressure ratio factor $(6)^{1/1.30} = 6^{0.76923} \approx 3.944$:
$${\eta_v = 1.05 - 0.05 \times 3.944 = 1.05 - 0.1972 = 0.8528 \implies 85.28\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta_v = 85.28\%$. Clearance volume reduced suction capacity by $14.72\%$.

Worked Example 2: Isothermal vs Polytropic Compression Work - Problem: Air of mass $m = 1 \text{ kg}$ at $T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$ is compressed from $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ to $P_2 = 4 \text{ bar}$. Calculate: (a) Minimum work required (isothermal), (b) Polytropic work for index $n = 1.30$. $R = 0.287 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate Isothermal Work $W_{\text{iso}}$:

$${W_{\text{iso}} = m R T_1 \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = 1 \times 0.287 \times 300 \times \ln(4) = 86.1 \times 1.3863 \approx 119.36 \text{ kJ}}$$
2. Calculate Polytropic Work $W_{\text{poly}}$:
$${W_{\text{poly}} = \frac{n}{n-1} m R T_1 \left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}} - 1\right] = \frac{1.30}{0.30} \times 86.1 \times \left[4^{\frac{0.30}{1.30}} - 1\right]}$$
$${W_{\text{poly}} = 4.3333 \times 86.1 \times [4^{0.23077} - 1] = 373.1 \times [1.3772 - 1] = 373.1 \times 0.3772 \approx 140.73 \text{ kJ}}$$
- Key Insight: $W_{\text{iso}} = 119.36 \text{ kJ}$, $W_{\text{poly}} = 140.73 \text{ kJ}$. Isothermal compression saves $21.37 \text{ kJ}$ $15.2\%$ of work!

Worked Example 3: Maximum Single-Stage Pressure Ratio Limit - Problem: A reciprocating compressor has clearance ratio $C = 0.04$ and expansion index $n = 1.25$. Determine the maximum pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$ beyond which volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$ becomes zero. - Solution: 1. Set $\eta_v = 0$ in volumetric efficiency equation:

$${\eta_v = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = 0 \implies 1 + 0.04 = 0.04 \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{1.25}}}$$
2. Simplify:
$${\frac{1.04}{0.04} = 26 = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{1.25}}}$$
3. Solve for maximum pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$:
$${\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 26^{1.25} \approx 57.65}$$
- Key Insight: At $P_2/P_1 = 57.65$, clearance gas expands to fill the entire cylinder volume during suction, reducing air intake to zero!

Worked Example 4: Isothermal Efficiency Calculation - Problem: A compressor requires an indicated power input of $P_{\text{ind}} = 50 \text{ kW}$ while compressing $0.20 \text{ kg/s}$ of air from $1 \text{ bar}, 300 \text{ K}$ to $8 \text{ bar}$. Calculate its isothermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate ideal isothermal power $P_{\text{iso}}$:

$${P_{\text{iso}} = \dot{m} R T_1 \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) = 0.20 \times 0.287 \times 300 \times \ln(8) = 17.22 \times 2.0794 \approx 35.81 \text{ kW}}$$
2. Calculate isothermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$:
$${\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{P_{\text{iso}}}{P_{\text{ind}}} = \frac{35.81}{50.0} = 0.7162 \implies 71.62\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta_{\text{iso}} = 71.62\%$. Compares actual indicated power against pure isothermal benchmark power.

3.3 Multi-Stage Compression & Intercooling Optimization

1. Concept Pointers
  • Need for Multi-Staging:
  • High single-stage pressure ratio causes low volumetric efficiency $\eta_v \to 0$, high delivery temperature (causes lubrication oil breakdown/explosion risk), and heavy piston forces requiring huge flywheels.
  • Role of Intercooling:
  • Heat exchanger placed between stages cools gas back to initial temperature $T_1$ (Perfect Intercooling).
  • Perfect intercooling reduces total work input (shaded area on $P$-$V$ diagram), lowers delivery temperature, improves volumetric efficiency, and reduces cylinder sizing.
  • Conditions for Minimum Work in $N$-Stage Compressor:
  • Equal pressure ratio across each stage: $r = (P_{\text{discharge}}/P_{\text{suction}})^{1/N}$.
  • Equal work done in each stage $W_1 = W_2 = \dots = W_N$.
  • Perfect intercooling between all stages ($T_{\text{inlet}}$ to each stage $= T_1$).
  • Optimum Intermediate Pressures:
  • Two-Stage Compressor ($P_1$ to $P_2$):
    $${\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}}}$$
  • Three-Stage Compressor ($P_1$ to $P_4$):
    $${P_{i1} = P_1^{2/3} P_4^{1/3}, \quad P_{i2} = P_1^{1/3} P_4^{2/3}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Geometric Progression of Pressures: For minimum work with perfect intercooling in multi-stage compressors, intermediate pressures form a GEOMETRIC PROGRESSION $P_1, P_{i1}, P_{i2}, \dots, P_N$. $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$ for 2 stages.

[!TRAP] Arithmetic vs Geometric Mean Trap: Examiner options often list intermediate pressure as arithmetic mean $P_i = \frac{P_1 + P_2}{2}$. This is WRONG! Correct formula is ALWAYS geometric mean $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$.

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Multi-Stage Intercooled Compressor Optimization
│
├── Is Perfect Intercooling specified? (T_inlet to all stages = T_1)
│   ├── YES ──► Minimum Work Conditions Apply:
│   │           ├── Stage Pressure Ratio: r = (P_delivery / P_suction)^(1/N)
│   │           ├── Intermediate Pressure (2-stage): P_i = sqrt(P_1 * P_2)
│   │           └── Total Work: W_total = N * W_single_stage(ratio = r)
│   │
│   └── NO  ──► Imperfect Intercooling (T_inlet2 > T_1): W_total > W_min
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Optimum intermediate pressure $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$.
  • [ ] Stage pressure ratio $r = (P_N / P_1)^{1/N}$.
  • [ ] Minimum work requires: equal pressure ratios, equal stage work, perfect intercooling.
  • [ ] Multi-staging INCREASES volumetric efficiency and REDUCES flywheel size.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Three-Stage Compressor Intermediate Pressures - Problem: A three-stage reciprocating air compressor receives air at $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ and delivers it at $P_4 = 27 \text{ bar}$. Assuming perfect intercooling and minimum work conditions, calculate: (a) Stage pressure ratio $r$, (b) First intercooler pressure $P_2$, (c) Second intercooler pressure $P_3$. - Solution: 1. Calculate stage pressure ratio $r$:

$${r = \left(\frac{P_4}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = \left(\frac{27}{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3.0}$$
2. Calculate First Intercooler Pressure $P_2$:
$${P_2 = P_1 \times r = 1 \times 3.0 = 3.0 \text{ bar}}$$
3. Calculate Second Intercooler Pressure $P_3$:
$${P_3 = P_2 \times r = 3.0 \times 3.0 = 9.0 \text{ bar}}$$
Check delivery: $P_4 = P_3 \times r = 9.0 \times 3.0 = 27.0 \text{ bar}$ (Verified!). - Key Insight: $P_2 = 3 \text{ bar}$, $P_3 = 9 \text{ bar}$. Pressures follow geometric progression $1 \to 3 \to 9 \to 27$.

Worked Example 2: Minimum Two-Stage Work Calculation - Problem: Air of mass $m = 1 \text{ kg}$ at $T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$ is compressed from $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ to $P_2 = 16 \text{ bar}$ in a two-stage compressor with perfect intercooling $n = 1.30, R = 0.287 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. Calculate the minimum total work input $W_{\text{min}}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate stage pressure ratio $r = \sqrt{16/1} = 4.0$. 2. Work per stage $W_{\text{stage}}$:

$${W_{\text{stage}} = \frac{n}{n-1} m R T_1 \left[r^{\frac{n-1}{n}} - 1\right] = \frac{1.30}{0.30} \times 0.287 \times 300 \times \left[4^{\frac{0.30}{1.30}} - 1\right]}$$
$${W_{\text{stage}} = 373.1 \times [1.3772 - 1] = 373.1 \times 0.3772 = 140.73 \text{ kJ}}$$
3. Total minimum 2-stage work:
$${W_{\text{min}} = 2 \times W_{\text{stage}} = 2 \times 140.73 = 281.46 \text{ kJ}}$$
- Key Insight: $W_{\text{min}} = 281.46 \text{ kJ}$. For comparison, single-stage polytropic work for $16 \text{ bar}$ ratio would be $373.1 \times [16^{0.23077} - 1] = 373.1 \times 0.8968 = 334.6 \text{ kJ}$ (two-stage saves $53.1 \text{ kJ}$ or $15.9\%$).

Worked Example 3: Four-Stage Intercooler Pressures - Problem: A four-stage air compressor operates between suction pressure $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ and final delivery pressure $P_5 = 16 \text{ bar}$ under minimum work conditions. Calculate the intermediate pressure $P_3$ at the inlet to the third stage. - Solution: 1. Calculate stage pressure ratio $r$:

$${r = (16/1)^{1/4} = (2^4)^{1/4} = 2.0}$$
2. Intermediate pressure at inlet to stage 3 $P_3 = P_1 \cdot r^2$:
$${P_3 = 1.0 \times (2.0)^2 = 4.0 \text{ bar}}$$
- Key Insight: $P_3 = 4.0 \text{ bar}$. Intermediate pressures are $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}, P_2 = 2 \text{ bar}, P_3 = 4 \text{ bar}, P_4 = 8 \text{ bar}, P_5 = 16 \text{ bar}$.

Worked Example 4: Work Saving Percentage by Perfect Intercooling - Problem: Compare single-stage work $W_1 = 334.6 \text{ kJ}$ vs two-stage work with perfect intercooling $W_2 = 281.46 \text{ kJ}$ for $P_2/P_1 = 16$. Calculate the percentage work saved by perfect intercooling. - Solution: 1. Work saved $\Delta W = W_1 - W_2 = 334.6 - 281.46 = 53.14 \text{ kJ}$. 2. Percentage work saved:

$${\% \text{ Work Saved} = \frac{\Delta W}{W_1} \times 100\% = \frac{53.14}{334.6} \times 100\% \approx 15.88\%}$$
- Key Insight: Perfect intercooling saves $15.88\%$ of compression work input.

3.4 Centrifugal Compressors (Impeller, Diffuser, Volute & Losses)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Static Pressure Rise Locations:
  • Air enters eye axially, turns radially, passes through rotating impeller, fixed vaned/vaneless diffuser, and volute casing.
  • Static pressure rises in ALL THREE components: Impeller (due to centrifugal force), Diffuser (conversion of kinetic energy to static pressure), and Volute Casing (diverging channel).
  • Slip Factor $\sigma$:
  • Fluid inertia causes relative circulation between impeller vanes, so actual exit whirl velocity $V_{w2}'$ is less than theoretical whirl velocity $V_{w2}$.
  • Slip factor $\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}'}{u_2}$. For finite radial vanes $\beta_2 = 90^\circ$, ideal $V_{w2} = u_2 \implies \sigma < 1$.
  • Blade Types & Performance Comparison:
  • Forward-Curved Vanes $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$: Gives highest pressure ratio and work per stage, but lowest efficiency and unstable operating characteristic.
  • Backward-Curved Vanes $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$: Gives highest efficiency and stable self-limiting head-capacity curve. Standard choice for industrial compressors.
  • Radial Vanes $\beta_2 = 90^\circ$: High mechanical strength, resists centrifugal stress at high rotational speeds.
  • Pre-Whirl & Inlet Guide Vanes:
  • Pre-whirl vanes impart initial swirl at eye entry to lower relative inlet velocity $V_{r1}$, preventing supersonic shock losses $M_1 < 1$, though slightly reducing stage work.
  • Incidence Loss:
  • Aerodynamic shock loss occurring at impeller entry when relative velocity vector angle does not match inlet blade angle.
💡 Key Concept: Pressure Rise Distribution: In a centrifugal compressor, static pressure rise occurs in ALL THREE stages: Impeller $\approx 50\%$, Diffuser $\approx 40\%$, and Volute Casing $\approx 10\%$. It is NEVER confined to the diffuser alone!

[!TRAP] Forward vs Backward Blade Trap: Forward-curved blades $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$ produce HIGHER pressure ratio per stage, but Backward-curved blades $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$ produce HIGHER efficiency!

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Centrifugal Compressor Aerodynamic Analysis
│
├── Where does static pressure rise?
│   └── Impeller + Diffuser + Volute Casing (All 3!)
│
├── Select Impeller Blade Geometry:
│   ├── Forward-Curved (beta_2 > 90 deg) ──► Highest pressure ratio, lower efficiency
│   ├── Radial Vanes    (beta_2 = 90 deg) ──► Maximum structural strength at high speed
│   └── Backward-Curved (beta_2 < 90 deg) ──► Highest efficiency, stable falling head curve
│
└── Calculate Actual Specific Work (Radial Vanes):
    └── W_actual = sigma * u_2^2   (where slip factor sigma = V_w2' / u_2)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Static pressure rises in impeller, diffuser, and volute.
  • [ ] Slip factor $\sigma = V_{w2}'/u_2 < 1$.
  • [ ] Radial vanes specific work $W = \sigma u_2^2$.
  • [ ] Backward blades $\implies$ maximum efficiency.
  • [ ] Pre-whirl reduces inlet Mach number $M_1 < 1$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Centrifugal Compressor Work & Slip Factor - Problem: A centrifugal compressor with radial impeller vanes $\beta_2 = 90^\circ$ has impeller tip speed $u_2 = 400 \text{ m/s}$. The slip factor $\sigma = 0.90$. Calculate: (a) Actual whirl velocity $V_{w2}'$, (b) Specific work input in $\text{kJ/kg}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate actual whirl velocity $V_{w2}'$:

$${\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}'}{u_2} \implies V_{w2}' = \sigma \cdot u_2 = 0.90 \times 400 = 360 \text{ m/s}}$$
2. Calculate specific work input $W$:
$${W = u_2 \cdot V_{w2}' = 400 \times 360 = 144000 \text{ J/kg} = 144.0 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
- Key Insight: $W = 144.0 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Direct application of $W = \sigma u_2^2$.

Worked Example 2: Centrifugal Stage Pressure Ratio Calculation - Problem: For the compressor in Example 1 $W = 144 \text{ kJ/kg}$, if entry temperature $T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$, isentropic efficiency $\eta_{\text{isen}} = 0.80$, $C_p = 1.005 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, and $\gamma = 1.40$, calculate the overall pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$. - Solution: 1. Actual temperature rise $\Delta T_{\text{actual}} = \frac{W}{C_p} = \frac{144}{1.005} \approx 143.28 \text{ K}$. 2. Isentropic temperature rise $\Delta T_{\text{isen}} = \eta_{\text{isen}} \times \Delta T_{\text{actual}} = 0.80 \times 143.28 = 114.62 \text{ K}$. 3. Exit isentropic temperature $T_{2s} = T_1 + \Delta T_{\text{isen}} = 300 + 114.62 = 414.62 \text{ K}$. 4. Calculate pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$:

$${\frac{P_2}{P_1} = \left(\frac{T_{2s}}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} = \left(\frac{414.62}{300}\right)^{3.5} = (1.38207)^{3.5} \approx 3.125}$$
- Key Insight: Stage pressure ratio $P_2/P_1 = 3.125$. High tip speed $400 \text{ m/s}$ produces a pressure ratio above 3 in a single centrifugal stage!

Worked Example 3: Static Pressure Ratio Split in Impeller vs Diffuser - Problem: In a centrifugal compressor stage, the total static pressure rise is $\Delta P_{\text{total}} = 2.0 \text{ bar}$. If the static pressure rise across the impeller is $\Delta P_{\text{impeller}} = 1.10 \text{ bar}$, calculate the static pressure rise across the diffuser and volute casing combined. - Solution: 1. Total static pressure rise $\Delta P_{\text{total}} = \Delta P_{\text{impeller}} + \Delta P_{\text{diffuser+volute}}$. 2. $\Delta P_{\text{diffuser+volute}} = 2.0 - 1.10 = 0.90 \text{ bar}$. 3. Percentage in impeller $= \frac{1.10}{2.0} = 55\%$; Percentage in diffuser/volute $= \frac{0.90}{2.0} = 45\%$. - Key Insight: Static pressure rise is shared nearly equally between impeller $55\%$ and diffuser/volute $45\%$.

3.5 Axial-Flow Compressors (Reaction, Loading, Stalling vs Surging)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Operation & Aircraft Preference:
  • Alternating rotor blade rows (moving) and stator blade rows (fixed).
  • Fluid enters and leaves in the axial direction.
  • Chosen for aircraft jet engines because of high mass flow capacity per unit frontal area and low aerodynamic drag.
  • Degree of Reaction $R$:
  • Ratio of static enthalpy rise in rotor to total enthalpy rise in stage:
    $${\boxed{R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}}}$$
  • For $50\%$ Reaction $R = 0.50$: Symmetric velocity triangles ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2$ and $\beta_1 = \alpha_2$). Gives optimal pressure recovery.
  • Blade Loading Coefficient $\psi$:
  • Non-dimensional stage work coefficient:
    $${\boxed{\psi = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{u^2}}}$$
  • For a fixed stage work $\Delta h$, blade loading coefficient is inversely proportional to square of blade speed $\psi \propto 1/u^2$.
  • Work-Done Factor $\Omega$:
  • Boundary layer growth on casing walls reduces axial velocity in center and alters work distribution across stages.
  • Work-done factor $\Omega = \frac{W_{\text{actual}}}{W_{\text{theoretical}}}$ ranges from $0.98$ in stage 1 down to $0.85$ in later stages (average $\approx 0.88 - 0.90$).
  • Stalling vs Surging (Crucial Exam Distinction):
  • Stalling: Local boundary layer flow separation from the suction surface of a blade aerofoil when incidence angle exceeds critical limit. Flow local and transient.
  • Surging: Global, large-amplitude pulsation of entire compressor flow characterized by complete flow reversal, severe vibration, and potential mechanical destruction.
💡 Key Concept: Stalling vs Surging Distinction: - Stalling: LOCAL flow separation from individual blade contour due to high positive incidence. - Surging: GLOBAL periodic flow reversal across entire compressor system when mass flow falls below stable limit.

[!TRAP] Loading Coefficient Trend: Blade loading coefficient $\psi = \frac{\Delta h}{u^2}$ is INVERSELY proportional to $u^2$. Doubling blade speed $u$ at constant stage work reduces loading coefficient $\psi$ to ONE-FOURTH $1/4$!

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Axial Compressor Aerodynamic Diagnosis
│
├── Evaluate Stage Enthalpy Distribution:
│   ├── R = 0.50 (50% Reaction) ──► Symmetric blading (alpha_1 = beta_2, beta_1 = alpha_2)
│   └── R = 0    (0% Reaction)  ──► Entire static pressure rise occurs in stator
│
├── Evaluate Flow Instability Type:
│   ├── Flow separates locally from blade suction side ──► STALLING (Incidence mismatch)
│   └── Massive periodic flow reversal across compressor ──► SURGING (Mass flow too low)
│
└── Calculate Blade Loading Coefficient:
    └── psi = Delta h / u^2   (psi is inversely proportional to u^2)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Axial flow preferred for aircraft (low frontal area, high mass flow).
  • [ ] Degree of Reaction $R = \Delta h_{\text{rotor}} / \Delta h_{\text{stage}}$.
  • [ ] $50\%$ Reaction $R = 0.50$ $\implies$ symmetric velocity triangles.
  • [ ] Blade loading $\psi = \Delta h / u^2$.
  • [ ] Work-done factor $\Omega = 0.98 \to 0.85$.
  • [ ] Stalling = local blade separation; Surging = global flow reversal.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Axial Stage Specific Work & Loading Coefficient - Problem: An axial-flow compressor stage operates with mean blade speed $u = 250 \text{ m/s}$. The stage specific enthalpy rise $\Delta h_{\text{stage}} = 25 \text{ kJ/kg} = 25000 \text{ J/kg}$. Calculate: (a) Blade loading coefficient $\psi$, (b) New loading coefficient $\psi'$ if blade speed is increased to $u' = 300 \text{ m/s}$ at constant stage enthalpy rise. - Solution: 1. Calculate initial blade loading coefficient $\psi$:

$${\psi = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{u^2} = \frac{25000}{(250)^2} = \frac{25000}{62500} = 0.40}$$
2. Calculate new blade loading coefficient $\psi'$ at $u' = 300 \text{ m/s}$:
$${\psi' = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{(u')^2} = \frac{25000}{(300)^2} = \frac{25000}{90000} \approx 0.2778}$$
- Key Insight: $\psi = 0.40$, $\psi' = 0.278$. Increasing blade speed reduces loading coefficient $\psi$.

Worked Example 2: Axial Stage Degree of Reaction Calculation - Problem: In an axial-flow compressor stage, the static enthalpy rise in the rotor is $\Delta h_{\text{rotor}} = 15 \text{ kJ/kg}$ and the static enthalpy rise in the stator is $\Delta h_{\text{stator}} = 15 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate: (a) Total stage enthalpy rise $\Delta h_{\text{stage}}$, (b) Degree of reaction $R$, (c) Blading type. - Solution: 1. Total stage enthalpy rise $\Delta h_{\text{stage}} = \Delta h_{\text{rotor}} + \Delta h_{\text{stator}} = 15 + 15 = 30 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 2. Degree of Reaction $R$:

$${R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{15}{30} = 0.50 \implies 50\%}$$
3. Blading type: $R = 0.50 \implies$ Symmetric velocity blading. - Key Insight: $R = 0.50$. Equal enthalpy rise in rotor and stator defines $50\%$ reaction symmetric blading.

Worked Example 3: Work-Done Factor Influence on Actual Temperature Rise - Problem: An 8-stage axial compressor has a mean blade speed $u = 200 \text{ m/s}$ and theoretical whirl velocity change $\Delta V_w = 120 \text{ m/s}$ per stage. If average work-done factor $\Omega = 0.86$ and $C_p = 1005 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, calculate the actual total temperature rise across the 8 stages. - Solution: 1. Theoretical work per stage $W_{\text{theo}} = u \cdot \Delta V_w = 200 \times 120 = 24000 \text{ J/kg} = 24 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 2. Actual work per stage $W_{\text{actual}} = \Omega \times W_{\text{theo}} = 0.86 \times 24000 = 20640 \text{ J/kg}$. 3. Total actual work for 8 stages $W_{\text{total}} = 8 \times 20640 = 165120 \text{ J/kg}$. 4. Total actual temperature rise $\Delta T_{\text{total}} = \frac{W_{\text{total}}}{C_p} = \frac{165120}{1005} \approx 164.3 \text{ K}$. - Key Insight: $\Delta T_{\text{total}} = 164.3 \text{ K}$. Work-done factor $\Omega = 0.86$ reduces actual temperature rise by $14\%$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (25 Core Conceptual Pairs)

Q1: How are compressors broadly classified based on energy transfer method?
A: Positive-displacement (trapped volume reduction) and Dynamic / Roto-dynamic (continuous velocity transfer by impellers/blades).

Q2: Name five common positive-displacement compressors.
A: Reciprocating, Roots blower, sliding-vane, screw, and scroll compressors.

Q3: Which compression process requires the minimum work input?
A: Isothermal compression ($n = 1$, $p v = C$).

Q4: Define Isothermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$.
A: $\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{W_{\text{iso}}}{W_{\text{actual}}} = \frac{\text{Ideal Isothermal Work}}{\text{Actual Indicated Work}}$.

Q5: Write the volumetric efficiency formula for a reciprocating compressor.
A: $\eta_v = 1 + C - C\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{1/n}$ (where $C = V_c/V_s$).

Q6: What is the primary purpose of clearance volume in reciprocating compressors?
A: To accommodate spring-loaded automatic valves and prevent piston collision with cylinder head.

Q7: Does clearance volume affect the indicated work done per kg of air delivered?
A: No, clearance volume reduces volumetric efficiency and mass delivered per stroke, but does not affect work done per kg of air.

Q8: Write the optimum intermediate pressure formula for a two-stage compressor with perfect intercooling.
A: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$.

Q9: What three conditions must be satisfied for minimum work in multi-stage compression?
A: (1) Equal pressure ratio per stage, (2) Equal work per stage, (3) Perfect intercooling back to inlet temperature $T_1$.

Q10: In a centrifugal compressor, where does static pressure rise occur?
A: Static pressure rises in ALL THREE components: Impeller, Diffuser, and Volute Casing.

Q11: Define Slip Factor $\sigma$ in centrifugal compressors.
A: $\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}'}{u_2} = \frac{\text{Actual Exit Whirl Velocity}}{\text{Impeller Tip Speed}}$.

Q12: Which type of impeller vane gives the highest efficiency in centrifugal compressors?
A: Backward-curved vanes $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$.

Q13: Which type of impeller vane gives the highest pressure ratio per stage?
A: Forward-curved vanes $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$.

Q14: Why is pre-whirl used at the eye inlet of centrifugal compressors?
A: To reduce relative inlet velocity $V_{r1}$ below Mach 1 and avoid shock wave losses.

Q15: Why are axial-flow compressors preferred in aircraft jet engines?
A: Because of their low frontal cross-sectional area (low drag) and high mass flow capacity per unit area.

Q16: Define Degree of Reaction $R$ in axial compressors.
A: $R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{\text{Static Enthalpy Rise in Rotor}}{\text{Total Enthalpy Rise in Stage}}$.

Q17: What geometry characterizes a 50% reaction $R = 0.50$ axial compressor stage?
A: Symmetric velocity triangles ($\alpha_1 = \beta_2$ and $\beta_1 = \alpha_2$).

Q18: Write the blade-loading coefficient formula $\psi$.
A: $\psi = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{u^2}$ (inversely proportional to $u^2$).

Q19: Define Stalling in axial compressors.
A: Local boundary layer flow separation from the suction surface of a blade caused by an excessively high positive angle of incidence.

Q20: Define Surging in axial compressors.
A: Global, severe, periodic flow breakdown and complete flow reversal across the entire compressor system.

Q21: How does compression by backflow occur in a Roots blower?
A: Air at delivery pressure flows backward from the receiver into the lobe space when the delivery port opens, compressing trapped atmospheric air irreversibly.

Q22: Why does multi-stage compression require a smaller flywheel compared to single-stage compression?
A: Multi-staging distributes crank effort across multiple out-of-phase cylinders, producing a smoother torque diagram with smaller energy fluctuations per revolution.

Q23: What is the effect of increasing intake air temperature on compressor volumetric efficiency?
A: Increasing intake temperature reduces mass density of drawn air, slightly reducing effective mass volumetric efficiency.

Q24: What is the typical single-stage pressure ratio of an axial compressor versus a centrifugal compressor?
A: Axial single-stage ratio $\approx 1.15 \text{ to } 1.30$; Centrifugal single-stage ratio $\approx 4.0 \text{ to } 5.0$.

Q25: Why does the Work-Done Factor decrease as the number of stages increases in an axial compressor?
A: Boundary layer thickness along casing walls grows progressively thicker across successive stages, creating an increasingly non-uniform axial velocity profile.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Classification:
  • Positive-Displacement: Reciprocating, Roots, Vane, Screw, Scroll.
  • Dynamic: Centrifugal, Axial.
  • Roots blower is ROTARY POSITIVE-DISPLACEMENT (not dynamic!).
  • Reciprocating Performance:
  • Minimum Work $\implies$ Isothermal $n = 1$. Maximum Work $\implies$ Isentropic $n = \gamma$.
  • $\eta_v = 1 + C - C(P_2/P_1)^{1/n}$. $\eta_v \downarrow$ as $C \uparrow$ or $P_2/P_1 \uparrow$.
  • Clearance volume reduces $\eta_v$, but DOES NOT affect work per kg!
  • Multi-Staging:
  • Minimum work conditions: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$ (geometric mean, NOT arithmetic!).
  • Multi-staging INCREASES volumetric efficiency and REDUCES flywheel size.
  • Centrifugal Compressors:
  • Static pressure rises in Impeller + Diffuser + Volute!
  • Slip factor $\sigma = V_{w2}'/u_2 < 1$.
  • Backward blades $\implies$ max efficiency; Forward blades $\implies$ max pressure ratio.
  • Axial Compressors:
  • Preferred in aircraft $\implies$ low frontal area, high mass flow.
  • $R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}$. $50\%$ Reaction $\implies$ symmetric triangles.
  • Stalling = local blade separation; Surging = global flow reversal.
