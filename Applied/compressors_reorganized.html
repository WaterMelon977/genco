2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Reciprocating Compressor Performance

  • Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_v = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}}}$$
  • $C = \frac{V_c}{V_s}$: clearance ratio (dimensionless fraction; e.g. $0.05$ for $5\%$)
  • $V_c$: clearance volume $\text{m}^3$
  • $V_s$: swept/stroke volume $\text{m}^3$
  • $P_1$: suction pressure ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{bar}$)
  • $P_2$: discharge pressure ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{bar}$)

  • $n$: polytropic expansion index of clearance gas

  • Isothermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{\text{Isothermal Work}}{\text{Actual Indicated Work}} = \frac{P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)}{W_{\text{actual}}}}}$$

  • Work Input (Single-Stage Polytropic):

    $${\boxed{W = \frac{n}{n-1} P_1 V_1 \left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}} - 1\right] = \frac{n}{n-1} m R (T_2 - T_1)}}$$

2. Multi-Stage Compression with Intercooling

  • Optimum Intermediate Pressure (2-Stage Perfect Intercooling):
    $${\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2}}}$$
  • $P_1$: initial suction pressure
  • $P_2$: final discharge pressure

  • $P_i$: intercooler pressure $P_{\text{inter}}$

  • Stage Pressure Ratio for $N$ Stages:

    $${\boxed{r = \left(\frac{P_N}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{N}}}}$$

  • $P_N$: final discharge pressure after $N$ stages

  • Pressure progression: $P_1 \to P_1 r \to P_1 r^2 \dots \to P_1 r^N = P_N$

  • Minimum Total Work Input ($N$ Stages with Perfect Intercooling):

    $${\boxed{W_{\text{total, min}} = N \cdot \frac{n}{n-1} P_1 V_1 \left[\left(\frac{P_N}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{N n}} - 1\right]}}$$

3. Centrifugal Compressors

  • Slip Factor $\sigma$:
    $${\boxed{\sigma = \frac{C_{\theta 2}}{u_2}}}$$
  • $C_{\theta 2}$: actual tangential whirl velocity at impeller exit $\text{m/s}$

  • $u_2$: impeller tip speed $\text{m/s}$

  • Euler Work Input per Unit Mass $w$:

    $${\boxed{w = u_2 C_{\theta 2} - u_1 C_{\theta 1}}}$$

  • For radial entry without pre-whirl $C_{\theta 1} = 0$:

    $${\boxed{w = u_2 C_{\theta 2} = \sigma u_2^2}}$$

  • Power Input Factor $\psi_p$:

    $${\boxed{\psi_p = \frac{w_{\text{actual}}}{\sigma u_2^2}}}$$

4. Axial-Flow Compressors

  • Degree of Reaction $R$:
    $${\boxed{R = \frac{\text{Static Enthalpy Rise in Rotor}}{\text{Stagnation Enthalpy Rise in Stage}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{h_3 - h_1}}}$$

  • For $50\%$ Symmetric Blading:

    $${\boxed{R = 0.50 \implies \alpha_1 = \beta_2, \quad \beta_1 = \alpha_2}}$$

  • Blade Loading Coefficient $\psi$:

    $${\boxed{\psi = \frac{\Delta h}{u^2} = \frac{C_a}{u} (\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2)}}$$

  • $C_a$: axial flow velocity $\text{m/s}$
  • $u$: mean blade speed $\text{m/s}$

  • $\beta_1, \beta_2$: relative blade inlet and exit angles

  • Work Done Factor $\Omega$:

    $${\boxed{\Omega = \frac{w_{\text{actual}}}{u C_a (\tan\beta_1 - \tan\beta_2)} \quad (0.85 - 0.98)}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Positive-Displacement Compressor: Machine that compresses gas by trapping a fixed volume in a chamber and mechanically reducing its size (e.g. Reciprocating, Roots, Vane, Screw, Scroll).
  • Dynamic Compressor: Machine that continuous imparts kinetic energy to gas via high-speed rotating blades and converts velocity to static pressure in a diffuser (e.g. Centrifugal, Axial-flow).
  • Clearance Ratio $C$: Ratio of clearance volume $V_c$ to swept/stroke volume $V_s$ $C = V_c / V_s$.
  • Isothermal Compression: Ideal compression process following $P V = \text{const}$ $n=1$; requires MINIMUM work input for given pressure limits.
  • Perfect Intercooling: Cooling intermediate gas between stages back to the initial suction temperature $T_i = T_1$.
  • Slip Factor $\sigma$: Ratio of actual exit whirl velocity to theoretical tip speed $\sigma = C_{\theta 2} / u_2$. Accounts for inertia of fluid slipping backward relative to impeller.
  • Stalling: Local separation of boundary-layer airflow from the suction surface of an axial compressor blade due to excessive incidence angle.
  • Surging: Complete breakdown of airflow through the compressor accompanied by violent, periodic pressure and mass flow back-and-forth oscillations.
  • Work-Done Factor $\Omega$: Ratio of actual work absorbed by an axial compressor stage to theoretical work calculated from mid-span velocity triangles $0.85 - 0.98$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Symmetric Axial Blading Degree of Reaction: $R = 0.50$ $50\%$.
  • Centrifugal Single-Stage Pressure Ratio: Typical maximum $4:1$ to $5:1$.
  • Axial Stage Pressure Ratio: Typical $1.1:1$ to $1.4:1$ per stage (requires 10-15 stages for high total ratios).
  • Work-Done Factor Range: $\Omega = 0.85 \text{ to } 0.98$ (decreases in later stages due to boundary layer buildup).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Compressor Classification & Working Principles

1. Concept Pointers
  • Compressors are broadly divided into two major families:
  • Positive-Displacement:
    • Reciprocating: Piston-cylinder with self-acting valves. Suitable for high pressure, low-to-medium mass flow, pulsating discharge.
    • Rotary Positive-Displacement: Roots blower (two-lobe/three-lobe), Sliding-vane, Screw (helical male/female rotors), Scroll. Continuous flow, higher speeds, no valves.
  • Dynamic (Turbomachinery):
    • Centrifugal: Radial flow direction. Medium pressure ratio per stage $4-5$, high mass flow.
    • Axial-Flow: Axial flow direction. Low pressure ratio per stage $1.1-1.4$, extremely high mass flow, small frontal area.
💡 Key Concept: Positive-Displacement vs Dynamic: - Positive-Displacement: Pressure rise by physical volume reduction $\Delta V \to \Delta P$. - Dynamic: Pressure rise by dynamic acceleration + diffuser deceleration $\Delta \frac{V^2}{2g} \to \Delta P$.

[!TRAP] Roots Blower Action: Roots blower is a rotary positive-displacement machine, but it does NOT perform internal compression! Compression occurs externally when gas is discharged against receiver pressure.

2. Decision Tree
Compressor Family Classification?
├─ Is gas trapped in a fixed volume and mechanically squeezed?
│  ├─ YES → Positive-Displacement
│  │  ├─ Reciprocating Piston → High pressure ratio, low/pulsating flow, has valves
│  │  └─ Rotary (Roots, Vane, Screw) → Moderate pressure, smooth continuous flow
│  └─ NO → Dynamic Turbomachine (Continuous flow)
│     ├─ Radial flow direction → Centrifugal (Medium pressure ratio 4-5, high flow)
│     └─ Axial flow direction → Axial-Flow (Low pressure ratio 1.2 per stage, maximum flow, small frontal area for aircraft)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Reciprocating: High pressure, low flow, valves.
  • [ ] Centrifugal: Radial flow, $P_2/P_1 \approx 4-5$.
  • [ ] Axial: Smallest frontal area, preferred in aircraft jet engines.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Which type of compressor is preferred for aircraft gas turbine jet engines and why? - Solution: - Axial-Flow Compressor is preferred. - Reason: It provides high mass flow rate per unit frontal area and small frontal diameter, reducing aerodynamic drag on the aircraft structure. - Key Insight: Small frontal area + high flow rate = Axial-flow compressor for aviation.

3.2 Reciprocating Compressors — Volumetric & Isothermal Efficiency

1. Concept Pointers
  • Ideal work input is MINIMUM during Isothermal Compression $n=1, P V = \text{const}$.
  • Polytropic work $n > 1$ > Isothermal work $n = 1$.
  • Isothermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{W_{\text{isothermal}}}{W_{\text{actual}}}$.
  • Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{1/n}$.
  • Increasing Pressure Ratio $\frac{P_2}{P_1} \implies \eta_v \downarrow$.
  • Increasing Clearance Ratio $C \implies \eta_v \downarrow$.
  • Clearance Trap: Clearance volume reduces volumetric efficiency (free air delivered), but has NEGLIGIBLE effect on work required per kilogram of air compressed!
💡 Key Concept: Minimum Work Condition: Compression work is minimum when process is ISOTHERMAL $n=1$. Water jackets and multi-stage intercooling aim to bring polytropic compression as close to isothermal as possible.

[!TRAP] Clearance Effect on Work: Clearance volume $V_c$ REDUCES volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$, but does NOT waste indicated energy because clearance gas expands back during re-expansion stroke and returns work to the piston!

2. Decision Tree
Reciprocating Performance Evaluation?
├─ Minimum Work Process Check → Isothermal Compression (n = 1)
├─ Volumetric Efficiency Formula → eta_v = 1 + C - C * (P2/P1)^(1/n)
└─ Effect of Clearance Ratio C → C increases implies eta_v decreases (work/kg unchanged)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Minimum work $\implies$ Isothermal $n=1$.
  • [ ] $\eta_v = 1 + C - C (P_2/P_1)^{1/n}$.
  • [ ] Clearance reduces $\eta_v$, but doesn't waste work per kg.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A reciprocating compressor has a clearance ratio $C = 5\%$ $0.05$, operates at pressure ratio $\frac{P_2}{P_1} = 4$, and expansion index $n = 1.30$. Calculate volumetric efficiency $\eta_v$. - Solution:

$${\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = 4^{\frac{1}{1.30}} = 4^{0.7692} \approx 2.908}$$
$${\eta_v = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} = 1 + 0.05 - 0.05 \times 2.908 = 1.05 - 0.1454 = 0.9046 = 90.46\%}$$
- Key Insight: Substitute $C=0.05$ and ratio $=4$ directly into $\eta_v$ formula.

3.3 Multi-Stage Compression & Intercooling

1. Concept Pointers
  • Why Multi-Stage?
  • High pressure ratios in single-stage lead to extremely low $\eta_v$, high discharge temperatures, and severe lubrication degradation.
  • Multi-staging reduces total work input, lowers discharge temperature, improves $\eta_v$, and reduces mechanical size/torque fluctuations.
  • Perfect Intercooling: Intermediate air cooled back to initial suction temperature $T_i = T_1$.
  • Optimum Intermediate Pressure (2-Stage):
    $${\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2}}}$$
  • Equal work distribution between stages:
    $${W_{\text{stage 1}} = W_{\text{stage 2}}}$$
  • For $N$ stages with perfect intercooling:
  • Stage Pressure Ratio $r = (P_N / P_1)^{1/N}$.
  • Intermediate pressures form a geometric progression: $P_1, P_1 r, P_1 r^2, \dots P_N$.
💡 Key Concept: Perfect Intercooling Condition: For minimum total work in a multi-stage compressor, the interstage pressure ratio MUST be equal for all stages $r_1 = r_2 = \dots = r_N$, and intermediate gas MUST be cooled to $T_1$.
2. Decision Tree
Multi-Stage Intercooler Calculation?
├─ Is intercooling perfect (T_inter = T1)?
│  ├─ 2-Stage Intermediate Pressure → P_i = sqrt(P1 * P2)
│  └─ N-Stage Stage Ratio → r = (P_N / P1)^(1/N)
└─ Intermediate Pressures → Form Geometric Progression P1, P1*r, P1*r^2...
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] 2-Stage optimum: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$.
  • [ ] Stage ratio for $N$ stages: $r = (P_N/P_1)^{1/N}$.
  • [ ] Equal work per stage under optimum intercooling.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A 3-stage compressor compresses air from $1 \text{ bar}$ $P_1$ to $27 \text{ bar}$ $P_4$ with perfect intercooling. Calculate the stage pressure ratio $r$ and the pressure entering the last intercooler $P_3$. - Solution:

$${N = 3, \quad P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}, \quad P_4 = 27 \text{ bar}}$$
$${r = \left(\frac{P_4}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = \left(\frac{27}{1}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = 3}$$
$${P_2 = P_1 \times r = 1 \times 3 = 3 \text{ bar}}$$
$${P_3 = P_2 \times r = 3 \times 3 = 9 \text{ bar}}$$
- Key Insight: Pressures form a geometric series $1, 3, 9, 27 \text{ bar}$. Pressure entering last intercooler is $P_3 = 9 \text{ bar}$.

3.4 Centrifugal Compressors — Impeller, Diffuser & Slip Factor

1. Concept Pointers
  • Energy Transfer: Impeller adds kinetic energy and static pressure to fluid; Diffuser and Volute Casing convert remaining kinetic energy into static pressure.
  • Static pressure rises in BOTH the impeller and diffuser!
  • Slip Factor $\sigma$:
    $${\sigma = \frac{C_{\theta 2}}{u_2} < 1.0}$$
    Due to fluid inertia, air trapped between rotating blades circulates backwards relative to blades, reducing exit whirl velocity below theoretical tip speed $u_2$.
  • Blade Types:
  • Backward-Curved $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$: Highest efficiency, stable operating range, self-limiting power characteristic. Standard for industrial & refrigeration.
  • Radial Blades $\beta_2 = 90^\circ$: High pressure ratio, high mechanical strength, easy manufacture. Common in turbochargers.
  • Forward-Curved $\beta_2 > 90^\circ$: Highest pressure rise for given tip speed, but unstable and lower efficiency.
💡 Key Concept: Static Pressure Rise Distribution: Static pressure rises in BOTH the rotating impeller and the stationary diffuser! It is NEVER confined to the diffuser alone.

[!TRAP] Slip Factor Definition: Slip factor $\sigma = C_{\theta 2} / u_2$ is the ratio of ACTUAL exit whirl velocity to theoretical blade tip speed. Slip REDUCES Euler work capacity for a given speed.

2. Decision Tree
Centrifugal Compressor Component Analysis?
├─ Where does static pressure rise? → BOTH Impeller and Diffuser
├─ Slip Factor Definition → sigma = C_theta2 / u2 (< 1.0)
└─ Blade Selection → Backward-curved (Highest efficiency), Radial (High strength/turbocharger)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Static pressure rises in both Impeller and Diffuser.
  • [ ] $\sigma = C_{\theta 2} / u_2$.
  • [ ] Backward-curved blades have highest efficiency.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A centrifugal compressor impeller rotates with a tip speed $u_2 = 400 \text{ m/s}$. The slip factor $\sigma = 0.90$. Assuming radial entry without pre-whirl $C_{\theta 1} = 0$, calculate Euler work per kg of air. - Solution:

$${C_{\theta 2} = \sigma \cdot u_2 = 0.90 \times 400 = 360 \text{ m/s}}$$
$${w = u_2 C_{\theta 2} = 400 \times 360 = 144000 \text{ J/kg} = 144 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
- Key Insight: $w = \sigma u_2^2 = 0.90 \times 400^2 = 144 \text{ kJ/kg}$.

3.5 Axial-Flow Compressors — Degree of Reaction, Stalling & Surging

1. Concept Pointers
  • Axial-flow compressor consists of alternating rows of rotor blades and stator blades.
  • Degree of Reaction $R$:
    $${R = \frac{\text{Static Enthalpy Rise in Rotor}}{\text{Stagnation Enthalpy Rise in Stage}}}$$
  • Symmetric Blading $R = 50\% = 0.50$:
  • Equal static pressure rise in rotor and stator.
  • $\alpha_1 = \beta_2, \quad \beta_1 = \alpha_2$.
  • Stalling: Local flow separation of air from blade surface when incidence angle becomes excessively positive. Air flow cannot follow blade contour.
  • Surging: Complete breakdown of flow through whole compressor accompanied by violent, periodic flow reversal and pressure oscillations.
  • Work Done Factor $\Omega$: $\Omega = 0.85 - 0.98$ due to boundary layer growth along annulus walls.
💡 Key Concept: Stalling vs Surging: - Stalling: LOCAL boundary layer flow separation from blade aerofoil. - Surging: GLOBAL system breakdown with violent back-and-forth flow oscillations.

[!TRAP] Axial Aircraft Advantage: Axial compressors are preferred in aircraft gas turbines because of HIGH MASS FLOW per unit frontal area and LOW FRONTAL DIAMETER (minimizing drag)!

2. Decision Tree
Axial-Flow Phenomena Identification?
├─ Local airflow boundary layer separation from blade → STALLING
├─ Complete violent flow breakdown & periodic reversal → SURGING
└─ Symmetric Blading Condition → Degree of Reaction R = 0.50 (50%)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $R = 50\%$ for symmetric blading.
  • [ ] Stalling = Local flow separation.
  • [ ] Surging = Global flow reversal.
  • [ ] Preferred in aircraft (small frontal area).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Differentiate clearly between Stalling and Surging in an axial-flow compressor. - Solution: - Stalling: A localized aerodynamic phenomenon where airflow separates from individual blade suction surfaces due to high angle of incidence. - Surging: A global system stability failure where mass flow fluctuates violently back and forth through the compressor, causing structural vibration and potential blade failure. - Key Insight: Stalling is localized blade separation; Surging is global back-and-forth flow oscillation.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Differentiate between positive-displacement and dynamic compressors.
A: Positive-displacement compressors trap a fixed volume of gas and compress it by reducing chamber volume (e.g. Reciprocating, Screw). Dynamic compressors impart kinetic energy to continuous gas flow via rotating impellers/blades and convert it to pressure in diffusers (e.g. Centrifugal, Axial).

Q2: What is the condition for minimum work input in a reciprocating compressor?
A: Minimum work input occurs when the compression process is Isothermal $n=1, P V = \text{const}$.

Q3: Define Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$ of a reciprocating compressor and state its formula.
A: $\eta_v = \frac{\text{Free Air Delivered}}{\text{Swept Volume}} = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{1/n}$, where $C = V_c / V_s$.

Q4: How does clearance volume affect the work required per kg of air in a reciprocating compressor?
A: Clearance volume reduces volumetric efficiency (free air delivered), but has virtually NO effect on work required per kilogram of air, because clearance gas expands back during the re-expansion stroke and returns work to the piston.

Q5: What is the optimum intermediate pressure for a 2-stage compressor with perfect intercooling?
A: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2}$.

Q6: What is the stage pressure ratio $r$ for an $N$-stage compressor with equal work distribution?
A: $r = \left(\frac{P_N}{P_1}\right)^{1/N}$.

Q7: Define Slip Factor $\sigma$ in centrifugal compressors.
A: $\sigma = \frac{C_{\theta 2}}{u_2}$, the ratio of actual exit whirl velocity to theoretical blade tip speed.

Q8: Where does static pressure rise occur in a centrifugal compressor?
A: Static pressure rises in BOTH the rotating impeller and the stationary diffuser/volute casing.

Q9: Which blade profile in centrifugal compressors offers the highest efficiency?
A: Backward-curved blades $\beta_2 < 90^\circ$.

Q10: Define Degree of Reaction $R$ in axial compressors.
A: $R = \frac{\text{Static Enthalpy Rise in Rotor}}{\text{Stagnation Enthalpy Rise in Stage}}$.

Q11: What is the value of Degree of Reaction $R$ for symmetric axial compressor blading?
A: $R = 0.50$ $50\%$.

Q12: Differentiate between Stalling and Surging in axial compressors.
A: Stalling is local flow separation from blade surfaces due to excessive incidence. Surging is complete, violent system-wide flow breakdown with periodic pressure and flow oscillations.

Q13: Why are axial-flow compressors preferred in aircraft gas turbine engines?
A: Because they provide maximum mass flow rate per unit frontal area with small frontal diameter, minimizing aerodynamic drag.

Q14: What is the function of an intercooler in multi-stage compression?
A: To cool the compressed gas leaving an earlier stage back to initial suction temperature $T_1$ before entering the next stage, reducing work input and discharge temperature.

Q15: What is the typical pressure ratio per stage in an axial-flow compressor?
A: Approximately $1.1:1 \text{ to } 1.4:1$ per stage.
