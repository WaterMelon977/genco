Classification & Working Principles of Compressors

1. Classification of Compressors

Compressors are machines that raise the pressure of a gas by doing work on it. They divide into two fundamental categories based on the mechanism of compression.

CategorySub-typeMechanismTypical application
Positive DisplacementReciprocating (piston-cylinder)Piston traps discrete gas volume; spring-loaded automatic valves control flowHigh-pressure industrial gas, refrigeration up to 100 TR
Rotary (Roots, vane, screw, scroll)Rotating elements trap and push gas; no internal compression in Roots blowerLow-pressure blowing, pneumatic conveying
Dynamic (Roto-dynamic)CentrifugalRadial-flow impeller imparts kinetic energy; diffuser/volute converts to pressure. $P_2/P_1$ per stage: 4–5Large refrigeration, petrochemical, process industry
Axial-flowAxial blading; low pressure ratio per stage (1.15–1.30) but very high mass flow per unit frontal areaAircraft jet engines, large gas turbines
  • Roots blower: rotary positive-displacement machine. Compression occurs by backflow from the high-pressure receiver when the delivery port opens — there is no internal compression during rotation.
  • Axial preferred for aircraft: reason is low frontal aerodynamic drag and high mass flow rate per unit area — not because pressure ratio per stage is higher (centrifugal is $4\times$ higher per stage).

2. Reciprocating Compressor — Work & Efficiencies

2.1 Work of Compression

Three ideal compression processes are compared for the same pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$:

$$W_{\text{iso}} = mRT_1\ln\!\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) \qquad (n=1,\text{ minimum work})$$
$$W_{\text{poly}} = \frac{n}{n-1}mRT_1\!\left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\!\frac{n-1}{n}}\!-1\right] \qquad (1 < n < \gamma)$$
$$W_{\text{isen}} = \frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}mRT_1\!\left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\!\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}\!-1\right] \qquad (n=\gamma,\text{ adiabatic})$$
  • Work order for same pressure ratio: $W_{\text{iso}} < W_{\text{poly}} < W_{\text{isen}}$
  • Isothermal work is minimum; achieved only with perfect cooling during compression
  • $m$ — mass of gas (kg); $R$ — specific gas constant (J/kg·K); $T_1$ — initial temperature (K)

2.2 Isothermal Efficiency

$$\eta_{\text{iso}} = \frac{W_{\text{iso}}}{W_{\text{actual}}} = \frac{mRT_1\ln(P_2/P_1)}{W_{\text{indicated}}}$$
  • Measures how close actual work is to the ideal isothermal minimum
  • Improved by intercooling, water jackets, and fin cooling

2.3 Clearance Volume & Volumetric Efficiency

At end of delivery stroke, a small clearance volume $V_c$ remains filled with high-pressure gas. This gas re-expands on the next suction stroke before new gas can enter, reducing the effective intake volume.

$$\eta_v = 1 + C - C\!\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\!\frac{1}{n}}$$
  • $C = V_c/V_s$ — clearance ratio (volumetric clearance as fraction of swept volume)
  • $\eta_v$ decreases as either clearance ratio $C$ or pressure ratio $P_2/P_1$ increases
  • At a sufficiently high pressure ratio, $\eta_v = 0$ — compressor delivers no gas despite running
  • Valid range: $n$ = polytropic index of re-expansion ($\approx 1.3\text{--}1.4$ for air)
  • Clearance volume independence: Clearance volume $V_c$ reduces free air delivery and $\eta_v$, but does NOT alter the indicated work done per kg of air delivered.

3. Multi-Stage Compression & Intercooling

Staging compression with intercooling between stages reduces total work and keeps delivery temperature manageable. For minimum total work with perfect intercooling (gas cooled back to $T_1$ between stages), each stage must have the same pressure ratio.

3.1 Optimum Intermediate Pressure (Two Stages)

$$P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2} \qquad \text{(geometric mean; gives equal stage ratios)}$$

3.2 N-Stage Equal Pressure Ratio

$$r_{\text{stage}} = \left(\frac{P_{\text{discharge}}}{P_{\text{suction}}}\right)^{\!1/N}$$
$$P_k = P_1 \cdot r_{\text{stage}}^k \qquad \text{(pressure after stage } k\text{)}$$

3.3 Minimum Total Work (Two Stages, Perfect Intercooling)

$$W_{\min} = 2\cdot\frac{n}{n-1}mRT_1\!\left[\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\!\frac{n-1}{2n}}\!-1\right]$$
  • Conditions for minimum work: equal stage pressure ratios AND perfect intercooling ($T$ after intercooler = $T_1$)
  • Adding stages and intercoolers approaches isothermal compression in the limit

4. Centrifugal Compressor

Air enters axially at the impeller eye, is turned 90° radially, and leaves at the tip with high kinetic energy. The diffuser and volute casing convert kinetic energy to pressure. Energy is transferred by Euler's turbomachinery equation.

4.1 Euler Work & Slip Factor

$$W_{\text{Euler}} = u_2 V_{w2} - u_1 V_{w1}$$

For radial entry ($V_{w1} = 0$) with backward-swept or radial blades:

$$W_{\text{Euler}} = u_2 V_{w2} = \sigma u_2^2$$
$$\sigma = \frac{V_{w2}'}{u_2} \qquad \text{(slip factor; always } \sigma < 1\text{)}$$
  • $u_2$ — impeller tip speed (m/s); $V_{w2}$ — actual whirl component of absolute exit velocity (m/s)
  • Slip factor $\sigma$ accounts for the fact that actual whirl velocity is less than the blade tip speed due to inter-blade flow recirculation
  • Typical $\sigma \approx 0.85\text{--}0.92$ for centrifugal compressors
  • Pressure rise distribution: Static pressure rise occurs in ALL THREE stages (Impeller $\approx 50\%$, Diffuser $\approx 40\%$, Volute Casing $\approx 10\%$).

4.2 Pressure Ratio

$$\frac{P_{02}}{P_{01}} = \left(1 + \frac{\sigma u_2^2}{C_p T_{01}}\right)^{\!\gamma/(\gamma-1)}$$
  • $T_{01}$ — stagnation inlet temperature (K); $C_p$ — specific heat at constant pressure (J/kg·K)
  • Pressure ratio increases strongly with tip speed $u_2^2$ — doubling tip speed quadruples the driving term

5. Axial-Flow Compressor

Alternate rows of rotating blades (rotor) and stationary blades (stator) make up each stage. The rotor imparts kinetic energy; the stator decelerates and converts it to pressure. Many stages (8–20) are needed for high overall pressure ratios.

5.1 Degree of Reaction

$$R = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{rotor}}}{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}} = \frac{h_2 - h_1}{h_3 - h_1}$$
  • $R = 0.5$ (50% reaction): most common design; static enthalpy rise split equally between rotor and stator — symmetric velocity triangles
  • $R = 0$: all energy added in rotor as kinetic energy; stator does all pressure conversion (impulse stage)
  • $R = 1$: all enthalpy rise in rotor; stator merely redirects flow

5.2 Blade Loading & Work-Done Factor

$$\psi = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{stage}}}{u^2} \qquad \text{(blade loading coefficient)}$$
$$\Omega = \frac{W_{\text{actual}}}{W_{\text{theoretical}}} < 1 \qquad \text{(work-done factor; accounts for annulus blockage)}$$

5.3 Stalling vs Surging

PhenomenonCauseEffectRemedy
StallingHigh angle of attack on blade; boundary layer separates on suction surfaceLocalised flow breakdown on one or more blade passages; reduced stage efficiencyVariable inlet guide vanes; blade-profile redesign
SurgingSystem back-pressure exceeds compressor delivery pressure capability; mass flow drops below minimum stable flowComplete flow reversal through compressor; violent oscillation; structural damageAnti-surge valve (blow-off valve); maintain flow above surge line
  • Stalling is a local blade phenomenon; surging is a system-level instability
  • Surging occurs at low mass flow / high pressure ratio operating point on the compressor map (left of surge line)

Key Points to Remember

  • Positive-displacement vs dynamic: PD traps volume mechanically; dynamic imparts velocity continuously.
  • Roots blower is a rotary positive-displacement machine — compression is by backflow from receiver, not internal compression.
  • Axial chosen for aircraft: low frontal area + high mass flow rate per unit area. Centrifugal has higher pressure ratio per stage (4–5 vs 1.15–1.3).
  • Work order for same $P_2/P_1$: $W_{\text{iso}} < W_{\text{poly}} < W_{\text{isen}}$. Isothermal is ideal minimum.
  • Volumetric efficiency: $\eta_v = 1 + C - C(P_2/P_1)^{1/n}$. Decreases with higher clearance $C$ or higher pressure ratio.
  • Optimum intermediate pressure for two-stage: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$. Conditions: equal stage ratios + perfect intercooling.
  • For $N$ stages with perfect intercooling, stage pressure ratio = $(P_{\text{out}}/P_{\text{in}})^{1/N}$.
  • Centrifugal slip factor $\sigma = V_{w2}'/u_2 < 1$. Specific work $= \sigma u_2^2$. Higher tip speed $u_2$ → higher pressure ratio ($\propto u_2^2$).
  • Axial degree of reaction $R = 0.5$ (50% reaction): most practical design; symmetric velocity triangles.
  • Stalling = local blade-level flow breakdown (high incidence angle). Surging = system-level oscillating flow reversal (most dangerous; can destroy machine).
  • Anti-surge protection: blow-off (recirculation) valve to maintain flow above the surge line on compressor map.
  • Intercooling reduces compression work and delivery temperature; moves the compression process toward isothermal.
