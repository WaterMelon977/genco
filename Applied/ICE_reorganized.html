2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Air-Standard Cycles

  • Otto Cycle Thermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{Otto}}$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}}}$$
  • $r = \frac{V_1}{V_2}$: compression ratio

  • $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$: specific heat ratio ($\approx 1.40$ for air; $\gamma - 1 = 0.40$)

  • Diesel Cycle Thermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{Diesel}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Diesel}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} \left[\frac{\rho^\gamma - 1}{\gamma (\rho - 1)}\right]}}$$

  • $\rho = \frac{V_3}{V_2}$: cut-off ratio $\rho > 1$

  • Dual Cycle Thermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{Dual}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Dual}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} \left[\frac{\alpha \rho^\gamma - 1}{(\alpha - 1) + \gamma \alpha (\rho - 1)}\right]}}$$

  • $\alpha = \frac{P_3}{P_2}$: explosion / pressure ratio at constant volume

2. Engine Geometry & Compression Ratio

  • Compression Ratio $r$:
    $${\boxed{r = \frac{V_{\text{total}}}{V_c} = \frac{V_s + V_c}{V_c} = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c}}}$$
  • $V_s$: swept / displacement volume ($\text{m}^3$ or $\text{cc}$)

  • $V_c$: clearance volume ($\text{m}^3$ or $\text{cc}$)

  • Swept Volume $V_s$:

    $${\boxed{V_s = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 L \times k}}$$

  • $D$: cylinder bore diameter $\text{m}$
  • $L$: piston stroke length $\text{m}$

  • $k$: number of cylinders

  • Stroke-to-Bore Ratio:

  • $L/D > 1 \implies$ Under-square / Long-stroke engine
  • $L/D = 1 \implies$ Square engine
  • $L/D < 1 \implies$ Over-square / Short-stroke engine

3. Engine Kinematics & Valve Timing

  • Camshaft Speed $N_{\text{cam}}$ for 4-Stroke Engine:
    $${\boxed{N_{\text{cam}} = \frac{N_{\text{crank}}}{2}}}$$

  • $N_{\text{crank}}$: crankshaft rotational speed $\text{rpm}$

  • Time Duration of Crank Angle Rotation:

    $${\boxed{t = \frac{\theta}{6 N} \quad (\text{seconds})}}$$

  • $\theta$: crank angle of injection or ignition $\text{degrees}$
  • $N$: crankshaft speed $\text{rpm}$

4. Engine Performance Parameters

  • Indicated Power $IP$:
    $${\boxed{IP = \frac{P_{mi} \cdot L \cdot A \cdot n \cdot k}{60000} \quad (\text{kW})}}$$
  • $P_{mi}$: indicated mean effective pressure ($\text{N/m}^2$ or $\text{Pa}$)

  • $n$: working strokes per minute ($n = N/2$ for 4-stroke, $n = N$ for 2-stroke)

  • Mean Effective Pressure $P_m$:

    $${\boxed{P_m = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{V_s} = \frac{\text{Area of Indicator Diagram}}{\text{Length of Indicator Diagram}} \times \text{Spring Constant}}}$$

  • Mechanical Efficiency $\eta_m$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_m = \frac{BP}{IP} = \frac{IP - FP}{IP}}}$$

  • $BP$: Brake Power $\text{kW}$

  • $FP$: Friction Power $\text{kW}$

  • Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_v = \frac{m_{\text{actual}}}{m_{\text{theoretical}}} = \frac{V_{\text{actual suction}}}{V_s}}}$$

  • Relative Efficiency $\eta_{\text{relative}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{relative}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{thermal, actual}}}{\eta_{\text{air-standard}}}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Otto Cycle: Air-standard thermodynamic cycle with constant-volume heat addition; basis for Spark-Ignition (SI) engines.
  • Diesel Cycle: Air-standard thermodynamic cycle with constant-pressure heat addition; basis for Compression-Ignition (CI) engines.
  • Dual Cycle: Mixed cycle with heat addition partly at constant volume and partly at constant pressure; basis for modern high-speed Diesel engines.
  • Swept Volume $V_s$: Volume displaced by the piston as it moves from Top Dead Center (TDC) to Bottom Dead Center (BDC).
  • Clearance Volume $V_c$: Volume remaining inside the cylinder when piston is at TDC.
  • Spark-Ignition (SI) Engine: Engine where premixed air-fuel mixture is ignited by an electric spark plug (uses Octane rating, Quantity governing).
  • Compression-Ignition (CI) Engine: Engine where pure air is compressed and liquid fuel is injected into hot compressed air to auto-ignite (uses Cetane rating, Quality governing).
  • Octane Number: Anti-knock rating of petrol fuel relative to a mixture of Iso-octane $\text{ON} = 100$ and n-Heptane $\text{ON} = 0$.
  • Cetane Number: Ignition-delay rating of diesel fuel relative to a mixture of Cetane $\text{CN} = 100$ and $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $\text{CN} = 0$.
  • Knocking in SI Engine: Auto-ignition of unburned end-gas ahead of flame front causing severe pressure spikes. Reduced by retarding spark, lowering $r$, or higher Octane fuel.
  • Knocking in CI Engine: Explosion of accumulated fuel after a long ignition delay. Reduced by higher Cetane fuel, higher $r$, or higher inlet air temperature.
  • Scavenging: Process of clearing burned exhaust gases from 2-stroke cylinder using fresh incoming charge (Cross-flow, Loop, Uniflow).
  • Mean Effective Pressure $P_m$: Fictitious constant pressure that, if acting on the piston during the power stroke, would produce the same net work output as the actual variable-pressure cycle.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • SI Engine Compression Ratio Range: $r = 6 \text{ to } 12$ (limited by detonation/knocking).
  • CI Engine Compression Ratio Range: $r = 16 \text{ to } 22$ (required to achieve auto-ignition temperature).
  • Air-Fuel Ratio (SI Engine):
  • Idling (Rich): $\approx 12:1$
  • Stoichiometric (Chemically Correct): $\approx 14.7:1 \text{ or } 15:1$
  • Maximum Power: $\approx 12.5:1 - 13.5:1$
  • Cruising (Lean): $\approx 16:1 - 18:1$
  • Indian Automotive Diesel Cetane Number: $\text{CN} \approx 41 \text{ to } 50$.

3.1 Air-Standard Cycles & Efficiency Comparisons

1. Concept Pointers
  • Ideal air-standard cycles assume air behaves as an ideal gas with constant specific heats $\gamma = 1.40$.
  • Efficiency Formulas:
  • Otto: $\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}$
  • Diesel: $\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} \left[\frac{\rho^\gamma - 1}{\gamma (\rho - 1)}\right]$
  • Diesel Efficiency Trend: As cut-off ratio $\rho$ INCREASES, Diesel efficiency DECREASES! As $\rho \to 1$, Diesel efficiency approaches Otto efficiency.
  • Master Efficiency Comparisons:
  • Same Compression Ratio $r$ & Heat Input:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}}}$$
    (Otto adds all heat at constant volume at highest average temperature).
  • Same Maximum Pressure $P_{\text{max}}$ & Heat Input:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}}}$$
    (Diesel can operate at much higher compression ratio for the same peak pressure).
💡 Key Concept: Comparison Rule: - Same compression ratio $r \implies \eta_{\text{Otto}}$ is MAXIMUM. - Same peak pressure $P_{\text{max}} \implies \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$ is MAXIMUM.

[!TRAP] Cut-Off Ratio Trend: Increasing cut-off ratio $\rho$ INCREASES load/power, but DECREASES thermal efficiency of a Diesel cycle!

2. Decision Tree
Cycle Efficiency Comparison Check?
├─ Is Compression Ratio r SAME for all cycles?
│  ├─ YES → eta_Otto > eta_Dual > eta_Diesel (Otto is maximum)
│  └─ NO → Check Maximum Peak Pressure P_max
│     └─ Same Peak Pressure P_max → eta_Diesel > eta_Dual > eta_Otto (Diesel is maximum)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - 1/r^{\gamma-1}$.
  • [ ] Same $r \implies \eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$.
  • [ ] Same $P_{\text{max}} \implies \eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.
  • [ ] Increasing cut-off ratio $\rho$ lowers Diesel efficiency.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An Otto cycle has a compression ratio $r = 8$ $\gamma = 1.4$. Calculate its air-standard thermal efficiency. - Solution:

$${\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} = 1 - \frac{1}{8^{1.4 - 1}} = 1 - \frac{1}{8^{0.4}} = 1 - \frac{1}{2.2974} = 1 - 0.4352 = 0.5648 = 56.48\%}$$
- Key Insight: Substitute $r=8$ and exponent $0.4$ directly.

3.2 Engine Geometry & Compression Ratio

1. Concept Pointers
  • Compression Ratio $r = \frac{V_s + V_c}{V_c} = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c}$.
  • Clearance volume $V_c$ is measured when piston is at Top Dead Center (TDC).
  • Swept volume $V_s = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 L$. Total displacement capacity $= V_s \times k$.
  • Typical Ranges:
  • SI Engines: $r = 6 \text{ to } 12$ (limited by knocking/detonation).
  • CI Engines: $r = 16 \text{ to } 22$ (required for auto-ignition).
💡 Key Concept: Compression Ratio Definition: $r = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c}$. ALWAYS add 1! $r = \frac{V_s}{V_c}$ is a frequent formula trap.

[!TRAP] Swept Volume vs Displacement: Engine capacity in cc (e.g. $1500 \text{ cc}$) represents the TOTAL SWEPT VOLUME of all cylinders combined $V_s \times k$, NOT the clearance volume!

2. Decision Tree
Compression Ratio Calculation?
├─ Identify Volumes → Swept Volume Vs, Clearance Volume Vc
├─ Formula → r = (Vs + Vc) / Vc = 1 + (Vs / Vc)
└─ Compression Ratio Range Check → SI: 6-12, CI: 16-22
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $r = 1 + V_s/V_c$.
  • [ ] SI $r = 6-12$, CI $r = 16-22$.
  • [ ] $V_s = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 L$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An engine has a clearance volume $V_c = 50 \text{ cc}$ and a swept volume $V_s = 450 \text{ cc}$. Calculate its compression ratio $r$. - Solution:

$${r = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c} = 1 + \frac{450}{50} = 1 + 9 = 10}$$
- Key Insight: $r = 10:1$. Always add 1 to the ratio $V_s/V_c$.

3.3 Two-Stroke vs Four-Stroke Engines & Kinematics

1. Concept Pointers
  • Four-Stroke Engine:
  • Cycle completed in 2 crankshaft revolutions ($720^\circ$ crank angle).
  • Contains 1 power stroke for every 2 revolutions.
  • Camshaft speed $N_{\text{cam}} = \frac{N_{\text{crank}}}{2}$.
  • Uses intake and exhaust valves. Higher thermal efficiency, lower fuel consumption.
  • Two-Stroke Engine:
  • Cycle completed in 1 crankshaft revolution ($360^\circ$ crank angle).
  • Contains 1 power stroke for every 1 revolution.
  • Uses ports (inlet, transfer, exhaust) uncovered by the piston.
  • Higher power-to-weight ratio, simpler construction, lower cost.
  • Scavenging in 2-Stroke:
  • Expulsion of exhaust gases by incoming fresh charge.
  • Types: Cross-flow (uses piston deflector), Loop scavenging, Uniflow scavenging (most efficient).
💡 Key Concept: Camshaft Speed Rule: In a 4-stroke engine, the camshaft rotates at HALF the speed of the crankshaft $N_{\text{cam}} = N_{\text{crank}}/2$.

[!TRAP] Power Strokes Comparison: At the same speed, a 2-stroke engine has TWICE as many power strokes per minute as a 4-stroke engine! However, theoretical power output is NOT double due to poor scavenging and charge loss.

2. Decision Tree
Engine Stroke & Kinematics Check?
├─ Is cycle completed in 2 revolutions (720°)? → 4-Stroke Engine (N_cam = N_crank / 2)
├─ Is cycle completed in 1 revolution (360°)? → 2-Stroke Engine (Ports, Scavenging)
└─ Scavenging Efficiency Ranking → Uniflow > Loop > Cross-flow
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] 4-stroke $= 2$ crank revolutions $720^\circ$.
  • [ ] $N_{\text{cam}} = N_{\text{crank}}/2$.
  • [ ] 2-stroke $= 1$ crank revolution $360^\circ$.
  • [ ] Uniflow scavenging is most efficient.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A 4-stroke engine runs at $1500 \text{ rpm}$. Calculate the speed of the camshaft and the number of power strokes per minute. - Solution:

$${N_{\text{cam}} = \frac{N_{\text{crank}}}{2} = \frac{1500}{2} = 750 \text{ rpm}}$$
$${\text{Power Strokes per min} = \frac{N}{2} = \frac{1500}{2} = 750}$$
- Key Insight: Both camshaft speed and power strokes per minute are $750$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Calculate the time duration in seconds for a fuel injection process lasting $\theta = 20^\circ$ crank angle in a Diesel engine running at $1200 \text{ rpm}$. - Solution:

$${t = \frac{\theta}{6 N} = \frac{20}{6 \times 1200} = \frac{20}{7200} = \frac{1}{360} \text{ seconds} \approx 0.00278 \text{ s} = 2.78 \text{ ms}}$$
- Key Insight: Use formula $t = \theta / (6N)$ directly.

3.4 SI vs CI Engines, Fuel Systems & Governing

1. Concept Pointers
  • Spark-Ignition (SI) Petrol Engines:
  • Fuel and air premixed in carburettor or intake port.
  • Ignited by electric spark plug near TDC compression.
  • Quantity Governing: Load controlled by throttling the total volume of air-fuel mixture entering the cylinder. Air-fuel ratio remains nearly constant.
  • Compression-Ignition (CI) Diesel Engines:
  • Pure air inducted during suction and compressed to high temperature.
  • Fuel injected near TDC compression and auto-ignites.
  • Quality Governing: Load controlled by varying fuel quantity injected into a constant mass of air. Air-fuel ratio changes with load.
  • Ignition Systems:
  • Battery Coil Ignition: Requires battery power; used in cars.
  • Magneto Ignition: Self-generating (no battery needed); used in motorcycles/aircraft.
💡 Key Concept: Governing Mechanism Distinction: - SI Engines $\implies$ Quantity Governing (Throttle valve controls mixture volume). - CI Engines $\implies$ Quality Governing (Fuel pump controls fuel quantity per stroke).
2. Decision Tree
Engine Type & Governing Identification?
├─ Is fuel premixed with air and spark-ignited? → SI Engine (Quantity Governing via Throttle)
└─ Is pure air compressed and fuel injected? → CI Engine (Quality Governing via Fuel Pump)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] SI $\implies$ Spark plug, Quantity governing.
  • [ ] CI $\implies$ Injector, Quality governing.
  • [ ] Stoichiometric AFR $\approx 15:1$.
  • [ ] Magneto ignition needs no battery.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Differentiate between Quantity and Quality governing in IC engines. - Solution: - Quantity Governing (SI Engines): Regulates the quantity (volume) of air-fuel mixture admitted to the cylinder using a throttle valve, while keeping the air-fuel ratio relatively constant. - Quality Governing (CI Engines): Regulates the quality (air-fuel ratio) of the mixture by varying the mass of fuel injected per stroke into a fixed quantity of compressed air. - Key Insight: SI = Quantity; CI = Quality.

3.5 Combustion, Knocking, Octane & Cetane Ratings

1. Concept Pointers
  • Knocking in SI Engines (Detonation):
  • Occurs due to auto-ignition of unburned end-gas ahead of the flame front.
  • Phenomenon occurs at the END of combustion.
  • To REDUCE SI Knocking:
    • RETARD spark timing.
    • LOWER compression ratio $r$.
    • INCREASE engine speed.
    • Use HIGHER Octane fuel.
    • Increase turbulence / reduce distance to end-gas.
  • Knocking in CI Engines:
  • Occurs due to long ignition delay period resulting in rapid explosion of accumulated fuel.
  • Phenomenon occurs at the BEGINNING of combustion.
  • To REDUCE CI Knocking:
    • REDUCE ignition delay.
    • INCREASE compression ratio $r$.
    • INCREASE inlet air temperature / pressure.
    • Use HIGHER Cetane fuel.
  • Fuel Ratings:
  • Octane Number (SI Fuel): Anti-knock rating relative to Iso-octane $\text{ON}=100$ and n-Heptane $\text{ON}=0$.
  • Cetane Number (CI Fuel): Ignition-delay quality relative to Cetane / n-hexadecane $\text{CN}=100$ and $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $\text{CN}=0$.
  • High Octane fuel has LOW Cetane number; High Cetane fuel has LOW Octane number!
💡 Key Concept: Opposite Knocking Characteristics: - Compression Ratio $r \uparrow \implies$ SI Knocking INCREASES, but CI Knocking DECREASES! - Ignition Delay $\uparrow \implies$ CI Knocking INCREASES. - High Octane fuel resists SI knock; High Cetane fuel resists CI knock.

[!TRAP] Spark Timing Adjustment for SI Knock: To eliminate knocking in an SI engine, SPARK MUST BE RETARDED (delayed), NOT ADVANCED! Advancing spark increases end-gas pressure and worsens knocking.

2. Decision Tree
Knocking & Fuel Rating Analysis?
├─ SI Engine Knocking (End-gas auto-ignition)
│  ├─ Cause: High compression ratio, advanced spark, low octane
│  └─ Remedy: Retard spark, lower r, use HIGHER Octane fuel (Iso-octane 100)
└─ CI Engine Knocking (Long ignition delay explosion)
   ├─ Cause: Low inlet temperature, low compression ratio, low cetane
   └─ Remedy: Increase r, increase inlet temp, use HIGHER Cetane fuel (Cetane 100)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] SI Knocking $\implies$ End-gas auto-ignition. Retard spark to fix.
  • [ ] CI Knocking $\implies$ Long ignition delay. Increase $r$ to fix.
  • [ ] Octane rating: Iso-octane $100$, n-Heptane $0$.
  • [ ] Cetane rating: Cetane $100$, $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: State the composition of reference fuels used to define Octane Number and Cetane Number. - Solution: - Octane Number: Percentage by volume of Iso-octane ($\text{ON} = 100$, high knock resistance) in a blend with n-Heptane ($\text{ON} = 0$, severe knock tendency). - Cetane Number: Percentage by volume of Cetane / n-hexadecane ($\text{CN} = 100$, short ignition delay) in a blend with $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene ($\text{CN} = 0$, long ignition delay). - Key Insight: Iso-octane/n-Heptane for Petrol; Cetane/$\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene for Diesel.

3.6 Engine Performance Parameters & Efficiencies

1. Concept Pointers
  • Mean Effective Pressure $P_m$:
    $${P_m = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{V_s} = \frac{\text{Work Done per Cycle}}{\text{Swept Volume}}}$$
  • Mechanical Efficiency $\eta_m$:
    $${\eta_m = \frac{BP}{IP} = \frac{IP - FP}{IP}}$$
  • Friction Power $FP = IP - BP$. Measured via Morse Test (multi-cylinder SI engines) or Willan's Line method (CI engines).
  • Volumetric Efficiency $\eta_v$:
    $${\eta_v = \frac{V_{\text{actual}}}{V_s}}$$
    Measures effectiveness of cylinder induction.
  • Relative Efficiency $\eta_{\text{relative}}$:
    $${\eta_{\text{relative}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{thermal, actual}}}{\eta_{\text{air-standard}}}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Morse Test Application: Morse test is used to measure Indicated Power $IP$ and Friction Power $FP$ of MULTI-CYLINDER engines by cutting off spark/fuel to one cylinder at a time!
2. Decision Tree
Performance Parameter Check?
├─ Mean Effective Pressure → P_m = W_net / V_s
├─ Mechanical Efficiency → eta_m = BP / IP = (IP - FP) / IP
└─ Friction Power Measurement → Morse Test (Multi-cylinder), Willan's Line (Unthrottled CI)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $P_m = W_{\text{net}} / V_s$.
  • [ ] $\eta_m = BP / IP$.
  • [ ] Morse test for multi-cylinder $IP$.
  • [ ] Relative efficiency $= \eta_{\text{actual}} / \eta_{\text{ideal}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A 4-stroke IC engine produces $40 \text{ kW}$ Brake Power $BP$ and has a Friction Power $FP = 10 \text{ kW}$. Calculate its Indicated Power $IP$ and Mechanical Efficiency $\eta_m$. - Solution:

$${IP = BP + FP = 40 + 10 = 50 \text{ kW}}$$
$${\eta_m = \frac{BP}{IP} = \frac{40}{50} = 0.80 = 80\%}$$
- Key Insight: $IP = 50 \text{ kW}$, $\eta_m = 80\%$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Write the formula for thermal efficiency of an ideal Otto cycle.
A: $\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}$, where $r$ is compression ratio and $\gamma$ is specific heat ratio.

Q2: Compare thermal efficiency of Otto, Diesel, and Dual cycles for the same compression ratio $r$ and heat input.
A: $\eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$.

Q3: Compare thermal efficiency of Otto, Diesel, and Dual cycles for the same peak pressure $P_{\text{max}}$ and heat input.
A: $\eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.

Q4: Define Compression Ratio $r$ of an IC engine.
A: $r = \frac{V_s + V_c}{V_c} = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c}$, where $V_s$ is swept volume and $V_c$ is clearance volume.

Q5: State the relationship between camshaft speed $N_{\text{cam}}$ and crankshaft speed $N_{\text{crank}}$ in a 4-stroke engine.
A: $N_{\text{cam}} = \frac{N_{\text{crank}}}{2}$.

Q6: Differentiate between Quantity and Quality governing.
A: Quantity governing (SI engines) varies the volume of air-fuel mixture admitted via a throttle valve while keeping air-fuel ratio constant. Quality governing (CI engines) varies the mass of fuel injected per stroke into a constant volume of air.

Q7: What is the cause of knocking in an SI engine?
A: Auto-ignition of the unburned end-gas ahead of the advancing flame front near the end of combustion.

Q8: How can knocking be reduced in an SI engine?
A: Retard spark timing, lower compression ratio $r$, increase engine speed, or use fuel with higher Octane number.

Q9: What is the cause of knocking in a CI engine?
A: Long ignition delay period resulting in excessive accumulation of fuel that explodes violently upon ignition.

Q10: How can knocking be reduced in a CI engine?
A: Increase compression ratio $r$, increase inlet air temperature/pressure, or use fuel with higher Cetane number.

Q11: Define Octane Number.
A: The percentage by volume of Iso-octane $\text{ON}=100$ in a mixture with n-Heptane $\text{ON}=0$ that matches the knock intensity of the test sample.

Q12: Define Cetane Number.
A: The percentage by volume of Cetane $\text{CN}=100$ in a mixture with $\alpha$-methyl-naphthalene $\text{CN}=0$ that matches the ignition delay of the test sample.

Q13: Define Mean Effective Pressure $P_m$.
A: $P_m = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{V_s}$, the fictitious constant pressure that produces the same net work output as the actual variable-pressure cycle.

Q14: What is the Morse Test used for?
A: To determine the Indicated Power $IP$ and Friction Power $FP$ of multi-cylinder IC engines by cutting off ignition/fuel to individual cylinders.

Q15: Name the three principal scavenging methods used in 2-stroke engines.
A: Cross-flow scavenging, Loop scavenging, and Uniflow scavenging.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Same $r$ vs Same $P_{\text{max}}$:
  • Same $r \implies \eta_{\text{Otto}}$ is highest.
  • Same $P_{\text{max}} \implies \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$ is highest.
  • Diesel Efficiency Trend: $\rho \uparrow \implies \eta_{\text{Diesel}} \downarrow$.
  • 4-Stroke Kinematics: $720^\circ$ crank angle per cycle; $N_{\text{cam}} = N_{\text{crank}} / 2$.
  • Governing: SI = Quantity (throttle); CI = Quality (fuel pump).
  • Knock Fixes:
  • SI Knocking $\implies$ Retard spark, lower $r$, higher Octane.
  • CI Knocking $\implies$ Increase $r$, higher inlet temp, higher Cetane.
  • Fuel Inverse Relation: High Octane $\implies$ Low Cetane. High Cetane $\implies$ Low Octane.
