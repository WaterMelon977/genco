2. Air-Standard Cycles

Air-standard analysis assumes the working fluid is air behaving as an ideal gas with constant specific heats ($\gamma = 1.4$ for air). Combustion is replaced by external heat addition; exhaust by heat rejection. These give upper bounds on efficiency for each cycle type.

2.1 Otto Cycle (SI Engine)

Heat addition at constant volume (isochoric); heat rejection at constant volume. Four reversible processes: 1–2 isentropic compression, 2–3 constant-volume heat addition, 3–4 isentropic expansion, 4–1 constant-volume heat rejection.

$$\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}$$
  • $r = V_1/V_2$ — compression ratio; efficiency depends only on $r$ and $\gamma$, not on heat input
  • Typical SI engine: $r = 6$–12 (limited by detonation/knocking)

2.2 Diesel Cycle (CI Engine — Ideal)

Heat addition at constant pressure (isobaric); heat rejection at constant volume.

$$\eta_{\text{Diesel}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}\left[\frac{\rho^\gamma - 1}{\gamma(\rho - 1)}\right]$$
  • $\rho = V_3/V_2$ — cut-off ratio (ratio of volume after to before heat addition)
  • As $\rho \to 1$, the bracketed term $\to 1$ and $\eta_{\text{Diesel}} \to \eta_{\text{Otto}}$ (same $r$)
  • Increasing $\rho$ (more fuel, higher load) reduces $\eta_{\text{Diesel}}$ for the same $r$
  • Typical CI engine: $r = 16$–22 (high $r$ needed for auto-ignition)

2.3 Dual Cycle (Mixed Cycle)

More realistic model for CI engines; partial constant-volume + partial constant-pressure heat addition.

$$\eta_{\text{Dual}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}\left[\frac{\alpha\rho^\gamma - 1}{(\alpha-1) + \gamma\alpha(\rho-1)}\right]$$
  • $\alpha = P_3/P_2$ — pressure ratio during constant-volume combustion phase
  • When $\alpha = 1$: Dual → Diesel; when $\rho = 1$: Dual → Otto

3. Engine Geometry & Compression Ratio

$$r = \frac{V_s + V_c}{V_c} = 1 + \frac{V_s}{V_c}$$
$$V_s = \frac{\pi}{4}D^2 L \qquad \text{(single cylinder swept volume)}$$
  • $V_s$ — swept volume (m³ or cc); $V_c$ — clearance volume at TDC (m³ or cc)
  • $D$ — bore diameter (m); $L$ — stroke length (m)
  • Engine displacement = $V_s \times k$ (total swept volume of all $k$ cylinders)
Stroke/Bore ratioEngine typeCharacteristics
$L/D > 1$Under-square / long-strokeBetter torque at low speed; higher piston speed; older designs
$L/D = 1$Square engineBalanced design
$L/D < 1$Over-square / short-strokeHigher revving; better volumetric efficiency; modern performance engines

  • Quantity governing (SI): throttle valve reduces both air and fuel — mixture ratio stays stoichiometric; power controlled by mass flow
  • Quality governing (CI): no throttle; fuel injection quantity varied; air always in excess (lean burning)

6. Engine Performance Parameters

6.1 Mean Effective Pressure

$$P_m = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{V_s} = \frac{\text{Area of P-V indicator diagram}}{\text{Length of indicator diagram}} \times \text{Spring constant}$$

6.2 Indicated Power & Brake Power

$$IP = \frac{P_{mi} \cdot L \cdot A \cdot n \cdot k}{60\,000} \quad \text{(kW)}$$
  • $n = N/2$ for 4-stroke; $n = N$ for 2-stroke (working strokes per minute)
  • $k$ — number of cylinders; $A = \pi D^2/4$ — bore area (m²)
$$BP = \frac{2\pi N T}{60\,000} = \frac{NT}{9549} \quad \text{(kW)}$$

6.3 Efficiencies

$$\eta_m = \frac{BP}{IP} = \frac{IP - FP}{IP} \qquad \text{(mechanical efficiency)}$$
$$\eta_v = \frac{m_{\text{actual}}}{m_{\text{theoretical}}} = \frac{V_{\text{actual suction}}}{V_s} \qquad \text{(volumetric efficiency)}$$
$$\eta_{\text{relative}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{thermal, actual}}}{\eta_{\text{air-standard}}} \qquad \text{(relative/indicated thermal efficiency)}$$

Key Points to Remember

  • Otto efficiency: $\eta = 1 - 1/r^{\gamma-1}$. Depends only on $r$ and $\gamma$, not heat input.
  • Diesel efficiency: increasing cut-off ratio $\rho$ (more load) decreases efficiency for the same compression ratio.
  • Same $r$ and $Q$: $\eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$. Same $P_{\max}$ and $Q$: $\eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.
  • Compression ratio formula: $r = 1 + V_s/V_c$. The "+1" is essential — common exam error to write $r = V_s/V_c$.
  • SI engine $r = 6$–12 (knock-limited); CI engine $r = 16$–22 (auto-ignition required).
  • 4-stroke camshaft speed = $N_{\text{crank}}/2$. Cam turns once per two crank revolutions.
  • 2-stroke fires every revolution; no camshaft; mist lubrication (oil premixed 2–3% in fuel).
  • SI governing: quantity (throttle controls mixture volume). CI governing: quality (throttle controls fuel only).
  • IP includes friction losses; BP is useful shaft output; FP = IP − BP.
  • Indicated power formula for 4-stroke: $IP = P_{mi} \cdot L \cdot A \cdot (N/2) \cdot k / 60\,000$ kW.
  • Mechanical efficiency $\eta_m = BP/IP$; typically 75–90% for automotive engines.
  • As $\rho \to 1$ in Diesel cycle, $\eta_{\text{Diesel}} \to \eta_{\text{Otto}}$ for the same $r$: constant-pressure heat addition approaches constant-volume limit.
