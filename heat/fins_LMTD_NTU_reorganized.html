2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Fins & Extended Surfaces

  • Fin Parameter $m$:
    $${\boxed{m = \sqrt{\frac{h \cdot P}{k \cdot A_c}} \quad (\text{m}^{-1})}}$$
  • $h$: convective heat transfer coefficient $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$
  • $P$: fin cross-sectional perimeter $\text{m}$
  • $k$: thermal conductivity of fin material $\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$

  • $A_c$: fin cross-sectional area $\text{m}^2$

  • Fin Efficiency ($\eta_f$, Insulated Tip):

    $${\boxed{\eta_f = \frac{\text{Actual Heat Transfer from Fin}}{\text{Heat Transfer if Entire Fin were at Base Temp } T_b} = \frac{\tanh(m \cdot L)}{m \cdot L}}}$$

  • $L$: length of fin $\text{m}$

  • Fin Efficiency ($\eta_f$, Convective Tip with Corrected Length $L_c$):

    $${\boxed{\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(m \cdot L_c)}{m \cdot L_c}}}$$

  • Rectangular Fin: $L_c = L + \frac{t}{2}$ ($t$ = thickness)

  • Cylindrical Pin Fin: $L_c = L + \frac{D}{4}$ ($D$ = diameter)

  • Fin Effectiveness $\varepsilon_f$:

    $${\boxed{\varepsilon_f = \frac{Q_{\text{fin}}}{Q_{\text{without fin}}} = \frac{Q_{\text{fin}}}{h \cdot A_c \cdot (T_b - T_\infty)} = \sqrt{\frac{k \cdot P}{h \cdot A_c}} \cdot \tanh(m \cdot L)}}$$

  • Fin justification criterion: $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$.

2. Heat Exchangers & Fouling Factor

  • Clean Overall Heat Transfer Coefficient $U_c$:

    $${\boxed{\frac{1}{U_c} = \frac{1}{h_i} + \frac{A_i \cdot \ln(r_o/r_i)}{2\pi \cdot k \cdot L} + \frac{A_i}{A_o \cdot h_o}}}$$

  • Fouling Factor $R_f$:

    $${\boxed{R_f = \frac{1}{U_{\text{dirty}}} - \frac{1}{U_{\text{clean}}} \quad (\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W})}}$$

  • $U_{\text{dirty}}$: overall HTC after scale/deposit buildup $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$

  • $U_{\text{clean}}$: overall HTC of fresh clean exchanger $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$

  • Dirty Overall Heat Transfer Coefficient $U_d$:

    $${\boxed{\frac{1}{U_d} = \frac{1}{U_c} + R_f}}$$

3. LMTD Method

  • Heat Transfer Rate Equation:
    $${\boxed{Q = U \cdot A \cdot \Delta T_{\text{lm}} = U \cdot A \cdot F \cdot \text{LMTD}_{\text{counter}} \quad (\text{W})}}$$

  • $F$: LMTD correction factor ($F \le 1.0$; $F = 1.0$ for single-pass parallel/counter flow)

  • Logarithmic Mean Temperature Difference $\text{LMTD}$:

    $${\boxed{\text{LMTD} = \frac{\Delta T_1 - \Delta T_2}{\ln\left(\frac{\Delta T_1}{\Delta T_2}\right)}}}$$

  • Terminal Differences $\Delta T_1, \Delta T_2$:

  • Parallel Flow: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,i} - T_{c,i}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,o} - T_{c,o}$

  • Counter Flow: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,i} - T_{c,o}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,o} - T_{c,i}$

  • Equal End Differences Special Case $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2$:

    $${\boxed{\text{LMTD} = \text{AMTD} = \Delta T_1}}$$

4. NTU–Effectiveness Method

  • Heat Capacity Rates & Capacity Ratio $C$:

    $${\boxed{C_h = \dot{m}h \cdot C{p,h}, \quad C_c = \dot{m}c \cdot C{p,c}}}$$
    $${\boxed{C_{\min} = \min(C_h, C_c), \quad C_{\max} = \max(C_h, C_c)}}$$
    $${\boxed{C = \frac{C_{\min}}{C_{\max}} \quad (0 \le C \le 1.0)}}$$

  • Number of Transfer Units $\text{NTU}$:

    $${\boxed{\text{NTU} = \frac{U \cdot A}{C_{\min}}}}$$

  • Heat Exchanger Effectiveness $\varepsilon$:

    $${\boxed{\varepsilon = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{Q_{\max}} = \frac{C_h \cdot (T_{h,i} - T_{h,o})}{C_{\min} \cdot (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})} = \frac{C_c \cdot (T_{c,o} - T_{c,i})}{C_{\min} \cdot (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})}}}$$
    $${\boxed{Q_{\max} = C_{\min} \cdot (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})}}$$

  • Effectiveness Formulas:

  • Parallel Flow: $\boxed{\varepsilon = \frac{1 - \exp[-\text{NTU}(1+C)]}{1+C}}$
  • Counter Flow: $\boxed{\varepsilon = \frac{1 - \exp[-\text{NTU}(1-C)]}{1 - C \cdot \exp[-\text{NTU}(1-C)]}}$
  • Phase Change ($C = 0$, Boilers & Condensers): $\boxed{\varepsilon = 1 - \exp(-\text{NTU})}$
  • Balanced Counter Flow $C = 1.0$: $\boxed{\varepsilon = \frac{\text{NTU}}{1 + \text{NTU}}}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Fins (Extended Surfaces): Protrusions extended from a primary heat transfer surface to increase convective area $A$, used primarily when convection coefficient $h$ is low (e.g. air/gas side).
  • Fin Parameter $m$: $\sqrt{hP / kA_c}$, representing the ratio of convective cooling rate to conductive heat supply along the fin length.
  • Fin Efficiency $\eta_f$: Ratio of actual heat transfer from the fin to the maximum heat transfer if the entire fin surface were maintained at base temperature $T_b$ $\eta_f \le 1.0$.
  • Fin Effectiveness $\varepsilon_f$: Ratio of heat transfer rate with the fin to the heat transfer rate through the same base area without the fin. A fin is physically justified if $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$.
  • Fouling Factor $R_f$: Additional thermal resistance introduced by scale, rust, algae, or chemical deposits on heat exchanger tube surfaces $\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$.
  • LMTD: Logarithmic mean temperature difference between hot and cold fluid streams across a heat exchanger.
  • Capacity Ratio $C$: Ratio of minimum heat capacity rate to maximum heat capacity rate $C = C_{\min}/C_{\max} \le 1.0$.
  • NTU: Number of Transfer Units $\text{NTU} = U A / C_{\min}$, a non-dimensional measure of physical heat exchanger size.
  • Effectiveness $\varepsilon$: Ratio of actual heat transfer rate to the maximum thermodynamically possible heat transfer rate $Q_{\text{actual}} / Q_{\max}$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Fin Justification Threshold: $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$.
  • Parallel Flow Max Effectiveness Limit: For $C=1.0$ and $\text{NTU} \to \infty$, $\varepsilon_{\text{parallel}} \to 50\%$ $0.50$.
  • Phase Change Capacity Ratio: $C = 0$ for boilers, evaporators, and condensers.
  • LMTD Comparison: For identical inlet and outlet temperatures, $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$.
  • Phase Change LMTD: When $C=0$, $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} = \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Fins & Extended Surfaces

1. Concept Pointers
  • Fin Purpose: Added to surfaces exposed to low convective heat transfer coefficients (e.g. gas side, air-cooled engines, motorcycle cylinders, electronic heat sinks).
  • Fin Parameter: $m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A_c}}$ $\text{m}^{-1}$.
  • High Performance Fins: Require HIGH thermal conductivity $k$, LONG length $L$, THIN cross-section (large $P/A_c$), and LOW convection coefficient $h$.
  • Fin Efficiency $\eta_f$:
  • Insulated tip: $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL)}{mL}$.
  • Convective tip: $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL_c)}{mL_c}$, using corrected length $L_c = L + \frac{t}{2}$ (rectangular) or $L_c = L + \frac{D}{4}$ (pin fin).
  • Fin Effectiveness $\varepsilon_f$:
    $${\varepsilon_f = \sqrt{\frac{k P}{h A_c}} \cdot \tanh(mL)}$$
  • Adding fins is justified ONLY if $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$. If $\varepsilon_f < 1.0$, adding fins actually acts as insulation and reduces heat transfer!
💡 Key Concept: Fin Effectiveness Rule: Adding fins increases heat transfer ONLY if $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$. High thermal conductivity $k$ and low convection coefficient $h$ give maximum effectiveness!

[!TRAP] Efficiency vs Effectiveness Trap: - Fin Efficiency $\eta_f \le 1.0$ (always less than or equal to $100\%$). - Fin Effectiveness $\varepsilon_f$ CAN BE $> 1.0$ (and MUST be $> 2.0$ to justify adding fins)!

2. Decision Tree
Fin Performance Query Check?
├─ Is it Fin Parameter m? → m = sqrt(h * P / (k * A_c))  [Unit: 1/m]
├─ Is it Fin Efficiency eta_f (insulated tip)? → eta_f = tanh(m * L) / (m * L)
├─ Is tip convective? → Use L_c = L + t/2 in tanh(m * L_c) / (m * L_c)
└─ Is fin justified? → Fin Effectiveness epsilon_f = sqrt(k * P / (h * A_c)) * tanh(mL) > 2.0
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $m = \sqrt{hP / (k A_c)}$.
  • [ ] Insulated tip $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL)}{mL}$.
  • [ ] Corrected length $L_c = L + t/2$.
  • [ ] Fin justified if $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A aluminum fin $k = 200 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ of thickness $t = 2 \text{ mm}$ and length $L = 50 \text{ mm}$ is exposed to air with $h = 50 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Calculate the fin parameter $m$ and fin efficiency $\eta_f$ assuming an insulated tip. - Solution: - Perimeter $P \approx 2w$, Area $A_c = w \cdot t \implies \frac{P}{A_c} = \frac{2}{t} = \frac{2}{0.002} = 1000 \text{ m}^{-1}$. - $m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A_c}} = \sqrt{\frac{50 \times 1000}{200}} = \sqrt{250} \approx 15.81 \text{ m}^{-1}$. - $m L = 15.81 \times 0.05 = 0.7906$. - $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(0.7906)}{0.7906} = \frac{0.6588}{0.7906} = 0.833 = 83.3\%$. - Key Insight: $\eta_f = 83.3\%$. Use $\frac{P}{A_c} \approx \frac{2}{t}$ for wide rectangular fins.

3.2 Heat Exchanger Fundamentals & Fouling Factor

1. Concept Pointers
  • Overall Heat Transfer Coefficient $U$:
    $${\frac{1}{U_c A} = \frac{1}{h_i A_i} + \frac{\ln(r_o/r_i)}{2\pi k L} + \frac{1}{h_o A_o}}$$
  • Fouling Factor $R_f$:
  • Deposits of rust, algae, scale, or chemical sludge create an additional thermal resistance.
  • Formula: $R_f = \frac{1}{U_{\text{dirty}}} - \frac{1}{U_{\text{clean}}}$ (Units: $\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$).
  • Fouling INCREASES thermal resistance and DECREASES overall heat transfer coefficient $U$.
  • Fluid Allocation Rules:
  • Corrosive / Dirty Fluid: Placed inside TUBES (easier to clean mechanically, cheaper to use corrosion-resistant alloys for tubes than whole shell).
  • High-Pressure Fluid: Placed inside TUBES (cheaper small-diameter tubing).
  • Viscous / High-Velocity Fluid: Placed inside SHELL.
💡 Key Concept: Fouling Factor Units & Effect: - Fouling Factor Units $\implies \text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$ (Thermal Resistance per unit area!). - Fouling INCREASES resistance $\implies R_f = \frac{1}{U_d} - \frac{1}{U_c}$.

[!TRAP] Corrosive Fluid Location: Corrosive or scaling fluids should ALWAYS be routed through the TUBE SIDE, never the shell side!

2. Decision Tree
Heat Exchanger Construction & Fouling Check?
├─ Fouling Factor Formula → R_f = (1 / U_dirty) - (1 / U_clean)  [Units: m^2*K/W]
├─ Corrosive / Scaling Fluid Placement → TUBE SIDE (Easier mechanical cleaning)
├─ High Pressure Fluid Placement → TUBE SIDE (Smaller diameter pipe handles pressure)
└─ Purpose of Baffles → Support tubes AND increase shell-side velocity/turbulence (boosting h_o)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $R_f = \frac{1}{U_d} - \frac{1}{U_c}$ $\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$.
  • [ ] Fouling decreases $U$.
  • [ ] Corrosive fluid on tube side.
  • [ ] Baffles boost shell-side $h_o$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A clean heat exchanger has an overall heat transfer coefficient $U_c = 800 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. After 1 year of operation, scale deposits reduce the overall heat transfer coefficient to $U_d = 500 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Calculate the fouling factor $R_f$. - Solution:

$${R_f = \frac{1}{U_d} - \frac{1}{U_c} = \frac{1}{500} - \frac{1}{800} = 0.0020 - 0.00125 = 0.00075 \text{ m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}}$$
- Key Insight: $R_f = 0.00075 \text{ m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$. Subtract clean reciprocal from dirty reciprocal.

3.3 Logarithmic Mean Temperature Difference (LMTD) Method

1. Concept Pointers
  • Used when all fluid inlet and outlet temperatures are known to calculate required surface area $A$.
  • LMTD Equation:
    $${\text{LMTD} = \frac{\Delta T_1 - \Delta T_2}{\ln\left(\frac{\Delta T_1}{\Delta T_2}\right)}}$$
  • Flow Configurations:
  • Parallel Flow: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,i} - T_{c,i}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,o} - T_{c,o}$.
  • Counter Flow: $\Delta T_1 = T_{h,i} - T_{c,o}$, $\Delta T_2 = T_{h,o} - T_{c,i}$.
  • LMTD Comparison:
  • For identical terminal temperatures: $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$.
  • Consequently, a Counter-Flow heat exchanger requires the MINIMUM HEAT TRANSFER SURFACE AREA $A$ for a given heat duty!
  • Equal End Differences Special Case:
  • When $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 \implies \text{LMTD} = \text{AMTD} = \Delta T_1$.
  • Multipass / Cross-Flow Exchangers:
  • $Q = U \cdot A \cdot F \cdot \text{LMTD}_{\text{counter}}$, where $F \le 1.0$ is the LMTD correction factor (depends only on terminal temperatures).
💡 Key Concept: Counter-Flow LMTD Advantage: For the same inlet and outlet fluid temperatures, $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$. Thus, counter-flow requires the LEAST SURFACE AREA $A$ and smallest equipment size!

[!TRAP] Equal End Difference LMTD Trap: If $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2$, $\ln(\Delta T_1 / \Delta T_2) = \ln(1) = 0$, giving an indeterminate $0/0$ form. By L'Hôpital's rule, $\text{LMTD} = \Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2$!

2. Decision Tree
LMTD Calculation & Comparison Check?
├─ Parallel Flow End Differences → Delta_T1 = Th,i - Tc,i ; Delta_T2 = Th,o - Tc,o
├─ Counter Flow End Differences → Delta_T1 = Th,i - Tc,o ; Delta_T2 = Th,o - Tc,i
├─ Are end differences equal (Delta_T1 = Delta_T2)? → LMTD = Delta_T1
└─ Surface Area Comparison → Counter-flow requires MINIMUM area A (since LMTD_counter is max)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\text{LMTD} = \frac{\Delta T_1 - \Delta T_2}{\ln(\Delta T_1/\Delta T_2)}$.
  • [ ] $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$.
  • [ ] Counter flow requires minimum area.
  • [ ] If $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 \implies \text{LMTD} = \Delta T_1$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a counter-flow heat exchanger, hot fluid cools from $150^\circ\text{C}$ to $100^\circ\text{C}$ while cold fluid warms from $30^\circ\text{C}$ to $80^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate the LMTD. - Solution: - Counter-flow end differences:

$${\Delta T_1 = T_{h,i} - T_{c,o} = 150 - 80 = 70^\circ\text{C}}$$
$${\Delta T_2 = T_{h,o} - T_{c,i} = 100 - 30 = 70^\circ\text{C}}$$
- Since $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 = 70^\circ\text{C}$, $\text{LMTD} = \text{AMTD} = 70^\circ\text{C}$. - Key Insight: $\text{LMTD} = 70^\circ\text{C}$. When end differences match, LMTD equals that difference.

3.4 NTU–Effectiveness Method

1. Concept Pointers
  • Used when outlet fluid temperatures are unknown to avoid tedious iterative LMTD calculations.
  • Heat Capacity Rates:
    $${C_h = \dot{m}h C{p,h}, \quad C_c = \dot{m}c C{p,c}}$$
    $${C_{\min} = \min(C_h, C_c), \quad C_{\max} = \max(C_h, C_c)}$$
    $${\text{Capacity Ratio } C = \frac{C_{\min}}{C_{\max}} \quad (0 \le C \le 1.0)}$$
  • Number of Transfer Units $\text{NTU}$:
    $${\text{NTU} = \frac{U A}{C_{\min}}}$$
    Non-dimensional measure of heat exchanger physical size. Larger $\text{NTU} \implies$ larger exchanger.
  • Effectiveness $\varepsilon$:
    $${\varepsilon = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{Q_{\max}} = \frac{C_h (T_{h,i} - T_{h,o})}{C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})} = \frac{C_c (T_{c,o} - T_{c,i})}{C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})}}$$
    Where maximum possible heat transfer $Q_{\max} = C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$.
💡 Key Concept: Maximum Heat Transfer Constant: $Q_{\max} = C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$. ALWAYS use $C_{\min}$ (the smaller heat capacity rate) to multiply the maximum overall fluid temperature difference!

[!TRAP] Effectiveness Formula Selection: - $Q_{\text{actual}}$ uses the actual fluid temperature change. - $Q_{\max}$ MUST use $C_{\min}$ and $(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$! Never use $C_{\max}$ in the denominator of effectiveness!

2. Decision Tree
NTU-Effectiveness Parameter Check?
├─ Capacity Ratio C → C = C_min / C_max  (0 <= C <= 1)
├─ NTU Formula → NTU = U * A / C_min
├─ Maximum Possible Heat Transfer Q_max → Q_max = C_min * (Th,i - Tc,i)
└─ Effectiveness Definition -> epsilon = Q_actual / Q_max
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $C = C_{\min} / C_{\max}$.
  • [ ] $\text{NTU} = U A / C_{\min}$.
  • [ ] $Q_{\max} = C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})$.
  • [ ] $\varepsilon = Q_{\text{actual}} / Q_{\max}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Hot fluid $C_h = 2000 \text{ W/K}$ enters a heat exchanger at $100^\circ\text{C}$. Cold fluid $C_c = 1000 \text{ W/K}$ enters at $20^\circ\text{C}$. The actual heat transfer rate is $Q = 40 \text{ kW}$. Calculate the heat exchanger effectiveness $\varepsilon$. - Solution: - $C_{\min} = \min(2000, 1000) = 1000 \text{ W/K} = 1.0 \text{ kW/K}$. - $Q_{\max} = C_{\min} (T_{h,i} - T_{c,i}) = 1.0 \text{ kW/K} \times (100 - 20)^\circ\text{C} = 80 \text{ kW}$. - $\varepsilon = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{Q_{\max}} = \frac{40 \text{ kW}}{80 \text{ kW}} = 0.50 = 50\%$. - Key Insight: $\varepsilon = 50\%$. $Q_{\max}$ uses $C_{\min} = 1000 \text{ W/K}$.

3.5 Special Cases: Phase Change $C=0$ & Balanced Flow $C=1$

1. Concept Pointers
  • Phase Change Fluids ($C = 0$, Boilers, Condensers, Evaporators):
  • During phase change, specific heat $C_p \to \infty \implies C_{\max} \to \infty \implies C = \frac{C_{\min}}{\infty} = 0$.
  • Effectiveness Formula:
    $${\varepsilon = 1 - \exp(-\text{NTU})}$$
  • Flow Arrangement Independence: For $C = 0$, parallel-flow and counter-flow heat exchangers have EXACTLY THE SAME EFFECTIVENESS AND LMTD!
  • Balanced Counter Flow ($C = 1.0$, Gas Turbine Regenerators):
  • When $C_h = C_c = C_{\min} \implies C = 1.0$.
  • Counter-Flow Effectiveness:
    $${\varepsilon = \frac{\text{NTU}}{1 + \text{NTU}}}$$
  • Temperature profiles are parallel straight lines $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 = \text{constant}$.
  • Parallel Flow Asymptotic Limit:
  • For parallel flow with $C = 1.0$, even if $\text{NTU} \to \infty$, the maximum effectiveness CANNOT EXCEED $50\%$ $\varepsilon_{\text{parallel, max}} = \frac{1}{1+1} = 0.50$.
💡 Key Concept: Phase Change $C=0$ Rule: When one fluid undergoes phase change (condenser/evaporator), $C = 0$, and $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-\text{NTU}}$ for BOTH parallel and counter flow! Flow direction does not matter.

[!TRAP] Parallel Flow Asymptote Trap: A parallel-flow heat exchanger with $C=1.0$ CAN NEVER exceed $50\%$ effectiveness, no matter how large the area $A$ or $\text{NTU}$ becomes!

2. Decision Tree
Special NTU Effectiveness Formula Check?
├─ Is one fluid condensing or boiling (Phase Change)? → C = 0 -> epsilon = 1 - exp(-NTU)  [Identical for parallel & counter]
├─ Is it Balanced Counter Flow (C = 1.0)? → epsilon = NTU / (1 + NTU)
└─ What is parallel flow max effectiveness for C=1 as NTU -> infinity? → Exactly 50% (0.50)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Phase change $C = 0$.
  • [ ] Phase change $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-\text{NTU}}$.
  • [ ] $C=0 \implies$ Parallel and counter effectiveness are identical.
  • [ ] Balanced counter flow $C=1$ $\implies \varepsilon = \frac{\text{NTU}}{1+\text{NTU}}$.
  • [ ] Parallel flow $C=1$ max $\varepsilon = 50\%$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A steam condenser has an overall heat transfer coefficient $U = 1000 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$, area $A = 2.0 \text{ m}^2$, and cold water capacity rate $C_c = 1000 \text{ W/K}$. Calculate the condenser effectiveness $\varepsilon$. - Solution: - Condensing steam $\implies C_{\max} \to \infty \implies C = 0 \implies C_{\min} = C_c = 1000 \text{ W/K}$. - $\text{NTU} = \frac{U A}{C_{\min}} = \frac{1000 \times 2.0}{1000} = 2.0$. - $\varepsilon = 1 - \exp(-\text{NTU}) = 1 - e^{-2.0} = 1 - 0.1353 = 0.8647 = 86.47\%$. - Key Insight: $\varepsilon = 86.47\%$. Use $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-\text{NTU}}$ for condensers $C=0$.

3.6 Shell-and-Tube Exchanger Features & Construction

1. Concept Pointers
  • Baffles:
  • Metal segment plates installed inside the shell.
  • Functions:
    1. Support the tubes physically to prevent sagging and vibration.
    2. Force shell-side fluid to flow back and forth across tubes in turbulence, increasing shell-side convection coefficient $h_o$.
  • Floating Head Construction:
  • One tube sheet is fixed to the shell, while the other tube sheet "floats" freely inside the shell cover.
  • Primary Purpose: Accommodate DIFFERENTIAL THERMAL EXPANSION between shell and tubes during large temperature changes, preventing tube warping or joint failure.
  • Multipass Exchangers:
  • Passing tube fluid multiple times (2-pass, 4-pass) increases fluid velocity and heat transfer rate.
  • Requires LMTD Correction Factor $F < 1.0$.
💡 Key Concept: Floating Head Purpose: Floating head construction is used EXCLUSIVELY to absorb DIFFERENTIAL THERMAL EXPANSION between tubes and shell under large temperature differences!
2. Decision Tree
Shell & Tube Feature Check?
├─ Why are baffles installed? → Support tubes AND increase shell-side turbulence/velocity (boosting h_o)
├─ Why is floating head used? → Accommodate differential thermal expansion under high Delta_T
└─ Where is corrosive fluid routed? → Tube side (Easier cleaning & cheaper alloy tubing)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Baffles support tubes & increase $h_o$.
  • [ ] Floating head handles thermal expansion.
  • [ ] Corrosive fluid on tube side.
  • [ ] Multipass uses LMTD correction factor $F < 1.0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: State the main reason for selecting a floating-head shell-and-tube heat exchanger over a fixed tube-sheet exchanger. - Solution: - To accommodate differential thermal expansion between the shell and the tubes when operating under large temperature differences. - Key Insight: Floating head handles thermal expansion.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Write the formula for fin parameter $m$ and state its SI unit.
A: $m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A_c}}$, with SI unit $\text{m}^{-1}$.

Q2: What is the formula for fin efficiency $\eta_f$ of a straight rectangular fin with an insulated tip?
A: $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL)}{mL}$.

Q3: How is corrected fin length $L_c$ defined for a rectangular fin of thickness $t$?
A: $L_c = L + \frac{t}{2}$.

Q4: When is adding fins to a surface physically justified?
A: When fin effectiveness $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$. If $\varepsilon_f < 1.0$, adding fins reduces heat transfer.

Q5: Write the formula for Fouling Factor $R_f$ and state its SI unit.
A: $R_f = \frac{1}{U_{\text{dirty}}} - \frac{1}{U_{\text{clean}}}$, with SI unit $\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$.

Q6: Why should corrosive or scaling fluids be routed through the tube side rather than the shell side?
A: Because tubes are easier to clean mechanically and require less expensive corrosion-resistant alloy material than a large shell.

Q7: Write the LMTD formula for a heat exchanger.
A: $\text{LMTD} = \frac{\Delta T_1 - \Delta T_2}{\ln(\Delta T_1 / \Delta T_2)}$.

Q8: Why does a counter-flow heat exchanger require less surface area than a parallel-flow exchanger for the same heat duty?
A: Because for identical terminal temperatures, $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}}$, providing a higher mean driving temperature difference.

Q9: What is the value of LMTD when the terminal temperature differences are equal $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2$?
A: $\text{LMTD} = \text{AMTD} = \Delta T_1$.

Q10: Define Capacity Ratio $C$ in the NTU-effectiveness method.
A: $C = \frac{C_{\min}}{C_{\max}} = \frac{\min(C_h, C_c)}{\max(C_h, C_c)}$, where $0 \le C \le 1.0$.

Q11: Write the formula for Number of Transfer Units $\text{NTU}$.
A: $\text{NTU} = \frac{U A}{C_{\min}}$.

Q12: Define Heat Exchanger Effectiveness $\varepsilon$.
A: $\varepsilon = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{Q_{\max}} = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{C_{\min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})}$.

Q13: What is the effectiveness formula for a condenser or boiler $C=0$?
A: $\varepsilon = 1 - \exp(-\text{NTU})$ for both parallel and counter flow.

Q14: Write the effectiveness formula for a balanced counter-flow heat exchanger $C=1.0$.
A: $\varepsilon = \frac{\text{NTU}}{1 + \text{NTU}}$.

Q15: What is the maximum theoretical effectiveness of a parallel-flow heat exchanger with $C=1.0$ as $\text{NTU} \to \infty$?
A: $50\%$ $\varepsilon = 0.50$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Fin Parameter: $m = \sqrt{\frac{hP}{kA_c}}$ $\text{m}^{-1}$.
  • Fin Efficiency: Insulated tip $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL)}{mL} \le 1.0$. Convective tip uses $L_c = L + t/2$.
  • Fin Effectiveness: $\varepsilon_f = \sqrt{\frac{kP}{hA_c}}\tanh(mL)$. Fin justified if $\varepsilon_f > 2.0$.
  • Fouling Factor: $R_f = \frac{1}{U_d} - \frac{1}{U_c}$ $\text{m}^2\cdot\text{K/W}$. Reduces overall HTC $U$.
  • Fluid Routing: Corrosive & high-pressure fluids $\implies$ TUBE SIDE. Viscous fluids $\implies$ SHELL SIDE.
  • Baffles: Support tubes & increase shell-side turbulence/velocity $\implies h_o \uparrow$.
  • Floating Head: Absorbs DIFFERENTIAL THERMAL EXPANSION under large $\Delta T$.
  • LMTD Comparison: $\text{LMTD}{\text{counter}} > \text{LMTD}{\text{parallel}} \implies$ Counter flow requires MINIMUM surface area $A$.
  • LMTD Equal End Differences: $\Delta T_1 = \Delta T_2 \implies \text{LMTD} = \Delta T_1$.
  • NTU: $\text{NTU} = \frac{UA}{C_{\min}}$. Non-dimensional size metric.
  • Effectiveness: $\varepsilon = \frac{Q_{\text{actual}}}{C_{\min}(T_{h,i} - T_{c,i})}$.
  • Phase Change $C=0$: $\varepsilon = 1 - e^{-\text{NTU}}$ for parallel AND counter flow.
  • Balanced Counter Flow $C=1$: $\varepsilon = \frac{\text{NTU}}{1+\text{NTU}}$.
  • Parallel Flow Limit: Max $\varepsilon_{\text{parallel}} = 50\%$ when $C=1$.
