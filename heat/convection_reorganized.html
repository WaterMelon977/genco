2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Core Dimensionless Parameters in Convection

  • Nusselt Number $Nu$:
    $${\boxed{Nu = \frac{h \cdot L}{k} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$
  • $h$: convective heat transfer coefficient $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$
  • $L$: characteristic length $\text{m}$
  • $k$: thermal conductivity of the FLUID $\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$

  • Physical meaning: Ratio of convective heat transfer to pure conductive heat transfer across fluid layer $L$. Minimum possible $Nu = 1.0$ (pure conduction).

  • Prandtl Number $Pr$:

    $${\boxed{Pr = \frac{\nu}{\alpha} = \frac{\mu \cdot C_p}{k} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • $\nu$: kinematic viscosity $\text{m}^2\text{/s}$
  • $\alpha$: thermal diffusivity of fluid $\text{m}^2\text{/s}$
  • $\mu$: dynamic viscosity $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$
  • $C_p$: specific heat at constant pressure $\text{J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$

  • Physical meaning: Ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity. Links velocity field and temperature field.

  • Reynolds Number $Re$:

    $${\boxed{Re = \frac{\rho \cdot V \cdot L}{\mu} = \frac{V \cdot L}{\nu} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • $\rho$: fluid density $\text{kg/m}^3$
  • $V$: free-stream or mean fluid velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • Physical meaning: Ratio of inertial force to viscous force in forced convection.

  • Grashof Number $Gr$:

    $${\boxed{Gr = \frac{g \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta T \cdot L^3}{\nu^2} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • $g$: acceleration due to gravity $9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$
  • $\beta$: volumetric thermal expansion coefficient ($\text{K}^{-1}$; for ideal gas $\beta = 1/T_{\text{abs}}$)
  • $\Delta T$: surface-to-fluid temperature difference ($T_s - T_\infty$, $\text{K}$)

  • Physical meaning: Ratio of buoyancy force to viscous force in free convection.

  • Rayleigh Number $Ra$:

    $${\boxed{Ra = Gr \cdot Pr = \frac{g \cdot \beta \cdot \Delta T \cdot L^3}{\nu \cdot \alpha} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • Physical meaning: Governs laminar-to-turbulent flow transition in free convection (Critical $Ra \approx 10^9$ for vertical plates).

  • Stanton Number $St$:

    $${\boxed{St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{h}{\rho \cdot V \cdot C_p} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • Reynolds Analogy (Flat Plate, $Pr = 1.0$, skin friction $C_f$): $\boxed{St = \frac{C_f}{2}}$

  • Colburn Analogy $0.6 < Pr < 60$: $\boxed{St \cdot Pr^{2/3} = \frac{C_f}{2} \iff Nu = 0.5 \cdot C_f \cdot Re \cdot Pr^{1/3}}$

  • Biot Number $Bi$:

    $${\boxed{Bi = \frac{h \cdot L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • $L_c$: characteristic length of solid body $= \frac{V}{A_s}$ $\text{m}$
  • $k_{\text{solid}}$: thermal conductivity of the SOLID BODY $\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$

  • Physical meaning: Ratio of internal conductive resistance to surface convective resistance. Valid for lumped analysis if $Bi < 0.1$.

  • Fourier Number $Fo$:

    $${\boxed{Fo = \frac{\alpha \cdot t}{L_c^2} \quad (\text{dimensionless})}}$$

  • $t$: time $\text{s}$
  • Physical meaning: Ratio of heat conduction rate to heat storage rate in transient conduction.

2. Boundary Layer Thickness Relations

  • Thermal to Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer Thickness Ratio:
    $${\boxed{\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} \approx Pr^{-1/3} \iff \frac{\delta}{\delta_t} \approx Pr^{1/3}}}$$
  • $\delta$: hydrodynamic / velocity boundary layer thickness $\text{m}$
  • $\delta_t$: thermal boundary layer thickness $\text{m}$
  • If $Pr = 1.0 \implies \delta = \delta_t$
  • If $Pr > 1.0 \implies \delta_t < \delta$ (thermal layer thinner than velocity layer)

  • If $Pr < 1.0 \implies \delta_t > \delta$ (thermal layer thicker than velocity layer)

  • Laminar Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer Growth (Flat Plate):

    $${\boxed{\delta(x) \propto x^{1/2} \iff \delta(x) = \frac{5.0 \cdot x}{\sqrt{Re_x}}}}$$

3. Forced Convection Flat Plate & Pipe Correlations

  • Laminar Flat Plate Forced Convection $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$:
  • Local Nusselt Number: $\boxed{Nu_x = 0.332 \cdot Re_x^{1/2} \cdot Pr^{1/3} \implies h_x \propto x^{-0.5}}$

  • Average Nusselt Number over Length $L$: $\boxed{Nu_L = 0.664 \cdot Re_L^{1/2} \cdot Pr^{1/3} \implies \bar{h}L = 2 \cdot h{x=L}}$

  • Turbulent Flat Plate Forced Convection $Re_x > 5 \times 10^5$:

  • Local Nusselt Number: $\boxed{Nu_x = 0.0296 \cdot Re_x^{0.8} \cdot Pr^{1/3} \implies h_x \propto x^{-0.2}}$

  • Turbulent Flow Inside Smooth Tubes (Dittus–Boelter Equation):

    $${\boxed{Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^n}}$$

  • Fluid Heating $T_w > T_b$: $n = 0.4 \implies Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^{0.4}$
  • Fluid Cooling $T_w < T_b$: $n = 0.3 \implies Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^{0.3}$
  • Validity: $Re_D > 10^4$, $0.7 \le Pr \le 160$, fully developed turbulent flow.

4. Free Convection Relations & Mixed Flow Criterion

  • Mixed / Combined Free & Forced Convection Regime Criterion:
    $${\boxed{0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10.0}}$$
  • $\frac{Gr}{Re^2} \ll 0.1 \implies$ Pure Forced Convection (buoyancy negligible)
  • $\frac{Gr}{Re^2} \gg 10.0 \implies$ Pure Free / Natural Convection (forced velocity negligible)

  • $0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10.0 \implies$ Combined / Mixed Convection

  • Free Convection Nusselt Correlation:

    $${\boxed{Nu = C \cdot (Gr \cdot Pr)^n = C \cdot Ra^n}}$$

  • Laminar Regime $10^4 < Ra < 10^9$: $n = 1/4 = 0.25 \implies Nu \propto Ra^{1/4}$
  • Turbulent Regime $Ra > 10^9$: $n = 1/3 \approx 0.33 \implies Nu \propto Ra^{1/3} \implies q'' \propto (\Delta T)^{1.33}$ (independent of length $L$)

5. Fins & Lumped Transient Conduction

  • Fin Parameter $m$ & Effectiveness $\varepsilon_f$:

    $${\boxed{m = \sqrt{\frac{h \cdot P}{k \cdot A_c}}, \quad \varepsilon_f = \sqrt{\frac{k \cdot P}{h \cdot A_c}} \cdot \tanh(m \cdot L)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\text{Infinite Fin Temp Profile: } \frac{T(x) - T_\infty}{T_b - T_\infty} = \exp(-m \cdot x)}}$$

  • Lumped System Transient Response $Bi < 0.1$:

    $${\boxed{\frac{T(t) - T_\infty}{T_i - T_\infty} = \exp\left(-\frac{h \cdot A_s \cdot t}{\rho \cdot V \cdot C_p}\right) = \exp(-Bi \cdot Fo)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\text{Thermal Time Constant: } \tau = \frac{\rho \cdot V \cdot C_p}{h \cdot A_s}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Convective Heat Transfer: Heat transfer between a solid surface and an adjacent moving fluid.
  • Nusselt Number $Nu$: Ratio of convective heat transfer rate to pure conductive heat transfer rate across fluid layer $L$. Fluid conductivity $k$ is used.
  • Prandtl Number $Pr$: Ratio of momentum diffusivity $\nu$ to thermal diffusivity $\alpha$. Determines relative growth of velocity vs thermal boundary layers $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3}$.
  • Grashof Number $Gr$: Ratio of buoyancy forces to viscous forces. The free-convection analogue of Reynolds number.
  • Rayleigh Number $Ra$: $Gr \cdot Pr$, governs natural convection flow regimes and transition to turbulence $Ra_{\text{crit}} \approx 10^9$.
  • Stanton Number $St$: Dimensionless heat transfer coefficient used exclusively in forced convection $St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{h}{\rho V C_p}$.
  • Biot Number $Bi$: Ratio of internal conduction resistance of a solid body to surface convection resistance $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}}$. Solid conductivity $k_{\text{solid}}$ is used. Lumped analysis requires $Bi < 0.1$.
  • Free / Natural Convection: Convective flow driven solely by density variations resulting from temperature gradients in a gravitational field. Fluid velocity is ZERO at the wall surface AND at the outer edge of the boundary layer.
  • Dittus–Boelter Equation: Standard empirical correlation for fully developed turbulent flow in smooth pipes ($Nu_D = 0.023 Re_D^{0.8} Pr^n$, with $n=0.4$ for fluid heating, $n=0.3$ for fluid cooling).
  • Lumped System Analysis: Transient heat conduction analysis valid when internal conduction resistance is negligible $Bi < 0.1$. Assumes spatial temperature uniformity inside the solid; temperature varies only with time.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Prandtl Number Ranges:
  • Gases (Air): $Pr \approx 0.7 - 1.0$
  • Water: $Pr \approx 5 - 10$
  • Liquid Metals (Mercury, Sodium): $Pr \ll 1$ ($0.004 - 0.03$; thermal layer much thicker than velocity layer)
  • Heavy Oils: $Pr \gg 1$ ($100 - 10000$; thermal layer much thinner than velocity layer)
  • Transition Criteria:
  • Flat Plate Forced Convection: Critical $Re_x \approx 5 \times 10^5$
  • Internal Pipe Forced Convection: Critical $Re_D \approx 2300$
  • Vertical Plate Free Convection: Critical $Ra_L = Gr_L \cdot Pr \approx 10^9$
  • Lumped System Limit: $Bi < 0.1$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Dimensionless Parameters in Convection

1. Concept Pointers
  • Nusselt Number $Nu$:
    $${Nu = \frac{h L}{k_{\text{fluid}}}}$$
  • Ratio of convective heat transfer to pure conductive heat transfer across a fluid layer.
  • Minimum possible value is $Nu = 1.0$ (pure conduction).
  • Uses FLUID thermal conductivity $k_{\text{fluid}}$.
  • Prandtl Number $Pr$:
    $${Pr = \frac{\nu}{\alpha} = \frac{\mu C_p}{k_{\text{fluid}}}}$$
  • Governs the relative thickness of hydrodynamic and thermal boundary layers: $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} \approx Pr^{-1/3}$.
  • When $Pr = 1.0 \implies \delta = \delta_t$ (velocity and temperature profiles coincide).
  • Reynolds Number $Re$:
    $${Re = \frac{\rho V L}{\mu} = \frac{V L}{\nu}}$$
  • Ratio of inertia forces to viscous forces in forced convection.
  • Grashof Number $Gr$:
    $${Gr = \frac{g \beta \Delta T L^3}{\nu^2}}$$
  • Ratio of buoyancy forces to viscous forces in natural convection.
  • Rayleigh Number $Ra$:
    $${Ra = Gr \cdot Pr = \frac{g \beta \Delta T L^3}{\nu \alpha}}$$
  • Governs transition from laminar to turbulent flow in natural convection.
  • Stanton Number $St$:
    $${St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{h}{\rho V C_p}}$$
  • Exclusively used in forced convection. Reynolds analogy links skin friction and heat transfer: $St = \frac{C_f}{2}$.
  • Biot Number $Bi$:
    $${Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}}, \quad L_c = \frac{V}{A_s}}$$
  • Ratio of internal conduction resistance to external convection resistance. Uses SOLID thermal conductivity $k_{\text{solid}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Fluid vs Solid Thermal Conductivity: - Nusselt Number $Nu = \frac{hL}{k_{\text{fluid}}}$ uses FLUID thermal conductivity $k_{\text{fluid}}$! - Biot Number $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}}$ uses SOLID thermal conductivity $k_{\text{solid}}$!

[!TRAP] Stanton Number Forced Convection Trap: Stanton number contains Reynolds number $St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr}$. It applies ONLY to forced convection! It cannot be used for pure natural convection.

2. Decision Tree
Dimensionless Parameter Identification Check?
├─ Is it Ratio of Convection to Pure Conduction? → NUSSELT NUMBER Nu = h * L / k_fluid
├─ Is it Ratio of Momentum Diffusivity to Thermal Diffusivity? → PRANDTL NUMBER Pr = nu / alpha
├─ Is it Ratio of Inertia to Viscous Forces (Forced Flow)? → REYNOLDS NUMBER Re = rho * V * L / mu
├─ Is it Ratio of Buoyancy to Viscous Forces (Free Flow)? → GRASHOF NUMBER Gr = g * beta * Delta_T * L^3 / nu^2
├─ Is it Product of Grashof and Prandtl (Free Flow Transition)? → RAYLEIGH NUMBER Ra = Gr * Pr
├─ Is it Ratio of Heat Transfer to Momentum Capacity? → STANTON NUMBER St = Nu / (Re * Pr)
└─ Is it Ratio of Internal Conduction to External Convection Resistance? → BIOT NUMBER Bi = h * Lc / k_solid
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $Nu = hL / k_{\text{fluid}}$.
  • [ ] $Pr = \nu / \alpha = \mu C_p / k_{\text{fluid}}$.
  • [ ] $Gr = g \beta \Delta T L^3 / \nu^2$.
  • [ ] $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$.
  • [ ] $St = Nu / (Re \cdot Pr) = C_f / 2$.
  • [ ] $Bi = h L_c / k_{\text{solid}}$ $L_c = V / A_s$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A steam pipe of outer diameter $D = 100 \text{ mm} = 0.1 \text{ m}$ loses heat to ambient air $k_{\text{air}} = 0.03 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$. If the Nusselt number $Nu = 25$, calculate the convective heat transfer coefficient $h$. - Solution: - $Nu = \frac{h \cdot D}{k_{\text{air}}} \implies 25 = \frac{h \times 0.1}{0.03}$. - $h = \frac{25 \times 0.03}{0.1} = \frac{0.75}{0.1} = 7.5 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. - Key Insight: $h = 7.5 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Use pipe diameter $D$ as characteristic length.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: For forced convection flow over a flat plate, given $Nu = 4000$, $Re = 40$, and $Pr = 20$. Calculate the Stanton number $St$. - Solution: - $St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{4000}{40 \times 20} = \frac{4000}{800} = 5.0$. - Key Insight: $St = 5.0$. Divide $Nu$ by $Re \cdot Pr$.

3.2 Free (Natural) Convection Heat Transfer

1. Concept Pointers
  • Flow Mechanism: Fluid motion is driven purely by density differences caused by temperature variations in a gravitational field.
  • Velocity Profile on Vertical Hot Plate:
  • Velocity is ZERO at the plate surface $y = 0$ due to no-slip condition.
  • Velocity increases to a MAXIMUM within the boundary layer due to buoyancy acceleration.
  • Velocity decays back to ZERO at the outer edge of the boundary layer $y \to \infty$ because ambient fluid is stationary.
  • Transition Criterion:
  • Controlled by the Rayleigh number $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$.
  • For vertical plates: $Ra_L < 10^9 \implies$ Laminar; $Ra_L > 10^9 \implies$ Turbulent.
  • Mixed / Combined Convection Criterion:
  • Governed by $\frac{Gr}{Re^2}$.
  • $\frac{Gr}{Re^2} \ll 0.1 \implies$ Pure Forced Convection.
  • $\frac{Gr}{Re^2} \gg 10.0 \implies$ Pure Natural Convection.
  • $0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10.0 \implies$ Mixed / Combined Convection.
  • Turbulent Heat Flux Scaling:
  • In turbulent natural convection $Ra > 10^9$, $Nu \propto Ra^{1/3} \propto L^1 \implies h$ is INDEPENDENT of height $L$, and heat flux $q'' \propto (\Delta T)^{4/3} = (\Delta T)^{1.33}$.
💡 Key Concept: Free Convection Velocity Profile: Unlike forced convection, free convection fluid velocity is ZERO AT THE WALL $y=0$ AND ZERO AT THE BOUNDARY LAYER OUTER EDGE $y \to \infty$! It reaches a maximum inside the boundary layer.

[!TRAP] Free Convection Transition Rule: Transition in natural convection is NEVER governed by Reynolds number alone! It is governed by Rayleigh number $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$.

2. Decision Tree
Free Convection Query Check?
├─ Velocity Profile at Wall & Outer Edge? → ZERO at wall AND ZERO at outer edge
├─ Transition Parameter? → Rayleigh Number Ra = Gr * Pr (Critical Ra ~ 10^9)
├─ Mixed Flow Regime Check? → Check ratio Gr / Re^2 (Mixed if 0.1 <= Gr/Re^2 <= 10)
└─ Heat Flux Dependence in Turbulent Regime? → q'' proportional to (Delta_T)^1.33
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Free convection driven by buoyancy $Gr$.
  • [ ] Velocity is zero at wall and outer edge.
  • [ ] Transition governed by $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr \approx 10^9$.
  • [ ] Mixed convection: $0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10$.
  • [ ] Turbulent $q'' \propto (\Delta T)^{1.33}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In natural convection heat transfer from a vertical heated plate, the transition from laminar to turbulent boundary layer flow is governed by the critical value of: (a) Reynolds number, (b) Grashof number and Prandtl number product, (c) Weber number, (d) Mach number. - Solution: - Transition in natural convection depends on Rayleigh number $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$. - Correct answer: (b) Grashof number and Prandtl number product. - Key Insight: Transition governed by $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$.

3.3 Forced Convection & Thermal / Hydrodynamic Boundary Layers

1. Concept Pointers
  • Hydrodynamic Boundary Layer $\delta$: Fluid layer where velocity rises from $0$ at wall to $0.99 V_\infty$. Laminar growth $\delta(x) \propto x^{1/2}$.
  • Thermal Boundary Layer $\delta_t$: Fluid layer where temperature rises from $T_w$ to $0.99 T_\infty$.
  • Boundary Layer Thickness Ratio:
    $${\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} \approx Pr^{-1/3} \iff \frac{\delta}{\delta_t} \approx Pr^{1/3}}$$
  • $Pr = 1.0 \implies \delta = \delta_t$ (hydrodynamic and thermal boundary layers grow at identical rates; velocity and temperature profiles coincide).
  • $Pr > 1.0$ (e.g. Water, Oil) $\implies \delta_t < \delta$ (thermal layer thinner).
  • $Pr < 1.0$ (e.g. Liquid Metals) $\implies \delta_t > \delta$ (thermal layer thicker).
  • Variation of Local Heat Transfer Coefficient $h_x$:
  • As fluid moves downstream over an isothermal flat plate, the boundary layer thickness $\delta_t(x)$ GROWS $\implies$ thermal resistance increases $\implies$ LOCAL HEAT TRANSFER COEFFICIENT $h_x$ DECREASES ALONG THE FLOW DIRECTION $x$ for both laminar and turbulent flows!
💡 Key Concept: Boundary Layer Thickness vs Prandtl Number: - $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3}$. - Liquid Metals $Pr \ll 1$ $\implies \delta_t \gg \delta$ (thermal layer much thicker). - Heavy Oils $Pr \gg 1$ $\implies \delta_t \ll \delta$ (thermal layer much thinner).

[!TRAP] Local Heat Transfer Coefficient Decay: Local heat transfer coefficient $h_x$ DECREASES downstream along a flat plate because boundary layer thickness grows! Do not think $h_x$ increases downstream.

2. Decision Tree
Boundary Layer & Prandtl Relation Check?
├─ Is Pr = 1.0? → delta_t = delta (Hydrodynamic & thermal boundary layers equal)
├─ Is Pr > 1.0 (Water/Oil)? → delta_t < delta (Thermal layer thinner)
├─ Is Pr < 1.0 (Liquid Metal)? → delta_t > delta (Thermal layer thicker)
└─ How does local h_x vary along a flat plate? → DECREASES downstream as x increases
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3}$.
  • [ ] $Pr = 1.0 \implies \delta = \delta_t$.
  • [ ] $h_x$ DECREASES downstream along flat plate.
  • [ ] Transition on flat plate at $Re_x \approx 5 \times 10^5$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air $Pr = 0.71$ flows over a flat plate. If the hydrodynamic boundary layer thickness at a location $x$ is $\delta = 10 \text{ mm}$, estimate the thermal boundary layer thickness $\delta_t$. - Solution: - $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3} = (0.71)^{-1/3} = (0.71)^{-0.3333} \approx 1.12$. - $\delta_t = 1.12 \times \delta = 1.12 \times 10 \text{ mm} = 11.2 \text{ mm}$. - Key Insight: $\delta_t = 11.2 \text{ mm}$. For $Pr < 1$, thermal boundary layer is thicker than velocity layer.

3.4 Flat Plate & Internal Pipe Flow Correlations

1. Concept Pointers
  • Flat Plate Laminar Forced Convection $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$:
  • Local Nusselt Number: $Nu_x = 0.332 Re_x^{1/2} Pr^{1/3} \implies h_x \propto x^{-0.5}$.
  • Average Heat Transfer Coefficient over plate length $L$:
    $${\bar{h}L = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L h_x dx = 2 \cdot h{x=L}}$$
  • The average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}_L$ is EXACTLY DOUBLE the local heat transfer coefficient at the trailing edge $x = L$!
  • Internal Pipe Flow — Dittus–Boelter Equation:
    $${Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^n}$$
  • Fluid HEATING by pipe wall $T_w > T_b$: $n = 0.4 \implies Nu_D = 0.023 Re_D^{0.8} Pr^{0.4}$.
  • Fluid COOLING by pipe wall $T_w < T_b$: $n = 0.3 \implies Nu_D = 0.023 Re_D^{0.8} Pr^{0.3}$.
  • Valid for fully developed turbulent flow $Re_D > 10^4$ in smooth pipes.
  • External Flow Over Sphere:
  • Characteristic length $L = D$ (sphere diameter). Heat loss $Q = h \cdot (\pi D^2) \cdot \Delta T$.
  • Turbulence Effect:
  • Turbulence promotes intense eddy mixing, drastically INCREASING convective heat transfer coefficient $h$ and heat transfer rate compared to laminar flow.
💡 Key Concept: Average vs Local $h$ for Flat Plate: For laminar flow over a flat plate, Average Heat Transfer Coefficient $\bar{h}L = 2 \cdot h{x=L}$ (Exactly twice the local value at trailing edge $x=L$).

[!TRAP] Dittus-Boelter Exponent Trap: - Heating Fluid $\implies n = 0.4$ $Pr^{0.4}$. - Cooling Fluid $\implies n = 0.3$ $Pr^{0.3}$. Do not swap these exponents in numerical problems!

2. Decision Tree
Forced Convection Correlation Check?
├─ Is it Flat Plate Laminar Flow? → Nu_x = 0.332 * Re_x^0.5 * Pr^(1/3) ; h_bar_L = 2 * h_x=L
├─ Is it Pipe Turbulent Flow (Dittus-Boelter)? → Nu_D = 0.023 * Re_D^0.8 * Pr^n
│    ├─ Fluid HEATING -> n = 0.4
│    └─ Fluid COOLING -> n = 0.3
└─ What is the effect of turbulence? → Drastically INCREASES heat transfer rate via eddy mixing
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Flat plate laminar $\bar{h}L = 2 h{x=L}$.
  • [ ] Dittus-Boelter heating $n=0.4$; cooling $n=0.3$.
  • [ ] Pipe transition $Re_D \approx 2300$; turbulent correlation $Re_D > 10^4$.
  • [ ] Turbulence increases heat transfer.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air at $27^\circ\text{C}$ flows over a sphere of diameter $D = 12 \text{ mm} = 0.012 \text{ m}$ maintained at $77^\circ\text{C}$. Given air thermal conductivity $k = 0.026 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ and Nusselt number $Nu = 31.4$. Calculate the heat loss rate from the sphere. - Solution: - Calculate $h$: $Nu = \frac{h \cdot D}{k} \implies h = \frac{31.4 \times 0.026}{0.012} = 68.033 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. - Surface area of sphere: $A_s = \pi \cdot D^2 = \pi \times (0.012)^2 = 0.0004524 \text{ m}^2$. - Temperature difference: $\Delta T = 77 - 27 = 50^\circ\text{C}$. - Heat loss rate: $Q = h \cdot A_s \cdot \Delta T = 68.033 \times 0.0004524 \times 50 = 1.539 \text{ W} \approx 1.54 \text{ W}$. - Key Insight: $Q = 1.54 \text{ W}$. Use $A_s = \pi D^2$ for sphere area.

3.5 Extended Surfaces (Fins) in Convective Environments

1. Concept Pointers
  • Fin Parameter: $m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A_c}}$ $\text{m}^{-1}$.
  • Fin Effectiveness: $\varepsilon_f = \frac{Q_{\text{fin}}}{Q_{\text{without fin}}} = \sqrt{\frac{k P}{h A_c}} \cdot \tanh(mL)$.
  • Fin Efficiency: $\eta_f = \frac{\tanh(mL)}{mL}$.
  • Infinite Fin Temperature Profile:
    $${\frac{T(x) - T_\infty}{T_b - T_\infty} = \exp(-m \cdot x)}$$
  • Performance Guidelines:
  • Thin, closely spaced fins with high thermal conductivity $k$ yield maximum effectiveness.
  • Fins are most effective when convection coefficient $h$ is LOW (e.g. gas/air side).
💡 Key Concept: Infinite Fin Temperature Decay: Temperature along an infinitely long fin decays EXPONENTIALLY: $\frac{T(x)-T_\infty}{T_b-T_\infty} = e^{-mx}$.
2. Decision Tree
Fin Parameter & Profile Check?
├─ Fin Parameter → m = sqrt(h * P / (k * A_c))
├─ Fin Effectiveness → epsilon_f = sqrt(k * P / (h * A_c)) * tanh(mL)
└─ Infinite Fin Temp Distribution → (T(x) - T_inf) / (T_b - T_inf) = exp(-m * x)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $m = \sqrt{hP / (kA_c)}$.
  • [ ] Infinite fin temp profile $\propto e^{-mx}$.
  • [ ] High $k$, low $h$, thin cross-section $\implies$ High effectiveness.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An infinitely long fin of diameter $D = 10 \text{ mm}$ $P = \pi D, A_c = \frac{\pi}{4}D^2 \implies \frac{P}{A_c} = \frac{4}{D} = 400 \text{ m}^{-1}$ and $k = 100 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$ is exposed to environment with $h = 25 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Base temperature $T_b = 120^\circ\text{C}$, ambient $T_\infty = 20^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate temperature at distance $x = 0.1 \text{ m}$ from base. - Solution: - $m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A_c}} = \sqrt{\frac{25 \times 400}{100}} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \text{ m}^{-1}$. - $m \cdot x = 10 \times 0.1 = 1.0$. - $\frac{T(x) - 20}{120 - 20} = e^{-1.0} = 0.3679$. - $T(x) - 20 = 100 \times 0.3679 = 36.79^\circ\text{C} \implies T(x) = 56.79^\circ\text{C}$. - Key Insight: $T(x) = 56.79^\circ\text{C}$. Use exponential decay $e^{-mx}$.

3.6 Lumped System Analysis (Transient Conduction)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Validity Criterion: Biot Number $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}} < 0.1$, where $L_c = \frac{V}{A_s}$.
  • Physical Meaning: Internal conduction resistance inside solid is negligible compared to external surface convection resistance.
  • Temperature Response: Spatial temperature gradients are negligible (uniform body temperature at any instant $t$). Temperature decays EXPONENTIALLY with time:
    $${\frac{T(t) - T_\infty}{T_i - T_\infty} = \exp\left(-\frac{h A_s t}{\rho V C_p}\right) = \exp(-Bi \cdot Fo)}$$
  • Thermal Time Constant $\tau$:
    $${\tau = \frac{\rho \cdot V \cdot C_p}{h \cdot A_s} \quad (\text{seconds})}$$
  • After $t = \tau$, temperature difference drops to $e^{-1} \approx 36.8\%$ of initial difference (body achieves $63.2\%$ of total temperature change).
💡 Key Concept: Lumped Analysis Assumptions: Lumped capacitance analysis assumes SPATIAL TEMPERATURE UNIFORMITY inside the body $Bi < 0.1$. Temperature varies ONLY WITH TIME exponentially!

[!TRAP] Biot Number Thermal Conductivity Trap: Biot number uses SOLID thermal conductivity $k_{\text{solid}}$ $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}}$. Never use fluid thermal conductivity in Biot number!

2. Decision Tree
Lumped System Analysis Check?
├─ Is Bi < 0.1? → Lumped System Analysis is VALID
├─ What is Characteristic Length L_c? → L_c = Volume / Surface Area = V / A_s
├─ Is temperature spatially uniform inside solid? → YES (No internal temp gradients)
└─ Temperature History Equation → (T(t) - T_inf) / (T_i - T_inf) = exp(-t / tau) = exp(-Bi * Fo)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}} < 0.1$.
  • [ ] $L_c = V / A_s$.
  • [ ] Exponential response $\frac{T(t)-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty} = e^{-Bi \cdot Fo}$.
  • [ ] Time constant $\tau = \frac{\rho V C_p}{h A_s}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A small copper sphere of diameter $D = 12 \text{ mm}$ $L_c = \frac{D}{6} = 0.002 \text{ m}$, $k_{\text{solid}} = 380 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, $\rho = 8900 \text{ kg/m}^3$, $C_p = 400 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ is quenched in an oil bath with $h = 190 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Verify if lumped system analysis is valid and calculate the thermal time constant $\tau$. - Solution: - Check Biot number: $Bi = \frac{h \cdot L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}} = \frac{190 \times 0.002}{380} = \frac{0.38}{380} = 0.001$. - Since $Bi = 0.001 < 0.1$, lumped analysis is valid. - Calculate time constant $\tau$:

$${\tau = \frac{\rho \cdot L_c \cdot C_p}{h} = \frac{8900 \times 0.002 \times 400}{190} = \frac{7120}{190} = 37.47 \text{ seconds}}$$
- Key Insight: $Bi = 0.001 < 0.1$ (valid); $\tau = 37.47 \text{ s}$. For sphere $L_c = D/6$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Define Nusselt number $Nu$ and state its physical significance.
A: $Nu = \frac{hL}{k_{\text{fluid}}}$. It represents the ratio of convective heat transfer rate to pure conductive heat transfer rate across a fluid layer.

Q2: What is the physical significance of Prandtl number $Pr$?
A: $Pr = \frac{\nu}{\alpha} = \frac{\mu C_p}{k_{\text{fluid}}}$. It represents the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity, governing the relative thickness of hydrodynamic and thermal boundary layers $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3}$.

Q3: How are hydrodynamic $\delta$ and thermal $\delta_t$ boundary layer thicknesses related when $Pr = 1.0$?
A: When $Pr = 1.0$, $\delta_t = \delta$ (both boundary layers grow at identical rates, and velocity and temperature profiles coincide).

Q4: Define Grashof number $Gr$ and state its role in natural convection.
A: $Gr = \frac{g \beta \Delta T L^3}{\nu^2}$. It represents the ratio of buoyancy forces to viscous forces and acts as the primary parameter governing natural convection flow.

Q5: What parameter governs the transition from laminar to turbulent flow in natural convection?
A: Rayleigh number $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$ (critical $Ra \approx 10^9$ for vertical plates).

Q6: What is the criterion for mixed (combined free and forced) convection?
A: Mixed convection occurs when $0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10.0$.

Q7: Define Stanton number $St$ and write its relation to skin friction coefficient under Reynolds analogy.
A: $St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{h}{\rho V C_p}$. Under Reynolds analogy $Pr=1.0$, $St = \frac{C_f}{2}$.

Q8: Define Biot number $Bi$ and state the criterion for lumped system analysis.
A: $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}}$, where $L_c = \frac{V}{A_s}$. Lumped system analysis is valid when $Bi < 0.1$.

Q9: Describe the velocity profile in natural convection over a vertical heated plate.
A: Velocity is zero at the plate wall $y=0$, increases to a maximum inside the boundary layer, and decays back to zero at the outer edge of the boundary layer $y \to \infty$.

Q10: Why does the local convective heat transfer coefficient $h_x$ decrease along the length of an isothermal flat plate?
A: Because the thermal boundary layer thickness $\delta_t(x)$ grows downstream, increasing thermal resistance to heat transfer.

Q11: What is the relationship between average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}L$ and local heat transfer coefficient $h{x=L}$ for laminar flow over a flat plate?
A: $\bar{h}L = 2 \cdot h{x=L}$ (average is twice the trailing edge local value).

Q12: State the Dittus–Boelter equation for turbulent flow in smooth tubes during fluid heating.
A: $Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^{0.4}$.

Q13: State the Dittus–Boelter equation for turbulent flow in smooth tubes during fluid cooling.
A: $Nu_D = 0.023 \cdot Re_D^{0.8} \cdot Pr^{0.3}$.

Q14: Write the temperature distribution equation along an infinitely long fin.
A: $\frac{T(x) - T_\infty}{T_b - T_\infty} = \exp(-m \cdot x)$, where $m = \sqrt{\frac{hP}{kA_c}}$.

Q15: What is the temperature-time response equation for a lumped thermal system?
A: $\frac{T(t) - T_\infty}{T_i - T_\infty} = \exp\left(-\frac{h A_s t}{\rho V C_p}\right) = \exp(-Bi \cdot Fo)$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Nusselt Number: $Nu = \frac{hL}{k_{\text{fluid}}}$. Minimum $Nu = 1.0$ (pure conduction).
  • Prandtl Number: $Pr = \frac{\nu}{\alpha} = \frac{\mu C_p}{k_{\text{fluid}}}$. $\frac{\delta_t}{\delta} = Pr^{-1/3}$.
  • $Pr = 1.0 \implies \delta = \delta_t$.
  • Liquid metals $Pr \ll 1 \implies \delta_t \gg \delta$.
  • Oils $Pr \gg 1 \implies \delta_t \ll \delta$.
  • Grashof & Rayleigh: $Gr = \frac{g \beta \Delta T L^3}{\nu^2}$, $Ra = Gr \cdot Pr$. Free flow transition at $Ra \approx 10^9$.
  • Mixed Flow Criterion: $0.1 \le \frac{Gr}{Re^2} \le 10.0$.
  • Stanton Number: $St = \frac{Nu}{Re \cdot Pr} = \frac{C_f}{2}$ (forced flow only!).
  • Biot Number: $Bi = \frac{h L_c}{k_{\text{solid}}} < 0.1$ for lumped systems $L_c = V / A_s$.
  • Free Convection Velocity Profile: Zero at wall AND zero at outer boundary edge.
  • Flat Plate Laminar: $Nu_x = 0.332 Re_x^{1/2} Pr^{1/3}$, $\bar{h}L = 2 h{x=L}$. Local $h_x$ DECREASES downstream.
  • Dittus-Boelter Pipe: $Nu_D = 0.023 Re_D^{0.8} Pr^n$ ($n=0.4$ heating, $n=0.3$ cooling).
  • Infinite Fin: Temp decay $\frac{T(x)-T_\infty}{T_b-T_\infty} = e^{-mx}$.
  • Lumped System: $Bi < 0.1$, spatial temp uniformity, exponential time response $\frac{T(t)-T_\infty}{T_i-T_\infty} = e^{-Bi \cdot Fo}$.
