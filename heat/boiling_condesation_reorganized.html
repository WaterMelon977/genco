2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Pool Boiling Regimes & Excess Temperature

  • Excess Temperature $\Delta T_e$:
    $${\boxed{\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}} \quad (\text{K or }^\circ\text{C})}}$$
  • $T_w$: heated wall / surface temperature $\text{K}$

  • $T_{\text{sat}}$: liquid saturation temperature at system pressure $\text{K}$

  • Pool Boiling Regime Excess Temperature Boundaries (PYQ Exam Values):

  • Natural / Free Convective Boiling: $\Delta T_e < 5^\circ\text{C}$
  • Nucleate Boiling: $\boxed{5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}}$
  • Transition Boiling: $50^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 120^\circ\text{C}$
  • Film Boiling: $\Delta T_e > 120^\circ\text{C}$ (Beyond Leidenfrost Point)

2. Heat Transfer Coefficient & Mode Rankings

  • Heat Transfer Magnitude Ranking (Ascending Order):

    $${\boxed{\text{Conduction in Air} < \text{Free Convection in Air} < \text{Forced Convection in Air} < \text{Boiling / Condensation}}}$$

  • Condensation Mode Heat Transfer Coefficient Comparison:

    $${\boxed{h_{\text{dropwise}} \gg h_{\text{filmwise}} \quad (\sim 5 \text{ to } 10 \times \text{ higher})}}$$

3. Nusselt Theory for Laminar Film Condensation on Vertical Plate

  • Average Heat Transfer Coefficient over Height $L$ $\bar{h}_L$:
    $${\boxed{\bar{h}L = 0.943 \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho_l \cdot (\rho_l - \rho_v) \cdot g \cdot h{fg} \cdot k_l^3}{\mu_l \cdot L \cdot (T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)} \right]^{1/4} \quad (\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K})}}$$
  • $\rho_l, \rho_v$: liquid and vapour densities $\text{kg/m}^3$
  • $g$: gravitational acceleration $9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$
  • $h_{fg}$: latent heat of vaporisation $\text{J/kg}$
  • $k_l$: liquid thermal conductivity $\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$
  • $\mu_l$: liquid dynamic viscosity $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$

  • $L$: vertical plate height $\text{m}$

  • Local Heat Transfer Coefficient at Distance $x$ from Top $h_x$:

    $${\boxed{h_x = 0.707 \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho_l \cdot (\rho_l - \rho_v) \cdot g \cdot h_{fg} \cdot k_l^3}{\mu_l \cdot x \cdot (T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)} \right]^{1/4} \quad (\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{K})}}$$

  • Relation between Average and Local Coefficient at $x = L$:

    $${\boxed{\bar{h}L = \frac{4}{3} \cdot h{x=L}}}$$

  • Condensate Film Thickness $\delta(x)$:

    $${\boxed{\delta(x) \propto x^{1/4}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Pool Boiling: Boiling that occurs on a heating surface submerged in a quiescent (still) bulk body of liquid without external forced flow.
  • Nukiyama Boiling Curve: A plot of boiling heat flux $q''$ $\text{W/m}^2$ versus excess temperature $\Delta T_e$ $T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$, showing the transition across natural convection, nucleate, transition, and film boiling regimes.
  • Nucleate Boiling: The boiling regime $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$ where discrete bubbles form at active surface nucleation cavities, grow, detach, and create intense fluid mixing, yielding extremely high heat transfer coefficients.
  • Leidenfrost Point: The point of minimum heat flux on the boiling curve marking the onset of stable film boiling.
  • Film Boiling: The regime $\Delta T_e > 120^\circ\text{C}$ where a continuous stable vapour film blankets the surface. Because vapour has very low thermal conductivity, heat flux drops drastically. Thermally regarded as the REVERSE OF CONDENSATION.
  • Filmwise Condensation: Condensation mode where liquid condensate forms a continuous wetting film over the surface, creating thermal resistance that limits heat transfer.
  • Dropwise Condensation: Condensation mode where discrete droplets form, grow, coalesce, and roll down the surface, repeatedly exposing bare surface area. Yields 5–10$\times$ higher heat transfer coefficients than filmwise, promoted on oily or hydrophobic non-wetting surfaces.
  • Nusselt Theory Assumptions (1916):
  • Condensate film flow is strictly laminar.
  • Inertia forces in the film are NEGLIGIBLE compared to viscous and gravity forces.
  • Heat transfer across the film is by PURE CONDUCTION (convection in liquid film & vapour is neglected).
  • Vapour velocity is LOW (zero interfacial shear drag on condensate film).

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Nucleate Boiling Excess Temp Window: $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$.
  • Nusselt Vertical Plate Constant: $0.943$ (for average coefficient $\bar{h}_L$).
  • Nusselt Local Coefficient Constant: $0.707$ (for local coefficient $h_x$).
  • Average vs Local Ratio: $\bar{h}L = \frac{4}{3} h{x=L} \approx 1.333 \cdot h_{x=L}$.
  • Dropwise vs Filmwise Ratio: $h_{\text{dropwise}} \approx 5 \text{ to } 10 \times h_{\text{filmwise}}$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Pool Boiling Regimes & Nukiyama Boiling Curve

1. Concept Pointers
  • Pool Boiling Definition: Heated surface submerged in a quiescent (non-flowing) bulk liquid.
  • Nukiyama Boiling Curve: Plots heat flux $q''$ $\text{W/m}^2$ vs excess temperature $\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$.
  • Sequence of Regimes:
  • Natural Convection Boiling $\Delta T_e < 5^\circ\text{C}$: Liquid moves by free convection; no bubbles form.
  • Nucleate Boiling $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$: Bubbles form at nucleation cavities, detach, and agitate the fluid. Reaches Critical Heat Flux (CHF, Point C).
  • Transition Boiling $50^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 120^\circ\text{C}$: Unstable partial vapour blanketing; $q''$ decreases as $\Delta T_e$ increases.
  • Film Boiling $\Delta T_e > 120^\circ\text{C}$: Stable continuous vapour film covers the surface from the Leidenfrost point (Point D) onward.
  • Exclusions: Flow boiling and slug flow are NOT pool boiling regimes!
  • Heat Transfer Magnitude Ranking:
    $${\text{Conduction in Air} < \text{Free Convection in Air} < \text{Forced Convection in Air} < \text{Boiling}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Boiling Curve Regimes Order: Natural Convection $\to$ Nucleate Boiling $\to$ Transition Boiling $\to$ Film Boiling. Peak heat flux occurs at Critical Heat Flux (CHF); minimum heat flux occurs at the Leidenfrost Point!

[!TRAP] Pool Boiling Exclusion Trap: Flow boiling, subcooled flow boiling, and slug flow are NOT pool boiling regimes! Pool boiling requires a QUIESCENT liquid body without forced flow.

2. Decision Tree
Boiling Regime Identification Check?
├─ Is Delta_T_e < 5 °C? → Natural Convection Boiling (No bubbles)
├─ Is 5 °C <= Delta_T_e <= 50 °C? → Nucleate Boiling (Bubbles form/detach, max practical h)
├─ Is 50 °C < Delta_T_e < 120 °C? → Transition Boiling (Unstable partial vapour blanket)
├─ Is Delta_T_e > 120 °C (beyond Leidenfrost)? → Film Boiling (Stable vapour film, low h)
└─ Are flow boiling or slug flow listed? → NOT pool boiling regimes!
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$.
  • [ ] Nucleate range: $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$.
  • [ ] CHF = Peak heat flux; Leidenfrost = Minimum heat flux.
  • [ ] Boiling heat transfer rate > Forced convection > Free convection > Conduction in air.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A surface immersed in atmospheric water $T_{\text{sat}} = 100^\circ\text{C}$ is maintained at $T_w = 120^\circ\text{C}$. Identify the active boiling regime. - Solution: - Calculate excess temperature: $\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}} = 120 - 100 = 20^\circ\text{C}$. - Since $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e (20^\circ\text{C}) \le 50^\circ\text{C}$, the active regime is Nucleate Boiling. - Key Insight: Nucleate boiling active range is $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$.

3.2 Nucleate Boiling Promotion & Surface Effects

1. Concept Pointers
  • Mechanism: Vaporisation occurs at active microscopic cavities (nucleation sites) on the heating surface.
  • Surface Roughness Effect:
  • Nucleate boiling is PROMOTED ON ROUGH SURFACES because surface cavities trap microscopic gas/vapour pockets that act as nucleation sites.
  • Smooth or polished surfaces SUPPRESS nucleate boiling (fewer active sites $\implies$ requires higher $\Delta T_e$ to initiate boiling).
  • Agitation Effect: Mechanical agitation or liquid stirring ENHANCES bubble detachment and fluid mixing, promoting nucleate heat transfer.
  • Excess Temperature Window: $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$ (standard PYQ exam standard).
💡 Key Concept: Nucleation Promotion Rule: Nucleate boiling is PROMOTED ON ROUGH SURFACES! Polished surfaces suppress nucleation cavities and decrease boiling heat transfer!

[!TRAP] Polished Surface Fallacy: Examiner trick questions often claim polished surfaces improve boiling. This is FALSE! Rough surfaces provide nucleation cavities and enhance boiling heat transfer.

2. Decision Tree
Nucleate Boiling Surface Check?
├─ Is surface rough? → PROMOTES nucleate boiling (More nucleation cavities)
├─ Is surface smooth / polished? → SUPPRESSES nucleate boiling (Fewer cavities)
├─ Is liquid agitated / stirred? → PROMOTES nucleate heat transfer
└─ What is the exam excess temp window? → 5 °C <= Delta_T_e <= 50 °C
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Rough surfaces promote nucleate boiling.
  • [ ] Polished surfaces suppress nucleate boiling.
  • [ ] Agitation promotes bubble detachment & mixing.
  • [ ] Range: $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Which of the following surface conditions promotes nucleate boiling: (a) Polished glass surface, (b) Rough metallic surface, (c) Mirror-finished chrome, (d) Teflon-coated smooth surface? - Solution: - Rough metallic surface provides microscopic cavities that serve as active nucleation sites for bubble formation. - Correct answer: (b) Rough metallic surface. - Key Insight: Rough surfaces promote nucleate boiling.

3.3 Film Boiling as Reverse of Condensation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Film Boiling Mechanics:
  • Occurs beyond the Leidenfrost point $\Delta T_e > 120^\circ\text{C}$.
  • A continuous, stable blanket of vapour insulates the heating surface from the liquid.
  • Heat transfer across the vapour film occurs primarily by low-conductivity vapour conduction and high-temperature radiation.
  • Analogy to Condensation:
  • Film boiling is thermally regarded as the REVERSE OF CONDENSATION.
  • In film condensation: A liquid film coats a cold surface and acts as thermal resistance.
  • In film boiling: A vapour film coats a hot surface and acts as thermal insulation.
💡 Key Concept: Reverse of Condensation Analogy: Stable FILM BOILING is the thermal inverse of film condensation. Vapour film blankets the hot surface just as liquid film blankets the cold surface in condensation!

[!TRAP] Transition vs Film Boiling Trap: Do not pick transition boiling as the reverse of condensation! Transition boiling is unstable partial blanketing; FILM BOILING is stable continuous film blanketing.

2. Decision Tree
Boiling vs Condensation Analogy Check?
├─ Which regime is reverse of film condensation? → FILM BOILING
├─ Why does film boiling have low heat flux? → Stable vapour film insulates surface
└─ At what point does stable film boiling start? → LEIDENFROST POINT (Minimum Heat Flux)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Film boiling = Reverse of condensation.
  • [ ] Starts at Leidenfrost point.
  • [ ] Vapour film acts as thermal insulation.
  • [ ] Low heat transfer coefficient $h$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Identify the pool boiling regime that is considered the thermal reverse of film condensation. - Solution: - Film Boiling Regime. A continuous vapour film insulates the heating surface, forming the inverse process of liquid film condensation. - Key Insight: Film boiling is the reverse of condensation.

3.4 Dropwise vs Filmwise Condensation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Filmwise Condensation:
  • Condensate wets the cold surface, forming a continuous liquid film flowing downward under gravity.
  • Heat must conduct through the liquid film $\implies$ liquid film acts as thermal resistance $\implies$ LOWER heat transfer coefficient.
  • Occurs on clean, smooth, wettable (hydrophilic) surfaces. Most common industrial condensation mode.
  • Dropwise Condensation:
  • Condensate forms discrete liquid droplets that grow, coalesce, and roll off under gravity, continually exposing fresh bare surface.
  • Bare surface exposure eliminates film resistance $\implies$ 5 to 10 TIMES HIGHER heat transfer coefficient $h_{\text{dropwise}} \gg h_{\text{filmwise}}$.
  • Occurs on OILY, contaminated, or non-wetting (hydrophobic) surfaces.
  • Sustainability Limitation: Extremely difficult to sustain long-term in industrial plants because surfaces eventually become wetted or contaminated.
💡 Key Concept: Dropwise Condensation Dominance: $h_{\text{dropwise}} \approx 5 \text{ to } 10 \times h_{\text{filmwise}}$. Dropwise condensation occurs on OILY / non-wetting surfaces and gives the HIGHEST heat transfer coefficients!

[!TRAP] Surface Preference Trap: - Clean / Smooth / Wettable Surface $\implies$ FILMWISE condensation. - Oily / Contaminated / Non-wetting Surface $\implies$ DROPWISE condensation. Do not select smooth/clean for dropwise condensation!

2. Decision Tree
Condensation Mode Check?
├─ Is condensate forming a continuous liquid layer? → FILMWISE condensation (Lower h)
├─ Are discrete droplets forming on surface? → DROPWISE condensation (5-10x higher h)
├─ What surface promotes dropwise condensation? → OILY / Non-wetting hydrophobic surface
└─ Is dropwise condensation easy to sustain industrially? → NO (Surfaces eventually become wetted)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $h_{\text{dropwise}} \gg h_{\text{filmwise}}$.
  • [ ] Dropwise occurs on oily / non-wetting surfaces.
  • [ ] Filmwise occurs on clean / wettable surfaces.
  • [ ] Dropwise is hard to sustain long-term.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Dropwise condensation is observed to occur on surfaces that are: (a) Polished clean steel, (b) Oily or non-wettable, (c) Glazed ceramic, (d) Pure copper without coating. - Solution: - Dropwise condensation requires non-wetting hydrophobic surface conditions, which are created by oily or contaminated surfaces. - Correct answer: (b) Oily or non-wettable. - Key Insight: Oily surfaces promote dropwise condensation.

3.5 Nusselt Theory for Laminar Film Condensation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Nusselt Analysis (1916):
  • Models pure saturated vapour condensing on an isothermal vertical flat plate of height $L$.
  • Governing Equations:
  • Average Heat Transfer Coefficient $\bar{h}_L$:
    $${\bar{h}L = 0.943 \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho_l (\rho_l - \rho_v) g h{fg} k_l^3}{\mu_l L (T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)} \right]^{1/4}}$$
  • Local Heat Transfer Coefficient $h_x$:
    $${h_x = 0.707 \cdot \left[ \frac{\rho_l (\rho_l - \rho_v) g h_{fg} k_l^3}{\mu_l x (T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)} \right]^{1/4}}$$
  • Ratio: $\bar{h}L = \frac{4}{3} h{x=L} \approx 1.333 \cdot h_{x=L}$.
  • Film Thickness: $\delta(x) \propto x^{1/4} \implies h_x \propto x^{-1/4} \implies \bar{h}_L \propto L^{-1/4}$.
  • Core Nusselt Assumptions (PYQ Tested):
  • Condensate film flow is strictly LAMINAR.
  • Inertia forces in the liquid film are NEGLIGIBLE compared to viscous forces and gravity/weight.
  • Heat flow across the film is by PURE CONDUCTION; convection inside liquid film and vapour is neglected.
  • Vapour velocity is LOW, so that vapour exerts ZERO drag / shear stress on the condensate interface.
💡 Key Concept: Nusselt Theory Key Assumptions: 1. Inertia forces in film $\to$ NEGLIGIBLE! 2. Heat transfer across film $\to$ PURE CONDUCTION! 3. Vapour velocity $\to$ LOW (No interfacial drag)!

[!TRAP] High Vapour Velocity Trap: PYQ options often state "Vapour velocity is very high". This is FALSE! High vapour velocity creates interfacial shear drag and invalidates Nusselt's basic theory!

2. Decision Tree
Nusselt Condensation Theory Check?
├─ Average Coefficient Formula → h_bar_L = 0.943 * [ (rho_l * (rho_l - rho_v) * g * h_fg * k_l^3) / (mu_l * L * Delta_T) ]^(1/4)
├─ Local vs Average Ratio → h_bar_L = (4/3) * h_x=L
├─ How does h_bar_L vary with plate height L? → h_bar_L proportional to L^(-1/4)
└─ Key Assumptions Check → (1) Inertia negligible, (2) Pure conduction across film, (3) LOW vapour velocity
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\bar{h}L = 0.943 \left[ \frac{\rho_l (\rho_l - \rho_v) g h{fg} k_l^3}{\mu_l L \Delta T} \right]^{1/4}$.
  • [ ] $\bar{h}L = \frac{4}{3} h{x=L}$.
  • [ ] $\bar{h}_L \propto L^{-1/4}$.
  • [ ] Inertia negligible; Pure conduction across film; Low vapour velocity.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Saturated steam at $T_{\text{sat}} = 100^\circ\text{C}$ condenses on a vertical plate of height $L = 1.0 \text{ m}$ maintained at $T_w = 80^\circ\text{C}$. Liquid properties: $\rho_l = 960 \text{ kg/m}^3$, $\rho_v \approx 0$, $h_{fg} = 2.257 \times 10^6 \text{ J/kg}$, $k_l = 0.68 \text{ W/m}\cdot\text{K}$, $\mu_l = 2.8 \times 10^{-4} \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$. Calculate the average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}L$. - Solution: - Temperature difference $\Delta T = T{\text{sat}} - T_w = 100 - 80 = 20^\circ\text{C}$. - Substitute into Nusselt formula:

$${\text{Term} = \frac{\rho_l^2 \cdot g \cdot h_{fg} \cdot k_l^3}{\mu_l \cdot L \cdot \Delta T} = \frac{(960)^2 \times 9.81 \times (2.257 \times 10^6) \times (0.68)^3}{(2.8 \times 10^{-4}) \times 1.0 \times 20}}$$
$${\text{Term} = \frac{921600 \times 9.81 \times 2257000 \times 0.314432}{0.0056} = \frac{6417757913}{0.0056} = 1.146 \times 10^{12}}$$
- Take the fourth root:
$${(1.146 \times 10^{12})^{1/4} \approx 32770.8}$$
- Average coefficient:
$${\bar{h}_L = 0.943 \times 32770.8 \approx 30902.8 \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K} \approx 30.9 \text{ kW/m}^2\cdot\text{K}}$$
- Key Insight: $\bar{h}_L \approx 30.9 \text{ kW/m}^2\cdot\text{K}$. Use $0.943$ constant for vertical plate average coefficient.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is pool boiling?
A: Boiling that occurs on a heating surface submerged in a quiescent (non-flowing) bulk body of liquid.

Q2: Define excess temperature $\Delta T_e$ in boiling.
A: $\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$, the difference between wall surface temperature and saturation temperature of the liquid.

Q3: State the excess temperature range for the nucleate boiling regime in PYQs.
A: $5^\circ\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50^\circ\text{C}$.

Q4: Name the four regimes of pool boiling in order of increasing excess temperature.
A: 1. Natural Convection Boiling, 2. Nucleate Boiling, 3. Transition Boiling, 4. Film Boiling.

Q5: What is the Critical Heat Flux (CHF) point on the boiling curve?
A: The peak maximum heat flux on the boiling curve at the end of the nucleate boiling regime.

Q6: What is the Leidenfrost Point?
A: The point of minimum heat flux on the boiling curve, marking the transition into stable film boiling.

Q7: Are flow boiling or slug flow considered pool boiling regimes?
A: No. Flow boiling and slug flow involve forced bulk fluid motion and are not pool boiling regimes.

Q8: Why is nucleate boiling promoted on rough surfaces?
A: Rough surfaces contain microscopic cavities that trap gas/vapour pockets, acting as active nucleation sites for bubble formation.

Q9: Which pool boiling regime is considered the thermal reverse of film condensation?
A: Stable Film Boiling.

Q10: Differentiate between filmwise and dropwise condensation.
A: Filmwise condensation forms a continuous liquid film that acts as thermal resistance ($h$ is lower). Dropwise condensation forms discrete droplets that roll off, exposing bare surface ($h$ is 5–10$\times$ higher).

Q11: On what type of surfaces does dropwise condensation occur?
A: Oily, contaminated, or non-wetting (hydrophobic) surfaces.

Q12: Why is dropwise condensation difficult to maintain in industrial condensers?
A: Because dropwise promoters degrade over time and surfaces eventually become wetted or contaminated.

Q13: State the Nusselt formula for average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}_L$ in laminar film condensation on a vertical plate.
A: $\bar{h}L = 0.943 \left[ \frac{\rho_l (\rho_l - \rho_v) g h{fg} k_l^3}{\mu_l L (T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)} \right]^{1/4}$.

Q14: What is the ratio between average heat transfer coefficient $\bar{h}L$ and local heat transfer coefficient $h{x=L}$ at the bottom of a vertical plate?
A: $\bar{h}L = \frac{4}{3} h{x=L} \approx 1.333 \cdot h_{x=L}$.

Q15: State three key assumptions of Nusselt theory for film condensation.
A: 1. Inertia forces in the liquid film are negligible, 2. Heat flow across the film is by pure conduction, 3. Vapour velocity is low (zero interfacial drag).
