1. Pool Boiling — Basic Concepts

Pool boiling occurs when a heating surface is submerged in a body of quiescent liquid (no bulk flow). Heat transfer depends on the excess temperature $\Delta T_e$, the difference between the wall temperature and the saturation temperature of the liquid:

Excess Temperature
$$\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$$
All boiling regimes are classified by the value of $\Delta T_e$. It is the primary independent variable on the Nukiyama (boiling) curve.

Flow boiling and slug flow involve externally imposed bulk fluid motion and are not pool boiling regimes — they belong to the forced convection boiling category.

2. Pool Boiling Regimes (Nukiyama Curve)

As $\Delta T_e$ increases from zero, the boiling curve passes through four distinct regimes, each with a characteristic mechanism and heat flux trend:

Regime$\Delta T_e$ RangeMechanismHeat flux trend
1. Natural Convection Boiling $< 5°\text{C}$ Liquid slightly superheated; bubbles form at surface but condense in bulk liquid. Heat transfer by single-phase free convection. Rises gently with $\Delta T_e$
2. Nucleate Boiling $5°\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 50°\text{C}$ Bubbles nucleate at active sites, detach, and rise. Vigorous agitation greatly enhances heat transfer. Peak (Critical Heat Flux) occurs at upper end. Rises steeply to maximum
3. Transition (Unstable Film) Boiling $50°\text{C} \le \Delta T_e \le 120°\text{C}$ Vapour film forms intermittently; part surface covered by vapour (poor conductor). Heat flux decreases with increasing $\Delta T_e$. Decreases
4. Stable Film Boiling $> 120°\text{C}$ (beyond Leidenfrost) Stable vapour film blankets the surface. Radiation becomes significant at very high $\Delta T_e$. Film boiling is the thermal reverse of film condensation. Rises again (radiation contribution)
Critical and Minimum Points

Critical Heat Flux (CHF) — the peak heat flux at the end of nucleate boiling. The most heat that can be transferred without triggering film boiling.

Leidenfrost Point — the minimum heat flux on the boiling curve, marking the stable onset of film boiling. Below this point, film boiling is unstable.

3. Effect of Surface Condition on Boiling

The condition of the heating surface has a strong influence on the nucleate boiling regime. Rough surfaces contain microscopic pits and cavities that trap small vapour/gas pockets — these act as active nucleation sites for bubble formation. Polished (smooth) surfaces have fewer nucleation sites and exhibit higher onset-of-boiling superheat.

In contrast, surface roughness has minimal effect on the film boiling regime since the surface is entirely blanketed by vapour and direct liquid-surface contact is absent.

4. Heat Transfer Magnitude Ranking

Exams frequently ask for the ranking of heat transfer coefficients across different modes. The general order from lowest to highest:

Heat Transfer Coefficient Ranking (Approximate)
$$\text{Conduction in air} < \text{Free convection} < \text{Forced convection} < \text{Nucleate boiling}$$
Dropwise condensation and nucleate boiling represent the upper end of single-surface heat transfer performance.

5. Condensation — Filmwise vs Dropwise

When vapour contacts a surface cooled below the saturation temperature, condensation occurs. The nature of the condensate determines the heat transfer performance:

ModeSurface typeMechanismHeat transfer coefficient
Filmwise condensation Clean, wettable (hydrophilic) surfaces Condensate forms a continuous liquid film that drains by gravity. The film acts as a thermal resistance between vapour and surface. Lower $h$
Dropwise condensation Oily, contaminated, or non-wetting (hydrophobic) surfaces Condensate forms discrete droplets that roll off, continuously exposing bare metal surface to vapour. No persistent thermal resistance layer. $h_{\text{dropwise}} \approx 5$–$10 \times h_{\text{filmwise}}$

Despite its superior performance, dropwise condensation is difficult to maintain in industrial condensers over long periods. Promoters (surface coatings that impart non-wettability) degrade with time, and surfaces revert to filmwise behaviour. Industrial condensers are therefore designed assuming filmwise condensation.

6. Nusselt Film Condensation Theory

Nusselt (1916) developed the classic analytical solution for laminar film condensation on a vertical plate. The result gives the average heat transfer coefficient as a function of fluid properties and geometry:

Average HTC — Laminar Film Condensation, Vertical Plate
$$\bar{h}_L = 0.943 \left[\frac{\rho_l(\rho_l - \rho_v)\,g\,h_{fg}\,k_l^3}{\mu_l\,L\,(T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)}\right]^{1/4}$$
$\rho_l, \rho_v$ = liquid/vapour densities  ·  $h_{fg}$ = latent heat  ·  $k_l$ = liquid thermal conductivity
$\mu_l$ = liquid dynamic viscosity  ·  $L$ = plate height  ·  $\Delta T = T_{\text{sat}} - T_w$
Local HTC at Position $x$ from Top
$$h_x = 0.707 \left[\frac{\rho_l(\rho_l - \rho_v)\,g\,h_{fg}\,k_l^3}{\mu_l\,x\,(T_{\text{sat}} - T_w)}\right]^{1/4}$$
Ratio — Average to Local at Bottom ($x = L$)
$$\bar{h}_L = \frac{4}{3}\,h_{x=L} \approx 1.333\,h_{x=L}$$
Also note: $\bar{h}_L \propto L^{-1/4}$ — longer plates give lower average coefficient because the film thickens.

Assumptions of Nusselt Theory

  • Inertia forces in the condensate film are negligible.
  • Heat transfer across the film is by pure conduction only.
  • Vapour velocity is low — zero interfacial shear drag.
  • Properties of the condensate are constant (evaluated at film temperature).

Key Points to Remember

Boiling & Condensation Summary

  • $\Delta T_e = T_w - T_{\text{sat}}$ — the single controlling variable for pool boiling regimes.
  • Regimes in order: Natural convection ($< 5°\text{C}$) → Nucleate ($5$–$50°\text{C}$) → Transition ($50$–$120°\text{C}$) → Film ($> 120°\text{C}$).
  • CHF = peak heat flux (end of nucleate boiling). Leidenfrost point = minimum heat flux (start of stable film boiling).
  • Rough surfaces promote nucleate boiling by providing more nucleation sites.
  • Smooth/polished surfaces suppress nucleate boiling (fewer sites).
  • Film boiling is the thermal reverse of film condensation.
  • Flow boiling and slug flow are NOT pool boiling regimes.
  • $h_{\text{dropwise}} \approx 5$–$10 \times h_{\text{filmwise}}$. Dropwise condensation occurs on oily/non-wetting surfaces.
  • Dropwise condensation cannot be sustained long-term in industrial equipment — design for filmwise.
  • Nusselt vertical plate: $\bar{h}_L = 0.943[\dots]^{1/4}$; local $h_x = 0.707[\dots]^{1/4}$.
  • $\bar{h}_L = \frac{4}{3}h_{x=L}$. Average is always 4/3 times the local value at the bottom.
  • $\bar{h}_L \propto L^{-1/4}$ — increasing plate height reduces average coefficient.
  • Nusselt assumptions: inertia negligible, pure conduction across film, zero interfacial drag.
