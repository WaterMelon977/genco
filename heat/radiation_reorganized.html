SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Fundamental Laws of Thermal Radiation

  • Stefan–Boltzmann Law (Black Body):
    $${\boxed{E_b = \sigma \cdot T^4 \quad (\text{W/m}^2)}}$$
    $${\boxed{Q = \sigma \cdot A \cdot T^4 \quad (\text{W})}}$$
  • $\sigma$: Stefan–Boltzmann constant $= 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$
  • $T$: absolute temperature ($\text{K}$; MUST be in Kelvin!)

  • Temperature Scaling Rule: Doubling $T$ $2T$ multiplies emissive power by $2^4 = 16$.

  • Wien's Displacement Law:

    $${\boxed{\lambda_{\text{max}} \cdot T = 2898 \quad (\mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}) \approx 2900 \quad (\mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K})}}$$

  • $\lambda_{\text{max}}$: wavelength corresponding to peak spectral emissive power $\mu\text{m}$

  • Physical meaning: Peak spectral emission shifts to SHORTER wavelengths as temperature increases $\lambda_{\text{max}} \propto \frac{1}{T}$.

  • Kirchhoff's Law of Radiation:

    $${\boxed{\varepsilon_\lambda = \alpha_\lambda \quad \text{and} \quad \varepsilon = \alpha}}$$

  • Valid at thermal equilibrium for a surface at a specified wavelength and temperature.

  • Planck's Law of Monochromatic Radiation:

    $${\boxed{E_{\lambda, b} = \frac{2\pi \cdot h \cdot c^2}{\lambda^5 \cdot \left(\exp\left(\frac{h \cdot c}{\lambda \cdot k \cdot T}\right) - 1\right)} \quad (\text{W/m}^2\cdot\mu\text{m})}}$$

2. Surface Radiation Properties

  • Incident Energy Conservation Balance:
    $${\boxed{\alpha + \rho + \tau = 1.0}}$$
  • $\alpha$: absorptivity (fraction of incident radiation absorbed)
  • $\rho$: reflectivity (fraction of incident radiation reflected)

  • $\tau$: transmissivity (fraction of incident radiation transmitted)

  • Black Body: $\boxed{\alpha = 1.0, \quad \varepsilon = 1.0, \quad \rho = 0, \quad \tau = 0}$

  • Opaque Body: $\boxed{\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0}$
  • White / Perfectly Reflecting Body: $\boxed{\rho = 1.0 \implies \alpha = 0, \quad \tau = 0}$
  • Gray Body: $\boxed{\varepsilon = \alpha = \text{constant} < 1.0}$ (independent of wavelength $\lambda$ and temperature $T$).

3. Radiosity, Irradiation & Intensity

  • Radiosity $J$:
    $${\boxed{J = E + \rho \cdot G = \varepsilon \cdot E_b + \rho \cdot G \quad (\text{W/m}^2)}}$$
  • $E$: emitted radiation flux $\text{W/m}^2$

  • $G$: incident radiation flux / irradiation $\text{W/m}^2$

  • Net Radiative Heat Flux Leaving Surface:

    $${\boxed{q_{\text{net}} = J - G = \frac{E_b - J}{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon \cdot A}} \quad (\text{W/m}^2)}}$$

  • Radiation Intensity $I$ & Emissive Power $E$:

    $${\boxed{E = \pi \cdot I \quad (\text{W/m}^2)}}$$

  • $I$: radiation intensity $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{sr}$

4. Shape Factors (View Factors / Angle Factors $F_{ij}$)

  • View Factor Definition:

    $${\boxed{F_{ij} = \frac{\text{Radiation Leaving Surface } i \text{ Intercepted directly by Surface } j}{\text{Total Radiation Leaving Surface } i}}}$$

  • Reciprocity Theorem:

    $${\boxed{A_1 \cdot F_{12} = A_2 \cdot F_{21}}}$$

  • Summation Rule for an Enclosure of $N$ Surfaces:

    $${\boxed{\sum_{j=1}^N F_{ij} = F_{i1} + F_{i2} + \dots + F_{iN} = 1.0}}$$

  • Self-View Factor Rules:

  • Flat or Convex Surface: $\boxed{F_{ii} = 0}$
  • Concave Surface: $\boxed{F_{ii} > 0}$
  • Infinite Parallel Planes: $\boxed{F_{12} = F_{21} = 1.0}$
  • Inner Surface $1$ of Concentric Spheres/Cylinders completely enclosed by outer Surface $2$: $\boxed{F_{12} = 1.0}$

5. Net Radiation Exchange & Network Analysis

  • Two Infinite Parallel Gray Planes:

    $${\boxed{q_{12} = \frac{\sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1} \quad (\text{W/m}^2)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\text{Equivalent Emissivity: } \varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1}}}$$

  • Two Concentric Cylinders / Spheres ($A_1 < A_2$, Inner Surface $1$, Outer Surface $2$):

    $${\boxed{Q_{12} = \frac{A_1 \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{A_1}{A_2}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1\right)} \quad (\text{W})}}$$

  • Small Convex Body $1$ inside Large Enclosure $2$ $A_1 \ll A_2 \implies \frac{A_1}{A_2} \to 0$:

    $${\boxed{Q_{12} = \varepsilon_1 \cdot A_1 \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4) \quad (\text{W})}}$$

  • Two Black Bodies (Any Geometry):

    $${\boxed{Q_{12} = A_1 \cdot F_{12} \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4) \quad (\text{W})}}$$

6. Radiation Shields

  • Heat Transfer with $n$ Identical Thin Shields between Parallel Planes $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2 = \varepsilon_s$:

    $${\boxed{Q_{\text{with } n \text{ shields}} = \frac{Q_{\text{without shields}}}{n + 1}}}$$

  • Percentage Reduction in Radiative Heat Transfer:

    $${\boxed{\text{Percentage Reduction} = \left(1 - \frac{1}{n + 1}\right) \times 100\%}}$$

  • $1$ Shield: $Q_1 = \frac{1}{2} Q_0 \implies \mathbf{50\% \text{ reduction}}$
  • $2$ Shields: $Q_2 = \frac{1}{3} Q_0 \implies \mathbf{66.7\% \text{ reduction}}$
  • $3$ Shields: $Q_3 = \frac{1}{4} Q_0 \implies \mathbf{75\% \text{ reduction}}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Thermal Radiation: Electromagnetic energy emitted by matter as a result of its absolute temperature $0.1 \mu\text{m} \le \lambda \le 100 \mu\text{m}$. Requires no medium and travels at the speed of light $c \approx 3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Black Body: An ideal surface that absorbs all incident radiation regardless of wavelength or direction $\alpha = 1.0$. At a given temperature, it emits the maximum possible thermal radiation $\varepsilon = 1.0$.
  • Gray Body: An idealized body whose monochromatic emissivity $\varepsilon_\lambda$ and absorptivity $\alpha_\lambda$ are constant and independent of wavelength $\lambda$ and temperature $T$.
  • Opaque Surface: A surface through which no radiation is transmitted $\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0$.
  • Irradiation $G$: The total thermal radiation incident on a surface per unit area per unit time $\text{W/m}^2$.
  • Radiosity $J$: The total thermal radiation leaving a surface per unit area per unit time, comprising both emitted and reflected components $J = \varepsilon E_b + \rho G$.
  • Shape Factor $F_{ij}$: The fraction of radiation leaving surface $i$ that is directly intercepted by surface $j$. It depends strictly on geometry and orientation, independent of surface temperatures or emissivities.
  • Radiation Shield: A thin high-reflectivity (low-emissivity) barrier placed between heat-exchanging surfaces to introduce extra thermal resistances and minimize radiative heat loss.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Stefan–Boltzmann Constant: $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$.
  • Wien's Displacement Constant: $b = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K} \approx 2900 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • Solar Surface Temperature: $T_{\text{sun}} \approx 5780 \text{ K} - 5800 \text{ K}$ $\lambda_{\text{max}} \approx 0.5 \mu\text{m}$.
  • Emissive Power / Intensity Ratio: $E = \pi \cdot I$.
  • 1 Radiation Shield Reduction: $50\%$ reduction $Q_1 = 0.5 Q_0$.
  • 3 Radiation Shields Reduction: $75\%$ reduction $Q_3 = 0.25 Q_0$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Fundamental Laws of Thermal Radiation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Nature of Thermal Radiation: Electromagnetic waves emitted by all matter above $0 \text{ K}$ due to vibrational/rotational states. Wavelength span: $0.1 \mu\text{m} - 100 \mu\text{m}$ (UV, visible, IR spectrum).
  • Stefan–Boltzmann Law:
    $${E_b = \sigma \cdot T^4 \quad (\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4)}$$
  • Emissive power is proportional to the FOURTH POWER of absolute temperature $T^4$.
  • Temperature MUST be in Kelvin $\text{K}$. Doubling $T \implies 2^4 = 16\times$ emissive power!
  • Wien's Displacement Law:
    $${\lambda_{\text{max}} \cdot T = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K} \approx 2900 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}}$$
  • As temperature rises, the peak emission wavelength $\lambda_{\text{max}}$ SHIFTS TO SHORTER WAVELENGTHS $\lambda_{\text{max}} \propto 1/T$.
  • Kirchhoff's Law:
    $${\varepsilon = \alpha}$$
  • At thermal equilibrium, the total emissivity of a surface equals its total absorptivity at the same temperature.
  • Planck's Law: Gives the spectral distribution of black-body emissive power. Integrating Planck's law over all wavelengths $\lambda = 0 \to \infty$ yields the Stefan–Boltzmann law $E_b = \sigma T^4$.
💡 Key Concept: Fourth-Power Temperature Rule: Total emissive power $E_b \propto T^4$. Temperature MUST be in Kelvin! If temperature is doubled $T_2 = 2T_1$, emissive power increases by $2^4 = 16$ times!

[!TRAP] Wien's Peak Shift Direction: Higher temperature means SHORTER peak wavelength $\lambda_{\text{max}} \propto 1/T$. Do not claim peak shifts to longer wavelengths as temperature rises!

2. Decision Tree
Thermal Radiation Law Check?
├─ Is query about total emissive power vs Temp? → STEFAN-BOLTZMANN LAW (Eb = sigma * T^4)
├─ Is query about peak wavelength shift vs Temp? → WIEN'S DISPLACEMENT LAW (lambda_max * T = 2898 um*K)
├─ Is query about emissivity vs absorptivity at equilibrium? → KIRCHHOFF'S LAW (epsilon = alpha)
└─ Is query about spectral intensity distribution? → PLANCK'S LAW
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$.
  • [ ] $E_b \propto T^4$ (always Kelvin).
  • [ ] $\lambda_{\text{max}} T = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • [ ] Kirchhoff: $\varepsilon = \alpha$ at equilibrium.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: The sun has a peak spectral emission wavelength $\lambda_{\text{max}} = 0.5 \mu\text{m}$. Estimate the surface temperature of the sun assuming it behaves as a black body. - Solution: - Apply Wien's displacement law: $\lambda_{\text{max}} \cdot T = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$. - $T = \frac{2898}{0.5} = 5796 \text{ K} \approx 5780 \text{ K}$. - Key Insight: $T \approx 5780 \text{ K}$. Divide Wien's constant by $\lambda_{\text{max}}$ in $\mu\text{m}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: The temperature of a black body is increased from $300 \text{ K}$ to $600 \text{ K}$. By what factor does its total emissive power increase? - Solution: - $E_{b1} = \sigma (300)^4$, $E_{b2} = \sigma (600)^4$. - $\frac{E_{b2}}{E_{b1}} = \left(\frac{600}{300}\right)^4 = (2)^4 = 16$. - Key Insight: Emissive power increases by a factor of 16.

3.2 Surface Radiation Properties (Black, Gray, Opaque, Selective)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Energy Conservation Balance:
    $${\alpha + \rho + \tau = 1.0}$$
  • Absorptivity $\alpha$, Reflectivity $\rho$, Transmissivity $\tau$.
  • Surface Classifications:
  • Black Body: $\alpha = 1.0, \varepsilon = 1.0, \rho = 0, \tau = 0$. Perfect absorber & emitter.
  • Opaque Body: $\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0$. (Opaque does NOT mean black; it only means no transmission).
  • Gray Body: Emissivity $\varepsilon$ and absorptivity $\alpha$ are constant and independent of wavelength and temperature $\varepsilon = \alpha < 1.0$.
  • White Body: $\rho = 1.0 \implies \alpha = 0, \tau = 0$. Perfect reflector.
  • Transparent Body: $\tau \approx 1.0 \implies \alpha \approx 0, \rho \approx 0$.
  • Selective Surfaces:
  • High absorptivity $\alpha$ in the shortwave solar spectrum $\lambda < 3 \mu\text{m}$ AND low emissivity $\varepsilon$ in the longwave thermal infrared spectrum $\lambda > 3 \mu\text{m}$. Ideal for solar thermal collectors.
💡 Key Concept: Opaque vs Black Body Distinction: An Opaque surface means $\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0$. It does NOT mean black! A white painted wall is opaque $\tau = 0$ but has high reflectivity $\rho$ and low absorptivity $\alpha$.

[!TRAP] Gray Body Definition Trap: A Gray body has constant $\varepsilon = \alpha < 1.0$ across all wavelengths. It is an idealization used to simplify engineering calculations.

2. Decision Tree
Surface Property Check?
├─ Is alpha = 1.0 and rho = tau = 0? → BLACK BODY
├─ Is tau = 0? → OPAQUE BODY (alpha + rho = 1.0)
├─ Is rho = 1.0? → WHITE / PERFECTLY REFLECTING BODY
├─ Is emissivity constant across all wavelengths? → GRAY BODY (epsilon = alpha < 1.0)
└─ High alpha for solar + low epsilon for thermal IR? → SELECTIVE SURFACE (Solar Collector)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\alpha + \rho + \tau = 1.0$.
  • [ ] Black body: $\alpha = \varepsilon = 1.0$.
  • [ ] Opaque body: $\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0$.
  • [ ] Gray body: $\varepsilon = \alpha = \text{const} < 1.0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A surface reflects $35\%$ of incident radiation $\rho = 0.35$ and transmits $25\%$ $\tau = 0.25$. If the body is in thermal equilibrium with its surroundings, calculate its emissivity $\varepsilon$. - Solution: - Energy balance: $\alpha + \rho + \tau = 1.0 \implies \alpha = 1.0 - 0.35 - 0.25 = 0.40$. - By Kirchhoff's law ($\varepsilon = \alpha$ at thermal equilibrium): $\varepsilon = 0.40$. - Key Insight: $\varepsilon = 0.40$. Apply $\alpha = 1 - \rho - \tau$ then $\varepsilon = \alpha$.

3.3 Radiosity, Irradiation & Intensity

1. Concept Pointers
  • Irradiation $G$: Total radiation incident on a surface per unit area $\text{W/m}^2$.
  • Radiosity $J$: Total radiation LEAVING a surface per unit area $\text{W/m}^2$.
    $${J = E + \rho \cdot G = \varepsilon \cdot E_b + \rho \cdot G}$$
  • For an Opaque Surface $\rho = 1 - \varepsilon$: $J = \varepsilon E_b + (1 - \varepsilon) G$.
  • For a Black Surface $\rho = 0$: $J = E_b = \sigma T^4$.
  • Net Heat Flux Leaving Surface:
    $${q_{\text{net}} = J - G = \frac{E_b - J}{\frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon \cdot A}}}$$
  • Surface resistance to radiation: $R_{\text{surface}} = \frac{1-\varepsilon}{\varepsilon \cdot A}$.
  • Intensity of Radiation $I$:
  • Radiation energy emitted per unit time, per unit projected area, per unit solid angle $\text{W/m}^2\cdot\text{sr}$.
  • Relation to total emissive power for a diffuse emitter:
    $${E = \pi \cdot I}$$
💡 Key Concept: Radiosity Composition: Radiosity $J = \text{Emitted Radiation } (E) + \text{Reflected Radiation } (\rho G)$. For a black body, $\rho = 0 \implies J = E_b = \sigma T^4$.

[!TRAP] Emissive Power vs Intensity Factor: Total emissive power $E = \pi \cdot I$. The factor is $\pi$ (Pi), NOT $2\pi$ or $4\pi$!

2. Decision Tree
Radiosity / Intensity Check?
├─ Is query about total radiation LEAVING a surface? → RADIOSITY J = epsilon * Eb + rho * G
├─ Is query about total radiation ARRIVING at a surface? → IRRADIATION G
├─ Is surface black (rho = 0)? → J = Eb = sigma * T^4
└─ What is relation between Emissive Power E and Intensity I? → E = pi * I
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $J = \varepsilon E_b + \rho G$.
  • [ ] Opaque $J = \varepsilon E_b + (1-\varepsilon) G$.
  • [ ] Black body $J = E_b$.
  • [ ] $E = \pi \cdot I$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An opaque gray surface $\varepsilon = \alpha = 0.5$ receives solar irradiation $G = 1200 \text{ W/m}^2$. The surface emissive power is $E = 600 \text{ W/m}^2$. Calculate the radiosity $J$. - Solution: - Opaque surface $\implies \tau = 0 \implies \rho = 1 - \alpha = 1 - 0.5 = 0.5$. - $J = E + \rho \cdot G = 600 + (0.5 \times 1200) = 600 + 600 = 1200 \text{ W/m}^2$. - Key Insight: $J = 1200 \text{ W/m}^2$. Include reflected flux $\rho G$.

3.4 Shape Factors (View Factors $F_{ij}$)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition: $F_{ij}$ = fraction of radiation leaving surface $i$ that directly reaches surface $j$.
  • Purely Geometric Property: Depends ONLY on shape, area, distance, and orientation of surfaces. Independent of temperature, emissivity, or thermal state!
  • Reciprocity Relation:
    $${A_1 \cdot F_{12} = A_2 \cdot F_{21}}$$
  • Summation Rule (Enclosure):
    $${\sum_{j=1}^N F_{ij} = F_{i1} + F_{i2} + \dots + F_{iN} = 1.0}$$
  • Surface Curvature Rules:
  • Flat or Convex Surface: Cannot see any part of itself $\implies F_{ii} = 0$.
  • Concave Surface: Sees a portion of itself $\implies F_{ii} > 0$.
  • Special Geometry View Factors:
  • Two Infinite Parallel Planes: $F_{12} = F_{21} = 1.0$.
  • Inner cylinder/sphere $1$ in concentric enclosure $2$: $F_{12} = 1.0$. By reciprocity, $F_{21} = \frac{A_1}{A_2}$. By summation, $F_{22} = 1 - F_{21} = 1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2}$.
💡 Key Concept: View Factor Geometric Independence: Shape factor $F_{ij}$ depends ONLY on geometry! Temperature and emissivity NEVER enter into the view factor $F_{ij}$.

[!TRAP] Self-View Factor Rule: - Flat Surface $\implies F_{11} = 0$. - Convex Surface $\implies F_{11} = 0$. - Concave Surface $\implies F_{11} > 0$!

2. Decision Tree
Shape Factor Calculation Check?
├─ Is surface 1 flat or convex? → F11 = 0
├─ Is surface 1 completely enclosed by surface 2? → F12 = 1.0
├─ Need to find F21 given F12 and Areas? → RECIPROCITY RULE: A1 * F12 = A2 * F21
└─ Need to find remaining view factor in N-surface enclosure? → SUMMATION RULE: sum(F1j) = 1.0
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $A_1 F_{12} = A_2 F_{21}$.
  • [ ] $\sum_j F_{ij} = 1.0$.
  • [ ] Flat/Convex $F_{11} = 0$.
  • [ ] Concentric inner $F_{12} = 1.0 \implies F_{22} = 1 - A_1/A_2$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A solid cylinder (surface $2$, diameter $d = 1 \text{ m}$, length $L = 1 \text{ m}$) is completely enclosed by a hollow sphere (surface $1$, diameter $D = 2 \text{ m}$). Calculate the self-view factor of the sphere $F_{11}$. - Solution: - Inner surface $2$ is completely enclosed by outer sphere $1 \implies F_{21} = 1.0$. - Area of sphere $A_1 = \pi D^2 = \pi (2)^2 = 4\pi \text{ m}^2$. - Area of cylinder $A_2 = \pi d L + 2\left(\frac{\pi}{4}d^2\right) = \pi(1)(1) + 0.5\pi = 1.5\pi \text{ m}^2$. - Reciprocity: $A_1 F_{12} = A_2 F_{21} \implies 4\pi \cdot F_{12} = 1.5\pi \cdot (1.0) \implies F_{12} = \frac{1.5}{4} = 0.375$. - Summation rule for sphere $1$: $F_{11} + F_{12} = 1.0 \implies F_{11} = 1.0 - 0.375 = 0.625$. - Key Insight: $F_{11} = 0.625$. Combine $F_{21} = 1.0$, reciprocity, and summation rule.

3.5 Net Radiative Heat Transfer & Network Analysis

1. Concept Pointers
  • Two Infinite Parallel Gray Planes:
    $${q_{12} = \frac{\sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1}}$$
  • Equivalent Emissivity: $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1}$.
  • If $\varepsilon_1 = \varepsilon_2 = \varepsilon \implies \varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{\varepsilon}{2 - \varepsilon}$.
  • Two Concentric Cylinders / Spheres $A_1 < A_2$:
    $${Q_{12} = \frac{A_1 \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{A_1}{A_2}\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1\right)}}$$
  • Small Body in Large Enclosure $A_1 \ll A_2 \implies \frac{A_1}{A_2} \to 0$:
    $${Q_{12} = \varepsilon_1 \cdot A_1 \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}$$
  • Black Surfaces:
    $${Q_{12} = A_1 \cdot F_{12} \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4)}$$
💡 Key Concept: Parallel Gray Planes Formula: $q_{12} = \frac{\sigma(T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1}$. The denominator includes a "$-1$" term! Do not omit the $-1$ term!

[!TRAP] Small Body in Large Enclosure: When $A_1 \ll A_2$, outer emissivity $\varepsilon_2$ plays NO ROLE! The heat transfer depends ONLY on inner body emissivity $\varepsilon_1$.

2. Decision Tree
Net Radiative Heat Transfer Calculation Check?
├─ Is geometry Infinite Parallel Gray Planes? → q12 = sigma * (T1^4 - T2^4) / (1/eps1 + 1/eps2 - 1)
├─ Is geometry Concentric Cylinders / Spheres? → Q12 = A1 * sigma * (T1^4 - T2^4) / [ 1/eps1 + (A1/A2)*(1/eps2 - 1) ]
├─ Is body 1 very small compared to enclosure 2 (A1 << A2)? → Q12 = eps1 * A1 * sigma * (T1^4 - T2^4)
└─ Are both surfaces Black (eps1 = eps2 = 1)? → Q12 = A1 * F12 * sigma * (T1^4 - T2^4)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Gray parallel planes $q = \frac{\sigma(T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1}$.
  • [ ] $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1}$.
  • [ ] Small body $A_1 \ll A_2 \implies Q_{12} = \varepsilon_1 A_1 \sigma (T_1^4 - T_2^4)$.
  • [ ] Black surfaces $Q_{12} = A_1 F_{12} \sigma (T_1^4 - T_2^4)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Two large infinite parallel gray planes with emissivities $\varepsilon_1 = 0.8$ and $\varepsilon_2 = 0.8$ are maintained at temperatures $T_1 = 1000 \text{ K}$ and $T_2 = 500 \text{ K}$. Calculate the net radiative heat flux $q_{12}$. - Solution: - Calculate equivalent emissivity:

$${\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{0.8} + \frac{1}{0.8} - 1} = \frac{1}{1.25 + 1.25 - 1} = \frac{1}{1.5} = \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667}$$
- Calculate temperature difference of fourth powers:
$${T_1^4 - T_2^4 = (1000)^4 - (500)^4 = 1 \times 10^{12} - 0.0625 \times 10^{12} = 0.9375 \times 10^{12} \text{ K}^4}$$
- Calculate net heat flux:
$${q_{12} = \varepsilon_{\text{eq}} \cdot \sigma \cdot (T_1^4 - T_2^4) = \frac{2}{3} \times (5.67 \times 10^{-8}) \times (0.9375 \times 10^{12})}$$
$${q_{12} = 0.6667 \times 5.67 \times 93750 = 35437.5 \text{ W/m}^2 \approx 35.44 \text{ kW/m}^2}$$
- Key Insight: $q_{12} \approx 35.44 \text{ kW/m}^2$. Combine $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = 2/3$ with $\sigma(T_1^4 - T_2^4)$.

3.6 Radiation Shields

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition & Purpose: Thin sheets of high reflectivity (low emissivity $\varepsilon_s$) inserted between heat exchanging surfaces to increase total radiation thermal resistance and reduce net heat transfer.
  • Equal Emissivity Parallel Plates Rule:
  • If $n$ identical radiation shields (emissivity $\varepsilon$) are inserted between two parallel plates of the same emissivity $\varepsilon$:
    $${\boxed{Q_{\text{with } n \text{ shields}} = \frac{1}{n + 1} \cdot Q_{\text{without shields}}}}$$
  • Reduction Factor Breakdown:
  • $1$ Shield $n=1$ $\implies Q_1 = \frac{1}{2} Q_0 = 50\% Q_0$ ($50\%$ heat loss reduction).
  • $2$ Shields $n=2$ $\implies Q_2 = \frac{1}{3} Q_0 = 33.3\% Q_0$ ($66.7\%$ heat loss reduction).
  • $3$ Shields $n=3$ $\implies Q_3 = \frac{1}{4} Q_0 = 25\% Q_0$ ($75\%$ heat loss reduction).
💡 Key Concept: Equal Emissivity Shield Formula: $Q_n = \frac{Q_0}{n+1}$. For $n$ identical shields, heat transfer drops to $\frac{1}{n+1}$ of the unshielded value!

[!TRAP] Shield Reduction Fraction Trap: - 1 shield reduces heat transfer BY $50\%$ (remains $50\%$). - 3 shields reduce heat transfer BY $75\%$ (remains $25\%$). Do not confuse "heat transfer remaining" with "heat transfer reduction"!

2. Decision Tree
Radiation Shield Calculation Check?
├─ Are all surface emissivities equal (eps1 = eps2 = eps_shield)? → Q_n = Q_0 / (n + 1)
├─ How many shields needed for 50% reduction? → 1 Shield (Q1 = Q0 / 2)
├─ How many shields needed for 75% reduction? → 3 Shields (Q3 = Q0 / 4)
└─ What makes an effective shield? → HIGH Reflectivity / LOW Emissivity (low eps_s)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $Q_n = \frac{Q_0}{n+1}$ (equal $\varepsilon$).
  • [ ] $n=1 \implies 50\%$ reduction.
  • [ ] $n=3 \implies 75\%$ reduction.
  • [ ] Shields require low emissivity $\varepsilon_s$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Two large parallel plates with emissivities $\varepsilon = 0.7$ exchange heat by radiation. How many thin parallel radiation shields of emissivity $\varepsilon = 0.7$ must be inserted between them to reduce the net heat transfer rate by $75\%$? - Solution: - Heat transfer reduction of $75\%$ means remaining heat transfer $Q_n = 25\% Q_0 = \frac{1}{4} Q_0$. - Apply equal emissivity shield formula: $Q_n = \frac{Q_0}{n + 1}$. - $\frac{1}{n + 1} = \frac{1}{4} \implies n + 1 = 4 \implies n = 3$. - Number of shields required $= 3$. - Key Insight: $n = 3$ shields yield $75\%$ reduction ($1/4$ remaining).

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: State Stefan-Boltzmann law and give the value of Stefan-Boltzmann constant $\sigma$.
A: $E_b = \sigma T^4$, where $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$ and $T$ is absolute temperature in Kelvin.

Q2: State Wien's displacement law and explain the effect of temperature on peak wavelength.
A: $\lambda_{\text{max}} T = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$. As temperature increases, the wavelength of maximum spectral emissive power shifts to shorter wavelengths $\lambda_{\text{max}} \propto 1/T$.

Q3: State Kirchhoff's law of radiation.
A: At thermal equilibrium, the total emissivity of a surface equals its total absorptivity $\varepsilon = \alpha$ at the same temperature and wavelength.

Q4: Define a Black Body.
A: An ideal surface that absorbs all incident radiation regardless of wavelength or direction $\alpha = 1.0, \varepsilon = 1.0, \rho = 0, \tau = 0$.

Q5: Define an Opaque Surface and write its property relation.
A: An opaque surface allows zero radiation transmission $\tau = 0$, so $\alpha + \rho = 1.0$.

Q6: Define a Gray Body.
A: An idealized body whose monochromatic emissivity $\varepsilon_\lambda$ and absorptivity $\alpha_\lambda$ are constant and independent of wavelength and temperature $\varepsilon = \alpha < 1.0$.

Q7: Differentiate between Irradiation $G$ and Radiosity $J$.
A: Irradiation $G$ is the total radiation arriving/incident on a surface. Radiosity $J$ is the total radiation leaving a surface $J = \varepsilon E_b + \rho G$.

Q8: Write the relationship between total emissive power $E$ and radiation intensity $I$ for a diffuse surface.
A: $E = \pi \cdot I$.

Q9: What is a Shape Factor $F_{ij}$? Is it dependent on surface temperature?
A: $F_{ij}$ is the fraction of radiation leaving surface $i$ that directly reaches surface $j$. It is purely geometric and completely independent of temperature or surface properties.

Q10: State the Reciprocity Theorem for view factors.
A: $A_1 F_{12} = A_2 F_{21}$.

Q11: What is the self-view factor $F_{11}$ for a flat or convex surface?
A: $F_{11} = 0$ (a flat or convex surface cannot see any part of itself).

Q12: Write the formula for equivalent emissivity $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}}$ of two infinite parallel gray planes.
A: $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{\varepsilon_1} + \frac{1}{\varepsilon_2} - 1}$.

Q13: Write the net heat transfer rate equation for a small convex body $1$ enclosed in a very large space $2$ $A_1 \ll A_2$.
A: $Q_{12} = \varepsilon_1 A_1 \sigma (T_1^4 - T_2^4)$.

Q14: Write the formula for heat transfer with $n$ identical radiation shields between two parallel gray planes of equal emissivity.
A: $Q_{\text{with } n \text{ shields}} = \frac{Q_{\text{without shields}}}{n + 1}$.

Q15: By what percentage does $1$ radiation shield reduce radiative heat transfer between two parallel plates of equal emissivity?
A: By $50\%$ $Q_1 = \frac{1}{2} Q_0$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Stefan-Boltzmann: $E_b = \sigma T^4$ $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8} \text{ W/m}^2\cdot\text{K}^4$. Always use Kelvin! Doubling $T \implies 16\times$ power.
  • Wien's Law: $\lambda_{\text{max}} T = 2898 \mu\text{m}\cdot\text{K}$. Peak shifts to shorter wavelengths as $T \uparrow$.
  • Kirchhoff's Law: $\varepsilon = \alpha$ at thermal equilibrium.
  • Surface Property Balance: $\alpha + \rho + \tau = 1.0$.
  • Black Body: $\alpha = \varepsilon = 1.0, \rho = \tau = 0$.
  • Opaque Body: $\tau = 0 \implies \alpha + \rho = 1.0$.
  • Gray Body: $\varepsilon = \alpha = \text{constant} < 1.0$.
  • Radiosity & Intensity: $J = \varepsilon E_b + \rho G$; Black body $J = E_b$; $E = \pi \cdot I$.
  • Shape Factors: $F_{ij}$ is purely geometric.
  • Reciprocity: $A_1 F_{12} = A_2 F_{21}$.
  • Summation: $\sum_j F_{ij} = 1.0$.
  • Flat / Convex: $F_{11} = 0$. Concave: $F_{11} > 0$.
  • Infinite Parallel Planes: $F_{12} = 1.0$.
  • Gray Parallel Planes: $q_{12} = \frac{\sigma(T_1^4 - T_2^4)}{1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1}$. $\varepsilon_{\text{eq}} = \frac{1}{1/\varepsilon_1 + 1/\varepsilon_2 - 1}$.
  • Small Body in Large Enclosure: $A_1 \ll A_2 \implies Q_{12} = \varepsilon_1 A_1 \sigma (T_1^4 - T_2^4)$.
  • Radiation Shields: $Q_n = \frac{Q_0}{n+1}$ (equal $\varepsilon$). 1 shield $\implies 50\%$ reduction; 3 shields $\implies 75\%$ reduction.
