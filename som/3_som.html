1. Shear Force and Bending Moment Diagrams

Shear Force (V) at a section is the algebraic sum of all transverse forces acting to one side of the section. Bending Moment (M) at a section is the algebraic sum of moments of all forces acting to one side of the section.

1.1 Sign Convention (Standard)

  • Shear force: Upward force on left side = positive. (Forces causing clockwise rotation on left = +ve).
  • Bending moment: Sagging (hogging from below, concave up) = positive. Hogging = negative.

1.2 Load–SF–BM Relations

$$\frac{dV}{dx} = -w(x) \quad\text{(rate of change of SF = negative of load intensity)}$$
$$\frac{dM}{dx} = V \quad\text{(rate of change of BM = shear force)}$$
$$\frac{d^2M}{dx^2} = -w(x)$$

$w(x)$ — distributed load intensity (N/m), positive downward $V$ — shear force (N) $M$ — bending moment (N·m)

Key rule: Maximum bending moment occurs where $dM/dx = V = 0$, i.e., where the SFD crosses zero. The point of zero shear is where BM is maximum (or minimum).

2. Bending Stresses in Beams

Based on the Euler-Bernoulli beam theory, the bending (flexural) stress at any point in the cross-section is derived from the flexure formula:

$$\frac{M}{I} = \frac{\sigma}{y} = \frac{E}{R}$$

$M$ — bending moment at the section (N·m) $I$ — second moment of area (moment of inertia) about the neutral axis (m⁴) $\sigma$ — bending stress at distance $y$ from NA (Pa) $y$ — distance from the neutral axis (m) $E$ — Young's Modulus (Pa) $R$ — radius of curvature (m)

$$\sigma = \frac{My}{I} = \frac{M}{Z}$$

2.1 Section Modulus

$$Z = \frac{I}{y_{max}}$$

Section modulus $Z$ (m³) is a measure of the bending strength of a cross-section. Higher $Z$ means more resistance to bending. Maximum bending stress occurs at the extreme fibre: $\sigma_{max} = M/Z$.

2.2 Assumptions of Euler-Bernoulli Beam Theory

  • Plane sections remain plane after bending (Bernoulli's hypothesis).
  • Material is homogeneous, isotropic, and linearly elastic (obeys Hooke's law).
  • The beam is initially straight and of uniform cross-section.
  • Deflections are small compared to beam length.
  • Shear deformations are neglected (Timoshenko beam theory includes them).
  • The neutral axis passes through the centroid of the cross-section.
Neutral Axis (NA): At the NA, bending stress = 0. The NA coincides with the centroidal axis for symmetric sections. Maximum bending stress (tensile or compressive) occurs at the extreme fibres, farthest from the NA.

3. Shear Stresses in Beams

Shear stress distribution across the cross-section of a beam is given by the shear formula:

$$\tau = \frac{V A' \bar{y}'}{I b}$$

$V$ — shear force at the section (N) $A'$ — area of cross-section above the layer of interest (m²) $\bar{y}'$ — distance of centroid of $A'$ from NA (m) $I$ — moment of inertia about NA (m⁴) $b$ — width of cross-section at the layer of interest (m)

Flange of I-section—Very low—
I-section efficiency: In an I-section, the flanges carry most of the bending stress (far from NA, large $y$). The web carries most of the shear stress. Flanges can be thin; web must be thick enough for shear. This is the basis for efficient beam design.

3.2 Shear Stress Distribution Patterns

  • Rectangle: Parabolic distribution — zero at top and bottom, max at NA.
  • Circle: Parabolic — zero at top and bottom, max at NA.
  • Triangle: NOT max at NA — max at $h/2$ from base (= $h/3$ from apex). This is a frequently tested exception.
  • I-section: Nearly uniform in the web (slight parabola); very low in flanges (drops sharply at flange-web junction).
Bending vs Shear stress locations: Bending stress is MAXIMUM at extreme fibres (max $y$) and ZERO at the NA. Shear stress is MAXIMUM at the NA and ZERO at the extreme fibres. These are complementary.

Key Points to Remember

  • $dV/dx = -w$; $dM/dx = V$. Point loads cause steps in SFD; UDL causes linear SFD and parabolic BMD.
  • Max BM occurs where $V = 0$ (SF crosses zero). Max SF occurs near supports or at point loads.
  • Point of contraflexure: $M = 0$ and changes sign. NOT the same as zero SF point.
  • Flexure formula: $M/I = \sigma/y = E/R$. Bending stress $\sigma = My/I$; max at extreme fibres.
  • Section modulus $Z = I/y_{max}$; $\sigma_{max} = M/Z$. Higher $Z$ → lower bending stress for same $M$.
  • Neutral axis: passes through centroid; $\sigma_{bending} = 0$ at NA.
  • Shear stress formula: $\tau = VA'\bar{y}'/(Ib)$. Max at NA for rectangle and circle.
  • $\tau_{max}/\tau_{avg}$: Rectangle = 1.5; Circle = 4/3; Triangle = 1.5 (at $h/2$ from base, NOT at NA).
  • I-section: flanges resist bending; web resists shear. Efficient — area placed far from NA.
  • Adding a flange dramatically increases $I$ (∝ $d^3$) with little extra area — hence efficient use of material.
