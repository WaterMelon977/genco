1. Metal Cutting Fundamentals

This guide covers metal cutting fundamentals, tool geometry, chip formation, Merchant theory, Taylor tool-life relation, cutting speed and forces, and tool wear for AP Genco AE-style preparation. The highest-frequency PYQ areas are ASA tool signature, chip thickness ratio, shear angle, Taylor’s equation, cutting speed calculations, force relations, and flank/crater wear identification.

2. Quick Reference Sheet

2.1 All Formulas

📌 ASA Tool Signature
\alpha_b - \alpha_s - \gamma_e - \gamma_s - C_e - C_s - r
$\alpha_b$: back rake | $\alpha_s$: side rake | $\gamma_e$: end relief | $\gamma_s$: side relief | $C_e$: end cutting edge | $C_s$: side cutting edge | $r$: nose radius (mm)
📌 Lip Angle Formula
\text{Lip angle} = 90^\circ - (\text{rake angle} + \text{clearance angle})
📌 Chip Thickness Ratio ($r$)
r = \frac{t}{t_c} = \frac{\sin\phi}{\cos(\phi-\alpha)} = \frac{l_c}{l}
$t$: uncut thickness | $t_c$: cut chip thickness | $l_c$: chip length | $l$: cut length | $\phi$: shear angle | $\alpha$: rake angle
📌 Merchant Relation & Shear Angle Formula
2\phi + \beta - \alpha = 90^\circ \implies \phi = \frac{\pi}{4} + \frac{\alpha - \beta}{2} \quad \text{and} \quad \tan\phi = \frac{r\cos\alpha}{1-r\sin\alpha}
$\beta$: friction angle | Minimum power consumption criterion
📌 Friction Coefficient, Speed, Forces & Power
\mu = \tan\beta = \frac{F_c\sin\alpha + F_t\cos\alpha}{F_c\cos\alpha - F_t\sin\alpha}, \quad V = \frac{\pi D N}{1000}, \quad V_c = V \cdot r
$F_s = F_c\cos\phi - F_t\sin\phi$, $F_n = F_c\sin\phi + F_t\cos\phi$, $P = F_c \cdot V$
📌 Taylor's Tool Life Equation
V T^n = C \implies V_1 T_1^n = V_2 T_2^n
$V$: cutting speed (m/min) | $T$: tool life (min) | Exponents: HSS (0.08–0.2), Carbide (0.2–0.4), Ceramic (0.5–0.7)

2.2 Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • ASA Tool Signature: Standard order of 7 tool angles/dimensions specified in the American Standards Association system.
  • Orthogonal Cutting: Cutting edge is perpendicular to the direction of tool travel, resulting in 2D force state.
  • Oblique Cutting: Cutting edge is inclined to tool travel, resulting in 3D chip flow and force components.
  • Built-up Edge (BUE): Layer of work material adhering to the rake face under high friction and temperature at low-to-medium speeds.
  • Flank Wear: Progressive wear on clearance faces due to sliding contact/abrasion against machined surface.
  • Crater Wear: Wear bowl formed on tool rake face caused primarily by high-temperature solid-state diffusion.

3. Main Body - Topic Breakdown

3.1 Tool Geometry & ASA Signature

Tool geometry dictates force components and tool life. In ASA notation, the reference planes are perpendicular to each other. Rake angles control chip flow direction and force magnitude, while relief angles prevent rubbing against the workpiece.

💡 ASA Signature Element Order
Memorize the exact 7-part sequence: 1. Back Rake, 2. Side Rake, 3. End Relief, 4. Side Relief, 5. End Cutting Edge Angle, 6. Side Cutting Edge Angle, 7. Nose Radius.

3.2 Mechanics of Chip Formation

Chips are produced by localized shear deformation along a shear plane. The three primary chip types are continuous chips (ductile material, high speed, high rake), discontinuous chips (brittle material), and continuous chips with BUE (ductile material, low speed, high friction).

3.3 Merchant's Force Analysis

Merchant's circle relates cutting force ($F_c$), thrust force ($F_t$), friction force ($F$), normal force ($N$), shear force ($F_s$), and normal force on shear plane ($F_n$). Merchant's expression assumes minimum energy dissipation during machining.

3.4 Tool Life & Wear Mechanisms

⚠️ Flank Wear vs Crater Wear Traps
  • Flank Wear: Located on relief face. Primary cause is abrasive friction. Criterion for tool replacement ($VB \approx 0.3 \text{ mm}$).
  • Crater Wear: Located on rake face. Primary cause is chemical diffusion at maximum temperature zone away from the cutting tip.

2. Machine Tools & Grinding

Covers lathe operations, shaper, planer, slotter, milling machines, drilling machines, and grinding wheel specifications (abrasive type, grain size, grade, structure, bond type).

Quick Reference & Key Formulas

📌 Grinding Wheel Marking Standard
\text{Manufacturer} - \text{A} - \text{46} - \text{K} - \text{5} - \text{V} - \text{Suffix}
A: Abrasive (Aluminium Oxide) | 46: Grain size (Medium) | K: Grade (Medium) | 5: Structure (Dense) | V: Bond (Vitrified)
🎯 Shaper vs Planer Comparison
  • Shaper: Tool reciprocates, work feeds. Used for small workpieces. Quick return mechanism (velocity during return stroke is higher).
  • Planer: Workpiece reciprocates, tool feeds. Used for large heavy workpieces. Table driven by rack and pinion or hydraulic drive.
💡 Up Milling vs Down Milling
  • Up Milling (Conventional): Cutter rotates against work feed. Chip thickness increases from zero to maximum. Tends to lift workpiece.
  • Down Milling (Climb): Cutter rotates with work feed. Chip thickness starts at maximum. Better finish, requires backlash eliminator.

3. Metrology & Measurement

Covers standards of measurement, limits, fits, tolerances, linear measurement, angular measurement (sine bar), comparators, LVDT, and surface roughness parameters ($R_a$, $R_z$).

2. Quick Reference Sheet

📌 Fundamental Tolerance Unit ($i$)
i = 0.45 \sqrt[3]{D} + 0.001 D \quad (\mu\text{m})
$D = \sqrt{D_1 \cdot D_2}$ geometric mean diameter of size range step (mm)
📌 Standard Tolerance IT Grades
\text{IT6} = 10i, \quad \text{IT7} = 16i, \quad \text{IT8} = 25i, \quad \text{IT9} = 40i, \quad \text{IT10} = 64i
📌 Clearances & Interferences
\text{Min Clearance} = LLH - HLS, \quad \text{Max Clearance} = HLH - LLS
LLH: Lower Limit Hole | HLH: Higher Limit Hole | LLS: Lower Limit Shaft | HLS: Higher Limit Shaft
📌 Sine Bar Angular Measurement
\sin\theta = \frac{h}{L} \implies h = L \sin\theta
$h$: slip gauge stack height | $L$: distance between roller centers
📌 Least Count Formulas
\text{LC (Vernier)} = 1\text{ MSD} - 1\text{ VSD}, \quad \text{LC (Micrometer)} = \frac{\text{Pitch}}{\text{Number of thimble divisions}}
📌 Surface Roughness $R_a$ & Best Wire Size
R_a = \frac{1}{L} \int_0^L |y|\,dx, \quad d = \frac{P}{2}\sec\frac{\alpha}{2}
$P$: thread pitch | $\alpha$: thread semi-angle

3. Main Body - Detailed Topic Breakdown

3.1 Limits, Fits, Tolerances and Allowance

  • Tolerance: Total permissible variation in size ($\text{Upper Limit} - \text{Lower Limit}$).
  • Allowance: Intentional difference between mating parts (minimum clearance or maximum interference).
  • Hole-Basis System: Basic size is lower limit of hole ($H$-hole lower deviation $= 0$). Most widely used.
  • Shaft-Basis System: Basic size is upper limit of shaft ($h$-shaft upper deviation $= 0$).

3.2 Taylor's Principle of Limit Gauge Design

🎯 Taylor's Principle Rules
  • GO Gauge: Checks maximum material condition (MMC). Must check all related dimensions (length, roundness) simultaneously.
  • NO-GO Gauge: Checks least material condition (LMC). Checks only one specific dimension at a time.
  • Wear Allowance: Provided only on the GO gauge since it constantly enters workpieces.

3.3 Linear & Angular Measurement (Sine Bar & Slip Gauges)

Slip gauges (Gauge blocks) are end standards joined by wringing (molecular attraction). Sine bars set angles using $\sin\theta = h/L$. Autocollimators measure very small angular tilt via optical reflection.

3.4 Surface Roughness & Precision Instruments

⚠️ Surface Roughness Trap
Higher $R_a$ value indicates a rougher surface, while a lower $R_a$ value indicates a smoother finish.

4. NC & CNC Automation

💡 Essential G-Codes & M-Codes Master List
  • G00: Rapid positioning (non-cutting motion)

  • G02: Circular interpolation Clockwise (CW)

  • G90 / G91: Absolute / Incremental positioning mode
  • M03 / M04 / M05: Spindle ON CW / Spindle ON CCW / Spindle STOP

  • M30: End of program and rewind

5. Jigs & Fixtures

Covers differences between jigs and fixtures, 3-2-1 principle of location, clamping devices, drill bushes, and indexing mechanisms.

🎯 Jig vs Fixture Core Difference
  • Jig: Holds and locates the workpiece AND guides the cutting tool (e.g., drill jig). Usually light, not bolted to table.
  • Fixture: Holds and locates the workpiece but DOES NOT guide the cutting tool. Heavy, rigidly clamped/bolted to machine table.
📌 3-2-1 Principle of Location
\text{Primary (3 pins)} \implies 5 \text{ DOF restricted} \quad | \quad \text{Secondary (2 pins)} \implies 3 \text{ DOF} \quad | \quad \text{Tertiary (1 pin)} \implies 1 \text{ DOF}
Restricts 9 degrees of freedom out of 12 total 3D degrees of freedom.

6. Non-Traditional Machining (EDM / ECM / USM)

Covers Electric Discharge Machining (EDM), Electrochemical Machining (ECM), Ultrasonic Machining (USM), Abrasive Jet Machining (AJM), Laser Beam Machining (LBM), and Plasma Arc Machining (PAM).

📌 Faraday's Law of ECM Material Removal Rate
\text{MRR} = \frac{A \cdot I}{z \cdot F} \quad (\text{g/s})
$A$: atomic weight | $I$: current (A) | $z$: valency | $F$: Faraday's constant (96,500 C/g-eq)
EDM (Electric Discharge)
• Mechanism: Thermal spark erosion
• Material: Conductive materials only
• Dielectric: Kerosene / Deionized water
ECM (Electrochemical)
• Mechanism: Anodic dissolution (Faraday's Law)
• Tool Wear: Zero tool wear
• Electrolyte: NaCl / NaNO₃ aqueous solution
USM (Ultrasonic)
• Mechanism: Abrasive slurry micro-chipping
• Application: Hard & brittle non-conductors (Glass, Quartz)
