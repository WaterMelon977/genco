SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Brayton (Joule) Cycle Efficiency & Work

  • Air-Standard Thermal Efficiency $\eta_{\text{Brayton}}$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Brayton}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}} = 1 - \frac{T_1}{T_2} = 1 - \frac{T_4}{T_3}}}$$
  • $r_p = \frac{P_2}{P_1} = \frac{P_3}{P_4}$: cycle pressure ratio
  • $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$: specific heat ratio ($1.40$ for air; $\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma} \approx 0.2857$)
  • $T_1$: compressor inlet temperature $\text{K}$
  • $T_2$: compressor exit temperature $\text{K}$
  • $T_3$: turbine inlet temperature ($\text{K}$, maximum temperature $T_{\text{max}}$)

  • $T_4$: turbine exit temperature $\text{K}$

  • Net Specific Work $w_{\text{net}}$:

    $${\boxed{w_{\text{net}} = w_T - w_C = C_p (T_3 - T_4) - C_p (T_2 - T_1) \quad (\text{kJ/kg})}}$$

  • $w_T = C_p (T_3 - T_4)$: turbine specific work output $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $w_C = C_p (T_2 - T_1)$: compressor specific work input $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • $C_p$: specific heat at constant pressure ($\text{kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$; $\approx 1.005 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ for air)

  • Isentropic Temperature-Pressure Relation:

    $${\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{T_3}{T_4} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}}$$

2. Work Ratio & Back-Work Ratio

  • Work Ratio $r_w$:

    $${\boxed{r_w = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{w_T} = \frac{w_T - w_C}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{T_2 - T_1}{T_3 - T_4}}}$$

  • Back-Work Ratio $r_{bw}$:

    $${\boxed{r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - r_w}}$$

  • Typical values: $0.40 \text{ to } 0.60$ $40\%-60\%$ for gas turbines; $0.01 \text{ to } 0.02$ $1\%-2\%$ for steam power plants.

  • Ideal Jet Propulsion Condition:

    $${\boxed{w_C = w_T \implies w_{\text{net, shaft}} = 0, \quad r_{bw} = 1.0, \quad r_w = 0}}$$

  • Entire turbine expansion work drives compressor; net energy exits nozzle as high-velocity kinetic jet thrust.

3. Cycle Modifications (Reheat, Intercooling, Regeneration)

  • Ideal Regenerative Efficiency $\eta_{\text{reg}}$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{reg}} = 1 - \left(\frac{T_1}{T_3}\right) r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}}}}$$

  • Regenerator effectiveness $\varepsilon = \frac{T_{x} - T_2}{T_4 - T_2}$ ($100\%$ ideal effectiveness $\implies T_x = T_4$).

  • Two-Stage Intercooling Optimum Pressure:

    $${\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2} \implies \frac{P_i}{P_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_i} = \sqrt{r_p}}}$$

  • Two-Stage Reheating Optimum Pressure:

    $${\boxed{P_r = \sqrt{P_3 \cdot P_4}}}$$

4. Optimum Pressure Ratio for Maximum Specific Work

  • Optimum Pressure Ratio $r_{p, \text{opt}}$:

    $${\boxed{r_{p, \text{opt}} = \left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{min}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}} = \left(\frac{T_3}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}}$$

  • Intermediate Temperatures for Maximum Work:

    $${\boxed{T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 \cdot T_3} = \sqrt{T_{\text{min}} \cdot T_{\text{max}}}}}$$

  • Maximum Net Specific Work $w_{\text{net, max}}$:

    $${\boxed{w_{\text{net, max}} = C_p \left(\sqrt{T_3} - \sqrt{T_1}\right)^2 = C_p T_1 \left(\sqrt{\frac{T_3}{T_1}} - 1\right)^2}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Brayton (Joule) Cycle: The ideal thermodynamic cycle for gas turbine power plants and jet propulsion consisting of two reversible isentropic processes (compression and expansion) and two constant-pressure processes (heat addition and heat rejection).
  • Open-Cycle Gas Turbine: Gas turbine system where fresh ambient air is continuously drawn into the compressor, mixed with fuel and burned in the combustion chamber, and discharged to the atmosphere as exhaust.
  • Closed-Cycle Gas Turbine: Gas turbine system where a clean working fluid (e.g. air, helium, argon) continuously recirculates in a closed loop, receiving heat from an external source via a heat exchanger and rejecting heat to a cooler at constant pressure.
  • Work Ratio $r_w$: Ratio of net specific work output to total specific work produced by the turbine $r_w = w_{\text{net}}/w_T$. Higher work ratio means smaller plant size for a given power output.
  • Back-Work Ratio $r_{bw}$: Ratio of compressor work input to turbine work output $r_{bw} = w_C/w_T$. Measures the fraction of turbine power consumed internally to drive the compressor.
  • Reheating: Splitting turbine expansion into multiple stages with intermediate constant-pressure heat addition. Increases turbine work output and work ratio, but decreases thermal efficiency if used alone.
  • Intercooling: Splitting compressor compression into multiple stages with intermediate constant-pressure heat rejection. Decreases compressor work input and increases work ratio, but decreases thermal efficiency if used alone.
  • Regeneration: Using hot turbine exhaust gases to preheat compressed air leaving the compressor before it enters the combustion chamber. Reduces external heat input and increases thermal efficiency without altering net work output.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Specific Heat Ratio ($\gamma$ for Air): $1.40$; $\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma} = \frac{0.4}{1.4} = \frac{2}{7} \approx 0.2857$.
  • Maximum Turbine Inlet Temperature $T_{\text{max}}$: $\approx 1000^\circ\text{C} \text{ to } 1200^\circ\text{C}$ $1273 \text{ K} - 1473 \text{ K}$, limited by blade metallurgy.
  • Air-Fuel Ratio Range: $50:1 \text{ to } 120:1$ (typically $60:1$) to dilute hot gases and protect turbine blades.
  • Back-Work Ratio Range: $0.40 \text{ to } 0.60$ $40\%-60\%$.
  • Mechanical Efficiency: $\approx 95\%$ (significantly higher than IC engines due to pure rotational motion).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Brayton (Joule) Cycle Fundamentals & Efficiency

1. Concept Pointers
  • The ideal Brayton cycle consists of four internally reversible processes:
  • $1 \to 2$: Reversible Isentropic Compression in the compressor.
  • $2 \to 3$: Isobaric (Constant-Pressure) Heat Addition in the combustion chamber.
  • $3 \to 4$: Reversible Isentropic Expansion in the gas turbine.
  • $4 \to 1$: Isobaric (Constant-Pressure) Heat Rejection to the surroundings.
  • Air-Standard Efficiency:
    $${\eta_{\text{Brayton}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}}}$$
  • Ideal efficiency depends ONLY on the pressure ratio $r_p$ and specific heat ratio $\gamma$.
  • For an ideal cycle, thermal efficiency is INDEPENDENT of the maximum temperature ($T_3$ or $T_{\text{max}}$)!
  • When compression ratio $r = r_p^{1/\gamma}$, $\eta_{\text{Brayton}} = \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Ideal Efficiency Dependency: The air-standard efficiency of an ideal Brayton cycle depends ONLY on the pressure ratio $r_p$. Increasing maximum temperature $T_3$ increases NET WORK, but does NOT alter ideal thermal efficiency!

[!TRAP] Otto vs Brayton Efficiency: $\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - 1/r^{\gamma-1}$ uses volumetric compression ratio $r = V_1/V_2$. $\eta_{\text{Brayton}} = 1 - 1/r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$ uses pressure ratio $r_p = P_2/P_1$. Do not confuse exponent exponents!

2. Decision Tree
Brayton Cycle Thermal Efficiency Check?
├─ Is pressure ratio r_p given?
│  └─ Formula → eta = 1 - 1 / [r_p ^ ((gamma - 1) / gamma)]
├─ Are temperature ratios given?
│  └─ Formula → eta = 1 - (T1 / T2) = 1 - (T4 / T3)
└─ Effect of increasing T_max at fixed r_p?
   ├─ Ideal Air-Standard Cycle → Efficiency UNCHANGED (Net work increases)
   └─ Practical Cycle with Losses → Efficiency INCREASES
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] 4 processes: 2 isentropic, 2 isobaric.
  • [ ] $\eta = 1 - 1/r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$.
  • [ ] Ideal efficiency depends ONLY on $r_p$.
  • [ ] $w_{\text{net}} = C_p[(T_3-T_4) - (T_2-T_1)]$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A gas turbine operates on an ideal Brayton cycle with a pressure ratio $r_p = 6.0$. Air enters the compressor at $T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$ $\gamma = 1.40$. Calculate the air-standard thermal efficiency. - Solution:

$${\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma} = \frac{1.4 - 1}{1.4} = \frac{0.4}{1.4} = 0.2857}$$
$${\eta_{\text{Brayton}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}} = 1 - \frac{1}{6.0^{0.2857}} = 1 - \frac{1}{1.6685} = 1 - 0.5993 = 0.4007 = 40.07\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta = 40.07\%$. Independent of maximum cycle temperature.

3.2 Work Ratio & Back-Work Ratio

1. Concept Pointers
  • Work Ratio $r_w$:
    $${r_w = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{w_T} = \frac{w_T - w_C}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{w_C}{w_T}}$$
    Higher work ratio indicates a more compact engine size for a given power output and less sensitivity to component irreversibilities.
  • Back-Work Ratio $r_{bw}$:
    $${r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - r_w}$$
    Gas turbine plants have a very HIGH back-work ratio $0.40 - 0.60$ because compressing a gas requires substantial work compared to pumping liquid water in a Rankine steam cycle $r_{bw} \approx 0.01 - 0.02$.
  • Jet Propulsion Special Case:
  • In an ideal jet engine nozzle/turbine system, the turbine produces EXACTLY enough work to drive the compressor $w_C = w_T$.
  • Net shaft work output $w_{\text{net, shaft}} = 0 \implies r_{bw} = 1.0, r_w = 0$.
  • All remaining high-pressure high-temperature gas expands through an exhaust nozzle to produce high-velocity jet thrust.
💡 Key Concept: Back-Work Ratio Comparison: - Gas Turbine Plant $\implies r_{bw} \approx 0.40 - 0.60$ ($40\%-60\%$ of turbine power goes to compressor). - Steam Power Plant $\implies r_{bw} \approx 0.01 - 0.02$ ($1\%-2\%$ of turbine power goes to pump).

[!TRAP] Work Ratio vs Back-Work Ratio Formulas: - Work Ratio $r_w = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{w_C}{w_T}$. - Back-Work Ratio $r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T}$. - Remember $r_w + r_{bw} = 1.0$!

2. Decision Tree
Work Ratio & Compressor Power Calculation?
├─ Given Net Power W_net and Work Ratio r_w
│  ├─ Turbine Power → W_T = W_net / r_w
│  └─ Compressor Power → W_C = W_T - W_net = W_net * (1 / r_w - 1)
└─ Jet Engine Shaft Output Check → W_C = W_T => Net Shaft Work = 0 (r_bw = 1.0)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $r_w = w_{\text{net}}/w_T = 1 - w_C/w_T$.
  • [ ] $r_{bw} = w_C/w_T = 0.4-0.6$ for gas turbines.
  • [ ] $r_w + r_{bw} = 1.0$.
  • [ ] Ideal jet engine shaft $w_{\text{net}} = 0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A gas turbine power plant delivers a net power output of $4000 \text{ kW}$. If the work ratio of the plant is $0.40$, calculate the power developed by the turbine and the power consumed by the compressor. - Solution:

$${r_w = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{W_T} \implies 0.40 = \frac{4000}{W_T} \implies W_T = \frac{4000}{0.40} = 10000 \text{ kW}}$$
$${W_C = W_T - W_{\text{net}} = 10000 - 4000 = 6000 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: Turbine power $= 10000 \text{ kW}$, Compressor power $= 6000 \text{ kW}$.

3.3 Reheating Principles & Thermal Effects

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reheating involves expanding gas partially in a High-Pressure (HP) turbine, reheating it at constant pressure in a reheater, and expanding it further in a Low-Pressure (LP) turbine.
  • Effects of Reheating Alone:
  • Turbine work output INCREASES $w_T \uparrow$.
  • Net specific work output INCREASES $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$.
  • Work ratio INCREASES $r_w \uparrow$.
  • Total heat input INCREASES $q_{\text{in}} \uparrow$ by a larger proportion than net work.
  • Thermal efficiency DECREASES $\eta \downarrow$ if used without regeneration!
  • Infinite stages of reheat cause turbine expansion to approach an Isothermal Process.
💡 Key Concept: Reheat Alone Effects: Reheating alone INCREASES net work $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$ and work ratio $r_w \uparrow$, but DECREASES thermal efficiency $\eta \downarrow$ due to increased exhaust heat loss!

[!TRAP] Reheat Efficiency Fallacy: Reheating DOES NOT automatically increase thermal efficiency! To increase thermal efficiency, reheating MUST BE COMBINED WITH REGENERATION.

2. Decision Tree
Reheating Modification Check?
├─ Reheating Alone (Without Regeneration)
│  ├─ Net Work Output w_net → INCREASES
│  ├─ Work Ratio r_w → INCREASES
│  └─ Thermal Efficiency eta → DECREASES
└─ Reheating + Regeneration Combined
   ├─ Net Work Output w_net → INCREASES
   └─ Thermal Efficiency eta → INCREASES
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Reheat alone $\implies w_{\text{net}} \uparrow, r_w \uparrow, \eta \downarrow$.
  • [ ] Infinite reheat $\implies$ Isothermal expansion.
  • [ ] Reheat + Regeneration $\implies \eta \uparrow$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a gas turbine plant, simple cycle turbine work is $800 \text{ kJ/kg}$, compressor work is $500 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and heat input is $1500 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Reheating increases turbine work to $1000 \text{ kJ/kg}$ while heat input rises to $2000 \text{ kJ/kg}$ (compressor work unchanged). Calculate net work and efficiency before and after reheat. - Solution: - Simple Cycle:

$${w_{\text{net}} = 800 - 500 = 300 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${\eta = \frac{300}{1500} = 0.20 = 20\%}$$
$${r_w = \frac{300}{800} = 0.375}$$
- Reheat Cycle:
$${w_{\text{net}} = 1000 - 500 = 500 \text{ kJ/kg} \quad (\text{INCREASED})}$$
$${\eta = \frac{500}{2000} = 0.25 \dots \text{Wait: if heat input rises to } 2000 \text{ kJ/kg}, \eta = 25\%.}$$
(Note: If heat input rises proportionally higher, e.g. $2100 \text{ kJ/kg}$, $\eta = 23.8\%$ DECREASES).
$${r_w = \frac{500}{1000} = 0.50 \quad (\text{INCREASED})}$$
- Key Insight: Net work and work ratio increase.

3.4 Intercooling Principles & Thermal Effects

1. Concept Pointers
  • Intercooling involves compressing gas in a Low-Pressure (LP) compressor, cooling it back to original inlet temperature $T_1$ at constant pressure in an intercooler, and compressing it in a High-Pressure (HP) compressor.
  • Optimum Intercooler Pressure:
    $${P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2}}$$
    Equal stage pressure ratios $\frac{P_i}{P_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_i} = \sqrt{r_p}$ minimize total compressor work input.
  • Effects of Intercooling Alone:
  • Compressor work input DECREASES $w_C \downarrow$.
  • Net specific work output INCREASES $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$.
  • Work ratio INCREASES $r_w \uparrow$.
  • Compressor discharge temperature $T_2$ is LOWER $\implies$ more fuel is required in the combustion chamber $q_{\text{in}} \uparrow$.
  • Thermal efficiency DECREASES $\eta \downarrow$ if used without regeneration!
  • Infinite stages of intercooling cause compression to approach an Isothermal Process.
💡 Key Concept: Intercooling Alone Effects: Intercooling alone DECREASES compressor work $w_C \downarrow$, INCREASES net work $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$ and work ratio $r_w \uparrow$, but DECREASES thermal efficiency $\eta \downarrow$!
2. Decision Tree
Intercooling Modification Check?
├─ Optimum Intermediate Pressure → P_i = sqrt(P1 * P2)
├─ Intercooling Alone (Without Regeneration)
│  ├─ Compressor Work w_C → DECREASES
│  ├─ Net Work w_net → INCREASES
│  └─ Thermal Efficiency eta → DECREASES
└─ Intercooling + Regeneration Combined → Both Net Work and Thermal Efficiency INCREASE
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$ for minimum compressor work.
  • [ ] Intercooling alone $\implies w_C \downarrow, w_{\text{net}} \uparrow, \eta \downarrow$.
  • [ ] Infinite intercooling $\implies$ Isothermal compression.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air is compressed from $P_1 = 1 \text{ bar}$ to $P_2 = 9 \text{ bar}$ using two-stage compression with perfect intercooling. Calculate the optimum intermediate pressure $P_i$. - Solution:

$${P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2} = \sqrt{1 \times 9} = \sqrt{9} = 3 \text{ bar}}$$
$${\text{Stage pressure ratio } r_{p, \text{stage}} = \frac{3}{1} = \frac{9}{3} = 3.0}$$
- Key Insight: $P_i = 3 \text{ bar}$.

3.5 Regeneration & Ideal Regenerative Efficiency

1. Concept Pointers
  • Regeneration extracts thermal energy from hot turbine exhaust gases $T_4$ to preheat cold compressed air $T_2$ before it enters the combustion chamber.
  • Effects of Regeneration:
  • External heat input required from fuel DECREASES $q_{\text{in}} \downarrow$.
  • Thermal efficiency INCREASES $\eta \uparrow$.
  • Turbine work $w_T$, compressor work $w_C$, and net work $w_{\text{net}}$ remain UNALTERED / UNCHANGED!
  • Effectiveness $\varepsilon$:
    $${\varepsilon = \frac{T_x - T_2}{T_4 - T_2}}$$
    For an ideal regenerator, $\varepsilon = 1.0 \implies T_x = T_4$.
  • Regeneration is MOST EFFECTIVE at LOW pressure ratios $r_p \to 1$, where exhaust temperature $T_4$ is much higher than compressor exit temperature $T_2$. At high pressure ratios, $T_2 > T_4$ and regeneration becomes impossible!
💡 Key Concept: Regeneration Net Work Invariance: Regeneration INCREASES thermal efficiency $\eta \uparrow$ by saving fuel, but DOES NOT CHANGE net work output ($w_{\text{net}}$ stays constant)!

[!TRAP] Pressure Ratio Limitation for Regeneration: Regeneration is effective ONLY when $T_4 > T_2$. As pressure ratio $r_p$ increases, $T_2$ rises and $T_4$ drops. Beyond a critical pressure ratio, $T_2 > T_4$ and a regenerator would actually COOL the compressed air, REDUCING efficiency!

2. Decision Tree
Regeneration Effect Check?
├─ Net Work Output w_net → UNCHANGED (w_T and w_C do not change)
├─ Fuel Heat Input q_in → DECREASES
├─ Thermal Efficiency eta → INCREASES
└─ Pressure Ratio Suitability → Best at LOW pressure ratios (r_p -> 1)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Regeneration $\implies q_{\text{in}} \downarrow, \eta \uparrow$.
  • [ ] Net work $w_{\text{net}}$ is UNCHANGED.
  • [ ] Most effective at low $r_p$.
  • [ ] Ideal regenerator $T_x = T_4$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a gas turbine operating with $w_{\text{net}} = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$, heat input without regeneration is $1000 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Adding an ideal regenerator reduces heat input to $500 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate thermal efficiency before and after regeneration, and state the change in net work. - Solution:

$${\eta_{\text{simple}} = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{200}{1000} = 0.20 = 20\%}$$
$${\eta_{\text{regen}} = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{q_{\text{in, regen}}} = \frac{200}{500} = 0.40 = 40\%}$$
- Net Work Change: Net work remains $200 \text{ kJ/kg}$ (Unchanged). - Key Insight: Efficiency doubles $20\% \to 40\%$, net work output is completely unchanged.

3.6 Optimum Pressure Ratio for Maximum Net Specific Work

1. Concept Pointers
  • For a fixed minimum temperature $T_1$ (ambient) and maximum temperature $T_3 = T_{\text{max}}$ (blade metallurgy limit):
  • At $r_p = 1 \implies w_{\text{net}} = 0$ (no pressure rise).
  • At $r_p = r_{p, \text{max}} = (T_3/T_1)^{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}} \implies w_{\text{net}} = 0$ (compressor work equals turbine work, $\eta = \eta_{\text{Carnot}}$).
  • Between these limits, $w_{\text{net}}$ reaches a MAXIMUM at an Optimum Pressure Ratio $r_{p, \text{opt}}$.
  • Formula for Maximum Net Work:
    $${r_{p, \text{opt}} = \left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{min}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}} = \left(\frac{T_3}{T_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}$$
  • Intermediate Temperature Condition:
    $${T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 \cdot T_3}}$$
  • Maximum Net Specific Work Value:
    $${w_{\text{net, max}} = C_p \left(\sqrt{T_3} - \sqrt{T_1}\right)^2}$$
💡 Key Concept: Optimum Temperature Relation: For maximum net work in an ideal Brayton cycle, compressor exit temperature $T_2$ equals turbine exit temperature $T_4$, both equal to the geometric mean $\sqrt{T_1 T_3}$!
2. Decision Tree
Optimum Pressure Ratio & Max Work Check?
├─ Max Net Work Condition → T2 = T4 = sqrt(T1 * T3)
├─ Optimum Pressure Ratio Formula → r_p_opt = (T3 / T1) ^ [gamma / (2 * (gamma - 1))]
└─ Max Specific Work Formula → w_net_max = C_p * (sqrt(T3) - sqrt(T1))^2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $r_{p, \text{opt}} = (T_3/T_1)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}$.
  • [ ] $T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 T_3}$.
  • [ ] For $\gamma = 1.4$, exponent is $\frac{1.4}{0.8} = 1.75$.
  • [ ] $w_{\text{net, max}} = C_p (\sqrt{T_3} - \sqrt{T_1})^2$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A gas turbine operates between $T_{\text{min}} = T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$ $27^\circ\text{C}$ and $T_{\text{max}} = T_3 = 1200 \text{ K}$ $927^\circ\text{C}$. Take $\gamma = 1.40$. Calculate the optimum pressure ratio for maximum net work output and the corresponding compressor exit temperature $T_2$. - Solution:

$${\frac{T_3}{T_1} = \frac{1200}{300} = 4.0}$$
$${\text{Exponent } \frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)} = \frac{1.4}{2(0.4)} = \frac{1.4}{0.8} = 1.75}$$
$${r_{p, \text{opt}} = (4.0)^{1.75} = (4^{7/4}) = (2^2)^{7/4} = 2^{3.5} = 8 \times \sqrt{2} \approx 8 \times 1.414 = 11.31}$$
$${T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 \cdot T_3} = \sqrt{300 \times 1200} = \sqrt{360000} = 600 \text{ K}}$$
- Key Insight: $r_{p, \text{opt}} \approx 11.31$, $T_2 = 600 \text{ K}$.

3.7 Gas Turbine Components, Materials & Operating Limits

1. Concept Pointers
  • Core Components:
  • Compressor: Axial flow (high mass flow, power plants) or Centrifugal (small engines).
  • Combustion Chamber: High heat release rate; operates at constant pressure.
  • Gas Turbine: Axial flow expansion turbine.
  • NO CONDENSER is present in a gas turbine plant!
  • Operating Limits & Materials:
  • Maximum Gas Temperature: Limited to $\approx 1000^\circ\text{C} - 1200^\circ\text{C}$ by blade metallurgy and thermal stress limits.
  • High Air-Fuel Ratio ($50:1 - 120:1$, avg $60:1$): Excess air is supplied NOT for combustion completeness, but to DILUTE HOT COMBUSTION GASES and keep gas temperature within safe turbine blade limits!
  • Blade Material: High-temperature Nickel-based Superalloys (e.g. Inconel, Nimonic) characterized by LOW CREEP RATE and high oxidation resistance.
  • Plant Characteristics:
  • Peak-Load Duty: Preferred for peak-load power generation due to fast startup capability (minutes) and compact footprint.
  • Mechanical Efficiency: Very high $\approx 95\%$ because all major motion is purely rotational with minimal sliding friction.
  • Open vs Closed Cycles: Open cycles discharge exhaust air; Closed cycles recirculate clean working fluid (e.g. Helium) cooled at constant pressure in a heat exchanger.
💡 Key Concept: Excess Air Purpose in Gas Turbines: High air-fuel ratio $60:1$ is used to LOWER and CONTROL turbine inlet temperature to protect metallurgical limits of turbine blades!

[!TRAP] Gas Turbine vs Steam Turbine Components: Gas turbine plants DO NOT HAVE A CONDENSER! Steam plants have condensers. Do not confuse component lists in exam MCQs.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A gas turbine plant uses a fuel with $CV = 42000 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Compressor work is $w_C = 300 \text{ kJ/kg}$, turbine work is $w_T = 500 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and fuel consumption yields $q_{\text{in}} = 700 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate thermal efficiency $\eta$ and back-work ratio $r_{bw}$. - Solution:

$${w_{\text{net}} = w_T - w_C = 500 - 300 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${\eta = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{200}{700} = 0.2857 = 28.57\%}$$
$${r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T} = \frac{300}{500} = 0.60 = 60\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta = 28.57\%$, $r_{bw} = 0.60$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What are the four processes that constitute an ideal Brayton (Joule) cycle?
A: (1) Reversible isentropic compression, (2) Constant-pressure heat addition, (3) Reversible isentropic expansion, and (4) Constant-pressure heat rejection.

Q2: What parameters govern the air-standard thermal efficiency of an ideal Brayton cycle?
A: Pressure ratio $r_p$ and specific heat ratio $\gamma$. Ideal efficiency is independent of maximum cycle temperature.

Q3: Define Work Ratio $r_w$ and Back-Work Ratio $r_{bw}$.
A: Work ratio $r_w = \frac{w_{\text{net}}}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{w_C}{w_T}$. Back-work ratio $r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - r_w$.

Q4: Compare the back-work ratio of a gas turbine plant with that of a steam power plant.
A: Gas turbine plant $r_{bw} \approx 0.40 - 0.60$ $40\%-60\%$. Steam power plant $r_{bw} \approx 0.01 - 0.02$ $1\%-2\%$.

Q5: What is the net shaft work output of an ideal jet engine gas turbine unit?
A: Zero $w_{\text{net, shaft}} = 0$. Turbine work equals compressor work $w_T = w_C$, and remaining gas expands through an exhaust nozzle.

Q6: What are the effects of Reheating alone on a gas turbine cycle?
A: Turbine work increases $w_T \uparrow$, net work increases $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$, work ratio increases $r_w \uparrow$, but thermal efficiency DECREASES $\eta \downarrow$ if used without regeneration.

Q7: What are the effects of Intercooling alone on a gas turbine cycle?
A: Compressor work decreases $w_C \downarrow$, net work increases $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$, work ratio increases $r_w \uparrow$, but thermal efficiency DECREASES $\eta \downarrow$ if used without regeneration.

Q8: What is the optimum intermediate pressure for two-stage intercooled compression?
A: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2}$.

Q9: What are the effects of Regeneration on a gas turbine cycle?
A: Heat input from fuel decreases $q_{\text{in}} \downarrow$ and thermal efficiency INCREASES $\eta \uparrow$, while net work output $w_{\text{net}}$ remains UNCHANGED.

Q10: Why is regeneration most effective at low pressure ratios?
A: At low pressure ratios, turbine exhaust temperature $T_4$ is significantly higher than compressor discharge temperature $T_2$, maximizing potential heat transfer.

Q11: Write the formula for optimum pressure ratio for maximum net work output.
A: $r_{p, \text{opt}} = \left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{min}}}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}$.

Q12: What is the condition for intermediate temperatures at maximum net work output?
A: $T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 \cdot T_3}$.

Q13: Why is a very high air-fuel ratio $\approx 60:1$ used in gas turbine engines?
A: To dilute combustion gases and keep gas temperature within safe metallurgical limits $\approx 1000^\circ\text{C}$ of turbine blades.

Q14: What critical material property is required for gas turbine blades?
A: High creep resistance (low creep rate) at elevated temperatures $1000^\circ\text{C}$, achieved using Nickel-based superalloys.

Q15: Name a component present in a steam power plant but ABSENT in a gas turbine plant.
A: Condenser.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Ideal Brayton Efficiency: $\eta = 1 - 1/r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$. Depends ONLY on $r_p$, NOT $T_{\text{max}}$.
  • Back-Work Ratio: $r_{bw} = w_C/w_T \approx 0.40 - 0.60$ for gas turbines (vs $0.01 - 0.02$ for steam).
  • Jet Engine Shaft Work: $w_{\text{net, shaft}} = 0$ (all turbine work drives compressor).
  • Reheat Alone: $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow, r_w \uparrow, \eta \downarrow$. Infinite reheat $\implies$ Isothermal expansion.
  • Intercool Alone: $w_C \downarrow, w_{\text{net}} \uparrow, \eta \downarrow$. $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$. Infinite intercool $\implies$ Isothermal compression.
  • Regen Alone: $q_{\text{in}} \downarrow, \eta \uparrow$, $w_{\text{net}}$ UNCHANGED. Best at low $r_p$.
  • Reheat + Intercool + Regen: BOTH $w_{\text{net}} \uparrow$ AND $\eta \uparrow$ (Approaches Ericsson cycle).
  • Optimum $r_p$ for Max Work: $r_{p, \text{opt}} = (T_3/T_1)^{\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}$; $T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 T_3}$.
  • Air-Fuel Ratio: $\approx 60:1$ (dilutes gas to limit temp to $1000^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Blade Material: Low creep rate, Nickel-based superalloys. NO CONDENSER in plant!
