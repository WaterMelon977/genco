1. Impulse vs Reaction — Classification & Degree of Reaction

All steam turbines convert the enthalpy of high-pressure steam into shaft work, but they differ fundamentally in where the pressure drop (and hence enthalpy conversion) takes place within a stage.

In a pure impulse turbine, steam pressure drops entirely in the fixed nozzles. By the time steam enters the moving rotor blades, it is already at the stage exit pressure. The rotor blades simply deflect the high-velocity jet — they act as buckets, not as nozzles. There is no pressure drop across the rotor, and consequently no tendency for steam to leak across blade tips due to a pressure differential.

In a reaction turbine, steam pressure drops continuously across both the fixed guide blades (stator) and the moving rotor blades. The rotor blades are shaped like nozzles — steam accelerates as it passes through them, and the reaction to this acceleration drives the rotor. Because a pressure differential exists across the rotor, tip leakage is significant, and reaction turbines require close clearances and are not suitable for very high-pressure stages without careful sealing.

Degree of Reaction

$$\boxed{R = \frac{\Delta h_m}{\Delta h_f + \Delta h_m}}$$
$\Delta h_m$ — isentropic enthalpy drop in moving (rotor) blades (kJ/kg) $\Delta h_f$ — isentropic enthalpy drop in fixed (stator/nozzle) blades (kJ/kg) $\Delta h_f + \Delta h_m$ — total stage isentropic enthalpy drop
Turbine Type$R$Pressure Drop Location
Pure impulse0Fixed nozzles only; $\Delta h_m = 0$
Parson's reaction0.50Equal split: $\Delta h_m = \Delta h_f$
Pure reaction (Hero's)1.0Moving blades only; $\Delta h_f = 0$

2. Euler Work Equation & Stage Efficiency

The fundamental work output per kilogram of steam in any turbine stage is given by the Euler turbomachinery equation, derived from the angular momentum theorem.

$$\boxed{W = u\,(V_{w1} \pm V_{w2}) \quad \text{J/kg}}$$
$u$ — mean blade peripheral velocity (m/s) $= \pi D N / 60$ $V_{w1}$ — inlet whirl (tangential) component of absolute steam velocity (m/s) $V_{w2}$ — exit whirl component; use $+$ when $V_{w2}$ opposes $u$ (most cases)
$$P = \dot{m}\,u\,(V_{w1} + V_{w2}) \quad \text{W}$$
$\dot{m}$ — steam mass flow rate (kg/s)

Diagram (Blade) Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_b = \frac{2u(V_{w1}+V_{w2})}{V_1^2}}$$
$V_1$ — absolute steam velocity at blade inlet (m/s) $V_1^2/2$ — kinetic energy supplied per kg from nozzle

Stage Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_s = \eta_b \times \eta_N}$$
$\eta_N = V_1^2/(2\,\Delta h_\text{nozzle,isentropic})$ — nozzle efficiency Stage efficiency is the product, not the quotient, of blade and nozzle efficiencies

Steam Rate (Specific Steam Consumption)

$$\boxed{\text{Steam Rate} = \frac{3600}{W_\text{net}} \quad \text{kg/kWh}}$$
$W_\text{net}$ — net specific work output (kJ/kg)

3. Parson's 50% Reaction Turbine

Parson's turbine has $R = 0.50$, meaning the stage enthalpy drop is shared equally between fixed and moving blades. A defining geometric consequence is that the fixed and moving blade profiles are identical — the velocity triangles at inlet and exit are symmetrical about the blade mid-plane.

Optimum Blade Speed Ratio

$$\boxed{\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{u}{V_1} = \cos\alpha}$$
$\rho$ — blade speed ratio $\alpha$ — nozzle/fixed-blade discharge angle from the plane of rotation

Maximum Diagram Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_\text{max} = \frac{2\cos^2\alpha}{1+\cos^2\alpha}}$$

Minimum Exit Kinetic Energy Condition

Exit kinetic energy is minimised when absolute exit velocity $V_2$ is purely axial ($V_{w2} = 0$):

$$V_{w2} = 0 \implies V_2 = \frac{u}{\cos\phi}$$
$\phi$ — moving blade exit angle

4. Compounding of Steam Turbines

Compounding distributes the total enthalpy drop across multiple stages to lower rotor speeds to safe mechanical limits ($1500$–$3000$ rpm).

Simple Single-Stage Impulse Turbine

$$\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{u}{V_1} = \frac{1}{2}\cos\alpha \qquad \eta_\text{max} = \cos^2\alpha$$

Velocity Compounding — Curtis Turbine

One set of nozzles feeds steam into multiple rows of moving blades separated by fixed guide blades. Pressure drops only in the nozzle ring.

$$\boxed{\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{u}{V_1} = \frac{\cos\alpha}{2n}}$$
$n$ — number of moving blade rows $n=1$: $\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{1}{2}\cos\alpha$  |  $n=2$: $\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{1}{4}\cos\alpha$

Pressure Compounding — Rateau Turbine

Multiple independent impulse stages are placed in series, each with its own set of nozzles. Pressure drops in steps across each nozzle ring.

5. Reheat Factor & Exhaust Moisture Limit

$$\boxed{RF = \frac{\sum \text{Stage Isentropic Enthalpy Drops}}{\text{Overall Direct Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} \approx 1.02 \text{ to } 1.08}$$

Exhaust Moisture Limit

The maximum allowable moisture content at the last stage exit is:

$$\boxed{x_\text{exhaust} \ge 0.88 \quad \text{(maximum 12\% moisture)}}$$

6. Special Turbines, Governing & Critical Speed

Back-Pressure & Pass-Out Turbines

Back-pressure turbines exhaust all steam at high pressure for process heat (no condenser). Pass-out turbines extract partial steam for process heating while the rest continues expanding to a condenser.

Governing Methods

MethodMechanismPart-Load Behaviour
Throttle governingGovernor valve throttles inlet steam pressureSimple; throttling loss at part load
Nozzle control governingNozzle groups in first stage cut in/outBetter part-load efficiency than throttle
Bypass governingFresh HP steam bypasses early stagesUsed at overload conditions

Critical Speed

The rotational speed (rpm) at which shaft angular velocity equals its natural frequency of transverse vibration, producing resonance.

🔥 Quick-Fire Checklist — Steam Turbines
  • Impulse: $\Delta p$ in nozzles only, $R = 0$. Reaction: $\Delta p$ in both fixed and moving blades, $R > 0$.
  • $R = \Delta h_m / (\Delta h_f + \Delta h_m)$ — always moving drop in numerator.
  • $W = u(V_{w1} + V_{w2})$ J/kg; $P = \dot{m} \cdot u(V_{w1} + V_{w2})$ W.
  • $\eta_b = 2u(V_{w1}+V_{w2})/V_1^2$; $\eta_s = \eta_b \times \eta_N$ (product, not quotient).
  • Steam Rate $= 3600/W_\text{net}$ kg/kWh.
  • Parson's ($R = 0.50$): $\rho_\text{opt} = \cos\alpha$; $\eta_\text{max} = 2\cos^2\alpha/(1+\cos^2\alpha)$; identical fixed and moving blades.
  • Simple impulse: $\rho_\text{opt} = \frac{1}{2}\cos\alpha$; $\eta_\text{max} = \cos^2\alpha$.
  • Curtis (velocity compounding): $\rho_\text{opt} = \cos\alpha/(2n)$; one nozzle set, $n$ moving rows.
  • Rateau (pressure compounding): multiple independent impulse stages in series.
  • Reheat Factor $= \sum$stage isentropic drops / overall isentropic drop $\approx 1.02$–$1.08$; always $>1$.
  • Exhaust moisture limit: max 12% ($x \ge 0.88$); exceeded $\Rightarrow$ LP blade erosion.
  • Back-pressure turbine: all exhaust to process; no condenser.
  • Pass-out turbine: partial extraction for process; remainder to condenser.
  • Critical speed: shaft rpm = natural frequency of transverse vibration — resonance condition.
