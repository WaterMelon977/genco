1. Propulsive Efficiency

Propulsive efficiency measures how much of the kinetic energy imparted to the jet by the engine is actually converted into useful propulsive work on the vehicle. When jet velocity equals flight velocity, all jet energy would be used productively — but this corresponds to zero thrust, which is why the maximum is approached but never achieved in practice.

Air-Breathing Jet Engine

For a turbojet or ramjet ingesting ambient air and ejecting a higher-velocity exhaust:

$$\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2u}{u + v}}$$
$u$ — aircraft forward flight velocity (m/s) $v$ — jet exhaust velocity relative to the aircraft ($v > u$ for thrust) As $u \to v$: $\eta_p \to 1$ but thrust $F \to 0$
⚠️ Exam Trap — Jet Velocity Symbol: In $\eta_p = \frac{2u}{u+v}$, $v$ is the jet velocity relative to the aircraft, NOT absolute velocity.

Rocket Engine

A rocket carries its own oxidiser and does not ingest atmospheric air. The inlet air velocity is zero. Propulsive efficiency peaks to exactly $100\%$ when the rocket forward velocity equals the jet exhaust velocity:

$$\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2} = \frac{2r}{1+r^2}}$$
$V_f$ — rocket forward flight velocity (m/s) $V_e$ — effective jet exhaust velocity relative to rocket (m/s) $r = V_f/V_e$ — velocity ratio; maximum $\eta_p = 100\%$ at $r = 1$ ($V_f = V_e$)

Water-Jet Ship (Right-Angle Inlet)

Water is drawn in perpendicular to the ship's motion (at right angles) and ejected astern. The geometry of the right-angle inlet limits the theoretical maximum efficiency:

$$\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2Vu}{V^2 + u^2} \qquad \eta_{p,\text{max}} = 50\% \text{ (at } V = u\text{)}}$$
$V$ — absolute velocity of issuing water jet (m/s) $u$ — ship forward velocity (m/s)
⚠️ Exam Trap — Right-Angle Inlet Efficiency Limit: For a water-jet ship with right-angle inlet, maximum theoretical propulsive efficiency is 50% (at $V = u$).

2. Thrust Equations

Thrust is the net reaction force produced by the momentum change of the working fluid passing through the propulsion system, following Newton's second law.

Turbojet Thrust

$$\boxed{F = \dot{m}(V_\text{exit} - V_\text{inlet}) \quad \text{N}}$$
$\dot{m}$ — air mass flow rate (kg/s) $V_\text{exit}$ — exhaust jet velocity relative to engine (m/s) $V_\text{inlet}$ — inlet air velocity relative to engine $= u$ (m/s)

Rocket Thrust

The rocket has no inlet air ($V_\text{inlet} = 0$), and nozzle exit pressure contributes if not perfectly expanded:

$$\boxed{F = \dot{m}V_e + (P_e - P_0)A_e \quad \text{N}}$$
$V_e$ — exhaust velocity at nozzle exit (m/s) $P_e$ — exit static pressure (Pa);   $P_0$ — ambient pressure (Pa) $A_e$ — nozzle exit area (m²) Perfectly expanded nozzle ($P_e = P_0$): $F = \dot{m}V_e$

Thrust Coefficient

$$\boxed{C_F = \frac{F}{P_c \cdot A_t}}$$
$P_c$ — combustion chamber pressure (Pa) $A_t$ — nozzle throat area (m²) $C_F$ is a dimensionless measure of pressure conversion efficiency

💡 Key Concept — Turboprop Sequence: The turboprop component sequence is Propeller → Compressor → Burner → Turbine. Most turbine expansion work drives the propeller via a reduction gearbox.
⚠️ Exam Trap — Ramjet Zero Static Thrust: A Ramjet has NO COMPRESSOR. It cannot produce static thrust at rest ($M = 0$) and requires an auxiliary booster to reach operating speeds.

Turbojet Combustion Chamber Requirements

Must achieve complete combustion, maintain flame stability across operating altitudes, and minimize pressure drop. A high rate of combustion is NOT an essential requirement.

4. Rocket Propulsion & Specific Impulse

Rockets carry both fuel and oxidiser onboard, enabling operation in space vacuum.

Propellant Classification

TypeFuelOxidiserNotes
Liquid propellantLiquid fuel (kerosene, LH₂)Liquid oxidiser (LOX)High performance; throttleable
Solid propellantSolid fuel (powdered Al)Solid oxidiser (AP)Simple; storable; non-throttleable
HybridSolid fuel (LiH)Liquid oxidiser (ClF₃)Lithium Hydride + Chlorine Trifluoride

Specific Impulse

$$\boxed{I_{sp} = \frac{F}{\dot{m} \cdot g_0} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}} \quad \text{seconds}}$$
$g_0 = 9.81$ m/s² — gravitational acceleration SPC — Specific Propellant Consumption [kg/(N·s)] Higher $I_{sp}$ = superior propellant efficiency

5. Mach Number, Speed of Sound & Mach Angle

When an object moves through a fluid at supersonic speed ($M > 1$), pressure disturbances form a conical envelope called the Mach cone.

$$\boxed{\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{v} \implies \mu = \sin^{-1}\!\left(\frac{1}{M}\right)}$$
$\mu$ — Mach angle (half-angle of cone) $M = v/a$ — Mach number ($M > 1$) $a$ — local speed of sound (m/s);   $v$ — object velocity (m/s)

Speed of Sound

$$\boxed{a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} \quad \text{m/s}}$$
$\gamma = 1.40$ (air);   $R = 287$ J/kg·K;   $T$ — absolute temperature (K) Speed of sound order: Solid > Liquid > Gas ($a_\text{water} \approx 4\,a_\text{air}$)
💡 Key Concept — Mach Cone Zones: Inside the Mach cone is the Zone of Action (disturbances felt). Outside is the Zone of Silence (no disturbance reached yet).
🔥 Quick-Fire Checklist — Jet & Rocket Propulsion
  • Air-breathing jet: $\eta_p = 2u/(u+v)$; $v$ is jet velocity relative to aircraft.
  • Rocket: $\eta_p = 2V_fV_e/(V_e^2+V_f^2)$; max $\eta_p = 100\%$ at $V_f = V_e$.
  • Ship right-angle inlet: $\eta_p = 2Vu/(V^2+u^2)$; theoretical max $= 50\%$.
  • Turbojet thrust: $F = \dot{m}(V_\text{exit} - V_\text{inlet})$.
  • Rocket: $V_\text{inlet} = 0$; $F = \dot{m}V_e + (P_e - P_0)A_e$.
  • Thrust coefficient: $C_F = F/(P_c A_t)$.
  • Turbojet AFR $\approx 60{:}1$; turboprop sequence: Propeller → Compressor → Burner → Turbine.
  • Ramjet: no moving parts; zero static thrust at $M = 0$ (no compressor).
  • Pulsejet: intermittent combustion; 2-stroke engine analogue ($2400\text{--}2700$ rpm).
  • Hybrid rocket: solid LiH fuel + liquid ClF₃ oxidiser.
  • $I_{sp} = 1/\text{SPC}$ (seconds); higher $I_{sp}$ = better propellant efficiency.
  • Turbojet combustor: high rate of combustion is NOT required.
  • Mach angle: $\sin\mu = 1/M = a/v$; forms only when $M > 1$.
  • Zone of Silence: outside Mach cone. Zone of Action: inside Mach cone.
  • $a = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$; $a_\text{water} \approx 4\,a_\text{air}$; Speed order: Solid > Liquid > Gas.
