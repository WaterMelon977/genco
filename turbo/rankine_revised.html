1. Ideal Rankine Cycle

The Rankine cycle is the standard thermodynamic cycle for steam power plants. It consists of four processes: isentropic pumping of the saturated liquid from condenser pressure to boiler pressure (1→2), isobaric heat addition in the boiler (2→3), isentropic expansion in the turbine (3→4), and isobaric heat rejection in the condenser (4→1). Heat is rejected in the condenser, not in the open atmosphere.

Thermal Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_R = \frac{W_T - W_P}{Q_\text{in}} = \frac{(h_1-h_2)-(h_4-h_3)}{h_1-h_4}}$$
$h_1$ — turbine inlet enthalpy (kJ/kg) $h_2$ — turbine exit / condenser inlet enthalpy (kJ/kg) $h_3$ — condenser exit (saturated liquid) (kJ/kg) $h_4$ — pump exit / boiler inlet enthalpy (kJ/kg)

An equivalent form using the mean temperature of heat addition $T_m$ brings the expression closer to Carnot form:

$$\eta_R = 1 - \frac{T_\text{condenser}}{T_m} \qquad T_m = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{s_1 - s_4} \quad \text{K}$$
Increasing $T_m$ (by superheating or raising boiler pressure) increases $\eta_R$

Pump Work

Since the pump handles liquid water (nearly incompressible), pump work is very small compared to turbine output — typically $1$–$2\%$ of $W_T$:

$$\boxed{W_P = h_4 - h_3 \approx v_3(P_\text{boiler} - P_\text{condenser}) \times 10^2 \quad \text{kJ/kg}}$$
$v_3$ — specific volume of saturated liquid at condenser pressure (m³/kg) Pressures in bar; factor $10^2$ converts bar·m³/kg to kJ/kg

2. Rankine Cycle Efficiency Improvements

Superheating

Superheating raises steam temperature above saturation at constant pressure before it enters the turbine. It increases the mean temperature of heat addition $T_m$, raising cycle efficiency; and increases turbine exhaust dryness fraction $x$, reducing LP blade erosion.

$$\Delta T_\text{sup} = T_\text{superheated} - T_\text{sat}$$

Reheating

Steam is expanded through the HP turbine, reheated at constant pressure in the boiler, and expanded through the LP turbine. The primary objective is to increase the dryness fraction at the LP turbine exit beyond the erosion limit ($x \ge 0.88$).

$$\boxed{RF = \frac{\sum \text{Stage Isentropic Enthalpy Drops}}{\text{Overall Direct Isentropic Drop}} \approx 1.02 \text{ to } 1.08}$$

Regenerative Feedwater Heating

Steam is bled (extracted) from intermediate turbine stages to preheat boiler feedwater. Regeneration yields the highest thermal efficiency between fixed pressure limits. An open feedwater heater also acts as a deaerator, removing dissolved $\text{O}_2$ and $\text{CO}_2$ to prevent boiler corrosion.

Bled Steam Fraction

$$\boxed{y = \frac{h_f - h_\text{cond}}{h_\text{bleed} - h_\text{cond}}}$$
$y$ — mass fraction of steam bled per kg of total working fluid $h_f$ — enthalpy of saturated liquid leaving heater (kJ/kg) $h_\text{cond}$ — enthalpy of condensate entering heater (kJ/kg) $h_\text{bleed}$ — enthalpy of bled steam extracted from turbine (kJ/kg)

3. Modified Rankine Cycle (Reciprocating Steam Engines)

Applied to reciprocating steam engines, full expansion requires an impractically long piston stroke. The Modified Rankine cycle terminates expansion early by opening the exhaust valve prematurely, removing the "toe" of the indicator diagram. This sacrifices a small work area to achieve a huge reduction in cylinder stroke and volume.

5. Boiler Mountings vs Accessories

Mountings are mandatory safety and control devices fitted directly on the boiler drum (e.g., safety valves, water level gauge, fusible plug). The fusible plug is a mounting that melts if the water level drops dangerously, dousing the fire.

Accessories are optional efficiency boosters located in the flue gas path. Flue gas sequence:

$$\text{Furnace} \to \text{Superheater} \to \text{Economiser} \to \text{Air Preheater} \to \text{ID Fan} \to \text{Chimney}$$

7. Boiler Performance Parameters & Power Plant Factors

Equivalent Evaporation

$$\boxed{m_e = \frac{m_s\,(h - h_{f1})}{2257} \quad \text{kg/h}}$$
$m_s$ — actual steam generated (kg/h) $h$ — enthalpy of steam (kJ/kg);   $h_{f1}$ — enthalpy of feedwater (kJ/kg) $2257$ kJ/kg — latent heat of vaporisation at $100\,^\circ\text{C}$

Power Plant Load Factors

$$\text{Load Factor} = \frac{\text{Average Load}}{\text{Maximum Demand}} < 1.0$$
$$\text{Diversity Factor} = \frac{\sum \text{Individual Maximum Demands}}{\text{Maximum Demand on Station}} > 1.0$$
🔥 Quick-Fire Checklist — Rankine Cycle & Steam Systems
  • $\eta_R = (W_T - W_P)/Q_\text{in} = 1 - T_\text{condenser}/T_m$; heat rejection in condenser.
  • $W_P \approx v_3(P_\text{boiler} - P_\text{condenser}) \times 10^2$ kJ/kg; typically 1–2% of $W_T$.
  • $T_m = Q_\text{in}/(s_1 - s_4)$ K — mean temperature of heat addition.
  • Superheating: raises $T_m \Rightarrow \eta_R \uparrow$; raises exhaust $x \Rightarrow$ LP blade erosion $\downarrow$.
  • Reheating primary goal: maintain exhaust $x \ge 0.88$ (max 12% moisture).
  • Reheat Factor $\approx 1.02$–$1.08$; always $> 1$.
  • Regeneration: highest $\eta_R$ among Rankine modifications; open FWH = deaerator (removes $\text{O}_2/\text{CO}_2$).
  • Bled fraction: $y = (h_f - h_\text{cond})/(h_\text{bleed} - h_\text{cond})$.
  • Modified Rankine (reciprocating engines): early exhaust release $\Rightarrow$ reduction of piston stroke.
  • $1\,\text{kg C} \Rightarrow 7/3\,\text{kg CO}$ (incomplete combustion).
  • Coal rank ascending CV: Peat < Lignite < Bituminous < Anthracite ($\approx 36{,}000$ kJ/kg).
  • Bomb calorimeter: solid/liquid fuels, constant volume, measures HCV.
  • Fusible plug: boiler mounting (safety device), not an accessory.
  • Flue gas path: Superheater → Economiser → Air Preheater → Chimney.
  • Natural draught efficiency $\approx 0.25\%$.
  • Equivalent evaporation: $m_e = m_s(h - h_{f1})/2257$ kg/h; latent heat at $100\,^\circ\text{C} = 2257$ kJ/kg.
  • Load Factor $< 1.0$; Diversity Factor $> 1.0$.
