1. Brayton (Joule) Cycle — Fundamentals & Efficiency

The ideal gas turbine operates on the Brayton cycle: isentropic compression (1→2), constant-pressure heat addition (2→3), isentropic expansion (3→4), and constant-pressure heat rejection (4→1). All heat interactions occur at constant pressure — this distinguishes the Brayton cycle from Otto and Diesel cycles.

Air-Standard Thermal Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_\text{Brayton} = 1 - \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}} = 1 - \frac{T_1}{T_2} = 1 - \frac{T_4}{T_3}}$$
$r_p = P_2/P_1$ — cycle pressure ratio $\gamma = C_p/C_v$ (air: 1.40; $(\gamma-1)/\gamma = 2/7 \approx 0.2857$) $T_1$ — compressor inlet (K);   $T_3$ — turbine inlet (K, max temp)
⚠️ Exam Trap — Otto vs Brayton Exponent: $\eta_\text{Otto}$ uses volumetric compression ratio $r$ with exponent $(\gamma-1)$. $\eta_\text{Brayton}$ uses pressure ratio $r_p$ with exponent $(\gamma-1)/\gamma$. Do not swap the exponents!

Ideal efficiency depends only on $r_p$ and $\gamma$ — completely independent of $T_3$. Increasing $T_3$ at fixed $r_p$ increases net work but does not change ideal efficiency.

Net Specific Work

$$\boxed{w_\text{net} = C_p(T_3 - T_4) - C_p(T_2 - T_1) \quad \text{kJ/kg}}$$
$w_T = C_p(T_3-T_4)$ — turbine work (kJ/kg) $w_C = C_p(T_2-T_1)$ — compressor work (kJ/kg) $C_p \approx 1.005$ kJ/kg·K for air

Isentropic Temperature–Pressure Relation

$$\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \frac{T_3}{T_4} = r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$$
💡 Key Concept: When $r = r_p^{1/\gamma}$ is substituted, $\eta_\text{Brayton}$ reduces to the same form as $\eta_\text{Otto}$. This equivalence holds only for the ideal air-standard case.

2. Work Ratio & Back-Work Ratio

A gas turbine compressor handles gas throughout — unlike a steam plant pump. This makes the fraction of turbine output consumed internally very large.

$$\boxed{r_w = \frac{w_\text{net}}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - \frac{T_2-T_1}{T_3-T_4}}$$
$$\boxed{r_{bw} = \frac{w_C}{w_T} = 1 - r_w}$$
Gas turbine: $r_{bw} \approx 0.40$–$0.60$ (40–60%) Steam power plant: $r_{bw} \approx 0.01$–$0.02$ (1–2%)
⚠️ Exam Trap: $r_w + r_{bw} = 1.0$ always. They are complementary quantities.

Jet Propulsion Special Case

In a pure jet engine, turbine work is entirely consumed by the compressor — no shaft work exits. Remaining enthalpy becomes nozzle thrust.

$$w_C = w_T \implies w_\text{net,shaft} = 0, \quad r_{bw} = 1.0, \quad r_w = 0$$

3. Cycle Modifications

The simple Brayton cycle can be modified in three independent ways, targeting different weaknesses.

Reheating

Turbine expansion split into two stages with constant-pressure heat addition between them. Optimum intermediate pressure:

$$P_r = \sqrt{P_3 \cdot P_4}$$

Reheating alone: $w_T \uparrow$, $w_\text{net} \uparrow$, $r_w \uparrow$, but $\eta \downarrow$. Infinite reheat stages → isothermal expansion → Ericsson cycle.

⚠️ Exam Trap — Reheat Efficiency Fallacy: Reheating alone does NOT increase thermal efficiency. To boost $\eta$, reheat must be combined with regeneration.

Intercooling

Compression split into two stages with constant-pressure cooling between them. Optimum intermediate pressure:

$$\boxed{P_i = \sqrt{P_1 \cdot P_2} \implies \frac{P_i}{P_1} = \frac{P_2}{P_i} = \sqrt{r_p}}$$

Intercooling alone: $w_C \downarrow$, $w_\text{net} \uparrow$, $r_w \uparrow$, but $\eta \downarrow$ (cooler air requires more fuel).

Regeneration

Hot turbine exhaust ($T_4$) preheats compressed air ($T_2$) before combustion chamber. Reduces external heat input.

$$\boxed{\eta_\text{reg} = 1 - \left(\frac{T_1}{T_3}\right) r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma}}$$
$$\varepsilon = \frac{T_x - T_2}{T_4 - T_2} \quad (\text{ideal: } \varepsilon = 1, \; T_x = T_4)$$

Regeneration alone: $q_\text{in} \downarrow$, $\eta \uparrow$, but $w_\text{net}$ is completely unchanged. Most effective at low $r_p$.

⚠️ Exam Trap — High $r_p$ Regeneration: At high pressure ratios, $T_2 > T_4$ — a regenerator would cool the compressed air, reducing efficiency. Requires $T_4 > T_2$.

4. Optimum Pressure Ratio for Maximum Net Work

For fixed $T_1$ (ambient) and $T_3$ (metallurgical limit), net specific work peaks at an intermediate optimum pressure ratio.

$$\boxed{r_{p,\text{opt}} = \left(\frac{T_3}{T_1}\right)^{\!\frac{\gamma}{2(\gamma-1)}}}$$
For $\gamma = 1.4$: exponent $= 1.4/0.8 = 1.75$

At this optimum, compressor exit equals turbine exit temperature:

$$\boxed{T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 \cdot T_3}}$$

Maximum net specific work:

$$w_{\text{net,max}} = C_p\!\left(\sqrt{T_3} - \sqrt{T_1}\right)^2$$
💡 Key Concept: Optimum $r_p$ for maximum net work is generally lower than the $r_p$ that maximises thermal efficiency. Practical designs pick a value between the two.

5. Components, Materials & Operating Limits

Three core components: compressor (axial-flow for power plants, centrifugal for small engines), combustion chamber, gas turbine expander. There is no condenser — exhaust discharges to atmosphere (open cycle).

⚠️ Exam Trap — No Condenser: Gas turbine plants do NOT have a condenser. Steam plants do. Do not confuse component lists.

Maximum turbine inlet temperature $\approx 1000$–$1200\,^\circ\text{C}$ (blade metallurgy limit). Air-to-fuel ratio $\approx 60{:}1$ (range $50{:}1$ to $120{:}1$) — excess air dilutes hot gases to protect blades, not for combustion completeness.

Blade material: nickel-based superalloys (Inconel, Nimonic) with low creep rate and high oxidation resistance. Mechanical efficiency $\approx 95\%$ (pure rotational motion). Preferred for peak-load power generation (fast startup: minutes).

🔥 Quick-Fire Checklist — Gas Turbines
  • $\eta_\text{Brayton} = 1 - r_p^{-(\gamma-1)/\gamma}$; depends only on $r_p$, not on $T_\text{max}$.
  • $r_w = w_\text{net}/w_T$; $r_{bw} = w_C/w_T = 1 - r_w$; gas turbine $r_{bw} \approx 0.4$–$0.6$.
  • Jet engine shaft work $= 0$; entire turbine output drives compressor.
  • Intercooling: $P_i = \sqrt{P_1 P_2}$; Reheating: $P_r = \sqrt{P_3 P_4}$.
  • Reheat alone: $w_\text{net}\uparrow$, $\eta\downarrow$.   Intercool alone: $w_\text{net}\uparrow$, $\eta\downarrow$.
  • Regen alone: $\eta\uparrow$, $w_\text{net}$ unchanged. Best at low $r_p$.
  • All three combined: $w_\text{net}\uparrow$ AND $\eta\uparrow$ — approaches Ericsson cycle.
  • $r_{p,\text{opt}} = (T_3/T_1)^{\gamma/[2(\gamma-1)]}$; at this $r_p$: $T_2 = T_4 = \sqrt{T_1 T_3}$.
  • Air-fuel ratio $\approx 60{:}1$ — temperature control, not combustion completeness.
  • Blade material: nickel superalloys, low creep rate. No condenser in gas turbine plant.
  • Mechanical efficiency $\approx 95\%$; preferred for peak-load duty.
