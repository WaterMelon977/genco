1. Nozzle Energy Conversion & Exit Velocity

A nozzle converts the enthalpy of high-pressure steam (or gas) into kinetic energy. The steam enters with relatively low velocity and exits at high velocity — the enthalpy drop appears entirely as an increase in kinetic energy, assuming no heat loss to surroundings and no work crossing the nozzle boundary.

$$\boxed{\frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2000} = h_1 - h_2 \quad \text{kJ/kg}}$$
$h_1, h_2$ — inlet and exit specific enthalpy (kJ/kg) $V_1, V_2$ — inlet and exit velocity (m/s) Factor 2000 converts m²/s² to kJ/kg (since 1 kJ/kg = 1000 m²/s²)

When inlet velocity $V_1$ is negligible compared to $V_2$ (standard assumption for steam nozzle calculations):

$$\boxed{V_2 = \sqrt{2000\,(h_1 - h_2)} \approx 44.72\sqrt{\Delta h} \quad \text{m/s}}$$
$\Delta h = h_1 - h_2$ — isentropic enthalpy drop (kJ/kg)

2. Critical Pressure Ratio & Choked Flow

As the back-pressure downstream of a nozzle is progressively reduced, the mass flow rate through the nozzle increases until the throat velocity reaches the local speed of sound ($M_\text{throat} = 1$). At this point, the flow is choked — the nozzle has reached its maximum possible mass flow rate for given inlet conditions. Further reduction in back-pressure produces no additional increase in mass flow.

The pressure ratio at which choking occurs is the critical pressure ratio:

$$\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \left(\frac{2}{n+1}\right)^{\!n/(n-1)}}$$
$p_c$ — critical (throat) static pressure at choked condition $p_1$ — inlet stagnation pressure $n$ — isentropic index of expansion
Working Fluid$n$Critical Ratio $p_c/p_1$
Wet steam1.1300.582
Dry saturated steam1.1350.577
Superheated steam1.3000.546
Air / diatomic gas1.4000.528
⚠️ Exam Trap — Critical Ratios: Dry saturated steam ratio is 0.577. Superheated steam ratio is 0.546. Air ($\gamma=1.4$) ratio is 0.528. Do not confuse these three benchmark numbers!

3. Area–Mach Number Relation

The behavior of compressible flow in a duct of varying cross-section is governed by the area–velocity relation derived from conservation of mass and momentum:

$$\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1)\,\frac{dV}{V}}$$

Three regimes emerge from this single equation:

Flow RegimeMach Number$(M^2-1)$ SignArea–Velocity RelationDevice
Subsonic$M < 1$Negative$dA < 0 \Rightarrow dV > 0$ (area decrease accelerates flow)Convergent nozzle
Sonic$M = 1$Zero$dA = 0$ (sonic flow ONLY at throat — minimum area)Throat of C-D nozzle
Supersonic$M > 1$Positive$dA > 0 \Rightarrow dV > 0$ (area increase accelerates flow)Divergent C-D section

The divergent section semi-cone angle must be kept small to prevent boundary layer separation and turbulence losses:

5. Normal Shock Waves

A normal shock wave is an extremely thin region of high pressure gradient perpendicular to the flow direction in supersonic flow. It is irreversible — entropy increases and total pressure decreases across it. Normal shocks form ONLY in supersonic flow ($M_1 > 1$) and always produce subsonic flow downstream ($M_2 < 1$).

Normal Shock Relations

$$\text{Shock Strength} = \frac{p_2 - p_1}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma+1}(M_1^2 - 1)$$
$p_1, M_1$ — static pressure and Mach number upstream ($M_1 > 1$) $p_2$ — static pressure downstream of shock
$$V_1 \cdot V_2 = a^{*2}$$
Prandtl-Meyer relation; $a^*$ — critical speed of sound at $M = 1$
💡 Key Concept — Property Jumps Across Normal Shock: Static pressure $p$, static temperature $T$, and density $\rho$ jump UP. Mach number drops from $>1$ to $<1$. Stagnation pressure drops, and entropy INCREASES.

6. Nozzle Efficiency & Discharge Coefficient

Real nozzles experience friction between steam and nozzle walls, converting part of the enthalpy drop into heat rather than kinetic energy.

Nozzle Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta_n = \frac{h_1 - h_2}{h_1 - h_{2s}} = \frac{V_2^2}{V_{2s}^2}}$$
$h_1 - h_2$ — actual enthalpy drop (with friction), kJ/kg $h_1 - h_{2s}$ — isentropic enthalpy drop (ideal), kJ/kg $V_{2s}$ — isentropic (ideal) exit velocity, m/s Typical range: $\eta_n \approx 0.90$–$0.95$

Coefficient of Discharge

$$\boxed{C_d = \frac{\dot{m}_\text{actual}}{\dot{m}_\text{theoretical}}}$$
Typical range: $C_d \approx 0.95$–$0.98$
🔥 Quick-Fire Checklist — Nozzles & Compressible Flow
  • $V_2 = \sqrt{2000\,\Delta h} \approx 44.72\sqrt{\Delta h}$ m/s (when $V_1 \approx 0$).
  • Critical pressure ratio: $p_c/p_1 = [2/(n+1)]^{n/(n-1)}$.
  • Dry saturated steam ($n=1.135$): $p_c/p_1 = 0.577$.
  • Superheated steam ($n=1.3$): $p_c/p_1 = 0.546$.
  • Wet steam ($n=1.13$): $p_c/p_1 = 0.582$.
  • Air ($n=1.4$): $p_c/p_1 = 0.528$.
  • Choked flow: $M_\text{throat} = 1$; further reducing back-pressure does NOT increase $\dot{m}$.
  • Area-Mach: $dA/A = (M^2-1)\,dV/V$.
  • Subsonic: decreasing area accelerates; Supersonic: increasing area accelerates.
  • C-D nozzle: Convergent ($M<1$) → Throat ($M=1$) → Divergent ($M>1$ at design).
  • Divergent semi-cone angle: $3^\circ$–$10^\circ$.
  • Normal shock: divergent section only; $M_1>1 \to M_2<1$; $p$, $T$, $\rho$ jump up; entropy increases.
  • Shock strength $= 2\gamma(M_1^2-1)/(\gamma+1)$.
  • Prandtl-Meyer: $V_1 \cdot V_2 = a^{*2}$ across normal shock.
  • $\eta_n = \Delta h_\text{actual}/\Delta h_\text{isentropic} = V_2^2/V_{2s}^2$; actual in numerator.
  • $C_d = \dot{m}_\text{actual}/\dot{m}_\text{theoretical}$; typically 0.95–0.98.
  • $a = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$; $a_\text{water} \approx 4\,a_\text{air}$; Solid > Liquid > Gas.
  • Zone of Silence: outside Mach cone. $\sin\mu = 1/M$.
