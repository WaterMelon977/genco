2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Propulsive Efficiencies

  • Air-Breathing Jet Engine Propulsive Efficiency $\eta_p$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2u}{u + v} = \frac{2}{1 + \frac{v}{u}}}}$$
  • $u$: flight (aircraft) forward velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • $v$: jet exhaust velocity relative to aircraft $\text{m/s}$

  • Rocket Engine Propulsive Efficiency $\eta_p$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2} = \frac{2 r}{1 + r^2}}}$$

  • $V_f$: rocket forward flight velocity $\text{m/s}$
  • $V_e$: effective jet exhaust velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • $r = \frac{V_f}{V_e}$: velocity ratio (maximum $\eta_p = 100\%$ occurs at $r = 1 \implies V_f = V_e$)

  • Water-Jet Ship Propulsion (Inlet Orifices at Right Angles):

    $${\boxed{\eta_p = \frac{2 V u}{V^2 + u^2}}}$$

  • $V$: absolute velocity of issuing water jet $\text{m/s}$
  • $u$: ship forward velocity $\text{m/s}$
  • Maximum theoretical efficiency $= 50\%$ (at $V = u$)

2. Thrust & Rocket Coefficient

  • Turbojet Thrust $F$:
    $${\boxed{F = \dot{m} \cdot (V_{\text{exit}} - V_{\text{inlet}}) \quad (\text{N or kN})}}$$
  • $\dot{m}$: mass flow rate of air $\text{kg/s}$
  • $V_{\text{exit}}$: exhaust jet velocity relative to engine $\text{m/s}$

  • $V_{\text{inlet}}$: inlet air velocity relative to engine $\text{m/s}$

  • Rocket Thrust $F$:

    $${\boxed{F = \dot{m} \cdot V_e + (P_e - P_0) \cdot A_e \quad (\text{N or kN})}}$$

  • $V_e$: effective jet exhaust velocity $\text{m/s}$
  • $P_e$: nozzle exit static pressure $\text{Pa}$
  • $P_0$: ambient atmospheric pressure $\text{Pa}$
  • $A_e$: nozzle exit area $\text{m}^2$

  • Note: Ambient air entry velocity for rocket is ZERO $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0$!

  • Rocket Thrust Coefficient $C_F$:

    $${\boxed{C_F = \frac{F}{P_c \cdot A_t}}}$$

  • $F$: measured engine thrust $\text{N}$
  • $P_c$: combustion chamber pressure $\text{Pa}$
  • $A_t$: nozzle throat area $\text{m}^2$

3. Specific Impulse & Mach Angle

  • Specific Impulse $I_{sp}$:
    $${\boxed{I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}} = \frac{F}{\dot{m} \cdot g_0} \quad (\text{seconds})}}$$
  • $\text{SPC}$: Specific Propellant Consumption ($\text{kg/(N}\cdot\text{s)}$ or $\text{s}^{-1}$)

  • $g_0$: standard acceleration due to gravity $9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$

  • Supersonic Mach Angle $\mu$:

    $${\boxed{\mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{v}\right)}}$$

  • $M = \frac{v}{a}$: flight Mach number $M > 1$
  • $a$: local speed of sound $\text{m/s}$
  • $v$: aircraft / projectile velocity $\text{m/s}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Jet Propulsion: Propulsion of an aircraft or vessel produced by the high-velocity discharge of a fluid jet creating reactive thrust according to Newton's third law of motion and conservation of linear momentum.
  • Turbojet Engine: An air-breathing jet engine comprising an inlet diffuser, dynamic axial/centrifugal compressor, combustion chamber, gas turbine, and exhaust nozzle. Operates with a continuous lean air-fuel ratio $\approx 60:1$.
  • Turboprop Engine: A gas turbine engine that uses most of its power to drive an external propeller via a reduction gearbox. Component sequence: Propeller $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Burner $\to$ Turbine.
  • Ramjet Engine: An air-breathing engine with NO MOVING PARTS (no compressor or turbine). Compression is achieved entirely by dynamic ramming of supersonic air in the inlet diffuser. Cannot produce static thrust at rest $M = 0$.
  • Pulsejet Engine: An air-breathing engine featuring intermittent combustion regulated by automatic intake reed valves, resembling a high-speed single-cylinder 2-stroke engine $2400-2700 \text{ rpm}$.
  • Rocket Engine: A non-air-breathing propulsion system that carries both fuel and oxidiser onboard. Independent of atmospheric oxygen; inlet air velocity is zero.
  • Hybrid Rocket: A rocket engine using solid fuel and a liquid/gaseous oxidiser (e.g. Lithium Hydride $\text{LiH}$ fuel + Chlorine Trifluoride $\text{ClF}_3$ oxidiser).
  • Thrust Coefficient $C_F$: Non-dimensional performance metric relating rocket thrust to combustion chamber pressure and nozzle throat area $C_F = \frac{F}{P_c A_t}$.
  • Mach Angle $\mu$: Half-angle of the supersonic Mach cone created by an object travelling at speed $v > a$ $\sin\mu = 1/M$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Turbojet Air-Fuel Ratio $\text{AFR}$: $\approx 60:1$ (diluted with excess air to limit turbine inlet temperature).
  • Rocket Inlet Air Velocity: $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0 \text{ m/s}$ (does not ingest atmospheric air).
  • Maximum Theoretical Ship Propulsive Efficiency (Right-Angle Inlet): $50\%$ (at $V = u$).
  • Pulsejet Frequency Analogue: $2400 - 2700 \text{ rpm}$ (high-speed 2-stroke engine equivalent).

3.1 Propulsive Efficiency Formulations

1. Concept Pointers
  • Air-Breathing Jet Engines:
  • Propulsive efficiency $\eta_p = \frac{\text{Propulsive Power}}{\text{Rate of KE Addition to Jet}} = \frac{2u}{u + v} = \frac{2}{1 + \frac{v}{u}}$.
  • $u$ is aircraft forward velocity; $v$ is jet velocity RELATIVE to the aircraft.
  • As jet velocity $v$ approaches flight velocity $u$, $\eta_p \to 100\%$ (minimum residual kinetic energy loss).
  • Rocket Engines:
  • Rocket carries its own oxidiser; exhaust velocity is absolute velocity $V_e$.
  • $\eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2} = \frac{2 r}{1 + r^2}$, where velocity ratio $r = \frac{V_f}{V_e}$.
  • Peak propulsive efficiency $\eta_p = 100\%$ occurs EXACTLY when forward velocity equals exhaust velocity $V_f = V_e \implies r = 1.0$.
  • Water-Jet Ship Propulsion (Right-Angle Inlets):
  • When water is drawn into the ship through orifices at right angles to ship motion, $\eta_p = \frac{2 V u}{V^2 + u^2}$.
  • Maximum theoretical efficiency is $50\%$ (occurring when jet velocity $V = u$).
💡 Key Concept — Jet vs Rocket Propulsive Efficiency:
- Air-Breathing Jet $\implies \eta_p = \frac{2u}{u+v}$ ($v$ is relative jet speed).
- Rocket Engine $\implies \eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2}$ ($V_e$ is absolute exhaust speed; peak at $V_f = V_e$).
⚠️ Exam Trap — Water-Jet Ship Inlet Angle: If inlet orifices are at RIGHT ANGLES to motion, $\eta_p = \frac{2 V u}{V^2 + u^2}$ (denominator is sum of squares!). Max efficiency is 50%. Do not confuse with parallel inlets!
2. Decision Tree
Propulsive Efficiency Formula Selection?
├─ Air-Breathing Jet Engine → eta_p = 2 * u / (u + v)  [v = relative jet speed]
├─ Rocket Engine → eta_p = 2 * Vf * Ve / (Ve^2 + Vf^2)  [Peak eta_p = 100% when Vf = Ve]
└─ Water-Jet Ship (Right-Angle Inlet) → eta_p = 2 * V * u / (V^2 + u^2)  [Max eta_p = 50%]
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Air-breathing jet $\eta_p = \frac{2u}{u+v}$.
  • [ ] Rocket $\eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2}$.
  • [ ] Rocket peak $\eta_p = 100\%$ at $V_f = V_e$.
  • [ ] Ship right-angle inlet $\eta_p = \frac{2 Vu}{V^2+u^2}$ (max $50\%$).
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: A rocket travels at forward velocity $V_f = 1500 \text{ m/s}$ with an effective exhaust jet velocity $V_e = 4000 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate the propulsive efficiency $\eta_p$.

Solution:
$${\eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2} = \frac{2 \times 1500 \times 4000}{4000^2 + 1500^2} = \frac{12000000}{16000000 + 2250000} = \frac{12000000}{18250000} = 0.6575 = 65.75\% \approx 66\%}$$
Key Insight: $\eta_p = 65.75\% \approx 66\%$. Use rocket formula with absolute velocities.

3.2 Thrust & Linear Momentum Principle

1. Concept Pointers
  • Jet propulsion operates directly on Newton's third law and conservation of linear momentum.
  • Turbojet Thrust $F$:
    $${F = \dot{m} \cdot (V_{\text{exit}} - V_{\text{inlet}})}$$
    Where $\dot{m}$ is air mass flow rate $\text{kg/s}$, $V_{\text{exit}}$ and $V_{\text{inlet}}$ are relative velocities $\text{m/s}$.
  • Rocket Thrust $F$:
    $${F = \dot{m} \cdot V_e + (P_e - P_0) \cdot A_e}$$
    Rocket carries all propellants onboard; atmospheric air ingestion rate is ZERO $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0$.
  • Rocket Thrust Coefficient $C_F$:
    $${C_F = \frac{F}{P_c \cdot A_t}}$$
    Requires measured thrust $F$, combustion pressure $P_c$, and nozzle throat area $A_t$.
💡 Key Concept — Rocket Inlet Velocity Constant: For a rocket engine, inlet atmospheric air velocity is ALWAYS ZERO $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0$. Rocket thrust depends strictly on propellant mass flow rate, exhaust velocity, and pressure difference across exit area!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Thrust Coefficient Parameters: $C_F = \frac{F}{P_c A_t}$. To compute $C_F$, you MUST have measured thrust $F$, chamber pressure $P_c$, AND throat area $A_t$!
2. Decision Tree
Propulsion Thrust Calculation?
├─ Turbojet Engine → F = m_dot * (V_exit - V_inlet)
├─ Rocket Engine → F = m_dot * Ve + (Pe - P0) * Ae  [V_inlet = 0]
└─ Rocket Thrust Coefficient → C_F = F / (P_c * A_t)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Linear momentum principle.
  • [ ] Turbojet $F = \dot{m}(V_{\text{exit}} - V_{\text{inlet}})$.
  • [ ] Rocket $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0$.
  • [ ] $C_F = F / (P_c A_t)$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: A turbojet ingests air at $\dot{m} = 40 \text{ kg/s}$. Relative inlet velocity is $V_{\text{inlet}} = 250 \text{ m/s}$ and relative exhaust velocity is $V_{\text{exit}} = 700 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate the thrust developed.

Solution:
$${F = \dot{m} \cdot (V_{\text{exit}} - V_{\text{inlet}}) = 40 \times (700 - 250) = 40 \times 450 = 18000 \text{ N} = 18 \text{ kN}}$$
Key Insight: $F = 18 \text{ kN}$. Aircraft ground speed is irrelevant when relative inlet velocity is given.

3.3 Air-Breathing Engine Classifications & Characteristics

1. Concept Pointers
  • Turbojet Engine:
  • Continuous flow path: Inlet $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Combustor $\to$ Turbine $\to$ Nozzle.
  • Operates at a very lean air-fuel ratio $\text{AFR} \approx 60:1$ to limit turbine inlet temperature.
  • Turboprop Engine:
  • Gas turbine core drives a large external propeller via a reduction gearbox.
  • Component sequence: Propeller $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Burner $\to$ Turbine.
  • Ramjet Engine:
  • Contains NO ROTATING PARTS (no compressor, no turbine).
  • Uses dynamic ram compression of high-speed air in a converging-diverging diffuser.
  • CANNOT PRODUCE STATIC THRUST AT REST $M = 0$; requires an external booster to reach supersonic flying speed.
  • Pulsejet Engine:
  • Intermittent combustion cycle featuring automatic inlet valve grids.
  • Thermodynamically analogous to a high-speed single-cylinder 2-stroke engine operating at $2400 - 2700 \text{ rpm}$.
💡 Key Concept — Ramjet Static Thrust Limitation: A Ramjet HAS NO COMPRESSOR! It relies entirely on forward air speed for ram compression. Consequently, a ramjet CANNOT PRODUCE STATIC THRUST AT REST $M = 0$ and cannot take off unassisted!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Turboprop Component Sequence: The correct component sequence for a Turboprop engine is: Propeller $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Burner $\to$ Turbine. Do not scramble the positions of propeller and compressor!
2. Decision Tree
Jet Engine Type Identification?
├─ Turbine drives external propeller → TURBOPROP (Sequence: Propeller -> Compressor -> Burner -> Turbine)
├─ No moving parts; zero static thrust at rest → RAMJET (Needs high-speed ram compression)
├─ Intermittent combustion with valve grid → PULSEJET (High-speed 2-stroke engine analogue)
└─ Continuous flow, turbine drives compressor → TURBOJET (AFR ~ 60:1)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Turbojet $\text{AFR} \approx 60:1$.
  • [ ] Turboprop sequence: Propeller $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Burner $\to$ Turbine.
  • [ ] Ramjet: zero static thrust at rest.
  • [ ] Pulsejet: intermittent 2-stroke analogue $2400-2700 \text{ rpm}$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: Which of the following air-breathing engines cannot produce static thrust at zero flight speed $M=0$? (a) Turbojet (b) Turboprop (c) Ramjet (d) Pulsejet.

Solution: (c) Ramjet.
Reason: Ramjets lack a mechanical compressor and rely exclusively on dynamic ram pressure from high-speed forward flight to compress intake air. At $M=0$, no compression occurs, so zero thrust is produced.

Key Insight: Ramjet cannot start statically.

3.4 Rocket Propulsion Specifics & Specific Impulse

1. Concept Pointers
  • Rocket Engine Features:
  • Non-air-breathing; carries both fuel and oxidiser on board.
  • Operates in space vacuum; independent of atmospheric oxygen.
  • Hybrid Rocket Engines:
  • Uses propellants in two different phases: typically SOLID fuel and LIQUID/GASEOUS oxidiser.
  • Classic exam example: Lithium Hydride $\text{LiH}$ solid fuel + Chlorine Trifluoride $\text{ClF}_3$ liquid oxidiser.
  • Specific Impulse $I_{sp}$:
  • Measures propellant consumption efficiency $I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}} = \frac{F}{\dot{m} g_0}$.
  • Expressed in seconds $\text{s}$. Higher $I_{sp} \implies$ superior propellant economy.
💡 Key Concept — Specific Impulse & SPC Reciprocal: Specific Impulse $I_{sp}$ is the EXACT RECIPROCAL of Specific Propellant Consumption $I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}}$!
2. Decision Tree
Rocket Parameter & Type Check?
├─ Solid Fuel + Liquid Oxidiser → HYBRID ROCKET (e.g. Lithium Hydride + Chlorine Trifluoride)
├─ Propellant Efficiency Metric → SPECIFIC IMPULSE (I_sp = 1 / SPC [seconds])
└─ Thrust Coefficient → C_F = F / (P_c * A_t)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Rocket carries fuel + oxidiser.
  • [ ] Hybrid rocket: $\text{LiH} + \text{ClF}_3$.
  • [ ] $I_{sp} = 1 / \text{SPC}$.
  • [ ] $I_{sp}$ units: seconds.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: A rocket engine has a Specific Propellant Consumption $\text{SPC} = 0.004 \text{ kg/(N}\cdot\text{s)}$. Calculate its Specific Impulse $I_{sp}$ (taking $g_0 = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$).

Solution:
$${I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC} \cdot g_0} = \frac{1}{0.004 \times 9.81} = \frac{1}{0.03924} \approx 25.48 \text{ s}}$$
(Or in force-based units: $I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}} = \frac{1}{0.004} = 250 \text{ N}\cdot\text{s/kg}$).

Key Insight: $I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}}$.

3.5 Supersonic Mach Angle

1. Concept Pointers
  • When a body travels through a fluid at supersonic speed $v > a \implies M > 1$, pressure disturbance waves form a conical shock envelope called the Mach Cone.
  • Mach Angle $\mu$:
  • The half-angle of the Mach cone.
  • Formula:
    $${\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{v} \implies \mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{v}\right)}$$
  • As Mach number $M$ INCREASES, Mach angle $\mu$ DECREASES (cone becomes narrower).
💡 Key Concept — Mach Angle Sine Law: $\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{v}$. The Mach angle is defined strictly using the SINE function!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Trigonometric Function Trap: Mach angle $\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/M)$. Do not accidentally select $\cos^{-1}$ or $\tan^{-1}$ options!
2. Decision Tree
Supersonic Mach Angle Calculation?
├─ Formula → mu = arcsin(1 / M) = arcsin(a / v)
├─ If velocity v and speed of sound a are given → M = v / a -> mu = arcsin(a / v)
└─ Trend → As Mach number M increases, Mach angle mu decreases
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Defined for $M > 1$.
  • [ ] $\sin\mu = 1/M = a/v$.
  • [ ] $\mu = \sin^{-1}(1/M)$.
  • [ ] $M \uparrow \implies \mu \downarrow$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: An aircraft flies at $v = 450 \text{ m/s}$ in air where the local speed of sound is $a = 300 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate the Mach angle $\mu$.

Solution:
- Mach number $M = \frac{v}{a} = \frac{450}{300} = 1.5$.
- Mach angle $\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{1}{1.5} = \frac{2}{3}$.
- $\mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right) \approx 41.81^\circ$.

Key Insight: $\mu = \sin^{-1}(2/3)$. Always use inverse sine.

3.6 Turbojet Combustion Chamber Requirements

1. Concept Pointers
  • Essential Requirements for a Turbojet Combustor:
  • Complete combustion of fuel.
  • High temperature maintenance with proper fuel atomisation for continuous flame stability.
  • Minimum pressure drop across the combustion chamber.
  • NOT an Essential Requirement:
  • High rate of combustion IS NOT REQUIRED (classic examiner trap!).
  • Unomittable Component:
  • An aircraft engine CANNOT be designed without a combustion chamber!
💡 Key Concept — Combustion Chamber False Requirement: A high rate of combustion IS NOT an essential requirement for a turbojet combustion chamber! Low pressure loss and flame stability are critical.
2. Decision Tree
Turbojet Combustor Requirement Check?
├─ Complete combustion → ESSENTIAL
├─ Flame stability & atomisation → ESSENTIAL
├─ Minimal pressure drop → ESSENTIAL
└─ High rate of combustion → NOT REQUIRED (Trap!)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Complete combustion required.
  • [ ] Flame stability required.
  • [ ] Low pressure drop required.
  • [ ] High combustion rate NOT required.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: Identify which of the following is NOT an essential requirement for a turbojet combustion chamber: (a) Complete combustion (b) Low pressure drop (c) Flame stability (d) High rate of combustion.

Solution: (d) High rate of combustion.
Reason: A combustor requires high efficiency, low pressure loss, and flame stability, but a high rate of combustion is not an essential design requirement.

Key Insight: High rate of combustion is not required.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Write the propulsive efficiency formula for an air-breathing jet engine.
A: $\eta_p = \frac{2u}{u + v}$, where $u$ is aircraft velocity and $v$ is jet velocity relative to the aircraft.

Q2: Write the propulsive efficiency formula for a rocket engine.
A: $\eta_p = \frac{2 V_f V_e}{V_e^2 + V_f^2} = \frac{2r}{1+r^2}$, where $V_f$ is forward flight speed and $V_e$ is absolute exhaust velocity.

Q3: When does a rocket engine achieve maximum propulsive efficiency?
A: When rocket forward velocity equals effective jet exhaust velocity $V_f = V_e \implies \eta_p = 100\%$.

Q4: Write the propulsive efficiency formula for a water-jet ship with right-angle inlet orifices.
A: $\eta_p = \frac{2 Vu}{V^2 + u^2}$, with a maximum theoretical efficiency of $50\%$.

Q5: Write the thrust equation for a pure turbojet engine neglecting fuel mass.
A: $F = \dot{m}(V_{\text{exit}} - V_{\text{inlet}})$.

Q6: What is the inlet air velocity for a rocket engine?
A: Zero $V_{\text{inlet}} = 0$, because a rocket carries its own oxidiser and does not ingest atmospheric air.

Q7: Write the formula for Rocket Thrust Coefficient $C_F$.
A: $C_F = \frac{F}{P_c A_t}$, where $F$ is measured thrust, $P_c$ is combustion chamber pressure, and $A_t$ is nozzle throat area.

Q8: What is the typical air-fuel ratio $\text{AFR}$ for a turbojet engine?
A: $\approx 60:1$ (diluted to limit turbine inlet temperature).

Q9: State the correct component sequence for a Turboprop engine.
A: Propeller $\to$ Compressor $\to$ Burner $\to$ Turbine.

Q10: Why can a Ramjet engine NOT produce static thrust at rest $M=0$?
A: Because a ramjet has no mechanical compressor; it relies entirely on dynamic ram pressure from high-speed forward flight to compress air.

Q11: Which jet engine type operates with an intermittent combustion cycle?
A: The Pulsejet engine (analogous to a high-speed single-cylinder 2-stroke engine at $2400-2700 \text{ rpm}$).

Q12: Give an example of propellants used in a Hybrid Rocket engine.
A: Solid Lithium Hydride $\text{LiH}$ fuel + Liquid Chlorine Trifluoride $\text{ClF}_3$ oxidiser.

Q13: How is Specific Impulse $I_{sp}$ related to Specific Propellant Consumption $\text{SPC}$?
A: $I_{sp} = \frac{1}{\text{SPC}}$, expressed in seconds.

Q14: Write the formula for supersonic Mach Angle $\mu$.
A: $\mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{v}\right)$.

Q15: State three essential requirements for a turbojet combustion chamber.
A: (1) Complete combustion, (2) Continuous flame stability & atomisation, and (3) Minimal pressure drop across chamber. (High rate of combustion is NOT required).
