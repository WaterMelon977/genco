SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Nozzle Energy Conversion & Exit Velocity

  • Energy Conversion Equation:
    $${\boxed{\Delta KE = \frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2000} = h_1 - h_2 \quad (\text{kJ/kg})}}$$
  • $h_1$: inlet specific enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_2$: exit specific enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $V_1$: inlet steam velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • $V_2$: exit steam velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • Exit Velocity ($V_2$ when $V_1 \approx 0$):

    $${\boxed{V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \cdot (h_1 - h_2)} \approx 44.72 \cdot \sqrt{\Delta h} \quad (\text{m/s})}}$$

  • $\Delta h = h_1 - h_2$: isentropic enthalpy drop $\text{kJ/kg}$

2. Critical Pressure Ratio & Choking

  • Critical Pressure Ratio $\frac{p_c}{p_1}$:
    $${\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \left( \frac{2}{n+1} \right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}}}$$
  • $p_c$: critical static pressure at nozzle throat ($\text{bar}$ or $\text{Pa}$)
  • $p_1$: inlet stagnation pressure ($\text{bar}$ or $\text{Pa}$)

  • $n$: isentropic expansion index

  • Standard Critical Pressure Ratio Values:

  • Dry Saturated Steam $n = 1.135$: $\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.577 \approx 0.58}$
  • Superheated Steam $n = 1.300$: $\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.546}$
  • Wet Steam $n \approx 1.130$: $\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} \approx 0.582}$

  • Dry Air / Diatomic Gas $n = 1.400$: $\boxed{\frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.528}$

  • Choked Flow Condition:

    $${\boxed{M_{\text{throat}} = 1.0 \implies \dot{m} = \dot{m}_{\text{max}}}}$$

  • Mass flow rate reaches its absolute maximum when throat pressure reaches $p_c$. Further lowering exit back-pressure DOES NOT increase mass flow rate.

3. Area-Mach Relation & Compressible Flow Regimes

  • Area-Mach Differential Relation:
    $${\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \cdot \frac{dV}{V} = -\left(1 - M^2\right) \cdot \frac{dP}{\rho V^2}}}$$
  • Subsonic Flow $M < 1$: $dA < 0 \implies dV > 0$ (Convergent nozzle accelerates flow).
  • Sonic Flow $M = 1$: $dA = 0$ (Occurs ONLY at the minimum cross-sectional area / throat).

  • Supersonic Flow $M > 1$: $dA > 0 \implies dV > 0$ (Divergent nozzle accelerates flow).

  • Divergent Section Semi-Cone Angle:

    $${\boxed{\theta_{\text{semi}} = 3^\circ \text{ to } 10^\circ}}$$
    (Kept small to prevent boundary layer separation and turbulence losses).

4. Shock Waves & Mach Relations

  • Normal Shock Strength:
    $${\boxed{\text{Shock Strength} = \frac{p_2 - p_1}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1} \cdot \left(M_1^2 - 1\right)}}$$
  • $p_1, p_2$: static pressure before and after shock wave $\text{Pa}$

  • $M_1$: Mach number upstream of shock $M_1 > 1.0$

  • Prandtl-Meyer Velocity Relation across Normal Shock:

    $${\boxed{V_1 \cdot V_2 = a^{*2}}}$$

  • $a^*$: critical speed of sound at $M=1$ $\text{m/s}$

  • Supersonic Mach Angle $\mu$:

    $${\boxed{\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{V} \implies \mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\right)}}$$

  • Speed of Sound $a$:

    $${\boxed{a = \sqrt{\gamma \cdot R \cdot T} \quad (\text{m/s})}}$$

5. Nozzle Efficiency & Discharge Coefficient

  • Nozzle Efficiency $\eta_n$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_n = \frac{\text{Actual Enthalpy Drop}}{\text{Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} = \frac{h_1 - h_2}{h_1 - h_{2s}} = \frac{V_2^2}{V_{2s}^2}}}$$
  • $h_1 - h_2$: actual enthalpy drop $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • $h_1 - h_{2s}$: ideal isentropic enthalpy drop $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • Coefficient of Discharge $C_d$:

    $${\boxed{C_d = \frac{\dot{m}{\text{actual}}}{\dot{m}{\text{theoretical}}}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Steam Nozzle: A duct of varying cross-sectional area designed to convert the heat and pressure energy of expanding steam into high-velocity kinetic energy.
  • Convergent-Divergent (de Laval) Nozzle: A nozzle with a converging inlet section, a minimum area throat, and a diverging outlet section. Used to expand high-pressure steam to supersonic velocities $M > 1$.
  • Critical Pressure Ratio $\frac{p_c}{p_1}$: The ratio of throat pressure to inlet pressure at which steam velocity at the throat reaches sonic speed $M=1$ and mass flow rate reaches its theoretical maximum.
  • Choked Flow: The condition occurring when the pressure ratio across a nozzle equals or falls below the critical pressure ratio. Mass flow rate becomes fixed at $\dot{m}_{\text{max}}$ and remains independent of downstream exit pressure.
  • Normal Shock Wave: A thin compression wave perpendicular to the flow direction that occurs in the divergent section of a C-D nozzle when back-pressure is above design pressure. Flow jumps abruptly from supersonic $M_1 > 1$ to subsonic $M_2 < 1$ with a sudden rise in static pressure, temperature, and entropy.
  • Mach Angle $\mu$: The half-angle of the supersonic Mach cone formed by a disturbance source travelling at speed $v > a$ $\sin\mu = 1/M$.
  • Zone of Silence: The region OUTSIDE the supersonic Mach cone where acoustic disturbances generated by a moving body cannot be detected.
  • Nozzle Efficiency $\eta_n$: The ratio of actual enthalpy drop experienced by steam to the ideal isentropic enthalpy drop between the same inlet and exit pressures $\eta_n = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{actual}}}{\Delta h_{\text{isentropic}}}$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Critical Pressure Ratio Values:
  • Dry Saturated Steam $n=1.135$: $0.577 \approx 0.58$.
  • Superheated Steam $n=1.300$: $0.546$.
  • Wet Steam $n \approx 1.130$: $0.582$.
  • Dry Air / Gas $n=1.400$: $0.528$.
  • Divergent Section Semi-Cone Angle: $3^\circ \text{ to } 10^\circ$.
  • Speed of Sound in Water vs Air: $a_{\text{water}} \approx 4 \times a_{\text{air}}$ ($\approx 1480 \text{ m/s}$ in water vs $340 \text{ m/s}$ in air).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Nozzle Function & Exit Velocity

1. Concept Pointers
  • A nozzle is a passageway that converts HEAT / ENTHALPY ENERGY $\to$ KINETIC ENERGY.
  • As steam flows through a nozzle, pressure falls continuously and velocity increases.
  • Energy Conversion Equation:
    $${\Delta KE = \frac{V_2^2 - V_1^2}{2000} = h_1 - h_2 \quad (\text{kJ/kg})}$$
  • When inlet velocity is negligible $V_1 \approx 0$:
    $${V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \cdot (h_1 - h_2)} \approx 44.72 \sqrt{\Delta h} \quad (\text{m/s})}$$
  • Three primary types:
  • Convergent Nozzle: Area decreases continuously from inlet to exit. Used when exit pressure $P_2 \ge P_c$. Maximum exit velocity is sonic $M \le 1$.
  • Divergent Nozzle: Area increases continuously. Used to expand supersonic flow.
  • Convergent-Divergent (C-D / de Laval) Nozzle: Converging section $\to$ Throat $\to$ Diverging section. Used when exit pressure $P_2 < P_c$ to accelerate steam to supersonic speeds $M > 1$.
💡 Key Concept: Nozzle Primary Function: A nozzle converts ENTHALPY (heat energy) into KINETIC ENERGY! A diffuser does the exact opposite (converts kinetic energy into pressure).

[!TRAP] Exit Velocity Constant Trap: $V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \cdot \Delta h} = 44.72 \sqrt{\Delta h}$. Make sure $\Delta h$ is in kJ/kg! The factor $2000$ accounts for converting $\text{kJ/kg}$ to $\text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$.

2. Decision Tree
Nozzle Exit Velocity Calculation?
├─ Is inlet velocity V1 negligible (V1 ~ 0)? → V2 = sqrt(2000 * Delta_h) = 44.72 * sqrt(Delta_h)
└─ Is inlet velocity V1 significant? → V2 = sqrt(2000 * Delta_h + V1^2)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Nozzle converts enthalpy $\to$ KE.
  • [ ] $V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \Delta h} \approx 44.72 \sqrt{\Delta h}$.
  • [ ] Convergent: max exit speed $M \le 1$.
  • [ ] C-D nozzle: exit speed $M > 1$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Steam expands isentropically through a nozzle from inlet enthalpy $h_1 = 3000 \text{ kJ/kg}$ to exit enthalpy $h_2 = 2600 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Assuming negligible inlet velocity $V_1 \approx 0$, calculate the exit steam velocity $V_2$. - Solution:

$${\Delta h = h_1 - h_2 = 3000 - 2600 = 400 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \cdot \Delta h} = \sqrt{2000 \times 400} = \sqrt{800000} \approx 894.43 \text{ m/s}}$$
- Key Insight: $V_2 = 894.43 \text{ m/s}$. Use factor $2000$ under the square root.

3.2 Critical Pressure Ratio & Choking

1. Concept Pointers
  • Critical Pressure Ratio $\frac{p_c}{p_1}$:
  • The ratio of static pressure at the throat to inlet stagnation pressure when steam velocity at the throat reaches the speed of sound $M_{\text{throat}} = 1.0$.
  • Derivation: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \left(\frac{2}{n+1}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}$.
  • Standard Values:
  • Dry Saturated Steam $n = 1.135$: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \mathbf{0.577} \approx 0.58$.
  • Superheated Steam $n = 1.300$: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \mathbf{0.546}$.
  • Wet Steam $n \approx 1.130$: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} \approx \mathbf{0.582}$.
  • Air / Diatomic Gas $n = 1.400$: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \mathbf{0.528}$.
  • Choking Phenomenon:
  • When the pressure ratio $\frac{P_{\text{exit}}}{P_1} \le \frac{p_c}{p_1}$, the nozzle becomes CHOKED.
  • Mass flow rate reaches its MAXIMUM theoretical limit $\dot{m}_{\text{max}}$.
  • Lowering exit back-pressure below $p_c$ HAS NO EFFECT on mass flow rate!
💡 Key Concept: Choking Condition: Choked flow occurs when $M_{\text{throat}} = 1.0$ at the critical pressure ratio! Once choked, mass flow rate $\dot{m}_{\text{max}}$ remains CONSTANT even if receiver pressure is reduced to a complete vacuum!

[!TRAP] Isentropic Index Selection: - For Dry Saturated Steam $\implies n = 1.135 \implies \frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.577$. - For Superheated Steam $\implies n = 1.300 \implies \frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.546$. Do not mix up the critical ratio values for saturated vs superheated steam!

2. Decision Tree
Critical Pressure Ratio Check?
├─ Dry Saturated Steam (n = 1.135) → p_c / p1 = 0.577 (~0.58)
├─ Superheated Steam (n = 1.300) → p_c / p1 = 0.546
├─ Wet Steam (n = 1.130) → p_c / p1 = 0.582
└─ Air / Diatomic Gas (n = 1.400) → p_c / p1 = 0.528
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = \left(\frac{2}{n+1}\right)^{n/(n-1)}$.
  • [ ] Dry sat: $0.577$.
  • [ ] Superheated: $0.546$.
  • [ ] Choked flow: $M_{\text{throat}} = 1.0 \implies \dot{m} = \dot{m}_{\text{max}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Dry saturated steam enters a nozzle at inlet pressure $p_1 = 10 \text{ bar}$. Calculate the critical throat pressure $p_c$. - Solution: - For dry saturated steam, $n = 1.135 \implies \frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.577$. - $p_c = 0.577 \times 10 \text{ bar} = 5.77 \text{ bar}$. - Key Insight: $p_c = 5.77 \text{ bar}$. Multiply inlet pressure by $0.577$.

3.3 Flow Regimes & Area-Mach Relation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Area-Mach Differential Equation:
    $${\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V}}$$
  • Flow Regimes in a Convergent-Divergent (C-D) Nozzle:
  • Convergent Section:
    • Flow is SUBSONIC $M < 1$.
    • Area decreases $dA < 0$ $\implies$ Velocity increases $dV > 0$, pressure falls.
  • Throat (Minimum Area Section):
    • Area change is zero $dA = 0$.
    • Flow is SONIC $M = 1.0$ when choked. Maximum velocity at throat is sonic speed.
  • Divergent Section:
    • Flow is SUPERSONIC $M > 1$.
    • Area increases $dA > 0$ $\implies$ Velocity increases $dV > 0$, pressure continues falling.
  • Divergent Semi-Cone Angle:
  • Kept small ($3^\circ \text{ to } 10^\circ$) to avoid flow separation from the walls and boundary layer eddy formation.
💡 Key Concept: Area-Mach Behavior: - Subsonic $M < 1$ $\implies$ Converging duct accelerates flow $dA < 0 \implies dV > 0$. - Supersonic $M > 1$ $\implies$ Diverging duct accelerates flow $dA > 0 \implies dV > 0$.

[!TRAP] Divergent Semi-Cone Angle Limit: The semi-cone angle of the divergent section is $3^\circ \text{ to } 10^\circ$. If the angle exceeds $10^\circ$, flow separation and severe friction losses occur!

2. Decision Tree
C-D Nozzle Section Regime Check?
├─ Convergent Section → Subsonic Flow (M < 1), Area decreases, Velocity increases
├─ Throat Section → Sonic Flow (M = 1), Minimum Area A_t, Choked Max Flow
└─ Divergent Section → Supersonic Flow (M > 1), Area increases, Velocity increases (Semi-cone angle 3° to 10°)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2-1)\frac{dV}{V}$.
  • [ ] Convergent: $M < 1$.
  • [ ] Throat: $M = 1$.
  • [ ] Divergent: $M > 1$.
  • [ ] Semi-cone angle: $3^\circ - 10^\circ$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a correctly designed convergent-divergent steam nozzle operating under choked design conditions, state the Mach number regime in (a) convergent section, (b) throat, and (c) divergent section. - Solution: - (a) Convergent section: Subsonic $M < 1$. - (b) Throat: Sonic $M = 1.0$. - (c) Divergent section: Supersonic $M > 1$. - Key Insight: Subsonic $\to$ Sonic $\to$ Supersonic.

3.4 Shock Waves in Nozzles

1. Concept Pointers
  • A Normal Shock Wave is an extremely thin compression region perpendicular to flow.
  • Location: Occurs ONLY in the DIVERGENT SECTION of a C-D nozzle when back-pressure is higher than the design exit pressure.
  • Property Changes Across Normal Shock:
  • Flow jumps abruptly from SUPERSONIC $M_1 > 1$ to SUBSONIC $M_2 < 1$.
  • Static pressure $P_2 > P_1$, static temperature $T_2 > T_1$, and density $\rho_2 > \rho_1$ INCREASE dramatically.
  • Stagnation pressure DECREASES $P_{02} < P_{01}$ and entropy INCREASES due to irreversibility.
  • Shock Strength:
    $${\text{Shock Strength} = \frac{p_2 - p_1}{p_1} = \frac{2\gamma}{\gamma + 1} (M_1^2 - 1)}$$
  • Prandtl-Meyer Relation: $V_1 \cdot V_2 = a^{*2}$.
💡 Key Concept: Shock Wave Location & Jump: Normal shock waves occur EXCLUSIVELY in the DIVERGENT section of a nozzle! Flow before shock is SUPERSONIC $M_1 > 1$; flow after shock is ALWAYS SUBSONIC $M_2 < 1$.
2. Decision Tree
Shock Wave in Nozzle Check?
├─ Location → Divergent Section ONLY (Occurs when back pressure > design pressure)
├─ Mach Number Change → Supersonic (M1 > 1) to Subsonic (M2 < 1)
└─ Property Changes → Static P, T, density jump UP; Stagnation P drops; Entropy INCREASES
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Shock in divergent section.
  • [ ] $M_1 > 1 \to M_2 < 1$.
  • [ ] Pressure & temp jump UP.
  • [ ] Entropy increases.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: If the back pressure of a convergent-divergent nozzle is higher than the design value, where does a normal shock wave form? - Solution: - In the divergent portion of the nozzle. - Reason: Flow accelerates to $M>1$ past the throat; elevated back pressure forces a sudden normal shock in the diverging section to compress flow back to subsonic speeds. - Key Insight: Normal shock forms in the divergent section.

3.5 Mach Number, Mach Angle & Speed of Sound

1. Concept Pointers
  • Speed of Sound $a$:
    $${a = \sqrt{\gamma R T} \quad (\text{m/s})}$$
  • Highest in SOLIDS $\to$ LIQUIDS $\to$ GASES (lowest).
  • Speed of sound in water $\approx 1480 \text{ m/s} \approx 4 \times a_{\text{air}}$ $340 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Mach Angle $\mu$:
    $${\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{v} \implies \mu = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{M}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{a}{v}\right)}$$
    Defined only for supersonic flight $M > 1$. As Mach number $M$ increases, Mach angle $\mu$ decreases.
  • Zone of Silence vs Zone of Action:
  • Zone of Action: Region INSIDE the supersonic Mach cone where disturbance pressure waves travel.
  • Zone of Silence: Region OUTSIDE the supersonic Mach cone where no pressure disturbance has arrived.
💡 Key Concept: Zone of Silence Definition: The Zone of Silence is the region OUTSIDE the supersonic Mach cone $\sin\mu = 1/M$!
2. Decision Tree
Mach Angle & Speed of Sound Calculation?
├─ Speed of Sound -> a = sqrt(gamma * R * T)  [Solid > Liquid > Gas]
├─ Mach Angle -> sin(mu) = 1 / M = a / v -> mu = arcsin(1 / M)
└─ Mach Cone Regions -> INSIDE = Zone of Action; OUTSIDE = Zone of Silence
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $a = \sqrt{\gamma RT}$.
  • [ ] $a_{\text{solid}} > a_{\text{liquid}} > a_{\text{gas}}$.
  • [ ] $\sin\mu = 1/M$.
  • [ ] Zone of silence OUTSIDE Mach cone.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A bullet is fired in standard air ($T = 300 \text{ K}$, $a = 343 \text{ m/s}$) creating a Mach cone with a Mach angle $\mu = 30^\circ$. Calculate the flight speed $v$ of the bullet. - Solution: - $\sin\mu = \sin 30^\circ = 0.5 = \frac{1}{M} \implies M = 2.0$. - Flight velocity $v = M \cdot a = 2.0 \times 343 \text{ m/s} = 686 \text{ m/s}$. - Key Insight: $v = 686 \text{ m/s}$. $M = 1 / \sin 30^\circ = 2.0$.

3.6 Nozzle Efficiency & Discharge Coefficient

1. Concept Pointers
  • Nozzle Efficiency $\eta_n$:
    $${\eta_n = \frac{\text{Actual Enthalpy Drop}}{\text{Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} = \frac{h_1 - h_2}{h_1 - h_{2s}} = \frac{V_2^2}{V_{2s}^2}}$$
    Due to fluid friction and turbulence, actual enthalpy drop is always less than isentropic drop $\eta_n \approx 0.90-0.95$.
  • Coefficient of Discharge $C_d$:
    $${C_d = \frac{\dot{m}{\text{actual}}}{\dot{m}{\text{theoretical}}}}$$
    Accounts for boundary layer displacement thickness at nozzle throat $C_d \approx 0.95-0.98$.
💡 Key Concept: Nozzle Efficiency Ratio: Nozzle efficiency $\eta_n = \frac{\text{Actual Enthalpy Drop}}{\text{Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}}$. Always put actual drop in the NUMERATOR!
2. Decision Tree
Nozzle Efficiency Calculation?
├─ Given Enthalpy Drops → eta_n = Delta_h_actual / Delta_h_isentropic
├─ Given Velocities → eta_n = (V2_actual / V2_isentropic)^2
└─ Coefficient of Discharge → C_d = m_dot_actual / m_dot_theoretical
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\eta_n = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{actual}}}{\Delta h_{\text{isentropic}}}$.
  • [ ] $\eta_n = \frac{V_2^2}{V_{2s}^2}$.
  • [ ] $C_d = \frac{\dot{m}{\text{actual}}}{\dot{m}{\text{theoretical}}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a steam nozzle, the isentropic enthalpy drop is $200 \text{ kJ/kg}$ and the actual enthalpy drop due to friction is $180 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate the nozzle efficiency $\eta_n$. - Solution:

$${\eta_n = \frac{\Delta h_{\text{actual}}}{\Delta h_{\text{isentropic}}} = \frac{180}{200} = 0.90 = 90\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta_n = 90\%$. Actual over isentropic.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What energy conversion takes place in a steam nozzle?
A: A steam nozzle converts heat energy (enthalpy drop $\Delta h$) into kinetic energy $\Delta KE$.

Q2: Write the exit steam velocity formula when inlet velocity is negligible.
A: $V_2 = \sqrt{2000 \cdot (h_1 - h_2)} \approx 44.72 \sqrt{\Delta h} \text{ m/s}$.

Q3: Define Critical Pressure Ratio $\frac{p_c}{p_1}$.
A: The throat-to-inlet static pressure ratio at which steam velocity at the throat reaches sonic speed $M_{\text{throat}}=1.0$ and mass flow rate reaches its theoretical maximum.

Q4: State the critical pressure ratio for dry saturated steam.
A: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.577 \approx 0.58$ $n = 1.135$.

Q5: State the critical pressure ratio for superheated steam.
A: $\frac{p_c}{p_1} = 0.546$ $n = 1.300$.

Q6: What is Choked Flow in a nozzle?
A: The operating state where throat velocity reaches sonic speed $M=1.0$ and mass flow rate reaches its maximum limit $\dot{m}_{\text{max}}$. Lowering back-pressure further does not increase mass flow.

Q7: What is the Mach number regime in the convergent section of a C-D nozzle?
A: Subsonic $M < 1$.

Q8: What is the Mach number regime at the throat of a choked C-D nozzle?
A: Sonic $M = 1.0$.

Q9: What is the Mach number regime in the divergent section of a design C-D nozzle?
A: Supersonic $M > 1$.

Q10: What is the recommended range for the divergent semi-cone angle in a steam nozzle?
A: $3^\circ \text{ to } 10^\circ$ (to prevent boundary layer separation).

Q11: Where does a normal shock wave form in a convergent-divergent nozzle?
A: Exclusively in the DIVERGENT section (when back pressure is higher than design pressure).

Q12: How do flow properties change across a normal shock wave?
A: Flow jumps from supersonic $M_1>1$ to subsonic $M_2<1$; static pressure, temperature, and density jump UP; entropy INCREASES.

Q13: Define Supersonic Mach Angle $\mu$.
A: $\sin\mu = \frac{1}{M} = \frac{a}{v} \implies \mu = \sin^{-1}(1/M)$, the half-angle of the supersonic Mach cone.

Q14: Differentiate between Zone of Action and Zone of Silence.
A: Zone of Action is the region INSIDE the supersonic Mach cone. Zone of Silence is the region OUTSIDE the Mach cone.

Q15: Write the formula for Nozzle Efficiency $\eta_n$.
A: $\eta_n = \frac{\text{Actual Enthalpy Drop}}{\text{Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} = \frac{h_1 - h_2}{h_1 - h_{2s}}$.
