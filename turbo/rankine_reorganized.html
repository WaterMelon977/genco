2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Ideal Rankine Cycle & Pump Work

  • Thermal Efficiency $\eta_R$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_R = \frac{(h_1 - h_2) - (h_4 - h_3)}{h_1 - h_4} = \frac{W_T - W_P}{Q_{\text{in}}} = 1 - \frac{T_{\text{rejection}}}{T_{m, \text{addition}}}}}$$
  • $h_1$: turbine inlet enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_2$: turbine exit / condenser inlet enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_3$: condenser exit saturated liquid enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_4$: pump exit boiler feed enthalpy $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $W_T = h_1 - h_2$: turbine specific work $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $W_P = h_4 - h_3$: pump specific work $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • $Q_{\text{in}} = h_1 - h_4$: heat added in boiler $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • Pump Specific Work $W_P$:

    $${\boxed{W_P \approx v_3 \cdot (P_4 - P_3) \times 10^2 \quad (\text{kJ/kg})}}$$

  • $v_3$: specific volume of saturated liquid at condenser pressure $\text{m}^3\text{/kg}$

  • $P_4, P_3$: boiler and condenser pressures ($\text{bar}$; $1 \text{ bar} = 100 \text{ kPa}$)

  • Mean Temperature of Heat Addition $T_m$:

    $${\boxed{T_m = \frac{Q_{\text{in}}}{s_1 - s_4} = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{s_1 - s_4} \quad (\text{K})}}$$

2. Superheat, Reheat & Regeneration

  • Degree of Superheat $\Delta T_{\text{sup}}$:

    $${\boxed{\Delta T_{\text{sup}} = T_{\text{superheated}} - T_{\text{sat}}}}$$

  • Reheat Factor $RF$:

    $${\boxed{RF = \frac{\sum \text{Stage Isentropic Enthalpy Drops}}{\text{Overall Direct Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} \quad (1.02 \text{ to } 1.08)}}}$$

  • Open Feedwater Heater Mass Fraction Extracted $y$:

    $${\boxed{y = \frac{h_f - h_{\text{cond}}}{h_{\text{bleed}} - h_{\text{cond}}}}}$$

  • $h_f$: enthalpy of liquid leaving heater $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_{\text{cond}}$: enthalpy of condensate entering heater $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_{\text{bleed}}$: enthalpy of bled steam extracted from turbine $\text{kJ/kg}$

3. Fuels, Stoichiometry & Boiler Performance

  • Incomplete Combustion of Carbon (CO Mass Yield):

    $${\boxed{m_{\text{CO}} = \frac{7}{3} \text{ kg CO per kg of Carbon} \quad (2.333 \text{ kg/kg})}}$$

  • Partial Pressure of Component $i$ in Flue Gas $p_i$:

    $${\boxed{p_i = x_i \cdot P_{\text{total}}}}$$

  • $x_i$: volume / mole fraction of component $i$

  • Equivalent Evaporation $m_e$:

    $${\boxed{m_e = \frac{m_s \cdot (h - h_{f1})}{2257} \quad (\text{kg/h or kg/kg fuel})}}$$

  • $m_s$: actual steam generated $\text{kg/h}$
  • $h$: enthalpy of steam generated $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $h_{f1}$: enthalpy of feedwater $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • $2257$: latent heat of vaporization of water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • Condenser Vacuum Efficiency $\eta_{\text{vac}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{vac}} = \frac{\text{Actual Vacuum}}{\text{Barometric Pressure} - P_{\text{steam}}} = \frac{P_{\text{barometer}} - P_{\text{condenser}}}{P_{\text{barometer}} - P_{\text{sat}}(T_{\text{steam}})}}}$$

4. Power Plant Load Factors

  • Load Factor:

    $${\boxed{\text{Load Factor} = \frac{\text{Average Load}}{\text{Maximum Demand}} < 1.0}}$$

  • Diversity Factor:

    $${\boxed{\text{Diversity Factor} = \frac{\sum \text{Individual Maximum Demands}}{\text{Maximum Demand on Station}} > 1.0}}$$

  • Plant Capacity Factor:

    $${\boxed{\text{Plant Capacity Factor} = \frac{\text{Average Load}}{\text{Rated Plant Capacity}}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Ideal Rankine Cycle: The standard thermodynamic cycle for steam power plants consisting of 2 isentropic processes (pump $1-2$, turbine $3-4$) and 2 isobaric processes (boiler $2-3$, condenser $4-1$).
  • Mean Temperature of Heat Addition $T_m$: The effective weighted average temperature at which heat is transferred into the working fluid inside the boiler. Thermal efficiency rises directly as $T_m$ increases.
  • Superheating: Heating steam above its saturation temperature at constant pressure. Increases $T_m$ (boosting efficiency) and raises dryness fraction at exhaust (preventing blade erosion).
  • Reheating: Expanding steam partially in a High-Pressure (HP) turbine, reheating it at constant pressure in a reheater, and expanding it further in a Low-Pressure (LP) turbine. Primary goal: control LP exhaust moisture $x > 0.85-0.90$.
  • Regeneration: Bleeding small portions of expanding steam from intermediate turbine stages to preheat boiler feedwater. Increases $T_m$ and yields the highest thermal efficiency among Rankine modifications.
  • Modified Rankine Cycle: The practical cycle followed by reciprocating steam engines where expansion is terminated early at a release point. Sacrifices a small work area ("toe" of diagram) to achieve a huge REDUCTION IN CYLINDER STROKE and volume.
  • Boiler Mountings: Essential safety and control devices fitted directly onto the boiler drum (e.g. Fusible Plug, Safety Valves, Water Level Indicator, Pressure Gauge). Mandatory.
  • Boiler Accessories: Auxiliary equipment installed to improve plant thermal efficiency (e.g. Superheater, Economiser, Air Preheater, Feed Pump). Optional.
  • Fusible Plug: A safety mounting installed in the crown of the furnace containing a low-melting-point alloy plug that melts when water level drops dangerously, releasing steam to extinguish the fire.
  • Equivalent Evaporation: Mass of water evaporated from and at $100^\circ\text{C}$ into dry saturated steam per hour by absorbing the same quantity of heat as under actual boiler operating conditions.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Ideal Rankine Cycle & Efficiency Fundamentals

1. Concept Pointers
  • The ideal Rankine cycle is the benchmark vapour power cycle used in thermal power stations.
  • Four Standard Processes:
  • $1 \to 2$: Isentropic compression of liquid water in the feed pump $W_P$.
  • $2 \to 3$: Isobaric heat addition in the boiler at pressure $P_{\text{boiler}}$ $Q_{\text{in}}$.
  • $3 \to 4$: Isentropic expansion of steam in the turbine $W_T$.
  • $4 \to 1$: Isobaric heat rejection in the condenser at pressure $P_{\text{condenser}}$ $Q_{\text{out}}$.
  • Efficiency Relations:
    $${\eta_R = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{W_T - W_P}{Q_{\text{in}}} = 1 - \frac{T_{\text{rejection}}}{T_{m, \text{addition}}}}$$
  • Pump Work $W_P$:
  • $W_P = h_2 - h_1 \approx v_1 (P_2 - P_1)$.
  • Liquid water is almost incompressible $v_1 \approx \text{constant}$; pump work consumes only $1-2\%$ of turbine power.
💡 Key Concept — Heat Rejection Location: In an ideal Rankine cycle, isobaric heat rejection occurs EXCLUSIVELY in the CONDENSER $4 \to 1$, NEVER in the turbine!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Pump Work Incompressibility Approximation: $W_P \approx v_1 (P_2 - P_1)$. Remember to convert pressure from $\text{kPa}$ to $\text{bar}$ or $\text{N/m}^2$ correctly ($1 \text{ bar} = 100 \text{ kPa} = 10^5 \text{ Pa}$).
2. Decision Tree
Rankine Efficiency & Pump Work Calculation?
├─ Is enthalpy data given? → eta_R = [(h3 - h4) - (h2 - h1)] / (h3 - h2)
├─ Is pump work calculated from pressure difference? → W_P = v_liquid * (P_boiler - P_condenser)
└─ Is mean heat addition temp Tm given? → eta_R = 1 - (T_condenser / T_m)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] 4 processes: 2 isentropic, 2 isobaric.
  • [ ] Heat rejection occurs in condenser.
  • [ ] $W_P \approx v_1 (P_{\text{boiler}} - P_{\text{condenser}})$.
  • [ ] $\eta_R = 1 - T_{\text{condenser}}/T_m$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: In a Rankine cycle, enthalpy at turbine inlet $h_3 = 3400 \text{ kJ/kg}$, turbine exit $h_4 = 2300 \text{ kJ/kg}$, condenser exit liquid $h_1 = 150 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and pump work $W_P = 5 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate net work, heat input, and thermal efficiency.

Solution:
- $h_2 = h_1 + W_P = 150 + 5 = 155 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Turbine work $W_T = h_3 - h_4 = 3400 - 2300 = 1100 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Net work $W_{\text{net}} = W_T - W_P = 1100 - 5 = 1095 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Heat input $Q_{\text{in}} = h_3 - h_2 = 3400 - 155 = 3245 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Efficiency $\eta_R = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{1095}{3245} = 0.3374 = 33.74\%$.

Key Insight: $\eta_R = 33.74\%$. Always include pump work subtraction.

3.2 Efficiency Boosting Methods & Superheated Rankine Cycle

1. Concept Pointers
  • Thermal efficiency increases whenever:
  • Mean temperature of heat addition $T_m$ INCREASES.
  • Temperature of heat rejection $T_{\text{rejection}}$ DECREASES (lowering condenser pressure).
  • Superheating Effects:
  • Heating steam above saturation temperature at CONSTANT PRESSURE in the superheater.
  • Raises average temperature of heat addition $T_m \implies$ INCREASES thermal efficiency $\eta_R$.
  • Shifts turbine expansion path to the right on T-s diagram $\implies$ INCREASES dryness fraction $x$ at exhaust $\implies$ PREVENTS TURBINE BLADE EROSION!
  • Increases net work output $W_{\text{net}}$ and reduces specific steam consumption.
💡 Key Concept — Superheating Dual Advantage: Superheating at constant pressure simultaneously INCREASES thermal efficiency (by raising $T_m$) AND IMPROVES exhaust steam quality (preventing moisture erosion of LP turbine blades)!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Superheating Pressure Rule: Superheating is carried out at CONSTANT PRESSURE inside boiler tubes! It is NOT a constant temperature or constant volume process.
2. Decision Tree
Rankine Efficiency Modification Check?
├─ Superheating Steam → Raises T_m -> Increases eta_R AND increases exhaust dryness fraction x
├─ Increasing Boiler Pressure → Raises T_m -> Increases eta_R BUT decreases exhaust dryness fraction x
└─ Lowering Condenser Pressure → Lowers T_rejection -> Increases eta_R
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\eta_R \uparrow$ as $T_m \uparrow$.
  • [ ] Superheating occurs at constant pressure.
  • [ ] Superheating reduces LP blade erosion.
  • [ ] $\Delta T_{\text{sup}} = T_{\text{sup}} - T_{\text{sat}}$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: Explain why superheating steam improves Rankine cycle performance in thermal power plants.

Solution:
- Superheating adds heat at higher temperatures, raising the mean temperature of heat addition $T_m$. Since $\eta_R = 1 - T_{\text{sink}}/T_m$, higher $T_m$ directly increases thermal efficiency.
- Superheating shifts the isentropic expansion line away from the liquid region, resulting in a higher dryness fraction $x > 0.88$ at turbine exhaust, protecting LP turbine blades from water droplet erosion.

Key Insight: Higher $T_m \implies \eta_R \uparrow$; Higher $x \implies$ Erosion $\downarrow$.

3.3 Reheat Rankine Cycle & Reheat Factor

1. Concept Pointers
  • High boiler pressure increases efficiency but causes unacceptable moisture at LP turbine exhaust $x < 0.85$.
  • Reheat Process:
  • Steam expands in High-Pressure (HP) turbine to an intermediate pressure.
  • Steam is returned to the boiler and REHEATED at CONSTANT PRESSURE in a reheater.
  • Reheated steam expands in Low-Pressure (LP) turbine to condenser pressure.
  • Primary Goal of Reheating: Control and INCREASE exhaust steam quality $x > 0.88-0.90$ to eliminate blade erosion.
  • Reheat Factor $RF$:
    $${RF = \frac{\sum \text{Stage Isentropic Enthalpy Drops}}{\text{Overall Direct Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} > 1.0}$$
  • $RF \approx 1.02 \text{ to } 1.08$ due to friction heat recovery in lower stages.
💡 Key Concept — Reheat Primary Purpose: The primary reason for reheating steam is NOT just efficiency gain, but to CONTROL MOISTURE CONTENT at the turbine exhaust $x > 0.88$!
2. Decision Tree
Reheat Cycle Identification?
├─ Primary Purpose → Prevent LP turbine blade erosion by raising exhaust dryness fraction x
├─ Reheat Pressure Process → Constant Pressure in reheater tubes
└─ Reheat Factor RF → Sum of stage drops / Overall drop > 1.0 (Typically 1.02 to 1.08)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Reheat occurs at constant pressure.
  • [ ] Primary goal: exhaust moisture control $x > 0.88$.
  • [ ] Reheat Factor $RF > 1.0$ ($1.02-1.08$).
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: In a reheat Rankine cycle, HP turbine enthalpy drop is $400 \text{ kJ/kg}$, LP turbine enthalpy drop is $600 \text{ kJ/kg}$, boiler heat input is $2800 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and reheater heat input is $500 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Neglecting pump work, calculate thermal efficiency.

Solution:
- Total turbine work $W_T = 400 + 600 = 1000 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Total heat input $Q_{\text{in}} = 2800 + 500 = 3300 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
- Efficiency $\eta = \frac{W_T}{Q_{\text{in}}} = \frac{1000}{3300} = 0.3030 = 30.30\%$.

Key Insight: $\eta = 30.30\%$. Sum turbine works and heat inputs.

3.4 Regenerative Rankine Cycle & Feedwater Heating

1. Concept Pointers
  • Regenerative Principle:
  • Small fractions of steam are bled (extracted) from intermediate turbine stages to preheat boiler feedwater before it enters the boiler.
  • Preheating feedwater raises the average temperature of heat addition $T_m \implies$ HIGHEST thermal efficiency among Rankine modifications for given pressure limits.
  • Extracted steam does no work in remaining LP stages, slightly reducing specific work output per kg of total flow, but efficiency $\eta_R$ rises substantially.
  • Open Feedwater Heater Mass Balance:
    $${y \cdot h_{\text{bleed}} + (1 - y) \cdot h_{\text{cond}} = 1.0 \cdot h_f}$$
    $${y = \frac{h_f - h_{\text{cond}}}{h_{\text{bleed}} - h_{\text{cond}}}}$$
  • Deaeration: At least one OPEN Feedwater Heater is used as a Deaerator to strip dissolved non-condensable gases $\text{O}_2, \text{CO}_2$ from feedwater to prevent boiler tube corrosion.
💡 Key Concept — Regeneration Efficiency Ranking: Among simple Rankine, superheated, reheat, and regenerative cycles operating between the same pressure limits, the REGENERATIVE CYCLE HAS THE HIGHEST THERMAL EFFICIENCY!
2. Decision Tree
Regenerative Cycle Feature Check?
├─ Thermal Efficiency Ranking → HIGHEST efficiency among Rankine modifications
├─ Bled Steam Energy Balance → y = (h_feedwater - h_condensate) / (h_bleed - h_condensate)
└─ Deaeration Function → Open Feedwater Heater removes dissolved O2 and CO2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Regeneration yields highest $\eta_R$.
  • [ ] $y = (h_f - h_{\text{cond}})/(h_{\text{bleed}} - h_{\text{cond}})$.
  • [ ] Open FWH acts as deaerator.
  • [ ] Preheats boiler feedwater.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: In an open feedwater heater, condensate enters at $h_{\text{cond}} = 150 \text{ kJ/kg}$, bled steam enters at $h_{\text{bleed}} = 2650 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and subcooled feedwater leaves at $h_f = 650 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate the fraction of steam $y$ bled from the turbine per kg of total flow.

Solution:
$${y = \frac{h_f - h_{\text{cond}}}{h_{\text{bleed}} - h_{\text{cond}}} = \frac{650 - 150}{2650 - 150} = \frac{500}{2500} = 0.20 = 20\%}$$
Key Insight: $y = 0.20$ ($20\%$ of total steam flow is extracted for feedwater heating).

3.5 Modified Rankine Cycle (Reciprocating Steam Engines)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Ideal Rankine cycle assumes expansion continues all the way down to condenser pressure $P_4$.
  • In reciprocating steam engines, full expansion requires an impractically long piston stroke and giant cylinder volume due to huge steam specific volume at low pressure.
  • Modified Rankine Cycle:
  • Expansion is terminated EARLY at a release point $P_5 > P_{\text{condenser}}$.
  • Steam is released to the condenser by a constant-volume pressure drop $5 \to 6$.
  • Truncates the indicator diagram, removing the "toe" of the P-V diagram $5-3-6$.
  • Primary Geometric Benefit:
  • Sacrifices a very small work area to achieve a MASSIVE REDUCTION IN CYLINDER STROKE AND VOLUME!
  • Allows compact, practical reciprocating engine construction.
💡 Key Concept — Modified Rankine Primary Benefit: The principal reason for modifying the Rankine cycle in reciprocating steam engines is REDUCTION OF CYLINDER STROKE and size!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Modified Rankine Work vs Geometry: Modified Rankine DOES NOT increase work output! It slightly REDUCES work output to achieve a huge SAVING IN CYLINDER SIZE.
2. Decision Tree
Modified Rankine Cycle Check?
├─ Application → Reciprocating Steam Engines (Not turbines)
├─ Cycle Feature → Early release truncates expansion (Removes "toe" 5-3-6 of P-V diagram)
└─ Primary Purpose / Advantage → REDUCTION OF CYLINDER STROKE and volume
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Applied to reciprocating steam engines.
  • [ ] Early release truncates P-V diagram.
  • [ ] Removes "toe" of indicator diagram.
  • [ ] Primary effect: REDUCTION OF STROKE.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: State the main practical advantage gained by modifying the ideal Rankine cycle for reciprocating steam engines.

Solution:
- The main advantage is the reduction of the stroke of the cylinder.
- Terminating expansion early avoids building impractically long cylinders to accommodate the huge specific volume of low-pressure steam, creating a compact engine with minimal work loss.

Key Insight: Reduction of cylinder stroke.

3.6 Fuels, Combustion & Flue Gas Analysis

1. Concept Pointers
  • Coal Rank Order (Increasing Carbon & CV):
    $${\text{Peat} \to \text{Lignite} \to \text{Bituminous} \to \text{Anthracite}}$$
  • Anthracite: Highest rank, highest fixed carbon $>90\%$, highest calorific value $\approx 36000 \text{ kJ/kg}$, burns smokelessly.
  • Bituminous: Most common and practical fuel for power plant boilers $\approx 33500 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
  • Incomplete Combustion Stoichiometry:
    $${\mathrm{C} + \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{O}_2 \to \mathrm{CO}}$$
    $${1 \text{ kg C} \implies \frac{28}{12} = \frac{7}{3} \text{ kg CO} \quad (2.333 \text{ kg CO/kg C})}$$
  • Calorimeters:
  • Bomb Calorimeter: Measures Higher Calorific Value (HCV) of SOLID and LIQUID fuels at CONSTANT VOLUME.
  • Junker's Calorimeter: Measures calorific value of GASEOUS fuels.
  • Flue Gas Partial Pressure:
    $${p_i = x_i \cdot P_{\text{total}}}$$
  • Fuel Mixtures & Fuel Cells:
  • LPG $= \text{Propane} + \text{Butane}$.
  • Gasohol $= 10\% \text{ Ethanol} + 90\% \text{ Gasoline}$.
  • Spacecraft fuel cell $\implies$ Alkaline Fuel Cell (produces drinking water).
  • SO₂ control in FBC $\implies$ Limestone $\text{CaCO}_3$ injection (Ca:S ratio $\approx 3$).
💡 Key Concept — Carbon Incomplete Combustion Factor: $1 \text{ kg}$ of Carbon under incomplete combustion produces EXACTLY $\frac{7}{3} \text{ kg}$ of CO ($2.333 \text{ kg}$).
2. Decision Tree
Fuel & Combustion Query Check?
├─ Highest CV Coal → Anthracite (36,000 kJ/kg)
├─ Preferred Power Plant Coal → Bituminous (33,500 kJ/kg)
├─ 1 kg C Incomplete Combustion CO Yield → 7/3 kg CO
├─ Solid / Liquid Fuel Calorimeter → Bomb Calorimeter (Constant volume HCV)
└─ Gaseous Fuel Calorimeter → Junker's Gas Calorimeter
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Coal rank: Peat < Lignite < Bituminous < Anthracite.
  • [ ] $1 \text{ kg C} \implies \frac{7}{3} \text{ kg CO}$.
  • [ ] Bomb calorimeter $\implies$ Constant volume HCV.
  • [ ] Junker's $\implies$ Gaseous fuel.
  • [ ] $p_i = x_i P_{\text{total}}$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: Flue gas at $P_{\text{total}} = 5 \text{ bar}$ ($500 \text{ kPa}$) contains $15\%$ $\text{CO}_2$, $5\%$ $\text{O}_2$, and $80\%$ $\text{N}_2$ by volume. Calculate the partial pressure of $\text{N}_2$.

Solution:
$${p_{\mathrm{N}_2} = x_{\mathrm{N}_2} \cdot P_{\text{total}} = 0.80 \times 5 \text{ bar} = 4.0 \text{ bar} = 400 \text{ kPa}}$$
Key Insight: Partial pressure $= 400 \text{ kPa}$.

3.7 Boiler Systems, Mountings, Accessories & Draught

1. Concept Pointers
  • Mountings vs Accessories:
  • Boiler Mountings (Mandatory Safety & Control): Fitted directly on boiler drum. Includes Fusible Plug, Safety Valves (min 2), Water Level Indicator, Pressure Gauge, Main Stop Valve, Feed Check Valve, Blow-off Cock.
  • Boiler Accessories (Optional Efficiency Boosters): Installed in flue gas path. Includes Economiser, Superheater, Air Preheater, Feed Pump, Steam Injector.
  • Fusible Plug: Fail-safe mounting in furnace crown containing low-melting alloy that melts when water level drops dangerously low, releasing steam to douse the fire.
  • Flue Gas Path Sequence:
    $${\text{Furnace} \to \text{Superheater} \to \text{Economiser} \to \text{Air Preheater} \to \text{ID Fan} \to \text{Chimney}}$$
  • Boiler Draught Systems:
  • Natural Draught: Produced solely by chimney height and gas temperature difference. Thermal efficiency $\approx 0.25\%$ (extremely low).
  • Forced Draught: Fan pushes atmospheric air into furnace under positive pressure.
  • Induced Draught: Fan placed at chimney base sucks flue gases out under partial vacuum (negative pressure).
  • Balanced Draught: Combination of Forced + Induced fans.
  • Equivalent Evaporation $m_e$:
    $${m_e = \frac{m_s (h - h_{f1})}{2257} \quad (\text{kg/h})}$$
    Evaluated "from and at $100^\circ\text{C}$". Latent heat $= 2257 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
💡 Key Concept — Mounting vs Accessory Rule: Fusible Plug is a MOUNTING (safety device)! Economiser, Superheater, and Air Preheater are ACCESSORIES (efficiency boosters)!
⚠️ Exam Trap — Flue Gas Order: Flue gases exit furnace and pass through: (1) Superheater $\to$ (2) Economiser $\to$ (3) Air Preheater. Do not scramble this sequence!
2. Decision Tree
Boiler Equipment & Draught Check?
├─ Safety / Control Device on Drum → BOILER MOUNTING (Fusible Plug, Safety Valve, Water Gauge)
├─ Thermal Efficiency Booster → BOILER ACCESSORY (Economiser, Superheater, Air Preheater)
├─ Flue Gas Sequence → Superheater -> Economiser -> Air Preheater -> ID Fan -> Chimney
├─ Chimney Natural Draught Efficiency → ~0.25% (Very low)
└─ Equivalent Evaporation Formula → m_e = m_s * (h - h_f1) / 2257 ("from and at 100°C")
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Fusible plug is a mounting.
  • [ ] Economiser, Superheater, Air Preheater are accessories.
  • [ ] Flue gas: Superheater $\to$ Economiser $\to$ Air Preheater.
  • [ ] Chimney natural draught $\eta \approx 0.25\%$.
  • [ ] $m_e = m_s(h-h_{f1})/2257$.
4. Worked Examples
Worked Example 1
Problem: A boiler generates $m_s = 5000 \text{ kg/h}$ of steam at $h = 2800 \text{ kJ/kg}$ from feedwater at $h_{f1} = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate equivalent evaporation $m_e$ from and at $100^\circ\text{C}$.

Solution:
$${m_e = \frac{m_s (h - h_{f1})}{2257} = \frac{5000 \times (2800 - 200)}{2257} = \frac{5000 \times 2600}{2257} = \frac{13000000}{2257} = 5759.86 \text{ kg/h}}$$
Key Insight: $m_e = 5759.86 \text{ kg/h}$. Divide heat absorbed by $2257 \text{ kJ/kg}$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What are the four processes of an ideal Rankine cycle?
A: (1) Isentropic pump compression $1-2$, (2) Isobaric heat addition in boiler $2-3$, (3) Isentropic turbine expansion $3-4$, and (4) Isobaric heat rejection in condenser $4-1$.

Q2: Where does isobaric heat rejection occur in an ideal Rankine cycle?
A: In the Condenser.

Q3: Write the formula for Rankine cycle thermal efficiency in terms of mean temperature of heat addition $T_m$.
A: $\eta_R = 1 - \frac{T_{\text{rejection}}}{T_{m, \text{addition}}}$.

Q4: How does superheating steam at constant pressure affect Rankine cycle efficiency and exhaust moisture?
A: Superheating raises $T_m$, which INCREASES thermal efficiency, and raises turbine exhaust dryness fraction $x$, PREVENTING LP blade erosion.

Q5: What is the primary purpose of reheating steam in a Rankine cycle?
A: To control and increase dryness fraction $x > 0.88$ at LP turbine exhaust to prevent blade erosion.

Q6: What is Reheat Factor $RF$ and what is its typical numerical range?
A: $RF = \frac{\sum \text{Stage Isentropic Enthalpy Drops}}{\text{Overall Direct Isentropic Enthalpy Drop}} \approx 1.02 \text{ to } 1.08$.

Q7: Which Rankine cycle modification yields the HIGHEST thermal efficiency between fixed pressure limits?
A: The Regenerative Rankine Cycle (using bled steam to preheat feedwater).

Q8: Write the energy balance formula for fraction $y$ bled to an open feedwater heater.
A: $y = \frac{h_f - h_{\text{cond}}}{h_{\text{bleed}} - h_{\text{cond}}}$.

Q9: Why is at least one open feedwater heater used in steam power plants?
A: To act as a Deaerator, stripping dissolved oxygen and carbon dioxide from feedwater to prevent boiler corrosion.

Q10: What is the primary practical benefit of modifying the Rankine cycle for reciprocating steam engines?
A: REDUCTION OF CYLINDER STROKE and engine volume by terminating expansion early.

Q11: How much Carbon Monoxide (CO) is produced by incomplete combustion of $1 \text{ kg}$ of Carbon?
A: $\frac{7}{3} \text{ kg}$ of CO ($2.333 \text{ kg}$).

Q12: Differentiate between a Bomb Calorimeter and a Junker's Calorimeter.
A: A Bomb calorimeter measures Higher Calorific Value (HCV) of solid and liquid fuels at constant volume. Junker's calorimeter measures calorific value of gaseous fuels.

Q13: Differentiate between Boiler Mountings and Boiler Accessories.
A: Mountings are mandatory safety and control devices on the drum (Fusible Plug, Safety Valves). Accessories are optional efficiency boosters in the flue gas path (Economiser, Superheater, Air Preheater).

Q14: What is the correct sequence of boiler accessories in the flue gas flow path?
A: Furnace $\to$ Superheater $\to$ Economiser $\to$ Air Preheater $\to$ ID Fan $\to$ Chimney.

Q15: Define Equivalent Evaporation and state its standard latent heat value.
A: Equivalent Evaporation $m_e = \frac{m_s (h - h_{f1})}{2257} \text{ kg/h}$, the mass of water evaporated from and at $100^\circ\text{C}$ into dry saturated steam per hour. Latent heat $= 2257 \text{ kJ/kg}$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

🔥 High-Yield Exam Traps — Rankine Cycle & Steam Power Systems
  • Ideal Rankine Efficiency: $\eta_R = \frac{W_T - W_P}{Q_{\text{in}}} = 1 - \frac{T_{\text{condenser}}}{T_m}$. Heat rejection in CONDENSER.
  • Pump Work: $W_P \approx v_1 (P_{\text{boiler}} - P_{\text{condenser}})$. Small $1-2\%$ but non-zero.
  • Superheating: Constant pressure process. Raises $T_m \implies \eta_R \uparrow$; Raises exhaust dryness fraction $x \implies$ Blade erosion $\downarrow$.
  • Reheating: Primary purpose is EXHAUST MOISTURE CONTROL $x > 0.88$. Reheat Factor $RF = 1.02-1.08$.
  • Regeneration: HIGHEST efficiency among Rankine modifications. Open FWH acts as Deaerator ($\text{O}_2/\text{CO}_2$ removal).
  • Modified Rankine: Applied to reciprocating steam engines. Truncates diagram ("toe" 5-3-6 removed) $\implies$ REDUCTION OF STROKE.
  • Carbon Incomplete Combustion: $1 \text{ kg C} \implies \frac{7}{3} \text{ kg CO}$.
  • Coal Ranks: Peat < Lignite < Bituminous (power plant fuel) < Anthracite ($36000 \text{ kJ/kg}$, highest CV).
  • Bomb Calorimeter: Solid/liquid fuels $\implies$ Constant volume HCV.
  • Fusible Plug: Boiler MOUNTING (safety), NOT an accessory.
  • Flue Gas Path: Superheater $\to$ Economiser $\to$ Air Preheater.
  • Chimney Natural Draught Efficiency: $\approx 0.25\%$ (extremely low).
  • Equivalent Evaporation: $m_e = \frac{m_s (h - h_{f1})}{2257} \text{ kg/h}$ ("from and at $100^\circ\text{C}$").
  • Power Plant Factors: Load Factor $< 1.0$; Diversity Factor $> 1.0$.
