2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Units of Refrigeration & Capacity

  • 1 Tonne of Refrigeration (Practical):
    $${\boxed{1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min} = 12600 \text{ kJ/h}}}$$

  • Theoretical value: $\approx 3.516 \text{ kW}$ or $232.6 \text{ kJ/min}$ (from latent heat of ice $335 \text{ kJ/kg}$).

  • Power Input from Capacity & $COP$:

    $${\boxed{W = \frac{Q_R}{COP}}}$$

  • $W$: power input $\text{kW}$
  • $Q_R$: refrigerating capacity $\text{kW} = \text{Tons} \times 3.5$
  • $COP$: coefficient of performance (dimensionless)

2. Reversed Carnot Cycle (Ideal Refrigerator & Heat Pump)

  • $COP$ of Carnot Refrigerator $COP_R$:
    $${\boxed{COP_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{Q_L}{W} = \frac{Q_L}{Q_H - Q_L}}}$$
  • $T_L$: absolute evaporator temperature $\text{K}$

  • $T_H$: absolute condenser temperature $\text{K}$

  • $COP$ of Carnot Heat Pump $COP_{HP}$:

    $${\boxed{COP_{HP} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{Q_H}{W} = \frac{Q_H}{Q_H - Q_L}}}$$

  • Relation between Heat Pump & Refrigerator $COP$:

    $${\boxed{COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1}}$$

  • Relation to Carnot Heat Engine Efficiency $\eta_{\text{Carnot}}$:

    $${\boxed{COP_R = \frac{1 - \eta_{\text{Carnot}}}{\eta_{\text{Carnot}}}, \quad COP_{HP} = \frac{1}{\eta_{\text{Carnot}}}}}$$

3. Air Refrigeration Systems (Bell-Coleman / Reversed Brayton)

  • $COP$ of Ideal Bell-Coleman Air Cycle:
    $${\boxed{COP = \frac{1}{r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1} = \frac{T_1 - T_4}{(T_2 - T_3) - (T_1 - T_4)}}}$$
  • $r_p = \frac{P_2}{P_1}$: pressure ratio across compressor / expander
  • $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$: specific heat ratio (for air $\gamma = 1.40$)

4. Vapour Compression Refrigeration Systems (VCRS)

  • $COP$ of VCRS Cycle:
    $${\boxed{COP_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1} = \frac{\text{Refrigerating Effect}}{\text{Compressor Work}}}}$$
  • $h_1$: enthalpy at compressor inlet (saturated/superheated vapour in $\text{kJ/kg}$)
  • $h_2$: enthalpy at compressor discharge $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • $h_3 = h_4$: enthalpy after throttling condenser liquid $\text{kJ/kg}$

  • Heat Rejection Ratio $HRR$:

    $${\boxed{HRR = \frac{Q_{\text{cond}}}{Q_R} = \frac{h_2 - h_3}{h_1 - h_4} = 1 + \frac{1}{COP_R}}}$$

  • Mass Flow Rate of Refrigerant $\dot{m}_r$:

    $${\boxed{\dot{m}_r = \frac{Q_R}{h_1 - h_4} = \frac{\text{Capacity in kW}}{h_1 - h_4} \quad (\text{kg/s})}}$$

5. Operating Parameter Effects & Modifications

  • Evaporator Temperature ($T_e \uparrow$, fixed $T_c$):

    $${\boxed{T_e \uparrow \implies COP \uparrow, \quad RE \uparrow, \quad W_{\text{compressor}} \downarrow}}$$

  • Condenser Temperature ($T_c \uparrow$, fixed $T_e$):

    $${\boxed{T_c \uparrow \implies COP \downarrow, \quad RE \downarrow, \quad W_{\text{compressor}} \uparrow}}$$

  • Sub-Cooling (Under-Cooling):

    $${\boxed{RE_{\text{subcooled}} = h_1 - h_4' > h_1 - h_4 \implies COP \uparrow, \quad W \text{ unchanged}}}$$

  • Superheating:

    $${\boxed{W_{\text{compressor}} \uparrow, \quad \text{Discharge Temperature } T_2 \uparrow, \quad COP \text{ usually } \downarrow}}$$

6. Compressors & Multi-Stage / Cascade Systems

  • Volumetric Efficiency of Reciprocating Compressor $\eta_v$:
    $${\boxed{\eta_v = \frac{V_{\text{actual}}}{V_{\text{swept}}} = 1 + C - C \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}}}$$

  • $C = \frac{V_c}{V_s}$: clearance ratio

  • Optimum Intermediate Temperature for Cascade System:

    $${\boxed{T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}}}$$

7. Refrigerant Designation Rules

  • Organic Halocarbon Compounds $R-a b c$:
    $${\boxed{R-(C-1)(H+1)F}}$$
  • $C$: number of Carbon atoms
  • $H$: number of Hydrogen atoms
  • $F$: number of Fluorine atoms

  • Chlorine atoms $Cl = 2(C) + 2 - H - F$ (for alkanes)

  • Inorganic Refrigerants $R-7xx$:

    $${\boxed{R-(700 + M)}}$$

  • $M$: molecular weight of compound $\text{g/mol}$
  • $R-717 = NH_3 (M=17)$, $R-718 = H_{2O} (M=18)$, $R-744 = CO_2 (M=44)$, $R-729 = \text{Air } (M=29)$

8. Vapour Absorption Refrigeration Systems (VARS)

  • First Law Energy Balance (Pump Work Neglected):
    $${\boxed{Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A}}$$
  • $Q_G$: heat supplied in generator $\text{kW}$
  • $Q_E$: heat absorbed in evaporator $\text{kW}$
  • $Q_C$: heat rejected in condenser $\text{kW}$

  • $Q_A$: heat rejected in absorber $\text{kW}$

  • Ideal Carnot $COP$ of VARS:

    $${\boxed{COP_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right) = \eta_{\text{engine}} \times COP_{\text{refrigerator}}}}$$

  • $T_E$: evaporator temperature $\text{K}$
  • $T_G$: generator temperature $\text{K}$
  • $T_O$: environment / sink / absorber / condenser temperature $\text{K}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Tonne of Refrigeration (TR): The rate of heat extraction required to freeze $1000 \text{ kg}$ of pure water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ in $24 \text{ hours}$ $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW}$.
  • Coefficient of Performance $COP$: Ratio of desired refrigerating or heating effect to the net work input ($COP_R = Q_L/W$, $COP_{HP} = Q_H/W$).
  • Reversed Carnot Cycle: The ideal reference cycle operating between two thermal reservoirs; gives maximum theoretical $COP$.
  • Bell-Coleman Cycle: Reversed Brayton / Joule gas refrigeration cycle using air as working fluid.
  • Vapour Compression Refrigeration System (VCRS): Mechanical refrigeration cycle consisting of Compressor, Condenser, Throttling Valve, and Evaporator.
  • Sub-Cooling: Cooling liquid refrigerant below its saturation temperature at condenser pressure before expansion $COP \uparrow$.
  • Superheating: Heating refrigerant vapour above saturation temperature at evaporator pressure before compression.
  • Capillary Tube: Constant-area restriction throttling device operating on fluid friction and momentum reduction.
  • Thermostatic Expansion Valve (TEV): Expansion valve regulated by sensing bulb to maintain constant evaporator outlet superheat.
  • Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP): Measure of a chemical's ability to destroy stratospheric ozone relative to baseline $R-11$ $ODP_{R-11} = 1.0$.
  • Azeotrope: Refrigerant mixture of two or more liquids that boils at a constant temperature with fixed liquid/vapour composition ($R-500$ series).
  • Vapour Absorption Refrigeration System (VARS): Heat-operated refrigeration system replacing mechanical compressor with Absorber, Generator, and Liquid Pump.
  • Electrolux Refrigerator: Three-fluid domestic absorption system $NH_3, H_{2O}, H_2$ operating completely without any mechanical pump or moving parts.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • 1 Tonne of Refrigeration: $3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min} = 12600 \text{ kJ/h}$.
  • Baseline ODP Reference: $ODP \text{ of } R-11 (\text{CCl}_3\text{F}) = 1.0$.
  • Inorganic Codes: $R-717 (NH_3)$, $R-718 (H_{2O})$, $R-744 (CO_2)$, $R-729 (\text{Air})$.
  • Condenser Selection: Air-cooled $\le 3 \text{ TR}$; Water-cooled $3 - 50 \text{ TR}$; Evaporative $> 50 \text{ TR}$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Units of Refrigeration & Capacity

1. Concept Pointers
  • $1 \text{ TR}$ is defined as the latent heat absorbed when $1000 \text{ kg}$ $1 \text{ metric ton}$ of water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ freezes into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ over $24 \text{ hours}$.
  • Latent heat of ice fusion $= 335 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
  • Theoretical value $= \frac{1000 \times 335}{24 \times 3600} = 3.877 \text{ kW}$ or $\frac{1000 \times 335}{24 \times 60} = 232.6 \text{ kJ/min}$.
  • Practical engineering standard accepted in all competitive exams: $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.
  • Power required to drive compressor: $W = \frac{Q_R}{COP}$.
💡 Key Concept: Power Conversion Rule: Always convert refrigerating capacity from Tons to kW by multiplying by $3.5$ BEFORE dividing by $COP$!

[!TRAP] Theoretical vs Practical Value Trap: Unless a question explicitly asks for the "theoretical exact value based on $335 \text{ kJ/kg}$", ALWAYS use $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$!

2. Decision Tree
Refrigeration Capacity Conversion?
├─ Is capacity given in Tons of Refrigeration (TR)?
│  ├─ Multiply by 3.5 → Capacity in kW
│  └─ Multiply by 210 → Capacity in kJ/min
└─ Calculate Power Input → W (kW) = Capacity (kW) / COP
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.
  • [ ] Power $W = Q_R / COP$.
  • [ ] Heat absorption happens at evaporator.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A refrigeration plant has a capacity of $6 \text{ TR}$ and operates with a $COP = 6$. Calculate the minimum power required to drive the compressor. - Solution:

$${Q_R = 6 \text{ TR} = 6 \times 3.5 = 21 \text{ kW}}$$
$${W = \frac{Q_R}{COP} = \frac{21}{6} = 3.5 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: Convert tons to kW $21 \text{ kW}$ before dividing by $COP$.

3.2 Reversed Carnot Cycle (Ideal Refrigerator & Heat Pump)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reversed Carnot cycle consists of 4 reversible processes:
  • Isothermal heat absorption at low temperature $T_L$ (Evaporator).
  • Isentropic compression $1 \to 2$.
  • Isothermal heat rejection at high temperature $T_H$ (Condenser).
  • Isentropic expansion $3 \to 4$.
  • Refrigerator desired effect $= Q_L$; Heat Pump desired effect $= Q_H$.
  • $COP_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L}, \quad COP_{HP} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_L} = COP_R + 1$.
💡 Key Concept: Absolute Temperature Mandate: Temperatures in Carnot $COP$ formulas MUST ALWAYS be in Kelvin $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$.

[!TRAP] Heat Pump vs Refrigerator COP: $COP_{HP}$ is ALWAYS greater than $COP_R$ by exactly $1.0$ for the same operating temperature limits $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$.

2. Decision Tree
Carnot Performance Calculation?
├─ Refrigerator COP → COP_R = T_L / (T_H - T_L)
├─ Heat Pump COP → COP_HP = T_H / (T_H - T_L) = COP_R + 1
└─ Engine Efficiency Relation → COP_R = (1 - eta) / eta, COP_HP = 1 / eta
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Temperatures in Kelvin.
  • [ ] $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$.
  • [ ] Carnot gives maximum theoretical performance.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A Carnot refrigerator operates between $-13^\circ\text{C}$ $260 \text{ K}$ and $27^\circ\text{C}$ $300 \text{ K}$. If it absorbs $1.0 \text{ kW}$ of heat from the cold space, calculate compressor power input and $COP_{HP}$ if run as a heat pump. - Solution:

$${T_L = -13 + 273 = 260 \text{ K}, \quad T_H = 27 + 273 = 300 \text{ K}}$$
$${COP_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{260}{300 - 260} = \frac{260}{40} = 6.5}$$
$${W = \frac{Q_L}{COP_R} = \frac{1.0}{6.5} = 0.1538 \text{ kW}}$$
$${COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1 = 6.5 + 1 = 7.5}$$
- Key Insight: $COP_R = 6.5$, $W = 0.1538 \text{ kW}$, $COP_{HP} = 7.5$.

3.3 Air Refrigeration Systems (Bell-Coleman / Reversed Brayton)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Working fluid remains gaseous throughout (no phase change).
  • Bell-Coleman cycle comprises:
  • Isentropic compression $1 \to 2$.
  • Constant-pressure heat rejection $2 \to 3$.
  • Isentropic expansion $3 \to 4$.
  • Constant-pressure heat absorption $4 \to 1$.
  • $COP = \frac{1}{r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1}$.
  • As pressure ratio $r_p$ increases, $COP$ decreases!
  • Used in aircraft refrigeration due to lightweight air medium.
💡 Key Concept: Pressure Ratio Trend: In gas refrigeration cycles, increasing pressure ratio $r_p$ DECREASES $COP$ (opposite of gas turbine power cycles!).
2. Decision Tree
Air Refrigeration (Bell-Coleman) Check?
├─ Is working fluid a gas without phase change? → Bell-Coleman Cycle
├─ Calculate COP → COP = 1 / (r_p^((gamma-1)/gamma) - 1)
└─ Pressure Ratio Effect → r_p increases implies COP decreases
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Bell-Coleman uses air (no phase change).
  • [ ] $COP = \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} - 1}$.
  • [ ] Used in aircraft cooling.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An air refrigeration system operating on reversed Brayton cycle has a pressure ratio $r_p = 4$ $\gamma = 1.4$. Calculate its $COP$. - Solution:

$${\frac{\gamma - 1}{\gamma} = \frac{1.4 - 1}{1.4} = \frac{0.4}{1.4} = \frac{2}{7} \approx 0.2857}$$
$${r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = 4^{0.2857} \approx 1.486}$$
$${COP = \frac{1}{1.486 - 1} = \frac{1}{0.486} \approx 2.058}$$
- Key Insight: $COP$ depends inversely on $r_p$.

3.4 Vapour Compression Refrigeration System (VCRS)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Most common commercial cycle with refrigerant phase change.
  • Processes:
  • Isentropic compression $1 \to 2$.
  • Isobaric heat rejection in condenser $2 \to 3$.
  • Isenthalpic throttling in expansion valve $3 \to 4, h_3 = h_4$.
  • Isobaric heat absorption in evaporator $4 \to 1$.
  • $COP_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1}$.
  • Heat Rejection Ratio $HRR = \frac{Q_{\text{cond}}}{Q_R} = 1 + \frac{1}{COP_R}$.
💡 Key Concept: Throttling Property: Expansion process $3 \to 4$ is ISENTHALPIC $h_3 = h_4$, NOT isentropic! Vertically straight line on $p-h$ diagram.

[!TRAP] Flash Chamber Effect: Flash chamber separates flash gas from liquid after throttling, but does NOT increase the specific refrigerating effect per kg of refrigerant passing through the evaporator!

2. Decision Tree
VCRS Cycle Property Evaluation?
├─ Identify Enthalpies → h1 (compressor inlet), h2 (compressor exit), h3=h4 (condenser exit / throttle)
├─ Compute Refrigerating Effect → RE = h1 - h4
├─ Compute Compressor Work → W = h2 - h1
└─ Calculate COP & HRR → COP = (h1 - h4)/(h2 - h1), HRR = 1 + 1/COP
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Throttling is isenthalpic $h_3 = h_4$.
  • [ ] $COP_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1}$.
  • [ ] $HRR = 1 + \frac{1}{COP_R}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a VCRS, enthalpy at compressor inlet is $h_1 = 1200 \text{ kJ/kg}$, compressor exit $h_2 = 1400 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and liquid leaving condenser $h_3 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate $COP$ and $HRR$. - Solution:

$${h_4 = h_3 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${RE = h_1 - h_4 = 1200 - 200 = 1000 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${W = h_2 - h_1 = 1400 - 1200 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}}$$
$${COP_R = \frac{RE}{W} = \frac{1000}{200} = 5.0}$$
$${HRR = 1 + \frac{1}{COP_R} = 1 + \frac{1}{5} = 1.20}$$
- Key Insight: $COP = 5.0$, $HRR = 1.20$.

3.5 Operating Parameter Effects & System Modifications

1. Concept Pointers
  • Evaporator Temperature $T_e \uparrow$: Higher suction pressure $\implies$ density of suction gas $\uparrow$, mass flow $\uparrow$, specific volume $\downarrow$, compressor work per kg $\downarrow$, refrigerating effect $\uparrow \implies COP \uparrow$.
  • Condenser Temperature $T_c \uparrow$: Higher discharge pressure $\implies$ compressor work per kg $\uparrow$, refrigerating effect $\downarrow \implies COP \downarrow$.
  • Sub-Cooling (Under-Cooling): Cooling liquid refrigerant below $T_{\text{sat}}$ before throttling $3 \to 3'$. Increases $RE$ $h_1 - h_4' > h_1 - h_4$ while compressor work stays unchanged $\implies COP \uparrow$.
  • Superheating: Heating suction vapour above $T_{\text{sat}}$ before compression $1 \to 1'$. Increases compressor work and discharge temperature $\implies COP$ usually decreases.
💡 Key Concept: Sub-Cooling Advantage: Sub-cooling ALWAYS improves $COP$ and increases refrigerating capacity without increasing compressor work!
2. Decision Tree
Parameter Modification Analysis?
├─ Sub-Cooling Liquid → RE increases, Work unchanged → COP increases
├─ Increase Evaporator Temp Te → RE increases, Work decreases → COP increases
├─ Increase Condenser Temp Tc → RE decreases, Work increases → COP decreases
└─ Superheating Suction Vapour → Work increases, Discharge Temp increases → COP usually decreases
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Sub-cooling increases $COP$.
  • [ ] Higher $T_e$ increases $COP$.
  • [ ] Higher $T_c$ decreases $COP$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Explain the effect of liquid sub-cooling on the $COP$ of a VCRS. - Solution: - Sub-cooling reduces liquid enthalpy at expansion valve inlet $h_3' < h_3 \implies h_4' < h_4$. - Refrigerating effect increases $h_1 - h_4' > h_1 - h_4$. - Compressor work $h_2 - h_1$ remains unaffected. - Therefore, $COP = \frac{RE}{W}$ increases. - Key Insight: Sub-cooling increases $COP$ by expanding the refrigerating effect.

3.6 System Components, Expansion Devices & Multi-Stage / Cascade Systems

1. Concept Pointers
  • Compressors:
  • Hermetic Reciprocating: Sealed motor + compressor unit; used in domestic refrigerators and window ACs.
  • Rotary: Used in small capacity systems (< 2 kW); low leakage.
  • Centrifugal: Used for large capacity systems (> 100 TR) with low pressure lifts.
  • Expansion Devices:
  • Capillary Tube: Fixed restriction; operates via wall friction and momentum drop; low starting torque required; cannot handle variable loads.
  • Thermostatic Expansion Valve (TEV): Controls flow to maintain constant superheat at evaporator exit.
  • Condenser Types:
  • Air-cooled $\le 3 \text{ TR}$
  • Water-cooled $3 - 50 \text{ TR}$
  • Evaporative $> 50 \text{ TR}$
  • Cascade & Multi-Stage:
  • Used for ultra-low temperatures ($-40^\circ\text{C}$ to $-80^\circ\text{C}$) to limit pressure ratios.
  • Optimum intermediate temperature: $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}$.
💡 Key Concept: Capillary Tube Function: Capillary tube relies on friction and pressure drop across narrow bore tubing. It requires NO moving parts and requires LOW starting torque motors!
2. Decision Tree
Expansion Device & Component Selection?
├─ Small Domestic Refrigerator / Window AC → Capillary Tube + Hermetic Compressor
├─ Variable Load Commercial System → Thermostatic Expansion Valve (TEV)
└─ Ultra-Low Temperature (-40°C to -80°C) → Cascade System (Optimum T_inter = sqrt(T_H * T_L))
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Capillary tube: Fixed restriction, low starting torque.
  • [ ] TEV: Maintains constant evaporator exit superheat.
  • [ ] Cascade optimum temperature $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H T_L}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A cascade refrigeration system operates between $T_H = 50^\circ\text{C}$ $323 \text{ K}$ and $T_L = -90^\circ\text{C}$ $183 \text{ K}$. Calculate the optimum intermediate temperature. - Solution:

$${T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L} = \sqrt{323 \times 183} = \sqrt{59109} \approx 243.12 \text{ K}}$$
$${T_{\text{opt}}(^\circ\text{C}) = 243.12 - 273.15 = -30.03^\circ\text{C} \approx -30^\circ\text{C}}$$
- Key Insight: Use Kelvin for geometric mean, then convert to Celsius.

3.7 Refrigerant Designation, Properties & Leak Detection

1. Concept Pointers
  • ASHRAE Designation:
  • Organic: $R-(C-1)(H+1)F$.
    • $R-12 (\text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2): C=1 \implies 0, H=0 \implies 1, F=2 \implies R-12$.
    • $R-22 (\text{CHClF}_2): C=1 \implies 0, H=1 \implies 2, F=2 \implies R-22$.
    • $R-134a (\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{F}_4): C=2 \implies 1, H=2 \implies 3, F=4 \implies R-134a$.
  • Inorganic: $R-(700 + M)$.
    • Ammonia $NH_3, M=17 \implies R-717$.
    • Water $H_{2O}, M=18 \implies R-718$.
    • Carbon Dioxide $CO_2, M=44 \implies R-744$.
    • Air $M=29 \implies R-729$.
  • Desirable Properties: High latent heat $h_{fg}$, low freezing point, high critical temperature, positive evaporator pressure (above atmospheric to prevent air ingress), non-toxic, non-flammable.
  • Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP): Reference baseline $ODP_{\text{R-11}} = 1.0$.
  • Leak Detection Methods:
  • Freon / CFC / HCFC / HFC: Halide Torch (flame turns bright green).
  • Ammonia $NH_3$: Burning Sulphur Sticks (dense white smoke $NH_{4Cl} / (NH_4)_{2SO}_4$).
💡 Key Concept: Leak Detection Matching: - Halogenated Refrigerants (Freons) $\implies$ Halide Torch (Green Flame) - Ammonia $NH_3$ $\implies$ Sulphur Stick (White Smoke)

[!TRAP] Ammonia & Copper Pipe Incompatibility: Ammonia ATTACKS copper and copper alloys! Ammonia systems MUST use steel or iron piping!

2. Decision Tree
Refrigerant Code & Leak Check?
├─ Organic Chemical Formula → R-(C-1)(H+1)F
├─ Inorganic Compound → R-(700 + Molecular Weight)
├─ Leak Test for Freon → Halide Torch (Green Flame)
└─ Leak Test for Ammonia → Sulphur Stick (White Smoke, Steel Piping Only!)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $R-11$ has $ODP = 1.0$.
  • [ ] $R-717 = NH_3$, $R-718 = H_{2O}$, $R-744 = CO_2$.
  • [ ] Halide torch for Freon, Sulphur stick for Ammonia.
  • [ ] Ammonia attacks copper $\implies$ Use steel pipes.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Determine the ASHRAE refrigerant designation for Dichlorodifluoromethane $\text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2$. - Solution: - Formula: $\text{C}_1 \text{H}_0 \text{Cl}_2 \text{F}_2$ - $C = 1 \implies C - 1 = 0$ - $H = 0 \implies H + 1 = 1$ - $F = 2 \implies F = 2$ - Designation: $R-012 = R-12$. - Key Insight: $R-12 = \text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2$.

3.8 Vapour Absorption Refrigeration Systems (VARS) & Electrolux

1. Concept Pointers
  • VARS replaces mechanical compressor with Absorber, Generator, and Pump. Low power consumption since pumping liquid requires minimal work compared to compressing gas.
  • First Law Energy Balance:
    $${\boxed{Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A}}$$
  • Ideal Carnot $COP$ of VARS:
    $${\boxed{COP_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)}}$$
  • Refrigerant-Absorbent Pairs:
  • Aqua-Ammonia System: Refrigerant $= NH_3$, Absorbent $= H_{2O}$ (Analyzer & Rectifier required to trap water vapour).
  • Lithium Bromide System: Refrigerant $= H_{2O}$, Absorbent $= LiBr$ (Water is refrigerant $\implies$ cannot operate below $0^\circ\text{C}$).
  • Electrolux (3-Fluid) Domestic Refrigerator:
  • Uses Ammonia $NH_3$, Water $H_{2O}$, and Hydrogen $H_2$.
  • Hydrogen provides partial pressure reduction in evaporator to produce cooling without ANY moving pump or motor! Completely silent.
💡 Key Concept: VARS COP Ideal Formula: $COP_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)$. It is the product of Carnot Engine Efficiency operating between $T_G$ and $T_O$ and Carnot Refrigerator $COP$ operating between $T_E$ and $T_O$.

[!TRAP] LiBr System Limit: In $LiBr - H_{2O}$ absorption systems, WATER is the refrigerant. Therefore, it CANNOT be used for refrigeration temperatures below $0^\circ\text{C}$!

2. Decision Tree
VARS System Check?
├─ Energy Balance → Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A
├─ Ideal COP → COP = [T_E / (T_G - T_E)] * [(T_G - T_O) / T_O]
├─ Pair: NH3-H2O → NH3 is refrigerant, H2O is absorbent (Requires Rectifier)
├─ Pair: LiBr-H2O → H2O is refrigerant, LiBr is absorbent (Cannot go below 0°C)
└─ Electrolux 3-Fluid → NH3 + H2O + H2 (No pump, completely silent)

  • [ ] Electrolux uses Hydrogen for partial pressure reduction (no pump).
  • $LiBr$ system uses water as refrigerant (limited to $>0^\circ\text{C}$).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In a VARS, heat is supplied in the generator at $100^\circ\text{C}$ $373 \text{ K}$, refrigeration occurs at $-10^\circ\text{C}$ $263 \text{ K}$, and heat rejection to environment is at $30^\circ\text{C}$ $303 \text{ K}$. Calculate maximum ideal $COP$. - Solution:

$${T_G = 373 \text{ K}, \quad T_E = 263 \text{ K}, \quad T_O = 303 \text{ K}}$$
$${COP_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)}$$
$${\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E} = \frac{263}{373 - 263} = \frac{263}{110} \approx 2.391}$$
$${\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O} = \frac{373 - 303}{303} = \frac{70}{303} \approx 0.231}$$
$${COP_{\text{ideal}} = 2.391 \times 0.231 \approx 0.552}$$
- Key Insight: $COP_{\text{ideal}} = 0.552$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Define 1 Tonne of Refrigeration (TR) and state its practical equivalent in kW.
A: 1 TR is the rate of heat removal required to freeze $1000 \text{ kg}$ of pure water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ in $24 \text{ hours}$. Practical engineering value $= 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.

Q2: What is the relationship between $COP$ of a Carnot Heat Pump and a Carnot Refrigerator?
A: $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$ for the same operating temperature limits.

Q3: How does the pressure ratio affect $COP$ in a Bell-Coleman air refrigeration cycle?
A: Increasing the pressure ratio $r_p$ decreases the $COP$ of the air refrigeration cycle $COP = \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} - 1}$.

Q4: Name the four standard processes of a simple Vapour Compression Refrigeration System (VCRS).
A: 1. Isentropic compression $1 \to 2$. 2. Isobaric heat rejection in condenser $2 \to 3$. 3. Isenthalpic throttling in expansion valve $3 \to 4, h_3 = h_4$. 4. Isobaric heat absorption in evaporator $4 \to 1$.

Q5: Define Heat Rejection Ratio $HRR$ and state its relationship with $COP$.
A: $HRR = \frac{Q_{\text{cond}}}{Q_R} = 1 + \frac{1}{COP_R}$.

Q6: What is the effect of liquid sub-cooling on the performance of VCRS?
A: Sub-cooling increases the specific refrigerating effect $h_1 - h_4'$ while keeping compressor work unchanged, thereby increasing $COP$.

Q7: How does increasing evaporator temperature $T_e$ affect VCRS performance?
A: Increasing $T_e$ increases refrigerating effect, reduces compressor work per kg, and increases $COP$.

Q8: What is the primary function of a capillary tube in a domestic refrigerator?
A: It acts as a fixed restriction expansion device, dropping refrigerant pressure from condenser to evaporator via wall friction and momentum drop.

Q9: What is the optimum intermediate temperature for a two-stage cascade refrigeration system?
A: $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}$ (geometric mean of high and low absolute temperatures).

Q10: State the ASHRAE designation formula for organic halocarbon refrigerants.
A: $R-(C-1)(H+1)F$.

Q11: What are the ASHRAE designations for Ammonia, Water, Carbon Dioxide, and Air?
A: Ammonia $= R-717$, Water $= R-718$, Carbon Dioxide $= R-744$, Air $= R-729$.

Q12: How are leaks detected in Freon and Ammonia systems?
A: Freon leaks are detected using a Halide Torch (flame turns bright green). Ammonia leaks are detected using burning Sulphur Sticks (dense white smoke).

Q13: Why is copper piping unsuitable for ammonia refrigeration systems?
A: Ammonia chemically attacks and corrodes copper and copper alloys in the presence of moisture. Steel or iron pipes must be used.

Q14: Write the First Law energy balance equation for a Vapour Absorption Refrigeration System (VARS).
A: $Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A$ (Generator heat + Evaporator heat $=$ Condenser heat + Absorber heat).

Q15: What is the unique feature of the Electrolux (3-fluid) absorption refrigerator?
A: It uses Ammonia $NH_3$, Water $H_{2O}$, and Hydrogen $H_2$. Hydrogen reduces ammonia partial pressure in the evaporator, allowing refrigeration without any mechanical pump or moving parts.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$: Always convert tons to kW first.
  • $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$: Essential identity for Carnot heat pump vs refrigerator.
  • Isenthalpic Throttling: VCRS expansion is $h_3 = h_4$, NOT isentropic $s_3 \neq s_4$.
  • Sub-Cooling vs Superheating: Sub-cooling ALWAYS increases $COP$. Superheating increases work and usually decreases $COP$.
  • Ammonia Material Limits: Use STEEL pipes for $NH_3$. Copper is strictly forbidden.
  • Leak Tests: Halide torch $\implies$ Green flame (Freon). Sulphur stick $\implies$ White smoke $NH_3$.
  • LiBr Limit: $LiBr - H_{2O}$ system cannot operate below $0^\circ\text{C}$ because water is the refrigerant.
