 Dimensionless Ratio of condenser heat rejected $Q_H$ to evaporator heat absorbed $Q_R$.

$${\boxed{\text{RE}_{\text{subcooled}} = h_1 - h_4' > h_1 - h_4}}$$
 $\text{kJ/kg}$ $h_4'$ = enthalpy after sub-cooling + throttling $h_4' < h_4$. Work $W$ unchanged.

$${\boxed{T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}}}$$
 $\text{K}$ Geometric mean temperature for maximum combined COP in 2-stage cascade.

$${\boxed{R\text{-}(C-1)(H+1)F}}$$
 Dimensionless $C, H, F$ = atom counts. Chlorine fills remaining valence bonds.
VARS Energy Balance
$${\boxed{Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A}}$$

$${\boxed{\text{COP}_{\text{VARS}} = \frac{Q_E}{Q_G + W_P} \approx \frac{Q_E}{Q_G}}}$$

$${\boxed{\text{COP}_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \times \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)}}$$
 Dimensionless $T_E$ = evaporator, $T_G$ = generator, $T_O$ = ambient sink (all in Kelvin).

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Tonne of Refrigeration (TR): The standard refrigeration unit defined as the rate of heat extraction required to freeze $1000 \text{ kg}$ of pure water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ in $24 \text{ hours}$ $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.
  • Reversed Carnot Cycle: The ideal theoretical refrigeration cycle consisting of two reversible isothermal heat-transfer processes and two reversible isentropic expansion/compression processes. Gives maximum COP between fixed temperatures.
  • Bell-Coleman / Reversed Brayton Cycle: An air-refrigeration cycle consisting of two isentropic processes and two constant-pressure processes. Operates entirely in the gas phase without phase change; standard for aircraft cabin cooling.
  • Vapour Compression Refrigeration System (VCRS): The standard commercial refrigeration cycle using phase change of a refrigerant across 4 processes: isentropic compression, isobaric condensation, isenthalpic throttling, and isobaric evaporation.
  • Isenthalpic Throttling: The irreversible expansion process across a capillary tube or expansion valve where specific enthalpy remains constant $h_3 = h_4$.
  • Sub-Cooling (Under-Cooling): The cooling of liquid refrigerant in the condenser below its saturation temperature $T_3 < T_{\text{sat}}$, which increases refrigerating effect and COP without changing compressor work.
  • Superheating: Heating refrigerant vapour above its saturation temperature before compressor entry. Ensures dry compression and prevents liquid hammer.
  • Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP): Non-dimensional index of a chemical's ability to destroy stratospheric ozone relative to baseline reference $R\text{-}11$ $\text{ODP of } R\text{-}11 = 1.0$.
  • Azeotrope: A blend of two or more refrigerants that behaves as a single pure substance with a constant boiling point and no temperature glide ($R\text{-}500$ series).
  • Hermetic Compressor: An enclosed assembly where the electric drive motor and compressor mechanism are sealed together inside a welded steel shell, eliminating shaft seals and refrigerant leakage.
  • Vapour Absorption Refrigeration System (VARS): A thermal energy-driven refrigeration system where the mechanical compressor is replaced by an absorber, a liquid pump, and a generator (heat-driven).
  • Aqua-Ammonia System: A VARS pair using Ammonia $\text{NH}_3$ as refrigerant and Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ as absorbent. Requires a rectifier to remove water vapour.
  • Lithium-Bromide Water System: A VARS pair using Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ as refrigerant and Lithium Bromide $\text{LiBr}$ as absorbent. Operates above $0^\circ\text{C}$ for air conditioning.

3.1 Units of Refrigeration, Refrigerating Capacity & Power Relations

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition of 1 Tonne of Refrigeration (TR):
  • Heat removal rate required to freeze $1000 \text{ kg}$ of water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ into ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ in $24 \text{ hours}$.
  • Practical value used in all numerical exams: $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.
  • Theoretical value: $\frac{1000 \times 335}{24 \times 60} = 232.6 \text{ kJ/min} = 3.516 \text{ kW}$ (or $3.87 \text{ kW}$ in old US short-ton definitions). Always use $3.5 \text{ kW}$ unless explicitly instructed.
  • Capacity and Power Calculations:
  • Refrigerating Capacity $Q_R$ in $\text{kW} = \text{Capacity in TR} \times 3.5$.
  • Mass flow rate $\dot{m} = \frac{Q_R}{\text{Refrigerating Effect (RE)}} = \frac{Q_R}{h_1 - h_4}$.
  • Power required by prime mover:
    $${\boxed{W = \frac{Q_R}{\text{COP}_R}}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Units Conversion Rule: Always convert refrigeration capacity given in Tons into kW by multiplying by $3.5$ before applying $W = Q_R / \text{COP}_R$ or energy balance equations!

[!TRAP] Theoretical vs Practical TR Trap: Do NOT use $3.87 \text{ kW}$ or $232.6 \text{ kJ/min}$ in numerical problems unless the question specifically specifies "theoretical exact ton". State AE exams universally use $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Refrigeration Power Requirement - Problem: A refrigeration plant has a capacity of $6 \text{ TR}$ and operates with a COP of $6.0$. Calculate the minimum power input required to drive the compressor in kW. - Solution: 1. Convert capacity from TR to kW:

$${Q_R = 6 \times 3.5 = 21.0 \text{ kW}}$$
2. Calculate power input $W$:
$${W = \frac{Q_R}{\text{COP}} = \frac{21.0}{6.0} = 3.5 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: $W = 3.5 \text{ kW}$. Converting TR to kW first is mandatory.

Worked Example 2: Heat Rejection from Capacity and Power - Problem: A 10 TR refrigerator requires $7.0 \text{ kW}$ power input. Calculate: (a) COP of the refrigerator, (b) Condenser heat rejection rate in kW. - Solution: 1. Refrigeration capacity $Q_R = 10 \times 3.5 = 35.0 \text{ kW}$. 2. Calculate $\text{COP}_R$:

$${\text{COP}_R = \frac{Q_R}{W} = \frac{35.0}{7.0} = 5.0}$$
3. Calculate condenser heat rejection rate $Q_H$:
$${Q_H = Q_R + W = 35.0 + 7.0 = 42.0 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}_R = 5.0$, $Q_H = 42.0 \text{ kW}$. $Q_H = Q_R + W$ applies to all steady cycles.

Worked Example 3: Refrigerant Mass Flow Rate Calculation - Problem: A VCRS plant has a capacity of $5 \text{ TR}$. The refrigerating effect per kg of refrigerant is $\text{RE} = 175 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate the mass flow rate of refrigerant $\dot{m}$ in $\text{kg/s}$. - Solution: 1. Convert capacity to kW:

$${Q_R = 5 \times 3.5 = 17.5 \text{ kW} = 17.5 \text{ kJ/s}}$$
2. Calculate mass flow rate $\dot{m}$:
$${\dot{m} = \frac{Q_R}{\text{RE}} = \frac{17.5}{175} = 0.10 \text{ kg/s}}$$
- Key Insight: $\dot{m} = 0.10 \text{ kg/s}$. Simple ratio of total capacity to specific refrigerating effect.

3.2 Reversed Carnot Cycle & Air Refrigeration (Bell-Coleman / Aircraft)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reversed Carnot Cycle:
  • Operating between fixed temperatures $T_H$ and $T_L$ (in Kelvin).
  • Refrigerator COP: $\text{COP}_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{Q_L}{W}$.
  • Heat Pump COP: $\text{COP}_{HP} = \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{Q_H}{W} = \text{COP}_R + 1$.
  • Relation to Carnot Engine: $\text{COP}R = \frac{1 - \eta{\text{engine}}}{\eta_{\text{engine}}}$ and $\text{COP}{HP} = \frac{1}{\eta{\text{engine}}}$.
  • Air Refrigeration (Bell-Coleman / Reversed Brayton Cycle):
  • Working fluid remains gas (air) throughout without phase change.
  • Four processes: (1-2) Isentropic compression, (2-3) Isobaric heat rejection, (3-4) Isentropic expansion, (4-1) Isobaric heat absorption.
  • Ideal COP formula:
    $${\boxed{\text{COP}_{\text{air}} = \frac{1}{r_p^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} - 1}}}$$
  • Key Trend: As pressure ratio $r_p$ INCREASES, COP DECREASES (opposite of gas-turbine Brayton engine efficiency!).
  • Aircraft Cooling Application: Air cycle is universally chosen for aircraft cabin cooling because air is freely available, system weight is extremely low, and no toxic refrigerant leaks can occur.
💡 Key Concept: Heat Pump vs Refrigerator COP Identity: For identical operating temperature limits $T_H$ and $T_L$, the COP of a Heat Pump is ALWAYS EXACTLY 1 GREATER than the COP of a Refrigerator $\text{COP}_{HP} = \text{COP}_R + 1$.

[!TRAP] Temperature Units & Pressure Ratio Trend: 1. Absolute temperatures in Kelvin $T + 273.15$ MUST be used in all Carnot equations! 2. In Bell-Coleman air refrigeration, increasing pressure ratio $r_p$ DECREASES COP!

  • [ ] $\text{COP}_{HP} = \text{COP}_R + 1$.
  • [ ] $\text{COP}{HP} = 1 / \eta{\text{Carnot}}$.
  • [ ] Bell-Coleman COP $= \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} - 1}$.
  • [ ] Bell-Coleman COP decreases as $r_p \uparrow$.
  • [ ] Aircraft cooling uses air cycle due to low weight.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Carnot Refrigerator Work Calculation - Problem: A Carnot refrigerator operates between $-23^\circ\text{C}$ and $27^\circ\text{C}$. If it absorbs $1.0 \text{ kW}$ of heat from the cold space, calculate the required power input $W$. - Solution: 1. Convert temperatures to Kelvin:

$${T_L = -23 + 273 = 250 \text{ K}, \quad T_H = 27 + 273 = 300 \text{ K}}$$
2. Calculate Carnot Refrigerator COP:
$${\text{COP}_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{250}{300 - 250} = \frac{250}{50} = 5.0}$$
3. Calculate power input $W$:
$${W = \frac{Q_L}{\text{COP}_R} = \frac{1.0}{5.0} = 0.20 \text{ kW} = 200 \text{ W}}$$
- Key Insight: $W = 0.20 \text{ kW}$. Always convert temperatures to Kelvin ($250 \text{ K}$ and $300 \text{ K}$).

Worked Example 2: Heat Pump vs Refrigerator COP - Problem: A Carnot heat engine has a thermal efficiency of $25\%$ $\eta = 0.25$. If the cycle is reversed to operate as: (a) a Refrigerator, (b) a Heat Pump, calculate the respective COPs. - Solution: 1. Calculate Refrigerator COP:

$${\text{COP}R = \frac{1 - \eta}{\eta} = \frac{1 - 0.25}{0.25} = \frac{0.75}{0.25} = 3.0}$$
2. Calculate Heat Pump COP:
$${\text{COP}{HP} = \frac{1}{\eta} = \frac{1}{0.25} = 4.0 \quad (\text{or } \text{COP}R + 1 = 3 + 1 = 4.0)}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}_R = 3.0$, $\text{COP}{HP} = 4.0$. $\text{COP}_{HP} = \text{COP}_R + 1$ holds true.

Worked Example 3: Bell-Coleman Air Cycle COP - Problem: A Bell-Coleman air refrigeration cycle operates between pressure limits giving a pressure ratio $r_p = 4.0$. For air with $\gamma = 1.40$, calculate the ideal COP. - Solution: 1. Calculate exponent $\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma} = \frac{0.40}{1.40} = \frac{2}{7} \approx 0.2857$. 2. Calculate pressure ratio term $r_p^{0.2857} = 4^{0.2857} \approx 1.486$. 3. Calculate COP:

$${\text{COP}{\text{air}} = \frac{1}{1.486 - 1.0} = \frac{1}{0.486} \approx 2.058}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}{\text{air}} = 2.06$. Increasing $r_p$ further to $6.0$ would drop COP to $1.49$.

3.3 Vapour Compression Refrigeration System (VCRS) & Operating Parameters

1. Concept Pointers
  • VCRS Four Processes:
  • 1-2 (Compressor): Isentropic compression $s_1 = s_2$ from low-pressure dry vapour to high-pressure superheated vapour.
  • 2-3 (Condenser): Isobaric heat rejection $p_2 = p_3$ to surrounding air/water. Exits as saturated liquid.
  • 3-4 (Expansion Device): Isenthalpic throttling $h_3 = h_4$ across capillary or valve. Exits as wet mixture.
  • 4-1 (Evaporator): Isobaric heat absorption $p_4 = p_1$ from refrigerated space.
  • Enthalpy COP & Heat Rejection Ratio:
  • Refrigerating Effect $\text{RE} = h_1 - h_4 = h_1 - h_3$.
  • Compressor Work $W = h_2 - h_1$.
  • Condenser Heat $Q_H = h_2 - h_3$.
  • $\text{COP}_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1}$.
  • Heat Rejection Ratio $\text{HRR} = \frac{Q_H}{Q_R} = \frac{h_2 - h_3}{h_1 - h_4} = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R}$.
  • Effects of Operating Parameters:
  • Increase in Evaporator Temperature $T_e \uparrow$: $\text{COP} \uparrow$, Refrigerating Effect $\text{RE} \uparrow$, Compressor Work $W \downarrow$.
  • Increase in Condenser Temperature $T_c \uparrow$: $\text{COP} \downarrow$, $\text{RE} \downarrow$, $W \uparrow$.
  • Sub-Cooling Liquid $T_3 < T_{\text{sat}}$: Shifts point 3 left $\implies h_4' < h_4 \implies \text{RE} \uparrow$ and $\text{COP} \uparrow$. Work $W$ remains UNCHANGED!
  • Superheating Vapour $T_1 > T_{\text{sat}}$: Increases work $W$; net COP usually decreases (except R-12 in mild ranges).
💡 Key Concept: Sub-Cooling Benefit: Sub-cooling liquid refrigerant after the condenser increases refrigerating effect $h_1 - h_4'$ and COP without increasing compressor work!

[!TRAP] Throttling Enthalpy Identity: Expansion across a capillary tube or expansion valve is ISENTHALPIC $h_3 = h_4$, NOT isentropic $s_3 \neq s_4$. On a p-h chart, throttling appears as a vertical line.

3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] VCRS processes: 1-2 Isentropic $s_1=s_2$, 2-3 Isobaric, 3-4 Isenthalpic $h_3=h_4$, 4-1 Isobaric.
  • [ ] $\text{COP}_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1}$.
  • [ ] $\text{HRR} = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R}$.
  • [ ] $T_e \uparrow \implies \text{COP} \uparrow$; $T_c \uparrow \implies \text{COP} \downarrow$.
  • [ ] Sub-cooling increases RE and COP without changing work.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: VCRS COP Calculation from Enthalpies - Problem: In a simple VCRS cycle, enthalpy at compressor entry $h_1 = 1200 \text{ kJ/kg}$, enthalpy at compressor exit $h_2 = 1400 \text{ kJ/kg}$, and enthalpy at condenser exit $h_3 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate: (a) Refrigerating effect, (b) Compressor work, (c) $\text{COP}_R$, (d) Heat Rejection Ratio (HRR). - Solution: 1. Throttling process $h_4 = h_3 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 2. Refrigerating effect $\text{RE} = h_1 - h_4 = 1200 - 200 = 1000 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 3. Compressor work $W = h_2 - h_1 = 1400 - 1200 = 200 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 4. Calculate $\text{COP}_R$:

$${\text{COP}_R = \frac{\text{RE}}{W} = \frac{1000}{200} = 5.0}$$
5. Calculate HRR:
$${\text{HRR} = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R} = 1 + \frac{1}{5.0} = 1 + 0.20 = 1.20}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}_R = 5.0$, $\text{HRR} = 1.20$. Direct application of enthalpy equations.

Worked Example 2: Heat Rejection Ratio to COP Conversion - Problem: A vapour compression refrigeration plant has a Heat Rejection Ratio $\text{HRR} = 1.25$. Determine the COP of the refrigerator. - Solution: 1. Apply HRR formula:

$${\text{HRR} = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R} \implies 1.25 = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R}}$$
2. Solve for $\text{COP}_R$:
$${\frac{1}{\text{COP}_R} = 1.25 - 1.0 = 0.25 \implies \text{COP}_R = \frac{1}{0.25} = 4.0}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}_R = 4.0$. Quick one-line calculation.

Worked Example 3: Effect of Sub-Cooling on Refrigerating Effect - Problem: In a VCRS system with $h_1 = 1400 \text{ kJ/kg}$ and saturated liquid enthalpy $h_3 = 400 \text{ kJ/kg}$, sub-cooling lowers liquid enthalpy at expansion inlet to $h_3' = 360 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate the percentage increase in refrigerating effect. - Solution: 1. Original refrigerating effect $\text{RE} = 1400 - 400 = 1000 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 2. Sub-cooled refrigerating effect $\text{RE}' = 1400 - 360 = 1040 \text{ kJ/kg}$. 3. Percentage increase:

$${\% \text{ Increase} = \frac{1040 - 1000}{1000} \times 100\% = \frac{40}{1000} \times 100\% = 4.0\%}$$
- Key Insight: Sub-cooling increased refrigerating effect by $4.0\%$ without changing compressor work.

3.4 Refrigerants, Designations, Desirable Properties & Leak Detection

1. Concept Pointers
  • ASHRAE Designation Rules:
  • Inorganic Refrigerants (700 Series): $R\text{-}(700 + \text{MW})$.
    • Ammonia $\text{NH}_3, \text{MW} = 17$: $R\text{-}717$.
    • Water $\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{MW} = 18$: $R\text{-}718$.
    • Carbon Dioxide $\text{CO}_2, \text{MW} = 44$: $R\text{-}744$.
    • Air $\text{MW} = 29$: $R\text{-}729$.
  • Organic Refrigerants: $R\text{-}(C-1)(H+1)F$ (Chlorine fills remaining valence).
    • $R\text{-}11 (\text{CCl}_3\text{F})$: $C=1 \to 0, H=0 \to 1, F=1 \implies R\text{-}11$. (Baseline ODP $= 1.0$).
    • $R\text{-}12 (\text{CCl}_2\text{F}_2)$: $C=1 \to 0, H=0 \to 1, F=2 \implies R\text{-}12$.
    • $R\text{-}22 (\text{CHClF}_2)$: $C=1 \to 0, H=1 \to 2, F=2 \implies R\text{-}22$.
    • $R\text{-}134a (\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{F}_4)$: Zero ODP HFC eco-friendly alternative.
  • Desirable Properties:
  • High Latent Heat of Vaporisation: Minimizes refrigerant mass flow rate for a given capacity.
  • Low Freezing Point: Prevents refrigerant solidification in evaporator.
  • Positive Evaporator Pressure: Prevents ambient air and moisture ingress.
  • High Critical Temperature: Ensures condensation at ambient temperatures.
  • Leak Detection Methods:
  • Freon / Halocarbons: Halide torch (flame changes from blue to bright green).
  • Ammonia $\text{NH}_3$: Sulphur sticks (dense white smoke of ammonium sulphite forms).
  • Ammonia Material Constraint: Ammonia corrodes copper and brass when moist $\implies$ MUST use steel tubing exclusively!
💡 Key Concept: Leak Detection & Material Compatibility Rules: - Freon Leaks: Halide Torch $\implies$ Bright Green Flame. - Ammonia Leaks: Sulphur Stick $\implies$ Dense White Smoke. - Ammonia Piping: Steel ONLY (Copper is severely corroded by Ammonia!).

[!TRAP] ODP Baseline Trap: Ozone Depletion Potential (ODP) is measured relative to R-11 $\text{CCl}_3\text{F}$ which is assigned $\text{ODP} = 1.0$. R-134a has $\text{ODP} = 0$.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Organic Refrigerant Designation Decoding - Problem: Determine the chemical formula for refrigerant $R\text{-}22$. - Solution: 1. Compare $R\text{-}22$ with $R\text{-}(C-1)(H+1)F$: - $C - 1 = 0 \implies C = 1$ carbon atom. - $H + 1 = 2 \implies H = 1$ hydrogen atom. - $F = 2$ fluorine atoms. 2. Methane derivative has 4 total valence bonds: - Chlorine atoms $= 4 - (H + F) = 4 - (1 + 2) = 1$ chlorine atom. 3. Chemical formula is $\text{CHClF}_2$ (Monochlorodifluoromethane). - Key Insight: $R\text{-}22 = \text{CHClF}_2$.

Worked Example 2: Inorganic Refrigerant Code Matching - Problem: What is the ASHRAE designation number for Ammonia $\text{NH}_3$? - Solution: 1. Molecular weight of Ammonia $\text{NH}_3$:

$${\text{MW} = 14 + (3 \times 1) = 17 \text{ g/mol}}$$
2. Apply inorganic formula:
$${R = 700 + \text{MW} = 700 + 17 = 717 \implies R\text{-}717}$$
- Key Insight: Ammonia $= R\text{-}717$.

Worked Example 3: R-134a Chemical Formula Decoding - Problem: Decode the chemical structure of eco-friendly refrigerant $R\text{-}134a$. - Solution: 1. Compare $134$ with $(C-1)(H+1)F$: - $C - 1 = 1 \implies C = 2$ carbon atoms (ethane derivative). - $H + 1 = 3 \implies H = 2$ hydrogen atoms. - $F = 4$ fluorine atoms. 2. Ethane derivative has $2 C + 2 = 6$ total bonds: - Chlorine atoms $= 6 - (H + F) = 6 - (2 + 4) = 0$ chlorine atoms. 3. Chemical formula is $\text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{F}_4$ (Tetrafluoroethane). Suffix 'a' indicates asymmetric structure $\text{CF}_3\text{CH}_2\text{F}$. - Key Insight: $R\text{-}134a = \text{C}_2\text{H}_2\text{F}_4$ has ZERO chlorine atoms, giving it $\text{ODP} = 0$.

3.5 System Components, Multi-stage, Cascade & Absorption Systems (VARS)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Expansion Devices:
  • Capillary Tube: Fixed-area copper tube producing isenthalpic throttling by friction and momentum change. Used in small hermetic systems (domestic refrigerators, water coolers, window ACs). Has no moving parts and cannot control capacity.
  • Thermostatic Expansion Valve (TEV): Regulates flow to maintain constant superheat at evaporator exit.
  • Evaporators & Accumulators:
  • Flooded Evaporator: Heat transfer surface covered by liquid refrigerant. Requires an Accumulator on compressor suction line to trap liquid droplets and prevent compressor liquid hammer.
  • Multi-Stage & Cascade Systems:
  • Cascade System: Uses two separate refrigerant loops with different refrigerants for ultra-low temperatures $-40^\circ\text{C} \text{ to } -80^\circ\text{C}$. Optimum intermediate temperature $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}$.
  • Vapour Absorption Refrigeration System (VARS):
  • Replaces mechanical compressor with Absorber + Liquid Pump + Generator (driven by low-grade thermal energy/waste heat/steam).
  • Energy Balance (neglecting pump work):
    $${\boxed{Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A}}$$
  • Ideal Carnot COP:
    $${\boxed{\text{COP}_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \times \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)}}$$
  • Aqua-Ammonia Pair: $\text{NH}_3$ = Refrigerant, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ = Absorbent. Needs Rectifier/Analyser to remove water vapour.
  • LiBr-Water Pair: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ = Refrigerant, $\text{LiBr}$ = Absorbent. Operates above $0^\circ\text{C}$ for air conditioning. Risk of LiBr crystallisation if condenser pressure drops.
  • Electrolux 3-Fluid System: $\text{NH}_3$ (refrigerant), $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ (absorbent), $\text{H}_2$ (inert gas). Uses bubble pump $\implies$ NO moving parts!
💡 Key Concept: Refrigerant-Absorbent Pair Mnemonic: - Aqua-Ammonia: Refrigerant = $\text{NH}_3$, Absorbent = $\text{H}_2\text{O}$. - LiBr-Water: Refrigerant = $\text{H}_2\text{O}$, Absorbent = $\text{LiBr}$. - Water is the REFRIGERANT in LiBr systems, but the ABSORBENT in Ammonia systems!

[!TRAP] VARS Heat Rejection Trap: Heat is rejected at TWO locations in a VARS plant: the Condenser $Q_C$ AND the Absorber $Q_A$. Total heat rejection $= Q_C + Q_A = Q_G + Q_E$.

  • [ ] TEV maintains constant superheat at evaporator exit.
  • [ ] Accumulator prevents liquid entering compressor in flooded systems.
  • [ ] Optimum cascade temperature $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}$.

  • [ ] Aqua-ammonia: $\text{NH}_3$ refrigerant, $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ absorbent.
  • [ ] LiBr-water: $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ refrigerant, $\text{LiBr}$ absorbent.
  • [ ] Electrolux system uses Hydrogen gas, no moving parts.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: VARS Heat Rejection and COP - Problem: A vapour absorption plant has a refrigeration capacity of $100 \text{ kW}$. The heat supplied to the generator is $160 \text{ kW}$. Neglecting liquid pump work, calculate: (a) Practical COP of the system, (b) Total heat rejected in the condenser and absorber combined. - Solution: 1. Calculate COP:

$${\text{COP} = \frac{Q_E}{Q_G} = \frac{100}{160} = 0.625}$$
2. Apply energy balance $Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A$:
$${Q_C + Q_A = 160 + 100 = 260 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP} = 0.625$, Total Heat Rejection $= 260 \text{ kW}$. Absorber and condenser together reject $260 \text{ kW}$.

Worked Example 2: Optimum Cascade Temperature - Problem: A two-stage cascade refrigeration system operates between a high condenser temperature $T_H = 47^\circ\text{C} = 320 \text{ K}$ and a low evaporator temperature $T_L = -73^\circ\text{C} = 200 \text{ K}$. Calculate the optimum intermediate cascade temperature $T_{\text{opt}}$ in ${^\circ}\text{C}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate $T_{\text{opt}}$ in Kelvin:

$${T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L} = \sqrt{320 \times 200} = \sqrt{64000} \approx 252.98 \text{ K}}$$
2. Convert to ${^\circ}\text{C}$:
$${T_{\text{opt}} = 252.98 - 273.15 \approx -20.17^\circ\text{C}}$$
- Key Insight: $T_{\text{opt}} \approx -20.2^\circ\text{C}$. Always take geometric mean in Kelvin.

Worked Example 3: Ideal VARS Carnot COP Calculation - Problem: An ideal VARS plant operates with generator temperature $T_G = 373 \text{ K} (100^\circ\text{C})$, evaporator temperature $T_E = 253 \text{ K} (-20^\circ\text{C})$, and ambient sink temperature $T_O = 298 \text{ K} (25^\circ\text{C})$. Calculate the ideal Carnot COP. - Solution: 1. Apply VARS ideal Carnot COP formula:

$${\text{COP}{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \times \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)}$$
2. Substitute values:
$${\text{COP}{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{253}{373 - 253}\right) \times \left(\frac{373 - 298}{298}\right) = \left(\frac{253}{120}\right) \times \left(\frac{75}{298}\right)}$$
$${\text{COP}{\text{ideal}} = 2.1083 \times 0.25168 \approx 0.5306}$$
- Key Insight: $\text{COP}{\text{ideal}} = 0.531$. Product of Carnot refrigerator COP $2.11$ and Carnot engine efficiency $0.252$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (25 Core Conceptual Pairs)

Q1: What is 1 Tonne of Refrigeration (TR) in kW and kJ/min?
A: $1 \text{ TR} = 3.5 \text{ kW} = 210 \text{ kJ/min}$ (theoretical value $= 3.516 \text{ kW} = 232.6 \text{ kJ/min}$).

Q2: Write the Carnot Refrigerator COP formula.
A: $\text{COP}_R = \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L} = \frac{Q_L}{W}$ (temperatures in Kelvin).

Q3: What is the relation between Heat Pump COP and Refrigerator COP for identical temperature limits?
A: $\text{COP}_{HP} = \text{COP}_R + 1$.

Q4: How does increasing pressure ratio $r_p$ affect COP in a Bell-Coleman air refrigeration cycle?
A: Increasing pressure ratio DECREASES COP $\text{COP} = \frac{1}{r_p^{(\gamma-1)/\gamma} - 1}$.

Q5: Why is the air refrigeration cycle used in aircraft cabin cooling?
A: Because air is freely available, unit weight per TR is extremely low, and no toxic refrigerant leaks can occur.

Q6: Name the four thermodynamic processes of a standard VCRS cycle.
A: (1-2) Isentropic compression, (2-3) Isobaric heat rejection, (3-4) Isenthalpic throttling, (4-1) Isobaric heat absorption.

Q7: Write the COP formula for a standard VCRS cycle in terms of enthalpies.
A: $\text{COP}_R = \frac{h_1 - h_4}{h_2 - h_1}$ (where $h_4 = h_3$).

Q8: Write the Heat Rejection Ratio (HRR) formula.
A: $\text{HRR} = \frac{Q_{\text{cond}}}{Q_{\text{evap}}} = 1 + \frac{1}{\text{COP}_R}$.

Q9: How does increasing evaporator temperature $T_e \uparrow$ affect VCRS performance?
A: Increases COP, increases refrigerating effect, and decreases compressor work.

Q10: What is the effect of sub-cooling liquid refrigerant on VCRS performance?
A: Increases refrigerating effect and COP, while compressor work remains unchanged.

Q11: What is the ASHRAE code for inorganic refrigerants?
A: $R\text{-}(700 + \text{MW})$ (e.g., $\text{NH}_3 = R\text{-}717$, $\text{H}_2\text{O} = R\text{-}718$, $\text{CO}_2 = R\text{-}744$, $\text{Air} = R\text{-}729$).

Q12: Which refrigerant is used as the reference baseline for Ozone Depletion Potential $\text{ODP} = 1.0$?
A: $R\text{-}11 (\text{CCl}_3\text{F})$.

Q13: How are leaks detected in Freon / Halocarbon refrigeration systems?
A: Using a Halide torch (the blue flame turns bright green upon detecting halogen gas).

Q14: How are leaks detected in Ammonia $\text{NH}_3$ refrigeration systems?
A: Using a Sulphur stick (reacts with leaking ammonia to produce dense white smoke).

Q15: Why must copper piping NEVER be used in Ammonia refrigeration plants?
A: Because ammonia severely corrodes copper and brass in the presence of moisture; steel piping must be used.

Q16: What expansion device is used in domestic refrigerators and window ACs?
A: Capillary tube (fixed-orifice isenthalpic throttling device).

Q17: What is the function of a Thermostatic Expansion Valve (TEV)?
A: Regulates refrigerant flow to maintain a constant degree of superheat at the evaporator exit.

Q18: What is the purpose of an accumulator placed on the compressor suction line?
A: Collects liquid refrigerant in flooded evaporator systems to prevent liquid slugging and compressor damage.

Q19: What is the optimum intermediate temperature for a two-stage cascade refrigeration system?
A: $T_{\text{opt}} = \sqrt{T_H \cdot T_L}$ (in Kelvin).

Q20: What three components replace the mechanical compressor in a Vapour Absorption System (VARS)?
A: Absorber, Liquid Pump, and Generator.

Q21: Write the energy balance equation for a VARS plant (neglecting pump work).
A: $Q_G + Q_E = Q_C + Q_A$ $Q_{\text{generator}} + Q_{\text{evaporator}} = Q_{\text{condenser}} + Q_{\text{absorber}}$.

Q22: Write the ideal Carnot COP formula for a VARS plant.
A: $\text{COP}_{\text{ideal}} = \left(\frac{T_E}{T_G - T_E}\right) \times \left(\frac{T_G - T_O}{T_O}\right)$.

Q23: Identify the refrigerant and absorbent in an Aqua-Ammonia absorption system.
A: Refrigerant $= \text{NH}_3$ (Ammonia); Absorbent $= \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Water).

Q24: Identify the refrigerant and absorbent in a Lithium-Bromide absorption system.
A: Refrigerant $= \text{H}_2\text{O}$ (Water); Absorbent $= \text{LiBr}$ (Lithium Bromide).

Q25: What working fluid provides circulation without a mechanical pump in an Electrolux 3-fluid refrigerator?
A: Hydrogen gas $\text{H}_2$ lowers ammonia partial pressure, while a bubble pump drives circulation without moving parts.
