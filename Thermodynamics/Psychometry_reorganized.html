1. Properties of Moist Air

Atmospheric air is a mixture of dry air and water vapour. The behaviour of this moist air mixture is the subject of psychrometry. Three temperature measurements define the moisture state of a given sample.

Dry-Bulb Temperature (DBT) is the actual temperature of the air–vapour mixture, measured by an ordinary thermometer. It is not affected by moisture.

Wet-Bulb Temperature (WBT) is the temperature measured by a thermometer whose bulb is covered with a wet wick and exposed to moving air. Evaporation from the wick cools the bulb. WBT is the equilibrium temperature of adiabatic saturation and is always less than or equal to DBT.

Dew-Point Temperature (DPT) is the temperature to which moist air must be cooled at constant pressure and constant moisture content before condensation begins. It depends only on the actual partial pressure of the water vapour $P_v$ in the mixture.

For unsaturated air: $\text{DBT} > \text{WBT} > \text{DPT}$.
At $100\%$ relative humidity (saturation): $\text{DBT} = \text{WBT} = \text{DPT}$.

Specific Humidity (Humidity Ratio) $\omega$

Specific humidity is the mass of water vapour per unit mass of dry air. It remains constant for any sensible heating or cooling process that does not add or remove moisture.

$$\boxed{\omega = 0.622\,\frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v}}$$
$\omega$ — specific humidity, kg wv / kg da $P_v$ — partial pressure of water vapour, kPa $P_t$ — total barometric pressure, kPa (standard: 101.325 kPa)

An equivalent form using relative humidity:

$$\omega = 0.622\,\frac{\phi\,P_{vs}}{P_t - \phi\,P_{vs}}$$
$P_{vs}$ — saturation pressure of water vapour at DBT, kPa $\phi$ — relative humidity (see below)

Relative Humidity $\phi$

$$\boxed{\phi = \frac{P_v}{P_{vs}}}$$
$\phi$ — dimensionless, expressed as a decimal or percentage $P_v$ — actual vapour partial pressure at DBT, kPa $P_{vs}$ — saturation vapour pressure at the same DBT, kPa

When moist air is heated at constant pressure without adding moisture, $P_v$ stays constant, so $\omega$ and DPT remain unchanged. However, $P_{vs}$ rises with temperature, causing $\phi$ to decrease.

Degree of Saturation $\mu$

$$\mu = \frac{\omega}{\omega_s} \approx \phi$$
$\omega_s$ — specific humidity of saturated air at the same DBT Numerically close to $\phi$ but not exactly equal

2. Enthalpy of Moist Air

The specific enthalpy of moist air is expressed per kilogram of dry air. It combines the sensible heat of the dry air and the enthalpy of the superheated water vapour.

$$\boxed{h = 1.005\,t + \omega\,(2500 + 1.88\,t) \quad \text{kJ/kg da}}$$
$t$ — dry-bulb temperature, $^\circ$C $\omega$ — specific humidity, kg/kg da $1.005$ kJ/kg·K — specific heat of dry air $C_{pa}$ $2500$ kJ/kg — latent heat of vaporisation at $0\,^\circ$C, $h_{fg,0}$ $1.88$ kJ/kg·K — specific heat of superheated water vapour $C_{pv}$

An equivalent grouped form:

$$h = (1.005 + 1.88\,\omega)\,t + 2500\,\omega$$

This equation is valid for moist air at standard atmospheric pressure (101.325 kPa) over the temperature range typical of HVAC applications ($-10\,^\circ\text{C}$ to $60\,^\circ\text{C}$).

3. The Psychrometric Chart

The psychrometric chart (Carrier chart) is a graphical representation of the thermodynamic properties of moist air. The standard chart plots DBT on the horizontal axis and $\omega$ (or $P_v$) on the vertical axis at a fixed total pressure of 101.325 kPa.

Line / CurveWhat It Represents
Vertical linesConstant DBT
Horizontal linesConstant $\omega$ (specific humidity)
Saturation curve (leftmost curved boundary)$\phi = 100\%$; DPT = WBT = DBT
Lines of constant WBTAlso lines of approximately constant enthalpy (inclined, slope down-right)
Lines of constant $\phi$Curves parallel to saturation curve

The DPT of a point on the chart is found by moving horizontally left (at constant $\omega$) until the saturation curve is reached. The WBT is found by moving up and left along the constant-WBT/enthalpy line.

Adiabatic Evaporative Cooling (Desert Cooler)

Water evaporates into the airstream. The latent heat of evaporation is supplied by the air itself, so the process is adiabatic. The process moves along a line of constant WBT and approximately constant enthalpy. DBT falls, $\omega$ rises. The air approaches the saturation curve but cannot cross it.

Cooling and Dehumidification

A cooling coil simultaneously lowers DBT below the dew point and removes moisture by condensation. For dehumidification to occur, the coil surface temperature (the Apparatus Dew Point, ADP) must be lower than the incoming air's dew-point temperature:

$$\text{Condition for dehumidification: } \text{ADP} < \text{DPT}_{\text{entering air}}$$

Chemical Dehumidification

A desiccant (silica gel, LiBr) absorbs moisture from the air. The latent heat released heats the air, so DBT rises and $\omega$ falls while enthalpy stays approximately constant. The process moves right and down on the chart.

5. Bypass Factor (BPF) & Apparatus Dew Point (ADP)

In any real coil, not all air contacts the coil surface. The fraction that bypasses the surface without thermal contact is the Bypass Factor. The ideal coil surface temperature — the temperature the leaving air would reach if all of it contacted the surface — is the Apparatus Dew Point (ADP). ADP is located where the extended process line meets the saturation curve on the psychrometric chart.

$$\boxed{BPF = \frac{T_2 - \text{ADP}}{T_1 - \text{ADP}}}$$
$T_1$ — entering air DBT, $^\circ$C $T_2$ — leaving air DBT, $^\circ$C ADP — Apparatus Dew Point (coil surface temperature), $^\circ$C For a cooling coil: $T_1 > T_2 > \text{ADP}$
$$\boxed{\eta_{\text{coil}} = 1 - BPF = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1 - \text{ADP}}}$$
$\eta_\text{coil}$ — coil contact efficiency
Key Points — BPF
  • Higher air velocity $\Rightarrow$ BPF increases (less contact time).
  • More tube rows $\Rightarrow$ BPF decreases (more contact surface).
  • Wider fin pitch $\Rightarrow$ BPF increases.
  • An ideal coil (BPF = 0) would bring all air to ADP temperature; a coil with BPF = 1 provides no conditioning.

6. Sensible Heat Factor (SHF)

Any space or process imposes both a sensible heat load (temperature change, no moisture change) and a latent heat load (moisture addition from occupants, infiltration, etc.). The ratio of sensible heat to total heat load characterises the process and determines the slope of the supply condition line on the psychrometric chart.

$$\boxed{SHF = \frac{Q_S}{Q_S + Q_L} = \frac{Q_S}{Q_T}}$$
$Q_S$ — sensible heat load, kW $Q_L$ — latent heat load, kW $Q_T$ — total heat load, kW
$$\text{Latent Heat Fraction} = 1 - SHF = \frac{Q_L}{Q_T}$$

When $SHF = 1$, the entire load is sensible — the process line is horizontal. When $SHF = 0$, the entire load is latent — the process line is vertical. High-occupancy spaces (auditoriums, gyms) have lower SHF values because of elevated moisture generation from breathing and perspiration.

The Room Sensible Heat Factor (RSHF) governs the room condition line slope. The Grand Sensible Heat Factor (GSHF) includes outdoor ventilation air loads in addition to the room loads.

7. Human Comfort & Effective Temperature

Effective Temperature (ET) is an empirical comfort index that combines the effects of dry-bulb temperature, relative humidity, and air velocity into a single equivalent value. It is defined as the temperature of still, saturated air ($RH = 100\%$, zero air velocity) that produces an identical sensation of warmth or cold to the actual environment.

At $100\%$ relative humidity, the air is already saturated and still (by definition), so $ET = DBT = WBT = DPT$. At lower humidity, the effective temperature is less than the DBT because evaporation from the skin provides additional cooling.

8. Cooling Towers

A cooling tower rejects heat from warm condenser water to the atmosphere by evaporating a small fraction of the water into an air stream. The primary cooling mechanism is evaporation (latent heat transfer), not sensible heat transfer. This is why the governing limit is the wet-bulb temperature of the incoming air, not its dry-bulb temperature.

Performance Parameters

$$\boxed{\text{Range} = T_{\text{water,in}} - T_{\text{water,out}}}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Approach} = T_{\text{water,out}} - WBT_{\text{air,in}}}$$
Range — temperature drop of the water through the tower, $^\circ$C Approach — how close the leaving water temperature gets to the entering air WBT, $^\circ$C

The theoretical minimum water exit temperature is the WBT of the entering air. In practice, the approach is always positive — the leaving water temperature can never equal or go below the entering air WBT.

Evaporative Energy Balance

$$\boxed{\dot{m}_w \cdot h_{fg} = \dot{m}_a \cdot C_p \cdot \Delta T_a}$$
$\dot{m}_w$ — mass flow rate of evaporated water, kg/s $h_{fg}$ — latent heat at the operating temperature, kJ/kg $\dot{m}_a$ — mass flow rate of dry air, kg/s $\Delta T_a$ — temperature rise of air, K

Natural draft cooling towers use a hyperbolic shell shape. The large cross-sectional area at the base transitions to a narrow throat, creating a chimney (stack) effect that induces natural upward airflow without fans. The hyperbolic geometry also provides superior structural resistance to wind loading.

Quick-Fire Checklist
  • $\omega = 0.622\,P_v/(P_t - P_v)$; units are kg wv / kg da.
  • $\phi = P_v / P_{vs}$; heating without moisture addition: $\omega$ constant, $\phi$ decreases.
  • At $100\%$ RH: DBT = WBT = DPT.
  • For unsaturated air: DBT > WBT > DPT.
  • $h = 1.005\,t + \omega(2500 + 1.88\,t)$ kJ/kg da.
  • ADP must be below incoming DPT for dehumidification to occur.
  • $BPF = (T_2 - \text{ADP})/(T_1 - \text{ADP})$; contact efficiency $= 1 - BPF$.
  • More tube rows $\Rightarrow$ BPF decreases. Higher velocity $\Rightarrow$ BPF increases.
  • $SHF = Q_S/(Q_S + Q_L)$; SHF = 1 is pure sensible, SHF = 0 is pure latent.
  • Comfort zone: $21\,^\circ\text{C}$–$25\,^\circ\text{C}$ DBT, $40\%$–$60\%$ RH.
  • Evaporative cooling: along constant WBT line, DBT $\downarrow$, $\omega$ $\uparrow$, $h$ constant.
  • Chemical dehumidification: DBT $\uparrow$, $\omega$ $\downarrow$, $h$ approximately constant.
  • Cooling tower: minimum exit water temperature = WBT of entering air.
  • Range = $T_\text{in,water} - T_\text{out,water}$; Approach = $T_\text{out,water} - WBT_\text{in,air}$.
  • Natural draft tower: hyperbolic shape for structural strength and chimney effect.
