2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Properties of Moist Air

  • Specific Humidity / Humidity Ratio $\omega$:
    $${\boxed{\omega = 0.622 \frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v}}}$$
  • $\omega$: specific humidity ($\text{kg wv / kg da}$ or $\text{kg/kg da}$)
  • $P_v$: actual partial pressure of water vapour ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{bar}$)

  • $P_t$: total atmospheric barometric pressure ($\text{kPa}$ or $\text{bar}$; Standard $P_t = 101.325 \text{ kPa} = 1.01325 \text{ bar}$)

  • Alternate Form (using Relative Humidity $\phi$):

    $${\boxed{\omega = 0.622 \frac{\phi P_{vs}}{P_t - \phi P_{vs}}}}$$

  • $P_{vs}$: saturation pressure of pure water vapour at dry-bulb temperature $DBT$ $\text{kPa}$

  • Relative Humidity $\phi$:

    $${\boxed{\phi = \frac{P_v}{P_{vs}}}}$$

  • $\phi$: relative humidity (dimensionless fraction or percentage $\%$)

  • Degree of Saturation $\mu$:

    $${\boxed{\mu = \frac{\omega}{\omega_s} = \frac{P_v (P_t - P_{vs})}{P_{vs} (P_t - P_v)} \approx \phi}}$$

  • $\omega_s$: specific humidity of saturated air at the same $DBT$

2. Enthalpy of Moist Air

  • Specific Enthalpy of Moist Air $h$:
    $${\boxed{h = 1.005 \, t + \omega(2500 + 1.88 \, t) \quad (\text{kJ/kg da})}}$$
  • $t$: dry-bulb temperature ($DBT$ in $^\circ\text{C}$)
  • $\omega$: specific humidity $\text{kg wv / kg da}$
  • $1.005 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$: specific heat of dry air $C_{pa}$
  • $2500 \text{ kJ/kg}$: latent heat of vaporisation of water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ $h_{fg,0}$

  • $1.88 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$: specific heat of superheated water vapour $C_{pv}$

  • Simplified Combined Enthalpy:

    $${\boxed{h = (1.005 + 1.88 \, \omega) t + 2500 \, \omega}}$$

3. Bypass Factor (BPF) & Apparatus Dew Point (ADP)

  • Bypass Factor $BPF$ for Cooling / Heating Coils:
    $${\boxed{BPF = \frac{T_2 - T_3}{T_1 - T_3} = \frac{\text{Leaving Air DBT} - \text{Coil ADP}}{\text{Entering Air DBT} - \text{Coil ADP}}}}$$
  • $T_1$: entering air dry-bulb temperature $^\circ\text{C}$
  • $T_2$: leaving air dry-bulb temperature $^\circ\text{C}$

  • $T_3 = ADP$: Apparatus Dew Point (coil surface temperature in $^\circ\text{C}$)

  • Coil Contact Efficiency $\eta_{\text{coil}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{coil}} = 1 - BPF = \frac{T_1 - T_2}{T_1 - T_3}}}$$

4. Sensible Heat Factor (SHF)

  • Sensible Heat Factor $SHF$:
    $${\boxed{SHF = \frac{SH}{SH + LH} = \frac{SH}{TH}}}$$
  • $SH$: sensible heat load $\text{kW}$
  • $LH$: latent heat load $\text{kW}$

  • $TH = SH + LH$: total heat load $\text{kW}$

  • Latent Heat Fraction:

    $${\boxed{\text{LHF} = 1 - SHF = \frac{LH}{TH}}}$$

5. Cooling Towers & Evaporative Cooling Performance

  • Cooling Tower Range:

    $${\boxed{\text{Range} = T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}}}}$$

  • Cooling Tower Approach:

    $${\boxed{\text{Approach} = T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}}}}$$

  • Evaporative Cooler Energy Balance:

    $${\boxed{\dot{m}w \cdot h{fg} = \dot{m}_a \cdot C_p \cdot \Delta T}}$$

  • $\dot{m}_w$: water evaporation rate $\text{kg/s}$
  • $h_{fg}$: latent heat of vaporisation $\text{kJ/kg}$
  • $\dot{m}_a$: dry air mass flow rate $\text{kg/s}$
  • $C_p$: specific heat of humid air $\approx 1.02 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$
  • $\Delta T$: air dry-bulb temperature drop $^\circ\text{C}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Dry-Bulb Temperature $DBT$: Temperature of air measured by a standard thermometer unaffected by radiation or moisture.
  • Wet-Bulb Temperature $WBT$: Temperature reached by adiabatic saturation; measured by a thermometer whose bulb is covered with a wetted wick exposed to rapid airflow.
  • Dew-Point Temperature $DPT$: Temperature at which condensation of water vapour begins when moist air is cooled at constant pressure.
  • Wet-Bulb Depression: Difference between dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperatures $DBT - WBT$. Zero at $100\%$ relative humidity.
  • Dew-Point Depression: Difference between dry-bulb and dew-point temperatures $DBT - DPT$.
  • Relative Humidity $\phi$: Ratio of actual partial pressure of water vapour $P_v$ to saturation pressure $P_{vs}$ at the same $DBT$.
  • Specific Humidity / Humidity Ratio $\omega$: Ratio of mass of water vapour to mass of dry air in a given volume $\text{kg wv / kg da}$.
  • Degree of Saturation $\mu$: Ratio of actual specific humidity $\omega$ to saturated specific humidity $\omega_s$ at the same $DBT$.
  • 100% Saturation Condition: State where $DBT = WBT = DPT$, $\phi = 1.0$, and wet-bulb depression $= 0$.
  • Apparatus Dew Point $ADP$: Surface temperature of a heating or cooling coil.
  • Bypass Factor $BPF$: Fraction of air stream that passes through a coil without coming into direct contact with the coil surface.
  • Sensible Heat Factor $SHF$: Ratio of sensible heat to total heat load.
  • Effective Temperature $ET$: An empirical index combining $DBT$, humidity, and air velocity into the equivalent temperature of still, saturated air producing identical thermal comfort.
  • Cooling Tower Approach: Difference between leaving water temperature and entering air wet-bulb temperature $T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
  • Cooling Tower Range: Difference between entering and leaving water temperatures $T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}}$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Standard Barometric Pressure $P_t$: $101.325 \text{ kPa} = 1.01325 \text{ bar} = 760 \text{ mm Hg}$.
  • Specific Heat of Dry Air $C_{pa}$: $1.005 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • Specific Heat of Water Vapour $C_{pv}$: $1.88 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • Latent Heat of Vaporisation of Water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ $h_{fg,0}$: $2500 \text{ kJ/kg}$.
  • Comfort Zone Design Standards: $21^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C} \ DBT$, $40\% - 60\% \ RH$ (Winter design: $21^\circ\text{C} \ DBT, \le 50\% \ RH$).
  • SHF Protractor Reference Point: $45^\circ$ line on chart corresponds to $SHF \approx 0.29$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Basic Properties of Moist Air (DBT, WBT, DPT, RH & Specific Humidity)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Moist air is a two-component mixture of dry air and water vapour.
  • $DBT$ measures molecular kinetic energy of air independent of moisture.
  • $WBT$ reflects latent heat exchange during evaporative cooling from a wet wick.
  • $DPT$ depends ONLY on partial pressure of water vapour $P_v$. If $P_v$ remains constant, $DPT$ remains constant!
  • At $100\%$ Relative Humidity (Saturation):
  • $DBT = WBT = DPT$
  • Wet-bulb depression $= 0$
  • Dew-point depression $= 0$
  • In unsaturated air $RH < 100\%$:
  • $DBT > WBT > DPT$
  • Water vapour exists in a superheated state.
💡 Key Concept: Property Independence: Specific humidity $\omega = 0.622 \frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v}$ depends ONLY on vapour partial pressure $P_v$ and barometric pressure $P_t$. Heating air at constant pressure leaves $\omega$ and $DPT$ unchanged, while Relative Humidity $\phi$ drops!

[!TRAP] Denominator Basis in Specific Humidity: Specific humidity $\omega$ is ALWAYS expressed per kilogram of DRY AIR $\text{kg wv / kg da}$, NEVER per kilogram of total moist air! Putting total mass in the denominator is a major calculation trap.

2. Decision Tree
Moist Air Saturation State Check?
├─ Is Relative Humidity phi = 100%?
│  ├─ YES → DBT = WBT = DPT (Saturation line on chart, Wet-bulb depression = 0)
│  └─ NO → DBT > WBT > DPT (Unsaturated state, Vapour is superheated)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\omega = 0.622 \frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v}$.
  • [ ] $\phi = P_v / P_{vs}$.
  • [ ] At saturation: $DBT = WBT = DPT$.
  • [ ] Heating air at constant $\omega$ lowers $\phi$ and keeps $DPT$ constant.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Moist air at a total pressure $P_t = 1.013 \text{ bar}$ has a vapour partial pressure $P_v = 0.016 \text{ bar}$. Calculate the specific humidity $\omega$ in $\text{kg wv / kg da}$. - Solution:

$${\omega = 0.622 \frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v} = 0.622 \times \frac{0.016}{1.013 - 0.016} = 0.622 \times \frac{0.016}{0.997} = 0.622 \times 0.016048 = 0.00998 \text{ kg/kg da} \approx 0.010 \text{ kg/kg da}}$$
- Key Insight: Always use $P_t - P_v$ in denominator.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Saturated vapour pressure at $20^\circ\text{C} \ DBT$ is $P_{vs} = 0.0234 \text{ bar}$. If relative humidity $\phi = 60\%$, find actual partial pressure of vapour $P_v$. - Solution:

$${\phi = \frac{P_v}{P_{vs}} \implies P_v = \phi \cdot P_{vs} = 0.60 \times 0.0234 = 0.01404 \text{ bar}}$$
- Key Insight: Actual partial pressure $P_v = \phi \cdot P_{vs}$.

3.2 Specific Enthalpy of Moist Air

1. Concept Pointers
  • Specific enthalpy of moist air $h$ is defined per unit mass of dry air: $h = h_a + \omega h_v$.
  • $h = 1.005 \, t + \omega (2500 + 1.88 \, t) \text{ kJ/kg da}$.
  • On a psychrometric chart, constant enthalpy lines run nearly parallel to constant $WBT$ lines.
  • Adiabatic saturation processes follow constant $h$ (and constant $WBT$) lines.
💡 Key Concept: Enthalpy Formula Breakdown: - $1.005 t$: Sensible heat of dry air. - $2500 \omega$: Latent heat of water vapour at $0^\circ\text{C}$. - $1.88 \omega t$: Sensible heat of water vapour.

[!TRAP] Units of Specific Humidity in Enthalpy: Specific humidity $\omega$ MUST be in $\text{kg/kg da}$ (e.g. $0.010$), NOT grams per kg (e.g. $10 \text{ g/kg}$). Using $g/kg$ directly makes enthalpy $2500 \times 10 = 25000 \text{ kJ/kg}$, which is an enormous error!

2. Decision Tree
Moist Air Enthalpy Calculation?
├─ Is specific humidity given in g/kg da? → Convert to kg/kg da (divide by 1000)
└─ Apply formula → h = 1.005 * t + omega * (2500 + 1.88 * t)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $h = 1.005 t + \omega(2500 + 1.88 t)$.
  • [ ] Enthalpy is expressed per kg of dry air.
  • [ ] Constant enthalpy lines $\approx$ Constant $WBT$ lines.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Calculate specific enthalpy of moist air at $21^\circ\text{C} \ DBT$ with specific humidity $\omega = 0.011 \text{ kg wv / kg da}$. - Solution:

$${h = 1.005 \times 21 + 0.011 \times (2500 + 1.88 \times 21)}$$
$${h = 21.105 + 0.011 \times (2500 + 39.48) = 21.105 + 0.011 \times 2539.48}$$
$${h = 21.105 + 27.934 = 49.039 \text{ kJ/kg da} \approx 49.04 \text{ kJ/kg da}}$$
- Key Insight: Substitute $t=21^\circ\text{C}$ and $\omega=0.011$ directly.

3.3 Psychrometric Processes (Sensible, Dehumidification, Evaporative Cooling, Chemical)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Sensible Heating: Horizontal to the right $\omega = \text{const}, DBT \uparrow, \phi \downarrow, h \uparrow$.
  • Sensible Cooling: Horizontal to the left $\omega = \text{const}, DBT \downarrow, \phi \uparrow, h \downarrow$.
  • Cooling & Dehumidification: Moves down and to the left $DBT \downarrow, \omega \downarrow, h \downarrow$. Requires coil surface temperature $ADP < DPT_{\text{entering}}$.
  • Heating & Humidification: Moves up and to the right $DBT \uparrow, \omega \uparrow, h \uparrow$. Typical winter air conditioning.
  • Adiabatic Evaporative Cooling (Desert Cooler): Moves along constant $WBT$ / constant $h$ line $DBT \downarrow, \omega \uparrow, WBT = \text{const}, h \approx \text{const}$.
  • Chemical (Adsorption) Dehumidification: Moves down and to the right $\omega \downarrow, DBT \uparrow, h \approx \text{const}$. Latent heat released heats the air.
💡 Key Concept: Psychrometric Process Directions: - Horizontal Right: Sensible Heating - Horizontal Left: Sensible Cooling - Vertical Down: Pure Dehumidification - Vertical Up: Pure Humidification - Down-Left: Cooling & Dehumidification (Summer AC) - Down-Right: Chemical Dehumidification (Desiccant dryer) - Along Constant WBT (Up-Left): Evaporative Cooling

[!TRAP] Cooling & Dehumidification Condition: Dehumidification CANNOT occur unless coil surface temperature $ADP$ is LESS THAN entering air dew-point temperature $DPT_1$. If coil $ADP > DPT_1$, only sensible cooling occurs!

2. Decision Tree
Process Vector Check on Psychrometric Chart?
├─ Horizontal Right → Sensible Heating (omega = const)
├─ Horizontal Left → Sensible Cooling (omega = const)
├─ Left & Down → Cooling & Dehumidification (Coil ADP < DPT_in)
├─ Along Constant WBT Line (Up-Left) → Adiabatic Evaporative Cooling (Desert Cooler)
└─ Right & Down → Chemical Dehumidification (Desiccant drying: omega down, DBT up)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Sensible processes: $\omega = \text{const}$ (horizontal lines).
  • [ ] Evaporative cooling: $WBT = \text{const}$ (constant enthalpy line).
  • [ ] Cooling & dehumidification requires $ADP < DPT_{\text{entering}}$.
  • [ ] Chemical dehumidification increases $DBT$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Outside air at $45^\circ\text{C} \ DBT$ and $10\% \ RH$ enters a desert cooler and leaves at $90\% \ RH$. Identify the psychrometric process and state what happens to $WBT$. - Solution: - Desert cooler operates on Adiabatic Evaporative Cooling (adiabatic saturation). - Process moves along constant $WBT$ line. - Therefore, $WBT$ remains constant, while $DBT$ decreases and specific humidity $\omega$ increases. - Key Insight: Evaporative cooling follows constant $WBT$ lines.

3.4 Bypass Factor (BPF) & Apparatus Dew Point (ADP)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Bypass Factor $BPF$ measures coil inefficiency (fraction of air bypassing coil contact).
  • $BPF = \frac{T_2 - T_3}{T_1 - T_3} = \frac{\text{Leaving Air DBT} - \text{ADP}}{\text{Entering Air DBT} - \text{ADP}}$.
  • Contact Efficiency $\eta_{\text{coil}} = 1 - BPF$.
  • Factors affecting $BPF$:
  • Air velocity $\uparrow \implies BPF \uparrow$
  • Fin spacing/pitch $\uparrow \implies BPF \uparrow$
  • Number of tube rows $\uparrow \implies BPF \downarrow$
💡 Key Concept: ADP Definition: Apparatus Dew Point $ADP$ is the effective surface temperature of the cooling/heating coil where the extended process line meets the $100\%$ saturation curve.

[!TRAP] BPF Formula Ordering: - For Cooling Coil $T_1 > T_2 > T_3$: $BPF = \frac{T_2 - T_3}{T_1 - T_3}$ - For Heating Coil $T_3 > T_2 > T_1$: $BPF = \frac{T_3 - T_2}{T_3 - T_1}$ Numerator is ALWAYS difference between leaving air and coil ADP; denominator is ALWAYS difference between entering air and coil ADP!

2. Decision Tree
Bypass Factor Calculation?
├─ Identify Temperatures → T1 (Entering), T2 (Leaving), T3 (Coil ADP)
├─ Cooling Coil → BPF = (T2 - ADP) / (T1 - ADP)
├─ Heating Coil → BPF = (ADP - T2) / (ADP - T1)
└─ Coil Efficiency → Contact Efficiency = 1 - BPF
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $BPF = \frac{T_2 - \text{ADP}}{T_1 - \text{ADP}}$.
  • [ ] Contact Efficiency $= 1 - BPF$.
  • [ ] More tube rows $\implies BPF \downarrow$.
  • [ ] Higher air velocity $\implies BPF \uparrow$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air enters a heating coil at $15^\circ\text{C}$. The coil surface temperature is $40^\circ\text{C}$ and air leaves at $25^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate $BPF$ and coil contact efficiency. - Solution:

$${T_1 = 15^\circ\text{C}, \quad T_2 = 25^\circ\text{C}, \quad T_3 = ADP = 40^\circ\text{C}}$$
$${BPF = \frac{T_3 - T_2}{T_3 - T_1} = \frac{40 - 25}{40 - 15} = \frac{15}{25} = 0.60}$$
$${\eta_{\text{coil}} = 1 - BPF = 1 - 0.60 = 0.40 = 40\%}$$
- Key Insight: $BPF = 0.60$, Contact Efficiency $= 40\%$.

3.5 Sensible Heat Factor (SHF, RSHF & GSHF)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Sensible Heat Factor $SHF = \frac{SH}{SH + LH} = \frac{SH}{TH}$.
  • $SHF = 1 \implies$ Pure sensible heat transfer $LH = 0$.
  • $SHF = 0 \implies$ Pure latent heat transfer $SH = 0$.
  • Room SHF $RSHF$ determines the slope of the room condition line connecting room state to supply air state.
  • Grand SHF $GSHF$ includes ventilation outdoor air load.
💡 Key Concept: SHF Protractor & Chart Slope: On standard psychrometric charts, the $SHF$ protractor aligns process line slopes. A $45^\circ$ inclined process line corresponds to $SHF \approx 0.29$.
2. Decision Tree
Sensible Heat Factor Calculation?
├─ Identify Heat Loads → SH (Sensible), LH (Latent)
├─ Compute Total Heat → TH = SH + LH
└─ Calculate SHF → SHF = SH / TH, Latent Heat Fraction = 1 - SHF
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $SHF = \frac{SH}{SH + LH}$.
  • [ ] Latent fraction $= 1 - SHF$.
  • [ ] High occupancy spaces $\implies$ lower $SHF$ (higher latent load).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An air conditioning space has a sensible heat load of $2.5 \text{ kW}$ and a latent heat load of $1.5 \text{ kW}$. Calculate the Sensible Heat Factor $SHF$. - Solution:

$${SH = 2.5 \text{ kW}, \quad LH = 1.5 \text{ kW}}$$
$${TH = SH + LH = 2.5 + 1.5 = 4.0 \text{ kW}}$$
$${SHF = \frac{SH}{TH} = \frac{2.5}{4.0} = 0.625}$$
- Key Insight: $SHF = 0.625$ ($62.5\%$ sensible, $37.5\%$ latent).

3.6 Effective Temperature & Human Comfort Standards

1. Concept Pointers
  • Effective Temperature $ET$ is an empirical comfort index combining $DBT$, humidity, and air velocity.
  • Defined as the temperature of still, saturated air $100\% \ RH$ that produces the same feeling of warmth or cold as the actual environment.
  • Typical ASHRAE Comfort Zone:
  • $21^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C} \ DBT$
  • $40\% - 60\% \ RH$
  • Winter design: $\approx 21^\circ\text{C} \ DBT, \le 50\% \ RH$.
💡 Key Concept: At 100% Saturation: Effective Temperature $ET$ equals Dry-Bulb Temperature $DBT$ $ET = DBT = WBT$.
2. Decision Tree
Human Comfort Check?
├─ Is RH between 40% and 60% and DBT between 21°C and 25°C?
│  ├─ YES → Inside Comfort Zone
│  └─ NO → Adjust heating/cooling or humidification/dehumidification
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $ET$ is measured relative to saturated still air.
  • [ ] Comfort zone: $21^\circ\text{C} - 25^\circ\text{C} \ DBT, 40\% - 60\% \ RH$.
  • [ ] At $100\% \ RH$, $ET = DBT$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Define Effective Temperature and state its value when relative humidity is $100\%$. - Solution: - Effective Temperature $ET$ is the temperature of saturated, still air that provides identical thermal comfort sensation as the given environment. - At $100\% \ RH$, $ET = DBT = WBT = DPT$. - Key Insight: At saturation, $ET$ equals $DBT$.

3.7 Cooling Towers & Evaporative Cooling Performance

1. Concept Pointers
  • Cooling towers cool warm condenser water by evaporating a small fraction of water into an air stream.
  • Lowest achievable water exit temperature $= WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
  • Approach $= T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
  • Range $= T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}}$.
  • Natural draft cooling towers use hyperbolic shape for structural stability and chimney stack effect.
💡 Key Concept: Cooling Limit: A cooling tower CANNOT cool water below entering air wet-bulb temperature $WBT_{\text{air, in}}$. Approach can never be zero or negative in practice!
2. Decision Tree
Cooling Tower Parameter Calculation?
├─ Range → T_water_in - T_water_out
├─ Approach → T_water_out - WBT_air_in
└─ Evaporative Energy Balance → m_dot_w * h_fg = m_dot_a * C_p * Delta T_air
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Range $= T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}}$.
  • [ ] Approach $= T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
  • [ ] Minimum water temp $= WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
  • [ ] Hyperbolic tower shape provides structural strength and draft stack effect.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Water enters a cooling tower at $40^\circ\text{C}$ and leaves at $30^\circ\text{C}$. Entering air has $DBT = 35^\circ\text{C}$ and $WBT = 25^\circ\text{C}$. Calculate Cooling Tower Range and Approach. - Solution:

$${\text{Range} = T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}} = 40 - 30 = 10^\circ\text{C}}$$
$${\text{Approach} = T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}} = 30 - 25 = 5^\circ\text{C}}$$
- Key Insight: Range $= 10^\circ\text{C}$, Approach $= 5^\circ\text{C}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: In an evaporative cooler, air flow rate is $\dot{m}a = 1.2 \text{ kg/s}$ $C_p = 1.0 \text{ kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. Water evaporates at $\dot{m}_w = 0.005 \text{ kg/s}$ $h{fg} = 2700 \text{ kJ/kg}$. Calculate air temperature drop $\Delta T$. - Solution:

$${\dot{m}w \cdot h{fg} = \dot{m}_a \cdot C_p \cdot \Delta T}$$
$${0.005 \times 2700 = 1.2 \times 1.0 \times \Delta T}$$
$${13.5 = 1.2 \, \Delta T \implies \Delta T = \frac{13.5}{1.2} = 11.25^\circ\text{C}}$$
- Key Insight: Air temperature drops by $11.25^\circ\text{C}$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Define Dry-Bulb Temperature $DBT$, Wet-Bulb Temperature $WBT$, and Dew-Point Temperature $DPT$.
A: $DBT$ is the actual air temperature measured by a dry thermometer. $WBT$ is the temperature of adiabatic saturation measured by a wetted thermometer wick in moving air. $DPT$ is the saturation temperature corresponding to actual vapour partial pressure $P_v$, where condensation begins upon constant-pressure cooling.

Q2: What is the relationship between $DBT$, $WBT$, and $DPT$ at $100\%$ Relative Humidity?
A: At $100\%$ relative humidity (saturation), $DBT = WBT = DPT$. Wet-bulb depression is zero.

Q3: What is the relationship between $DBT$, $WBT$, and $DPT$ for unsaturated air?
A: For unsaturated air $RH < 100\%$, $DBT > WBT > DPT$.

Q4: Define Specific Humidity $\omega$ and Relative Humidity $\phi$.
A: Specific humidity $\omega = 0.622 \frac{P_v}{P_t - P_v}$ is the mass of water vapour per unit mass of dry air $\text{kg wv / kg da}$. Relative humidity $\phi = \frac{P_v}{P_{vs}}$ is the ratio of actual vapour partial pressure to saturated vapour pressure at the same $DBT$.

Q5: What happens to Relative Humidity $\phi$ and Dew-Point Temperature $DPT$ when moist air is heated at constant pressure without adding moisture?
A: Specific humidity $\omega$ and $DPT$ remain constant (since $P_v$ is unchanged), while Relative Humidity $\phi$ decreases because saturation pressure $P_{vs}$ increases with rising temperature.

Q6: What is the formula for the specific enthalpy of moist air?
A: $h = 1.005 \, t + \omega(2500 + 1.88 \, t) \text{ kJ/kg da}$, where $t$ is $DBT$ in $^\circ\text{C}$ and $\omega$ is in $\text{kg/kg da}$.

Q7: Which psychrometric process describes the operation of a desert evaporative air cooler?
A: Adiabatic Evaporative Cooling (Adiabatic Saturation). The process moves along a constant $WBT$ / constant enthalpy line $DBT \downarrow, \omega \uparrow, WBT = \text{const}$.

Q8: Describe the process vector for Chemical (Desiccant) Dehumidification.
A: Chemical dehumidification moves down and to the right on the psychrometric chart: specific humidity drops $\omega \downarrow$ and dry-bulb temperature rises $DBT \uparrow$ due to the release of latent heat during moisture absorption, while enthalpy remains approximately constant $h \approx \text{const}$.

Q9: What is the requirement for a cooling coil to achieve dehumidification?
A: The coil surface temperature (Apparatus Dew Point, $ADP$) MUST be LESS THAN the entering air dew-point temperature $ADP < DPT_{\text{entering}}$.

Q10: Define Bypass Factor $BPF$ of a coil.
A: $BPF = \frac{T_2 - ADP}{T_1 - ADP}$, representing the fraction of air that passes through the coil without contacting the surface. Contact efficiency $\eta_{\text{coil}} = 1 - BPF$.

Q11: How do air velocity and tube rows affect coil Bypass Factor $BPF$?
A: Higher air velocity increases $BPF$; increasing the number of tube rows decreases $BPF$.

Q12: Define Sensible Heat Factor $SHF$.
A: $SHF = \frac{SH}{SH + LH} = \frac{SH}{TH}$, the ratio of sensible heat to total heat load.

Q13: Define Effective Temperature $ET$.
A: An empirical index combining $DBT$, humidity, and air velocity into the equivalent temperature of still, saturated air producing identical thermal comfort sensation.

Q14: Define Cooling Tower Range and Approach.
A: Range $= T_{\text{water, in}} - T_{\text{water, out}}$ (water temperature drop). Approach $= T_{\text{water, out}} - WBT_{\text{air, in}}$ (difference between leaving water temperature and entering air $WBT$).

Q15: What is the theoretical minimum water temperature achievable in a cooling tower?
A: The entering air wet-bulb temperature $WBT_{\text{air, in}}$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Saturation Equality: $DBT = WBT = DPT$ ONLY at $100\% \ RH$. For unsaturated air, $DBT > WBT > DPT$.
  • Constant $DPT$ Condition: Dew-point temperature $DPT$ depends ONLY on $P_v$. Heating or cooling without moisture change keeps $DPT$ constant.
  • Cooling & Dehumidification Rule: Requires coil $ADP < DPT_{\text{entering}}$.
  • Desert Cooler vs Chemical Dryer:
  • Desert Cooler: Evaporative cooling along constant $WBT$ line $DBT \downarrow, \omega \uparrow$.
  • Chemical Dryer: Desiccant dehumidification along constant $h$ line $DBT \uparrow, \omega \downarrow$.
  • Cooling Tower Limit: Lowest water temp $= WBT_{\text{air, in}}$, NOT $DBT_{\text{air, in}}$.
