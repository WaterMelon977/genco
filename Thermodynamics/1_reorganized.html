2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Heat Capacity & Basic Properties

  • Heat Capacity:
    $${\boxed{C = m \cdot c}}$$
  • $C$: heat capacity ($\text{J/K}$ or $\text{kJ/K}$)
  • $m$: mass $\text{kg}$
  • $c$: specific heat capacity ($\text{J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ or $\text{kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$)

2. First Law of Thermodynamics & Energy Conservation

  • First Law (Closed System Non-Flow):
    $${\boxed{\Delta U = Q - W}}$$
  • $\Delta U$: change in internal energy $\text{kJ}$
  • $Q$: net heat added to system ($\text{kJ}$; positive in, negative out)

  • $W$: net work done by system ($\text{kJ}$; positive out, negative in)

  • Differential Form:

    $${\boxed{\delta Q = dU + \delta W}}$$

  • General Energy Balance:

    $${\boxed{Q - W = \Delta U + \Delta KE + \Delta PE}}$$

  • Cyclic Process:

    $${\boxed{\oint \delta Q = \oint \delta W \implies \oint (\delta Q - \delta W) = 0}}$$

  • Enthalpy Definition:

    $${\boxed{H = U + P V \quad \text{or} \quad h = u + P v}}$$

  • $H$: enthalpy $\text{kJ}$
  • $U$: internal energy $\text{kJ}$
  • $P$: absolute pressure $\text{kPa}$
  • $V$: volume $\text{m}^3$

3. Work & Heat in Thermodynamic Processes

  • Quasi-Static Boundary Work:

    $${\boxed{W = \int_{V_1}^{V_2} P \, dV}}$$

  • Constant Pressure (Isobaric, $n = 0$):

    $${\boxed{W = P(V_2 - V_1), \quad Q = \Delta H = m C_p (T_2 - T_1)}}$$

  • Constant Volume (Isochoric, $n = \infty$):

    $${\boxed{W = 0, \quad Q = \Delta U = m C_v (T_2 - T_1)}}$$

  • Isothermal Process (Ideal Gas, $T = \text{const}, n = 1$):

    $${\boxed{W = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = m R T \ln\left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right), \quad \Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W}}$$

  • Reversible Adiabatic (Isentropic, $Q = 0, n = \gamma$):

    $${\boxed{W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1} = \frac{m R (T_1 - T_2)}{\gamma - 1}, \quad Q = 0 \implies \Delta U = -W}}$$
    $${\boxed{\frac{T_2}{T_1} = \left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma}} = \left(\frac{V_1}{V_2}\right)^{\gamma-1}}}$$

  • Polytropic Process $P V^n = \text{const}$:

    $${\boxed{W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{n - 1} = \frac{m R (T_1 - T_2)}{n - 1}}}$$
    $${\boxed{Q = W \left(\frac{\gamma - n}{\gamma - 1}\right)}}$$

  • Free Expansion (Unrestricted expansion into vacuum):

    $${\boxed{Q = 0, \quad W = 0 \implies \Delta U = 0 \implies T_1 = T_2 \quad (\text{Ideal Gas})}}$$

  • Throttling Process (Joule-Thomson Expansion through porous plug/valve):

    $${\boxed{Q = 0, \quad W = 0, \quad \Delta KE \approx 0 \implies h_1 = h_2 \quad (\text{Isenthalpic})}}$$

4. Ideal Gas Law & Specific Heat Relations

  • Ideal Gas Equation of State:
    $${\boxed{P V = m R T = n R_u T}}$$
  • $R = \frac{R_u}{M}$: characteristic gas constant ($\text{kJ/kg}\cdot\text{K}$; Air $R \approx 0.287$, $O_2 R \approx 0.260$)

  • $R_u$: universal gas constant $8.314 \text{ kJ/kmol}\cdot\text{K}$

  • Mayer's Relation:

    $${\boxed{C_p - C_v = R}}$$

  • Specific Heat Ratio $\gamma = C_p / C_v$:

    $${\boxed{C_p = \frac{\gamma R}{\gamma - 1}, \quad C_v = \frac{R}{\gamma - 1}}}$$

5. Second Law of Thermodynamics, Carnot Cycle & Refrigeration

  • Carnot Engine Efficiency:
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_L}{T_H} = \frac{W_{\text{net}}}{Q_H} = \frac{Q_H - Q_L}{Q_H}}}$$

  • $T_H, T_L$: absolute source and sink temperatures $\text{K}$

  • Coefficient of Performance (Refrigerator):

    $${\boxed{COP_R = \frac{Q_L}{W_{\text{net}}} = \frac{Q_L}{Q_H - Q_L} \xrightarrow{\text{Carnot}} \frac{T_L}{T_H - T_L}}}$$

  • Coefficient of Performance (Heat Pump):

    $${\boxed{COP_{HP} = \frac{Q_H}{W_{\text{net}}} = \frac{Q_H}{Q_H - Q_L} \xrightarrow{\text{Carnot}} \frac{T_H}{T_H - T_L}}}$$

  • COP Relationship:

    $${\boxed{COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1}}$$

6. Entropy, Clausius Inequality & Entropy Generation

  • Clausius Inequality:
    $${\boxed{\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \le 0}}$$
  • $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} = 0$: Reversible cycle
  • $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} < 0$: Irreversible possible cycle

  • $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} > 0$: Impossible cycle

  • Entropy Change (Reversible Process):

    $${\boxed{dS = \frac{\delta Q_{\text{rev}}}{T} \implies \Delta S = \int_1^2 \frac{\delta Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}}}$$

  • Entropy Generation & Principle of Increase of Entropy:

    $${\boxed{S_{\text{gen}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} \ge 0}}$$

  • $S_{\text{gen}} = 0$: Reversible process
  • $S_{\text{gen}} > 0$: Irreversible process

  • $S_{\text{gen}} < 0$: Impossible process

  • Ideal Gas Entropy Change Formulas:

    $${\boxed{\Delta s = C_v \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) + R \ln\left(\frac{v_2}{v_1}\right)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\Delta s = C_p \ln\left(\frac{T_2}{T_1}\right) - R \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\Delta s = C_v \ln\left(\frac{P_2}{P_1}\right) + C_p \ln\left(\frac{v_2}{v_1}\right)}}$$

7. Availability (Exergy), Irreversibility & Gouy-Stodola Theorem

  • Gouy-Stodola Theorem (Irreversibility / Lost Work):
    $${\boxed{I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}} = W_{\text{rev}} - W_{\text{actual}}}}$$

  • $T_0$: dead-state ambient temperature $\text{K}$

  • Closed System Availability (Non-Flow Exergy):

    $${\boxed{A = \phi_1 - \phi_0 = (U - U_0) - T_0(S - S_0) + P_0(V - V_0)}}$$

  • Open System Availability (Flow / Stream Exergy):

    $${\boxed{\psi = (h - h_0) - T_0(s - s_0) + \frac{V^2}{2} + g z}}$$

  • Unavailable Energy (UE):

    $${\boxed{\text{UE} = T_0 \Delta S}}$$

  • Second-Law Efficiency $\eta_{II}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{II} = \frac{W_{\text{actual}}}{W_{\text{rev}}} = \frac{\eta_{\text{thermal}}}{\eta_{\text{Carnot}}}}}$$

8. Air-Standard Cycles

  • Otto Cycle Efficiency (Constant Volume Heat Addition):
    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Otto}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}}}$$

  • $r = \frac{V_1}{V_2}$: compression ratio

  • Diesel Cycle Efficiency (Constant Pressure Heat Addition):

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{Diesel}} = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} \left[\frac{\rho^\gamma - 1}{\gamma (\rho - 1)}\right]}}$$

  • $\rho = \frac{V_3}{V_2}$: cutoff ratio $\rho > 1$

9. Pure Substances, Phase Rules & Dryness Fraction

  • Gibbs Phase Rule:
    $${\boxed{F = C - P + 2}}$$
  • $F$: degrees of freedom
  • $C$: number of chemical components

  • $P$: number of phase states in equilibrium

  • Dryness Fraction (Vapour Quality $x$):

    $${\boxed{x = \frac{m_g}{m_g + m_f}}}$$

  • $m_g$: mass of dry saturated vapour $\text{kg}$

  • $m_f$: mass of saturated liquid water $\text{kg}$

  • Wet Mixture Property Equations:

    $${\boxed{v = v_f + x v_{fg} = (1-x)v_f + x v_g}}$$
    $${\boxed{u = u_f + x u_{fg}}}$$
    $${\boxed{h = h_f + x h_{fg}}}$$
    $${\boxed{s = s_f + x s_{fg}}}$$

10. Real Gases, van der Waals & Joule-Thomson Effect

  • Compressibility Factor $Z$:
    $${\boxed{Z = \frac{P v}{R T}}}$$
  • Ideal Gas: $Z = 1$

  • van der Waals Gas at Critical Point: $Z_c = \frac{P_c v_c}{R T_c} = 0.375 = \frac{3}{8}$

  • van der Waals Equation of State:

    $${\boxed{\left(P + \frac{a}{v^2}\right)(v - b) = R T}}$$

  • $a$: inter-molecular force attraction parameter

  • $b$: co-volume / molecular volume parameter

  • Joule-Thomson Coefficient $\mu$:

    $${\boxed{\mu = \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_h}}$$

  • $\mu > 0$: Cooling on expansion ($T$ decreases as $P$ drops)
  • $\mu < 0$: Heating on expansion ($T$ increases as $P$ drops)
  • $\mu = 0$: Inversion curve point / Ideal Gas

11. Thermodynamic Potentials, Maxwell Relations & Clapeyron Equation

  • Helmholtz Free Energy $F$:

    $${\boxed{F = U - T S \implies dF = -S dT - P dV}}$$

  • Gibbs Free Energy $G$:

    $${\boxed{G = H - T S \implies dG = -S dT + V dP}}$$

  • The Four Fundamental Maxwell Relations:

    $${\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial V}\right)_S = -\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial S}\right)_V}}$$
    $${\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_S = \left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\right)_P}}$$
    $${\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial V}\right)_T = \left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial T}\right)_V}}$$
    $${\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial P}\right)_T = -\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial T}\right)_P}}$$

  • Clausius-Clapeyron Phase Change Slope:

    $${\boxed{\frac{dP}{dT} = \frac{h_{fg}}{T (v_g - v_f)} = \frac{\Delta s}{\Delta v}}}$$

  • Isobaric Slope on Mollier $h-s$ Chart:

    $${\boxed{\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P = T}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Thermodynamic System: A defined quantity of matter or region in space selected for analysis.
  • Closed System (Control Mass): Fixed mass system where energy (heat and work) crosses boundary, but mass transfer is zero $\Delta m = 0$.
  • Open System (Control Volume): System where both mass and energy cross the control boundary.
  • Isolated System: System with zero mass transfer and zero energy transfer ($\Delta m = 0, \Delta E = 0$; e.g. Universe).
  • Intensive Property: Property independent of system mass (e.g. Temperature $T$, Pressure $P$, Density $\rho$, Viscosity $\mu$, Specific Enthalpy $h$, Thermal Conductivity $k$).
  • Extensive Property: Property directly proportional to total mass (e.g. Mass $m$, Volume $V$, Internal Energy $U$, Enthalpy $H$, Entropy $S$).
  • Point Function (State Function): Property dependent ONLY on end states, independent of path history $\oint d\phi = 0$.
  • Path Function: Variable dependent on the specific path taken between states ($\oint \delta Q \neq 0, \oint \delta W \neq 0$; e.g. Heat $Q$, Work $W$).
  • Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If two bodies are each in thermal equilibrium with a third body, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other (defines Temperature $T$).
  • First Law of Thermodynamics: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; for a closed system, $\Delta U = Q - W$.
  • Second Law of Thermodynamics: Sets direction of natural processes and quality of energy. Kelvin-Planck prohibits 100% thermal efficiency; Clausius prohibits spontaneous heat transfer from cold to hot without work.
  • Third Law of Thermodynamics (Nernst): The entropy of a pure crystalline substance approaches zero at absolute zero temperature $0 \text{ K}$.
  • Carnot Cycle: Fully reversible ideal cycle consisting of two reversible isotherms and two reversible adiabatics (maximum possible efficiency $\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - T_L/T_H$).
  • Entropy $S$: A property representing molecular disorder and unavailable energy $dS = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}/T$.
  • Availability (Exergy): Maximum useful work extractable from a system as it transitions reversibly to the dead state $T_0, P_0$.
  • Irreversibility $I$: Work lost due to irreversibilities $I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}}$.
  • Triple Point: Unique state where solid, liquid, and vapour phases coexist in equilibrium (Water: $0.01^\circ\text{C} = 273.16 \text{ K}, 0.611 \text{ kPa}$).
  • Critical Point: State where liquid and vapour boundaries meet; latent heat of vaporisation drops to zero ($h_{fg} = 0$; Water: $374.14^\circ\text{C}, 22.06 \text{ MPa}$).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Thermodynamic Systems, Properties & Zeroth Law

1. Concept Pointers
  • Systems are classified based on boundary permeability:
  • Closed: Mass fixed $\Delta m = 0$, energy transfers $Q, W$.
  • Open: Mass and energy both cross control volume boundary.
  • Isolated: No mass, no heat, no work $\Delta m = 0, \Delta E = 0$.
  • Intensive properties (size-independent) vs Extensive properties (size-dependent). Specific properties (per unit mass) are intensive.
  • State functions (point functions) have exact differentials $\oint d\phi = 0$. Path functions (heat, work) have inexact differentials $\oint \delta Q \neq 0$.
  • Zeroth Law establishes thermal equilibrium transitivity, forming the theoretical basis for thermometer calibration.
💡 Key Concept: Property Test: If a quantity depends only on initial and final states, it is a property (point function). If it depends on the process trajectory, it is a path function (heat $Q$, work $W$). System possesses energy, NOT heat or work!

[!TRAP] Extensive vs Intensive Traps: - Specific volume $v = V/m$, specific enthalpy $h$, specific entropy $s$, and density $\rho = m/V$ are INTENSIVE. - Heat capacity $C = m \cdot c$ is EXTENSIVE; specific heat $c$ is INTENSIVE. - Thermal conductivity $k$ and viscosity $\mu$ are INTENSIVE properties.

2. Decision Tree
System Classification Check?
├─ Is mass crossing the boundary?
│  ├─ YES → Open System (Control Volume: Turbine, Nozzle, Pump)
│  └─ NO → Check energy transfer
│     ├─ Energy crosses boundary → Closed System (Control Mass: Piston-Cylinder without valves)
│     └─ Energy does NOT cross boundary → Isolated System (Universe, Thermos Flask)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Closed System: $\Delta m = 0$, $Q, W \neq 0$.
  • [ ] Open System: Mass & Energy transfer.
  • [ ] Isolated System: $\Delta m = 0, \Delta E = 0 \implies \Delta U = 0$.
  • [ ] Zeroth Law $\implies$ Temperature concept.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A rigid container holds $2 \text{ kg}$ of air. Heat is added until temperature rises from $300 \text{ K}$ to $400 \text{ K}$. Classify the system and state if volume is intensive or extensive. - Solution: - Since mass is fixed $2 \text{ kg}$ and rigid walls prevent mass transfer while heat crosses, it is a Closed System. - Total volume $V$ is extensive (proportional to mass); specific volume $v = V/m$ is intensive. - Key Insight: Rigid boundary means constant volume process $W=0$.

3.2 First Law of Thermodynamics & Energy Conservation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Energy is conserved in all processes: $\Delta U = Q - W$.
  • Sign Convention:
  • Heat added to system: $+Q$; Heat rejected by system: $-Q$.
  • Work done BY system: $+W$; Work done ON system: $-W$.
  • Internal Energy $U$ of an ideal gas depends ONLY on absolute temperature $U = f(T)$.
  • Enthalpy $H = U + PV$. For ideal gas, $H = f(T)$ only $h = C_p T$.
💡 Key Concept: First Law for Cyclic Processes: $\oint \delta Q = \oint \delta W$. Net heat interaction equals net work done over a complete cycle.

[!TRAP] Free Expansion Energy Conservation: In free expansion into a vacuum $Q=0, W=0$, $\Delta U = 0$. For an ideal gas, $\Delta U = 0 \implies T_1 = T_2$. However, free expansion is highly IRREVERSIBLE, so entropy increases $\Delta S > 0$!

2. Decision Tree
First Law Energy Balance?
├─ Closed System Process → Delta U = Q - W
├─ Cyclic Process → Integral dQ = Integral dW (Delta U_cycle = 0)
└─ Ideal Gas Property → U = f(T) only, Delta U = m * C_v * (T2 - T1)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Sign convention: $+Q$ in, $+W$ out.
  • [ ] Ideal gas internal energy $U = f(T)$ only.
  • [ ] Cyclic process: $\oint \delta Q = \oint \delta W$.
  • [ ] Enthalpy $H = U + PV$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A system undergoes a process where it absorbs $50 \text{ kJ}$ of heat while expanding and performing $30 \text{ kJ}$ of work. Calculate the change in internal energy. - Solution:

$${Q = +50 \text{ kJ}, \quad W = +30 \text{ kJ}}$$
$${\Delta U = Q - W = 50 - 30 = +20 \text{ kJ}}$$
- Key Insight: Internal energy increases by $20 \text{ kJ}$.

3.3 Work & Heat in Processes (Polytropic, Isothermal, Adiabatic, Free Expansion, Throttling)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Boundary work $W = \int P dV$ requires quasi-static frictionless path.
  • Isobaric $n=0$: $W = P(V_2 - V_1), Q = m C_p \Delta T$.
  • Isochoric $n=\infty$: $W = 0, Q = m C_v \Delta T = \Delta U$.
  • Isothermal $n=1$: $W = P_1 V_1 \ln(V_2/V_1), \Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W$.
  • Reversible Adiabatic $n=\gamma$: $Q = 0, W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{\gamma - 1}$.
  • Polytropic $P V^n = \text{const}$: $W = \frac{P_1 V_1 - P_2 V_2}{n - 1}, Q = W \left(\frac{\gamma - n}{\gamma - 1}\right)$.
  • Throttling: Isenthalpic process $h_1 = h_2$.
💡 Key Concept: Polytropic Index $n$ Map: - $n = 0 \implies$ Isobaric (Horizontal line on P-V chart) - $n = 1 \implies$ Isothermal (Hyperbola) - $n = \gamma \implies$ Isentropic (Steeper hyperbola than isothermal) - $n = \infty \implies$ Isochoric (Vertical line on P-V chart)

[!TRAP] Throttling vs Free Expansion: - Throttling: $h_1 = h_2$ (Isenthalpic), $Q=0, W=0, \Delta KE \approx 0$. - Free Expansion: $U_1 = U_2$ (Isenergetic), $Q=0, W=0$.

2. Decision Tree
Process Work Calculation?
├─ P = const (Isobaric) → W = P * (V2 - V1)
├─ V = const (Isochoric) → W = 0
├─ T = const (Isothermal Ideal Gas) → W = P1*V1*ln(V2/V1)
├─ Reversible Adiabatic (Q=0) → W = (P1*V1 - P2*V2) / (gamma - 1)
├─ Polytropic (P*V^n = const) → W = (P1*V1 - P2*V2) / (n - 1)
└─ Free Expansion / Throttling → W = 0
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Isothermal: $W = P_1 V_1 \ln(V_2/V_1)$.
  • [ ] Adiabatic slope is steeper than isothermal on P-V chart $\gamma > 1$.
  • [ ] Throttling is isenthalpic $h_1 = h_2$.
  • [ ] Free expansion $W = 0, Q = 0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air at $200 \text{ kPa}$ and $0.1 \text{ m}^3$ expands isothermally to $0.4 \text{ m}^3$. Calculate work done. - Solution:

$${W = P_1 V_1 \ln\left(\frac{V_2}{V_1}\right) = (200 \times 10^3) \times 0.1 \times \ln\left(\frac{0.4}{0.1}\right) = 20000 \times \ln(4) = 20000 \times 1.3863 = 27726 \text{ J} = 27.73 \text{ kJ}}$$
- Key Insight: In isothermal process of ideal gas, $Q = W = 27.73 \text{ kJ}$.

3.4 Second Law of Thermodynamics, Carnot Engine, Refrigerator & Heat Pump

1. Concept Pointers
  • Kelvin-Planck Statement: Impossible to construct a device operating in a cycle that produces net work while exchanging heat with a single thermal reservoir (PMM-II impossible).
  • Clausius Statement: Impossible to construct a device operating in a cycle that transfers heat from a lower temperature body to a higher temperature body without external work input.
  • Carnot cycle provides the upper theoretical limit for thermal efficiency $\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - T_L/T_H$.
  • Heat Pump vs Refrigerator: $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$.
💡 Key Concept: Carnot Efficiency Principle: Carnot efficiency depends ONLY on absolute reservoir temperatures ($T_H, T_L$ in Kelvin), completely independent of the working fluid!

[!TRAP] Absolute Temperature Trap: ALWAYS convert temperatures to Kelvin $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$ when calculating Carnot efficiency or COP! Using Celsius gives completely incorrect values.

2. Decision Tree
Thermal Machine Performance?
├─ Heat Engine → Efficiency eta = W_net / Q_H = 1 - Q_L / Q_H <= 1 - T_L / T_H
├─ Refrigerator → COP_R = Q_L / W_net <= T_L / (T_H - T_L)
└─ Heat Pump → COP_HP = Q_H / W_net <= T_H / (T_H - T_L) = COP_R + 1
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Carnot efficiency $\eta = 1 - T_L/T_H$.
  • [ ] Temperatures MUST be in Kelvin.
  • [ ] $COP_{HP} = COP_R + 1$.
  • [ ] PMM-II violates Second Law.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A Carnot heat engine operates between $600^\circ\text{C}$ $873.15 \text{ K}$ and $30^\circ\text{C}$ $303.15 \text{ K}$. Calculate its maximum theoretical efficiency. - Solution:

$${T_H = 600 + 273.15 = 873.15 \text{ K}, \quad T_L = 30 + 273.15 = 303.15 \text{ K}}$$
$${\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{303.15}{873.15} = 1 - 0.3472 = 0.6528 = 65.28\%}$$
- Key Insight: Convert Celsius to Kelvin before calculating.

3.5 Entropy, Clausius Inequality & Entropy Generation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Clausius Inequality: $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \le 0$.
  • Entropy $S$ is a state property $dS = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}/T$.
  • Principle of Entropy Increase: Total entropy of an isolated system (or Universe) never decreases $\Delta S_{\text{universe}} = \Delta S_{\text{sys}} + \Delta S_{\text{surr}} = S_{\text{gen}} \ge 0$.
  • Isentropic process is internally reversible and adiabatic $dS = 0$.
💡 Key Concept: Entropy Change of Universe: - Reversible Process: $S_{\text{gen}} = 0 \implies \Delta S_{\text{universe}} = 0$. - Irreversible Process: $S_{\text{gen}} > 0 \implies \Delta S_{\text{universe}} > 0$. - Impossible Process: $S_{\text{gen}} < 0$.

[!TRAP] Isentropic vs Adiabatic: Isentropic means BOTH reversible and adiabatic. An adiabatic process that is irreversible has $S_{\text{gen}} > 0$, so $\Delta S > 0$ (NOT isentropic)!

2. Decision Tree
Entropy Problem?
├─ Reversible Process → dS = dQ_rev / T, S_gen = 0
├─ Irreversible Process → S_gen > 0, Delta S_universe > 0
└─ Ideal Gas Delta S → cv*ln(T2/T1) + R*ln(v2/v1) = cp*ln(T2/T1) - R*ln(P2/P1)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Clausius inequality $\oint \delta Q / T \le 0$.
  • [ ] $S_{\text{gen}} \ge 0$ always.
  • [ ] Isentropic = Reversible + Adiabatic.
  • [ ] T-S area represents heat transfer for reversible process.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Heat transfer of $15 \text{ kJ}$ occurs from a thermal reservoir at $300 \text{ K}$. Calculate the entropy change of the reservoir. - Solution:

$${\Delta S_{\text{reservoir}} = \frac{Q}{T} = \frac{+15 \text{ kJ}}{300 \text{ K}} = +0.05 \text{ kJ/K} = +50 \text{ J/K}}$$
- Key Insight: Heat added to a reservoir increases its entropy by $Q/T$.

3.6 Availability (Exergy), Irreversibility & Lost Work

1. Concept Pointers
  • Availability (Exergy) is the maximum theoretical useful work obtainable as a system reaches dead state $T_0, P_0$.
  • Gouy-Stodola Theorem: Irreversibility $I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}} = W_{\text{rev}} - W_{\text{actual}}$.
  • Unavailable Energy $\text{UE} = T_0 \Delta S$.
💡 Key Concept: Exergy Destruction: Energy is conserved (First Law), but Exergy (Availability) is DESTROYED by irreversibilities like friction and finite temperature difference heat transfer!
2. Decision Tree
Availability / Irreversibility Problem?
├─ Lost Work / Irreversibility → I = T_0 * S_gen = W_rev - W_actual
├─ Unavailable Energy → UE = T_0 * Delta S
└─ Second-Law Efficiency → eta_II = W_actual / W_rev
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}}$.
  • [ ] Exergy is destroyed, energy is conserved.
  • [ ] Dead state: $T_0, P_0$ in equilibrium with environment.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: $150 \text{ kJ}$ of heat is transferred from $800 \text{ K}$ to $400 \text{ K}$ with ambient $T_0 = 300 \text{ K}$. Calculate irreversibility. - Solution:

$${S_{\text{gen}} = \Delta S_{\text{sink}} + \Delta S_{\text{source}} = \frac{150}{400} - \frac{150}{800} = 0.375 - 0.1875 = 0.1875 \text{ kJ/K}}$$
$${I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}} = 300 \times 0.1875 = 56.25 \text{ kJ}}$$
- Key Insight: Irreversibility $I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}} = 56.25 \text{ kJ}$.

3.7 Air-Standard Cycles (Otto, Diesel & Dual Cycles)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Otto Cycle: Spark-ignition engines, constant volume heat addition $V = \text{const}$. Efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}}$.
  • Diesel Cycle: Compression-ignition engines, constant pressure heat addition $P = \text{const}$. Efficiency $\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{r^{\gamma-1}} \left[\frac{\rho^\gamma - 1}{\gamma(\rho - 1)}\right]$.
  • For same compression ratio $r$: $\eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$.
  • For same peak pressure and heat supply: $\eta_{\text{Diesel}} > \eta_{\text{Dual}} > \eta_{\text{Otto}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Compression Ratio Comparison: - Same $r$: Otto is most efficient. - Same max pressure & temperature: Diesel is most efficient.
2. Decision Tree
Air-Standard Cycle Check?
├─ Constant Volume Heat Addition → Otto Cycle (eta = 1 - 1/r^(gamma-1))
├─ Constant Pressure Heat Addition → Diesel Cycle (eta = 1 - (1/r^(gamma-1)) * [(rho^gamma - 1) / (gamma*(rho-1))])
└─ Dual Heat Addition → Dual Cycle (Combination of const V and const P)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Otto cycle: Isochoric heat addition.
  • [ ] Diesel cycle: Isobaric heat addition.
  • [ ] Same $r \implies \eta_{\text{Otto}} > \eta_{\text{Diesel}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An Otto cycle has a compression ratio $r = 8$ $\gamma = 1.4$. Calculate thermal efficiency. - Solution:

$${\eta = 1 - \frac{1}{8^{1.4 - 1}} = 1 - \frac{1}{8^{0.4}} = 1 - \frac{1}{2.2974} = 1 - 0.4352 = 0.5648 = 56.48\%}$$
- Key Insight: Otto efficiency depends only on $r$ and $\gamma$.

3.8 Properties of Pure Substances & Steam Tables

1. Concept Pointers
  • Gibbs Phase Rule: $F = C - P + 2$.
  • Dryness Fraction $x = \frac{m_g}{m_g + m_f}$.
  • Saturated State: Liquid and vapour coexist at constant saturation temperature $T_{\text{sat}}$ for a given $P_{\text{sat}}$.
  • Critical Point of Water: $374.14^\circ\text{C}, 22.06 \text{ MPa}$; latent heat $h_{fg} = 0$.
  • Triple Point of Water: $0.01^\circ\text{C}, 0.611 \text{ kPa}$; $F = 0$.
💡 Key Concept: Property Evaluation in Wet Mixture: - Volume: $v = v_f + x v_{fg}$ - Enthalpy: $h = h_f + x h_{fg}$ - Entropy: $s = s_f + x s_{fg}$

[!TRAP] Latent Heat at Critical Point: Latent heat of vaporisation $h_{fg}$ is ZERO at the critical point, NOT maximum!

2. Decision Tree
Pure Substance State Determination?
├─ x = 0 → Saturated Liquid (v = v_f, h = h_f)
├─ 0 < x < 1 → Wet Vapour Mixture (h = h_f + x*h_fg)
├─ x = 1 → Dry Saturated Vapour (h = h_g)
└─ T > T_sat at P → Superheated Vapour (Behaves approximately as ideal gas)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Gibbs Phase Rule $F = C - P + 2$.
  • [ ] Dryness fraction $x = m_g / (m_g + m_f)$.
  • [ ] Water triple point: $0.01^\circ\text{C}, 0.611 \text{ kPa}$.
  • [ ] Critical point $h_{fg} = 0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A wet steam sample contains $1.5 \text{ kg}$ water liquid in suspension with $48.5 \text{ kg}$ dry steam. Calculate dryness fraction $x$. - Solution:

$${m_f = 1.5 \text{ kg}, \quad m_g = 48.5 \text{ kg}}$$
$${x = \frac{m_g}{m_g + m_f} = \frac{48.5}{48.5 + 1.5} = \frac{48.5}{50} = 0.97}$$
- Key Insight: Dryness fraction $x = 0.97$ ($97\%$ dry).

3.9 Real Gases, van der Waals Equation & Joule-Thomson Effect

1. Concept Pointers
  • Compressibility Factor $Z = \frac{P v}{R T}$. For ideal gas $Z = 1$.
  • van der Waals equation: $\left(P + \frac{a}{v^2}\right)(v - b) = R T$.
  • Joule-Thomson Coefficient $\mu = \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_h$.
  • $\mu > 0 \implies$ Cooling on throttling expansion.
  • $\mu < 0 \implies$ Heating on throttling expansion.
  • $\mu = 0 \implies$ Inversion temperature point.
💡 Key Concept: van der Waals Critical Compressibility: At critical point, van der Waals gas gives $Z_c = \frac{P_c v_c}{R T_c} = 0.375 = \frac{3}{8}$.
2. Decision Tree
Joule-Thomson Throttling Effect?
├─ mu > 0 → Temperature drops on expansion (Cooling)
├─ mu < 0 → Temperature rises on expansion (Heating: H2, He at room temp)
└─ mu = 0 → Inversion Temperature / Ideal Gas (No temperature change)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] van der Waals $Z_c = 0.375$.
  • [ ] $\mu = (\partial T / \partial P)_h$.
  • [ ] $\mu > 0$ means cooling on pressure drop.
  • [ ] Ideal gas $\mu = 0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: What is the value of the Joule-Thomson coefficient for an ideal gas? - Solution: - For an ideal gas, enthalpy $h = f(T)$ only. Since throttling is isenthalpic $h = \text{const}$, temperature $T$ must remain constant. - Thus, $\mu = \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_h = 0$. - Key Insight: Ideal gas exhibits zero temperature change during throttling $\mu = 0$.

3.10 Thermodynamic Potentials, Maxwell Relations, Clapeyron Equation & Born Square

1. Concept Pointers
  • Fundamental Potentials:
  • Internal Energy $U(S,V)$
  • Enthalpy $H(S,P) = U + PV$
  • Helmholtz Free Energy $F(T,V) = U - TS$
  • Gibbs Free Energy $G(T,P) = H - TS$
  • Clausius-Clapeyron Equation: $\frac{dP}{dT} = \frac{h_{fg}}{T (v_g - v_f)}$ gives phase-change curve slope on $P-T$ diagram.
  • Slope of isobar on Mollier $h-s$ chart equals absolute temperature $T$: $\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P = T$.
💡 Key Concept: Born Thermodynamic Square Mnemonic: - Corners (clockwise from top-left): $S \to P \to V \to T$ - Sides (clockwise from top): $U$ (top), $H$ (right), $G$ (bottom), $F$ (left) - Diagonals give natural variable exact differentials and Maxwell relation signs!
2. Decision Tree
Maxwell Relation Identification?
├─ From dU = T*dS - P*dV → (dT/dV)_S = -(dP/dS)_V
├─ From dH = T*dS + V*dP → (dT/dP)_S = (dV/dS)_P
├─ From dF = -S*dT - P*dV → (dS/dV)_T = (dP/dT)_V
└─ From dG = -S*dT + V*dP → (dS/dP)_T = -(dV/dT)_P
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Helmholtz $F = U - TS$.
  • [ ] Gibbs $G = H - TS$.
  • [ ] 4 Maxwell relations derived from exact differentials.
  • [ ] Isobaric slope on Mollier chart $= T$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Prove that the slope of a constant pressure line on a Mollier $h-s$ diagram equals absolute temperature $T$. - Solution: - From $T ds = dh - v dp$. - At constant pressure $dp = 0$, $T ds = dh \implies \left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P = T$. - Key Insight: Isobaric slope on $h-s$ chart is $T$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is an isolated system? Give an example.
A: An isolated system is one in which neither mass nor energy (heat or work) crosses the boundary $\Delta m = 0, \Delta E = 0$. The Universe is the ultimate isolated system; a perfectly insulated thermos flask is a close practical approximation.

Q2: Differentiate between intensive and extensive properties.
A: Intensive properties are independent of system mass (e.g. Temperature $T$, Pressure $P$, Density $\rho$, Viscosity $\mu$). Extensive properties are directly additive with mass (e.g. Mass $m$, Volume $V$, Internal Energy $U$, Entropy $S$).

Q3: State the Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics and its physical significance.
A: If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. It provides the theoretical basis for temperature measurement.

Q4: Why is internal energy $U$ of an ideal gas a function of temperature alone?
A: Because ideal gas molecules are assumed to exert no inter-molecular attractive forces. Hence, internal potential energy is zero, leaving internal kinetic energy (proportional to absolute temperature $T$) as the sole component of $U$.

Q5: What is free expansion and what is the work done during free expansion?
A: Free expansion occurs when a fluid expands into an evacuated space (vacuum) without encountering resisting force. Since resisting pressure $P_{\text{ext}} = 0$, boundary work $W = \int P_{\text{ext}} dV = 0$.

Q6: What is a throttling process and what property remains constant?
A: Throttling is an irreversible steady-flow process where fluid passes through a restriction (porous plug, orifice, or partially closed valve) without heat transfer $Q=0$ or external work $W=0$. Enthalpy remains constant ($h_1 = h_2$, isenthalpic).

Q7: State Kelvin-Planck and Clausius statements of the Second Law.
A: Kelvin-Planck: It is impossible to construct a heat engine operating in a cycle that produces net work while exchanging heat with a single reservoir. Clausius: It is impossible to construct a cyclic device that transfers heat from a colder body to a hotter body without external work.

Q8: What is a Perpetual Motion Machine of the Second Kind (PMM-II)?
A: A hypothetical heat engine that absorbs heat from a single thermal reservoir and converts 100% of it into work $\eta = 100\%$. It violates the Kelvin-Planck statement of the Second Law.

Q9: Define Carnot efficiency and state the factors it depends on.
A: $\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_L}{T_H}$. It is the maximum theoretical efficiency between two reservoirs and depends ONLY on absolute source temperature $T_H$ and sink temperature $T_L$ in Kelvin.

Q10: What is Clausius Inequality?
A: $\oint \frac{\delta Q}{T} \le 0$. Equality holds for reversible cycles, less than zero for irreversible cycles, and greater than zero represents an impossible cycle.

Q11: Define Entropy and state the Principle of Increase of Entropy.
A: Entropy $S$ is a thermodynamic state property defined by $dS = \delta Q_{\text{rev}}/T$. The total entropy of an isolated system (or Universe) never decreases $\Delta S_{\text{isolated}} \ge 0$.

Q12: Define Availability (Exergy) and Irreversibility.
A: Availability is the maximum theoretical useful work extractable as a system transitions reversibly to the dead state $T_0, P_0$. Irreversibility $I$ is work lost due to irreversibilities $I = T_0 S_{\text{gen}}$.

Q13: Define Dryness Fraction (Vapour Quality) $x$.
A: The mass fraction of dry saturated vapour in a wet mixture: $x = \frac{m_g}{m_g + m_f}$. $x=0$ for saturated liquid and $x=1$ for dry saturated vapour.

Q14: What is the significance of the Critical Point of water?
A: The state $374.14^\circ\text{C}, 22.06 \text{ MPa}$ where saturated liquid and saturated vapour lines meet, and latent heat of vaporisation drops to zero $h_{fg} = 0$.

Q15: State the Joule-Thomson coefficient $\mu$ and its physical meaning.
A: $\mu = \left(\frac{\partial T}{\partial P}\right)_h$. $\mu > 0$ indicates fluid cools upon pressure drop; $\mu < 0$ indicates fluid heats upon expansion; $\mu = 0$ corresponds to ideal gases or inversion states.

  • Free Expansion vs Throttling:
  • Free Expansion: $U_1 = U_2$ (Isenergetic for ideal gas), $Q=0, W=0, \Delta S > 0$.
  • Throttling: $h_1 = h_2$ (Isenthalpic), $Q=0, W=0, \Delta S > 0$.
  • Isentropic vs Adiabatic:

  • Isentropic means BOTH Reversible AND Adiabatic $\Delta S = 0$.

  • Mollier Diagram Isobaric Slope:
  • Slope of constant-pressure line on $h-s$ chart is absolute temperature $T$: $\left(\frac{\partial h}{\partial s}\right)_P = T$.
