 Dimensionless $\theta$ = tilt angle with vertical towards turn center ($+$ same rotation, $-$ opposite).

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Axis of Spin: The axis about which a rotor, propeller, wheel, or disc rotates at high angular velocity $\omega$.
  • Axis of Precession: The axis about which the axis of spin rotates or turns at angular velocity $\omega_p$.
  • Axis of Gyroscopic Couple: The axis perpendicular to both the Axis of Spin and Axis of Precession, along which the active/reactive gyroscopic torque acts.
  • Active Gyroscopic Couple: The external torque required to be applied on a spinning body to cause its spin axis to precess at angular speed $\omega_p$.
  • Reactive Gyroscopic Couple: The equal and opposite reaction torque exerted BY the spinning body ON its supporting frame, chassis, vessel, or aircraft structure $C_{\text{reactive}} = -C_{\text{active}}$.
  • Pitching (Ship Motion): The upward and downward angular oscillation of the ship's bow and stern in a vertical plane about a transverse axis.
  • Steering (Ship Motion): The turning of a ship to the left (Port) or right (Starboard) in a horizontal plane about a vertical axis.
  • Rolling (Ship Motion): The side-to-side rocking of a ship in a transverse plane about a longitudinal axis (parallel to the rotor shaft axis).
  • Angle of Heel $\theta$: The inclination angle of a two-wheeled vehicle from the vertical toward the center of curvature while negotiating a turn.

3.1 Gyroscopic Couple Derivation & 3 Perpendicular Planes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Derivation of Gyroscopic Couple $C$:
  • Consider a disc of mass moment of inertia $I$ spinning about axis $OX$ with angular velocity $\omega$.
  • Angular momentum vector along $OX$: $\vec{H} = I \omega$.
  • Let axis $OX$ precess in the horizontal plane $XOY$ through a small angle $\delta\theta$ about vertical axis $OZ$ in time $\delta t$.
  • New angular momentum vector $\vec{H}' = I(\omega + \delta\omega)$ along $OX'$.
  • Change in angular momentum vector:
    • Change along initial direction $OX$: $I(\omega + \delta\omega) \cos(\delta\theta) - I \omega \approx I \cdot \delta\omega$.
    • Change perpendicular to $OX$ (along $OY$): $I(\omega + \delta\omega) \sin(\delta\theta) \approx I \cdot \omega \cdot \delta\theta$.
  • Active Gyroscopic Torque $C = \lim_{\delta t \to 0} \frac{I \omega \delta\theta}{\delta t} = I \cdot \omega \cdot \frac{d\theta}{dt}$:
    $${\boxed{C = I \cdot \omega \cdot \omega_p}}$$
  • The Three Mutually Perpendicular Planes & Axes:
  • Plane of Spin (Axis $OX$): Disc rotates in plane $YOZ$ about spin axis $OX$.
  • Plane of Precession (Axis $OZ$): Spin axis $OX$ precesses in horizontal plane $XOY$ about vertical precession axis $OZ$.
  • Plane of Gyroscopic Couple (Axis $OY$): Active/reactive gyroscopic couple acts in vertical plane $ZOX$ about couple axis $OY$.
  • Fundamental Rule: Spin axis, precession axis, and couple axis MUST ALWAYS BE MUTUALLY PERPENDICULAR (occupy three mutually perpendicular planes).
💡 Key Concept: Three Perpendicular Axes Rule: 1. Axis of Spin $OX$ 2. Axis of Precession $OZ$ 3. Axis of Couple $OY$ If any two axes become parallel (e.g., during ship rolling), precession vanishes $\omega_p = 0$ and gyroscopic couple becomes ZERO!

[!TRAP] Active vs Reactive Couple: - Active Couple = External torque applied to cause precession. - Reactive Couple = Torque felt BY the vehicle frame/chassis $C_{\text{reactive}} = -C_{\text{active}}$. In exam MCQs asking "what happens to aircraft/ship", ALWAYS determine the REACTIVE couple direction!

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Basic Gyroscopic Couple Calculation - Problem: A rotor of mass $100 \text{ kg}$ has a radius of gyration $k = 0.4 \text{ m}$. It rotates at $1800 \text{ rpm}$. The rotor housing precesses about a vertical axis at $0.2 \text{ rad/s}$. Calculate the gyroscopic couple in $\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate moment of inertia $I$:

$${I = m k^2 = 100 \times (0.4)^2 = 100 \times 0.16 = 16 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
2. Calculate spin speed $\omega$:
$${\omega = \frac{2\pi N}{60} = \frac{2\pi \times 1800}{60} = 60\pi \approx 188.5 \text{ rad/s}}$$
3. Precession speed $\omega_p = 0.2 \text{ rad/s}$. 4. Calculate Gyroscopic Couple $C$:
$${C = I \omega \omega_p = 16 \times 188.5 \times 0.2 = 603.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
- Key Insight: $C = 603.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Always convert $N$ from rpm to rad/s first!

Worked Example 2: Turning Vehicle Gyroscopic Couple (PYQ Pattern) - Problem: A turbine rotor of a ship has mass moment of inertia $I = 200 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and rotates at $\omega = 200 \text{ rad/s}$. The ship travels at linear velocity $v = 5 \text{ m/s}$ along a curve of radius $R = 100 \text{ m}$. Calculate the gyroscopic couple exerted on the ship structure. - Solution: 1. Calculate precession speed $\omega_p$:

$${\omega_p = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{5}{100} = 0.05 \text{ rad/s}}$$
2. Calculate Gyroscopic Couple $C$:
$${C = I \omega \omega_p = 200 \times 200 \times 0.05 = 2000 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 2 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}}$$
- Key Insight: $C = 2000 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Direct application of $C = I \omega (v/R)$.

Worked Example 3: Active vs Reactive Vector Couple Direction - Problem: A disc of $I = 10 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ spins about horizontal axis $OX$ (pointing North) at $\omega = 100 \text{ rad/s}$ clockwise when viewed from South. The spin axis is turned towards East at precession rate $\omega_p = 0.5 \text{ rad/s}$ in horizontal plane. Determine: (a) Active couple vector direction, (b) Reactive couple vector direction and effect on disc bearings. - Solution: 1. Spin vector $\vec{H} = I \omega = 10 \times 100 = 1000 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}$ points NORTH. 2. Turning spin axis towards EAST causes $\delta\vec{H}$ vector to point EAST. 3. Active couple vector $\vec{C}{\text{active}}$ points EAST. By right-hand rule (thumb pointing East), active couple tends to lift the South end and press down the North end. 4. Reactive couple vector $\vec{C}{\text{reactive}}$ points WEST. By right-hand rule (thumb pointing West), reactive couple lifts the North end and presses down the South end of the bearing support! - Key Insight: Active couple vector is along $\delta\vec{H}$ (East); Reactive couple vector is opposite (West).

3.2 Gyroscopic Effects on Aeroplanes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Aeroplane Coordinate Frame:
  • Longitudinal Axis $OX$: Along fuselage from tail to nose (Spin Axis of propeller).
  • Lateral/Transverse Axis $OY$: Along wings (Pitching Axis / Couple Axis).
  • Vertical Axis $OZ$: Perpendicular to wings & fuselage (Precession Axis during turn / Yawing Axis).
  • Step-by-Step Vector Right-Hand Rule Method:
  • Draw spin vector $\vec{H}$ along longitudinal axis pointing forward (or backward depending on view).
    • Clockwise spin viewed from TAIL $\implies$ Vector points towards NOSE.
  • Turn vector $\vec{H}$ in horizontal plane in direction of turn (Left turn = counter-clockwise from above).
  • Active couple vector $\vec{C}_{\text{active}}$ points in direction of angular momentum change $\delta\vec{H}$.
  • Reactive couple vector $\vec{C}{\text{reactive}} = -\vec{C}{\text{active}}$.
  • Apply right-hand thumb rule to $\vec{C}_{\text{reactive}}$:
    • For Clockwise spin (viewed from tail) + Left turn: Reactive vector points to RIGHT WING $\implies$ RAISES NOSE, DIPS TAIL.
    • For Clockwise spin (viewed from tail) + Right turn: Reactive vector points to LEFT WING $\implies$ DIPS NOSE, RAISES TAIL.
💡 Key Concept: Aeroplane Rule (Clockwise from Tail): - LEFT TURN $\implies$ RAISE NOSE, DIP TAIL. - RIGHT TURN $\implies$ DIP NOSE, RAISE TAIL. Reversing propeller spin (anticlockwise from tail) REVERSES both effects!

[!TRAP] Viewpoint Trap (Tail vs Front): Pay strict attention to the examiner's viewpoint! - "Clockwise viewed from TAIL/REAR" = Standard rule (Left turn raises nose). - "Clockwise viewed from FRONT/NOSE" = Opposite rule (Left turn dips nose).

  • [ ] Clockwise from tail + Right turn $\implies$ Nose DOWN, Tail UP.
  • [ ] Formula $C = I \omega (v/R)$.
  • [ ] Convert flight speed to m/s $v = \text{km/h} / 3.6$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Aeroplane Gyroscopic Couple Magnitude & Direction - Problem: An aeroplane flies at $300 \text{ km/h}$ $83.33 \text{ m/s}$ along a left-hand curve of radius $600 \text{ m}$. The propeller has moment of inertia $I = 60 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and rotates clockwise at $1200 \text{ rpm}$ when viewed from the tail. Calculate: (a) Gyroscopic couple magnitude, (b) Effect on aeroplane attitude. - Solution: 1. Calculate spin velocity $\omega$:

$${\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 1200}{60} = 40\pi \approx 125.66 \text{ rad/s}}$$
2. Calculate precession velocity $\omega_p$:
$${\omega_p = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{83.33}{600} \approx 0.1389 \text{ rad/s}}$$
3. Calculate Gyroscopic Couple $C$:
$${C = I \omega \omega_p = 60 \times 125.66 \times 0.1389 \approx 1047.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
4. Determine direction: Clockwise spin (from tail) + Left turn $\implies$ Reactive couple RAISES NOSE AND DIPS TAIL. - Key Insight: $C = 1047.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$; Nose raises, tail dips.

Worked Example 2: Anticlockwise Propeller Rotation (Viewed from Tail) - Problem: An aeroplane engine propeller rotates ANTICLOCKWISE when viewed from the tail. If the plane takes a RIGHT turn, determine the attitude change of the aircraft. - Solution: 1. Base rule for Clockwise from tail + Right turn: Dips nose, raises tail. 2. Since spin is ANTICLOCKWISE, reverse the outcome: Anticlockwise from tail + Right turn $\implies$ RAISES NOSE AND DIPS TAIL. - Key Insight: Nose raises, tail dips.

Worked Example 3: Viewpoint Conversion (Clockwise Viewed from Nose) - Problem: An aeroplane propeller rotates CLOCKWISE when viewed from the NOSE (front). The aeroplane makes a LEFT turn. Determine whether the nose raises or dips. - Solution: 1. Clockwise viewed from NOSE $\equiv$ ANTICLOCKWISE viewed from TAIL. 2. Anticlockwise from tail + Left turn $\implies$ DIPS NOSE AND RAISES TAIL. - Key Insight: Nose dips, tail raises.

Worked Example 4: Elevator Control Force Balancing Gyroscopic Pitch - Problem: For the aeroplane in Example 1 ($C = 1047.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, nose raising effect), the distance between center of gravity and elevator tail control surface is $L = 5 \text{ m}$. Calculate the additional downward aerodynamic force required on the tail elevator to maintain level pitch flight. - Solution: 1. Gyroscopic reactive couple $C = 1047.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ acts in pitch plane. 2. Downward elevator force $F_e$ at distance $L = 5 \text{ m}$ creates restoring couple $F_e \times L = C$:

$${F_e \times 5 = 1047.2 \implies F_e = \frac{1047.2}{5} \approx 209.44 \text{ N}}$$
- Key Insight: Tail elevator must supply $209.44 \text{ N}$ downward aerodynamic force to prevent nose pitching up.

3.3 Gyroscopic Effects on Naval Ships (Steering, Pitching, Rolling)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Ship Terminology:
  • Bow (Fore): Front end of the ship.
  • Stern (Aft): Rear end of the ship.
  • Port: Left side of the ship when facing the bow (front).
  • Starboard: Right side of the ship when facing the bow (front).
  • Motion 1: Steering (Turning Left / Right in Horizontal Plane):
  • Precession axis is VERTICAL $OZ$.
  • Spin axis is LONGITUDINAL $OX$.
  • Couple axis is TRANSVERSE $OY$ $\implies$ Affects Bow and Stern vertical position!
  • Clockwise Rotor (viewed from Stern/Rear):
    • Steering LEFT (Port) $\implies$ Reactive couple RAISES BOW, LOWERS STERN.
    • Steering RIGHT (Starboard) $\implies$ Reactive couple LOWERS BOW, RAISES STERN.
  • Motion 2: Pitching (Bow Rising / Falling in Vertical Plane):
  • Pitching motion is Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) about transverse axis:
    $${\theta = \theta_0 \sin(\omega_0 t) \quad \text{where } \omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T_p}}$$
  • Precession velocity $\omega_p = \frac{d\theta}{dt} = \theta_0 \omega_0 \cos(\omega_0 t)$.
  • Maximum precession velocity $\omega_{p,\text{max}} = \theta_0 \cdot \omega_0$.
  • Maximum Gyroscopic Couple:
    $${\boxed{C_{\text{max}} = I \cdot \omega \cdot (\theta_0 \cdot \omega_0)}}$$
  • Direction Rule for Pitching (Clockwise rotor viewed from Stern):
    • Bow RISING $\implies$ Reactive couple TURNS SHIP TO STARBOARD (RIGHT).
    • Bow FALLING $\implies$ Reactive couple TURNS SHIP TO PORT (LEFT).
  • Motion 3: Rolling (Side-to-Side Rocking):
  • Rolling takes place about longitudinal axis $OX$.
  • Rotor spin axis is ALSO along longitudinal axis $OX$.
  • Spin axis and Rolling axis are PARALLEL $\implies$ No rotation of spin vector in space $\implies \omega_p = 0$.
  • Gyroscopic Couple during Rolling = ZERO $C_{\text{roll}} = 0$.
💡 Key Concept: Ship Summary Table (Clockwise Rotor from Stern): - Steering Left $\implies$ Bow UP, Stern DOWN. - Pitching Bow UP $\implies$ Turns to STARBOARD (Right). - Rolling $\implies$ ZERO EFFECT $C = 0$.

[!TRAP] Rolling Question Trap: MCQs often ask "What is the gyroscopic effect during rolling of a ship?". Options list various pitching or steering effects. Correct choice is ALWAYS NO GYROSCOPIC EFFECT!

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Ship Pitching Gyroscopic Couple - Problem: The turbine rotor of a ship has mass $m = 8 \text{ tonnes} = 8000 \text{ kg}$ and radius of gyration $k = 0.6 \text{ m}$. It rotates at $1800 \text{ rpm}$ clockwise when viewed from the stern. The ship pitches with Simple Harmonic Motion with periodic time $T_p = 16 \text{ sec}$ and total amplitude of pitch $= 12^\circ$ ($6^\circ$ on either side of mean position). Calculate: (a) Maximum precession speed $\omega_{p,\text{max}}$, (b) Maximum gyroscopic couple $C_{\text{max}}$, (c) Direction of couple when bow is rising. - Solution: 1. Calculate moment of inertia $I$:

$${I = m k^2 = 8000 \times (0.6)^2 = 8000 \times 0.36 = 2880 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
2. Calculate spin speed $\omega$:
$${\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 1800}{60} = 60\pi \approx 188.5 \text{ rad/s}}$$
3. Calculate pitch amplitude $\theta_0$ in radians:
$${\theta_0 = 6^\circ = 6 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = 0.1047 \text{ rad}}$$
4. Calculate SHM angular frequency $\omega_0$:
$${\omega_0 = \frac{2\pi}{T_p} = \frac{2\pi}{16} = 0.3927 \text{ rad/s}}$$
5. Calculate maximum precession speed $\omega_{p,\text{max}}$:
$${\omega_{p,\text{max}} = \theta_0 \cdot \omega_0 = 0.1047 \times 0.3927 \approx 0.0411 \text{ rad/s}}$$
6. Calculate Maximum Gyroscopic Couple $C_{\text{max}}$:
$${C_{\text{max}} = I \cdot \omega \cdot \omega_{p,\text{max}} = 2880 \times 188.5 \times 0.0411 \approx 22312 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 22.31 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}}$$
7. Direction: Clockwise rotor from stern + Bow Rising $\implies$ Reactive couple TURNS SHIP TO STARBOARD (RIGHT). - Key Insight: $C_{\text{max}} = 22.31 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$; Turns ship to Starboard.

Worked Example 2: Ship Steering Left Numerical Calculation - Problem: A ship's turbine rotor has $I = 400 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and spins at $2400 \text{ rpm}$ clockwise from stern. The ship steers to Port (Left) along a curve of radius $R = 80 \text{ m}$ at speed $v = 10 \text{ m/s}$ $36 \text{ km/h}$. Calculate gyroscopic couple and bow/stern motion. - Solution: 1. $\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 2400}{60} = 80\pi \approx 251.33 \text{ rad/s}$. 2. $\omega_p = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{10}{80} = 0.125 \text{ rad/s}$. 3. $C = I \omega \omega_p = 400 \times 251.33 \times 0.125 = 12566.4 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 12.57 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$. 4. Direction: Clockwise from stern + Left turn $\implies$ RAISES BOW, LOWERS STERN. - Key Insight: $C = 12.57 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$; Bow raises, stern lowers.

Worked Example 3: Ship Pitching with Bow Falling - Problem: For the ship in Example 1, determine the direction of the reactive gyroscopic couple when the bow is FALLING during pitching. - Solution: 1. Base pitching rule (Clockwise from stern + Bow Rising): Turns ship to Starboard (Right). 2. When Bow is FALLING, the direction of precession reverses: Clockwise from stern + Bow Falling $\implies$ TURNS SHIP TO PORT (LEFT). - Key Insight: Turns ship to Port (Left).

3.4 Stability of Four-Wheeled Automobile on Curved Track

1. Concept Pointers
  • System Description:
  • Vehicle of mass $m$ negotiates a turn of radius $R$ at linear velocity $v$.
  • 4 road wheels each of moment of inertia $I_w$ and radius $r_w$.
  • Engine rotor of moment of inertia $I_e$ rotating at speed $\omega_e = G \cdot \omega_w$, where $G$ is gear ratio.
  • Track width $= 2 x$, center of gravity height $= h$.
  • Centrifugal Overturning Couple $C_c$:
  • Centrifugal force $F_c = \frac{m v^2}{R}$ acts horizontally outward at center of gravity height $h$.
  • Centrifugal Couple $C_c = F_c \cdot h = \frac{m v^2 h}{R}$.
  • Effect: Tends to overturn vehicle outwards, DECREASING load on inner wheels and INCREASING load on outer wheels by $\frac{C_c}{2 x}$.
  • Gyroscopic Couple $C_g$:
  • Wheel precession speed $\omega_p = v/R$, wheel spin speed $\omega_w = v/r_w$.
  • Total Wheel Gyroscopic Couple $C_{g,w} = 4 I_w \omega_w \omega_p$.
  • Engine Gyroscopic Couple $C_{g,e} = I_e \omega_e \omega_p = I_e (G \omega_w) \omega_p$.
  • Total Gyroscopic Couple $C_g = C_{g,w} \pm C_{g,e} = (4 I_w \pm G I_e) \omega_w \omega_p$.
    • $+ G I_e$ when engine rotates in SAME sense as wheels.
    • $- G I_e$ when engine rotates in OPPOSITE sense to wheels.
  • Effect: For engine rotating in wheel direction during turn, reactive gyroscopic couple tilts vehicle OUTWARDS, further DECREASING inner wheel load and INCREASING outer wheel load by $\frac{C_g}{2 x}$.
  • Vertical Wheel Reactions:
  • Static reaction per wheel $W_s = \frac{m g}{4}$.
  • Inner Wheel Reaction $W_i$:
    $${\boxed{W_i = \frac{m g}{4} - \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}}}$$
  • Outer Wheel Reaction $W_o$:
    $${\boxed{W_o = \frac{m g}{4} + \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}}}$$
  • Condition for Stability (No Capsizing / Wheels Lifting):
  • Inner wheels will lift off the road if $W_i \le 0$:
    $${\boxed{W_i > 0 \implies \frac{m g}{4} > \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Inner vs Outer Wheel Reaction: Inner wheels lose ground contact force due to both centrifugal and gyroscopic couples! The maximum safe speed is limited by the condition $W_i = 0$.
2. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $C_c = \frac{m v^2 h}{R}$.
  • [ ] $C_g = (4 I_w \pm G I_e) \omega_w \omega_p$.
  • [ ] Inner wheel reaction $W_i = \frac{mg}{4} - \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}$.
  • [ ] Outer wheel reaction $W_o = \frac{mg}{4} + \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}$.
  • [ ] Capsizing condition: $W_i \le 0$.
3. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: 4-Wheeler Wheel Reaction & Maximum Safe Speed - Problem: A four-wheeled motor car of mass $m = 1000 \text{ kg}$ has track width $2 x = 1.4 \text{ m}$ $x = 0.7 \text{ m}$ and height of center of gravity $h = 0.6 \text{ m}$. Each of the four wheels has $I_w = 1.2 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and radius $r_w = 0.3 \text{ m}$. The engine rotating parts have $I_e = 1.5 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ and gear ratio $G = 3$ (same rotation sense as wheels). If car negotiates a turn of radius $R = 50 \text{ m}$ at speed $v = 15 \text{ m/s}$ $54 \text{ km/h}$, calculate: (a) Centrifugal couple $C_c$, (b) Gyroscopic couple $C_g$, (c) Inner and Outer wheel vertical reactions. - Solution: 1. Calculate static load per wheel:

$${W_s = \frac{m g}{4} = \frac{1000 \times 9.81}{4} = 2452.5 \text{ N}}$$
2. Calculate Centrifugal Couple $C_c$:
$${C_c = \frac{m v^2 h}{R} = \frac{1000 \times 15^2 \times 0.6}{50} = \frac{1000 \times 225 \times 0.6}{50} = 2700 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
3. Calculate wheel spin speed $\omega_w$ and precession speed $\omega_p$:
$${\omega_w = \frac{v}{r_w} = \frac{15}{0.3} = 50 \text{ rad/s}}$$
$${\omega_p = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{15}{50} = 0.3 \text{ rad/s}}$$
4. Calculate Total Gyroscopic Couple $C_g$:
$${C_g = (4 I_w + G I_e) \omega_w \omega_p = (4(1.2) + 3(1.5)) \times 50 \times 0.3}$$
$${C_g = (4.8 + 4.5) \times 15 = 9.3 \times 15 = 139.5 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
5. Calculate net couple term $\frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}$:
$${\frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x} = \frac{2700 + 139.5}{1.4} = \frac{2839.5}{1.4} \approx 2028.2 \text{ N}}$$
6. Calculate vertical reactions:
$${W_i = W_s - 2028.2 = 2452.5 - 2028.2 = 424.3 \text{ N}}$$
$${W_o = W_s + 2028.2 = 2452.5 + 2028.2 = 4480.7 \text{ N}}$$
- Key Insight: $W_i = 424.3 \text{ N} > 0 \implies$ Vehicle is stable and will not capsize at $15 \text{ m/s}$.

Worked Example 2: Maximum Capsizing Speed Calculation - Problem: For the 4-wheeled car in Example 1, determine the maximum linear speed $v_{\text{max}}$ $\text{m/s}$ at which inner wheel lift occurs $W_i = 0$. - Solution: 1. Set $W_i = 0 \implies \frac{m g}{4} = \frac{C_c + C_g}{2 x}$. 2. Express $C_c$ and $C_g$ in terms of $v^2$:

$${C_c = \left(\frac{m h}{R}\right) v^2 = \left(\frac{1000 \times 0.6}{50}\right) v^2 = 12 v^2}$$
$${C_g = \frac{4 I_w + G I_e}{r_w R} v^2 = \frac{4.8 + 4.5}{0.3 \times 50} v^2 = \frac{9.3}{15} v^2 = 0.62 v^2}$$
3. Combine terms: $C_c + C_g = 12.62 v^2$. 4. Substitute into limit equation:
$${2452.5 = \frac{12.62 v^2}{1.4} = 9.0143 v^2}$$
5. Solve for $v^2$ and $v_{\text{max}}$:
$${v^2 = \frac{2452.5}{9.0143} \approx 272.07 \implies v_{\text{max}} = \sqrt{272.07} \approx 16.49 \text{ m/s} \approx 59.4 \text{ km/h}}$$
- Key Insight: Maximum safe speed without inner wheel lifting is $16.49 \text{ m/s}$ $59.4 \text{ km/h}$.

3.5 Stability of Two-Wheeled Motor Vehicle & Angle of Heel

1. Concept Pointers
  • System Mechanics:
  • Two-wheeler of mass $m$, center of gravity height $h$, negotiating turn of radius $R$ at velocity $v$.
  • Must heel (tilt) inward at angle $\theta$ with vertical to maintain dynamic equilibrium.
  • Overturning Couples:
  • Centrifugal overturning couple about contact line: $C_c = \frac{m v^2}{R} h \cos\theta$.
  • Total Gyroscopic Couple: $C_g = (2 I_w \omega_w \pm I_e \omega_e \cos\theta) \omega_p = \frac{v^2}{R} \left(2 \frac{I_w}{r_w} \pm G \frac{I_e}{r_w} \cos\theta\right) \cos\theta$.
  • Approximating for small angles ($\cos\theta \approx 1$ in gyro term): Total Overturning Couple $C_{\text{overturn}} = \frac{v^2}{R} \left(m h + \frac{2 I_w \pm G I_e}{r_w}\right) \cos\theta$.
  • Restoring Gravity Couple:
  • Restoring moment about wheel-road contact line: $C_{\text{restore}} = m g h \sin\theta$.
  • Angle of Heel Equation $\theta$:
  • Equating restoring moment to overturning moment $m g h \sin\theta = C_{\text{overturn}}$:
    $${\boxed{\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g R} \left[1 + \frac{2 I_w \pm G I_e}{m \cdot h \cdot r_w}\right]}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Angle of Heel Effect: Without gyroscopic couple, $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g R}$. The presence of wheel and engine gyroscopic couples INCREASES the required angle of heel $\theta$ needed to prevent slipping/skidding outwards!
2. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Basic tilt angle without gyro: $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g R}$.
  • [ ] Complete tilt angle with gyro: $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g R} [1 + \frac{2 I_w \pm G I_e}{m h r_w}]$.
  • [ ] Gyroscopic action requires GREATER tilt angle $\theta$.
3. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Two-Wheeler Angle of Heel Calculation - Problem: A motorcycle has total mass $m = 250 \text{ kg}$, height of center of gravity $h = 0.6 \text{ m}$, wheel radius $r_w = 0.3 \text{ m}$, wheel moment of inertia $2 I_w = 1.8 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$. Engine moment of inertia $I_e = 0.2 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ with gear ratio $G = 5$. If negotiating a curve of radius $R = 40 \text{ m}$ at $v = 20 \text{ m/s}$ $72 \text{ km/h}$, calculate angle of heel $\theta$. - Solution: 1. Calculate term $\frac{2 I_w + G I_e}{m h r_w}$:

$${\frac{1.8 + 5(0.2)}{250 \times 0.6 \times 0.3} = \frac{1.8 + 1.0}{45} = \frac{2.8}{45} \approx 0.06222}$$
2. Calculate base factor $\frac{v^2}{g R}$:
$${\frac{v^2}{g R} = \frac{20^2}{9.81 \times 40} = \frac{400}{392.4} \approx 1.01937}$$
3. Calculate $\tan\theta$:
$${\tan\theta = 1.01937 \times (1 + 0.06222) = 1.01937 \times 1.06222 \approx 1.0828}$$
4. Calculate $\theta$:
$${\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.0828) \approx 47.28^\circ}$$
- Key Insight: Angle of heel $\theta = 47.28^\circ$. Gyroscopic effect increased $\tan\theta$ by $6.2\%$.

Worked Example 2: Motorcycle with Engine Rotating Opposite to Wheels - Problem: For the motorcycle in Example 1, calculate the required angle of heel if the engine rotates in OPPOSITE sense to the road wheels. - Solution: 1. Calculate modified term $\frac{2 I_w - G I_e}{m h r_w}$:

$${\frac{1.8 - 5(0.2)}{45} = \frac{1.8 - 1.0}{45} = \frac{0.8}{45} \approx 0.01778}$$
2. Calculate $\tan\theta$:
$${\tan\theta = 1.01937 \times (1 + 0.01778) = 1.01937 \times 1.01778 \approx 1.0375}$$
3. Calculate $\theta$:
$${\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.0375) \approx 46.06^\circ}$$
- Key Insight: Opposite engine rotation REDUCES the required angle of heel from $47.28^\circ$ to $46.06^\circ$ because engine gyroscopic couple partially cancels wheel gyroscopic couple!

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (20 Core Conceptual Pairs)

Q1: What is a Gyroscopic Couple?
A: The torque generated when the spin axis of a rotating body precesses (changes orientation in space), equal to $C = I \cdot \omega \cdot \omega_p$.

Q2: Name the three fundamental axes in gyroscopic motion.
A: Axis of Spin, Axis of Precession, and Axis of Gyroscopic Couple.

Q3: What is the relative spatial orientation of the three gyroscopic axes?
A: They are always mutually perpendicular to each other (occupying three perpendicular planes).

Q4: What is the relation between active and reactive gyroscopic couples?
A: They are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction $C_{\text{reactive}} = -C_{\text{active}}$.

Q5: Write the formula for precession velocity $\omega_p$ of a vehicle taking a turn.
A: $\omega_p = \frac{v}{R}$ (where $v$ is linear speed and $R$ is turn radius).

Q6: What is the effect of an engine rotor spinning clockwise (viewed from tail) on an aeroplane making a left turn?
A: The reactive gyroscopic couple raises the nose and dips the tail.

Q7: What is the effect of an engine rotor spinning clockwise (viewed from tail) on an aeroplane making a right turn?
A: The reactive gyroscopic couple dips the nose and raises the tail.

Q8: What is the effect on a ship when rotor spins clockwise (viewed from stern) and ship turns to Port (left)?
A: The reactive gyroscopic couple raises the bow and lowers the stern.

Q9: What happens to a ship when rotor spins clockwise (viewed from stern) and bow rises during pitching?
A: The reactive gyroscopic couple turns the ship towards Starboard (right).

Q10: What is the gyroscopic effect during rolling of a ship?
A: ZERO gyroscopic effect, because the spin axis and rolling axis are parallel $\omega_p = 0$.

Q11: Define Bow, Stern, Port, and Starboard in naval terminology.
A: Bow = front end, Stern = rear end, Port = left side facing front, Starboard = right side facing front.

Q12: How does pitching motion of a ship produce precession?
A: Pitching causes vertical angular oscillation of the longitudinal spin axis, producing precession about the transverse axis with maximum rate $\omega_{p,\text{max}} = \theta_0 \omega_0$.

Q13: Write the maximum gyroscopic couple formula during ship pitching.
A: $C_{\text{max}} = I \cdot \omega \cdot (\theta_0 \cdot \omega_0)$.

Q14: In a 4-wheeled vehicle taking a turn, which wheels experience a reduction in vertical reaction force?
A: The inner wheels experience reduced load $W_i = \frac{mg}{4} - \frac{C_c + C_g}{2x}$.

Q15: What is the condition to prevent a 4-wheeled vehicle from capsizing on a curve?
A: $W_i > 0$, meaning static load per wheel must exceed the combined overturning reaction $\frac{C_c + C_g}{2x}$.

Q16: Write the formula for Centrifugal Overturning Couple $C_c$ of a vehicle.
A: $C_c = \frac{m \cdot v^2 \cdot h}{R}$.

Q17: Write the formula for total Gyroscopic Couple $C_g$ of a 4-wheeler.
A: $C_g = (4 I_w \pm G I_e) \omega_w \omega_p$.

Q18: Why does a two-wheeler need to heel (tilt) inward when taking a turn?
A: To create a restoring gravity moment $m g h \sin\theta$ that balances the combined centrifugal and gyroscopic overturning moments.

Q19: Write the angle of heel formula for a two-wheeler including gyroscopic effects.
A: $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{g R} \left[1 + \frac{2 I_w \pm G I_e}{m h r_w}\right]$.

Q20: Does gyroscopic action increase or decrease the required angle of heel for a two-wheeler?
A: Gyroscopic action INCREASES the required angle of heel $\theta$ when engine rotates in wheel direction.
