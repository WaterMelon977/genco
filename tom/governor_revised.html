Governors & Speed Control
High-Yield Exam Revision Guide | APGENCO AE • GATE • SSC JE

1. Watt Governor

The simplest pendulum centrifugal governor. Equilibrium occurs when centrifugal moment balances gravitational moment.

$$\text{Watt Governor Height: } h = \frac{g}{\omega^2} = \frac{895}{N^2} \quad [\text{m}]$$
$N$: rotational speed ($\text{rpm}$) $h$: vertical height from ball plane to pivot ($\text{m}$)
⚠️ Exam Trap: Watt governor height $h = 895/N^2$ is completely independent of ball mass $m$! Adding heavier flyballs does not change $h$ or sensitivity. Watt governors become insensitive at $N > 80 \text{ rpm}$ due to $dh/dN = -1790/N^3$.

2. Porter & Proell Governors (Gravity Loaded)

$$\text{Porter Governor Height (Equal Arms, No Friction): } h = \frac{m + M}{m} \cdot \frac{895}{N^2} \quad [\text{m}]$$
$$\text{Porter with Friction } f \text{ and Link Ratio } q = \frac{\tan\beta}{\tan\alpha}: h = \frac{m\,g + \left(M\,g \pm f\right)\!\left(\dfrac{1+q}{2}\right)}{m\,g} \cdot \frac{895}{N^2}$$
$$\text{Proell Governor Height: } h = \frac{a}{e} \cdot \frac{m + M}{m} \cdot \frac{895}{N^2} \quad [\text{m}]$$
$M$: central dead-weight sleeve mass ($\text{kg}$) $f$: sleeve friction force ($\text{N}$) ($+f$ sleeve moving up, $-f$ moving down) $a$: lower link length; $e$: extension arm length to ball ($a/e < 1$)

3. Hartnell Governor (Spring-Loaded)

$$\text{Spring Stiffness: } k = 2 \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2 \frac{F_2 - F_1}{r_2 - r_1} \qquad \text{(Equal Arms } a = b: k = 2 \frac{F_2 - F_1}{r_2 - r_1}\text{)}$$
$$\text{Sleeve Lift: } x = (r_2 - r_1) \frac{b}{a}$$
$a$: vertical sleeve arm length; $b$: horizontal ball arm length $F_1, F_2$: centrifugal forces at $r_1, r_2$; Factor of 2 = two bell-cranks

4. Governor Performance Characteristics

Characteristic Definition Key Formula / Condition
Sensitiveness Ratio of speed range to mean speed $S = \frac{N_1 - N_2}{N_m} = \frac{2(N_1 - N_2)}{N_1 + N_2}$
Stability Unique equilibrium radius for every speed $\frac{dN}{dr} > 0$ (Radius increases as speed rises)
Isochronism Same equilibrium speed for all radii $N_1 = N_2 \implies S \to \infty$ (Infinite sensitiveness)
Hunting Continuous violent oscillation of sleeve Caused by over-sensitivity ($S \to \infty$) or governor lag
Insensitiveness Sleeve fails to respond to small speed changes Caused by sleeve friction $f$ (creates dead band $\Delta N = \pm f$)
🔴 EXAM FOCUS: An isochronous governor has constant speed across all radii ($N_1 = N_2$). While theoretically ideal, it causes severe hunting in practice. Practical governors maintain controlled stability ($\frac{dN}{dr} > 0$).

5. Governor Effort & Power

$$\text{Governor Effort (Speed Change Fraction } c\text{): } E = \frac{c}{1+c} (m + M) g$$
$$\text{Governor Power: } P = E \times x_{\text{sleeve}}$$

6. Key Points to Remember

Exam Survival Checklist
  • Watt Governor: $h = 895/N^2$ [m]. Independent of ball mass $m$. Limited to $N < 80 \text{ rpm}$.
  • Porter Governor: $h = \frac{m+M}{m} \cdot \frac{895}{N^2}$. Dead-weight sleeve $M$ raises operating speed.
  • Porter Friction: $+f$ for rising sleeve (speed up), $-f$ for falling sleeve (speed down).
  • Proell Governor: $h = \frac{a}{e}\cdot\frac{m+M}{m}\cdot\frac{895}{N^2}$. Extension arm $e$ reduces ball mass.
  • Hartnell Stiffness: $k = 2(a/b)^2 (F_2 - F_1)/(r_2 - r_1)$. Sleeve lift $x = (r_2 - r_1) b/a$.
  • Stability: $dN/dr > 0$. Isochronism: $N_1 = N_2$ ($S \to \infty$).
  • Hunting: Over-sensitive governor oscillates continuously.
  • Flywheel vs Governor: Flywheel = cyclic speed control within stroke; Governor = mean speed control across cycles.
