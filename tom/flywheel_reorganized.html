 $\text{m/s}$ Maximum allowable rim speed to avoid bursting.

 $\text{J}$ Triangular load assumption ($t_{\text{sheet}}$ = thickness).

$${\boxed{\Delta E = E_{\text{punch}} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{t_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}\right)}}$$
 $\text{J}$ Net energy delivered by flywheel during punch time.

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Flywheel: A heavy rotating mass attached to a shaft that acts as a kinetic energy reservoir. It absorbs energy when energy supply exceeds demand (during surplus periods) and releases energy when demand exceeds supply (during deficit periods), thereby controlling speed fluctuations WITHIN a single cycle.
  • Turning Moment Diagram (TMD): A graphical plot of turning moment (torque $T$ on crankshaft) along the ordinate against crank angle $\theta$ along the abscissa. The area under the TMD curve represents the total work done by or on the engine crankshaft.
  • Mean Resisting Torque $T_{\text{mean}}$: The constant resisting torque against which the engine performs work over a complete cycle. It is represented by a horizontal line on the Turning Moment Diagram such that the area under $T_{\text{mean}}$ equals the net area under the actual torque curve.
  • Maximum Fluctuation of Energy $\Delta E$: The maximum algebraic difference between the maximum kinetic energy and the minimum kinetic energy stored in the flywheel during a single operation cycle. Represented by the largest single surplus or cumulative area loop above/below $T_{\text{mean}}$ on the Turning Moment Diagram.
  • Coefficient of Fluctuation of Speed $C_s$: The ratio of the maximum speed variation $\omega_1 - \omega_2$ to the mean angular velocity $\omega_m$. It quantifies the permissible speed variation band required for a specific machinery application.
  • Coefficient of Steadiness $m$: The reciprocal of the coefficient of fluctuation of speed $m = 1/C_s$. A higher coefficient of steadiness indicates tighter speed control and smoother operation.
  • Coefficient of Fluctuation of Energy $C_e$: The ratio of the maximum fluctuation of energy $\Delta E$ to the total work done per cycle $W_{\text{cycle}}$.
  • Solid Disc vs Rim Flywheel:
  • Solid Disc: Uniform mass distribution across radius. Radius of gyration $k = R/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.707 R$. Lower moment of inertia per unit mass.
  • Rim-Type Flywheel: Mass concentrated primarily in the outer rim. Radius of gyration $k \approx R$. Maximizes moment of inertia per unit mass $I = m R^2$, storing twice the energy of a disc of identical mass and outer radius!
  • Rim Hoop Stress $\sigma = \rho v^2$: The circumferential tensile stress generated in a thin flywheel rim due to centrifugal force under rotation. Depends ONLY on density $\rho$ and peripheral velocity $v_{\text{rim}}$, independent of rim cross-sectional area!
  • Flywheel vs Governor (Critical Distinction):
  • Flywheel: Controls cyclic speed variations WITHIN EACH CYCLE caused by internal torque fluctuations. Operates purely mechanically by passive kinetic energy storage $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$. Does NOT control mean speed across changing external loads.
  • Governor: Controls mean speed OVER MULTIPLE CYCLES across changing external loads by actively regulating fuel/working fluid supply. Does NOT smooth out cyclic torque variations within a single stroke.

3.1 Turning Moment Diagrams & Work Done

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition & Plotting:
  • Turning moment (crankshaft torque $T$) plotted against crank angle $\theta$.
  • Crankshaft torque expression for single-cylinder engine:
    $${T = F_p \cdot r \left( \sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2n} \right)}$$
    where $F_p$ is net effort on piston, $r$ is crank radius, and $n = l/r$.
  • Area Property:
  • Area under Turning Moment Diagram over angle $\theta$ represents Work Done $W = \int T \, d\theta$.
  • For a constant resisting torque $T_{\text{mean}}$, work done per cycle:
    $${\boxed{W_{\text{cycle}} = T_{\text{mean}} \cdot \theta_{\text{cycle}}}}$$
  • $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 2\pi \text{ rad}$ $360^\circ$ for 2-stroke IC engine or steam engine.
  • $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 4\pi \text{ rad}$ $720^\circ$ for 4-stroke IC engine.
  • Mean Resisting Torque $T_{\text{mean}}$:
  • $T_{\text{mean}} = \frac{W_{\text{cycle}}}{\theta_{\text{cycle}}} = \frac{P}{\omega_m} = \frac{60 P}{2\pi N_m}$.
  • Points where the actual torque curve intersects the $T_{\text{mean}}$ line represent points of zero acceleration $\alpha = 0$, where engine speed reaches local extrema (maxima or minima)!
💡 Key Concept: Intersections of $T$ and $T_{\text{mean}}$: At any crank angle where actual torque $T$ equals mean torque $T_{\text{mean}}$, net torque $T_{\text{net}} = I \alpha = 0$, meaning rotational acceleration is ZERO. Consequently, flywheel speed reaches local maximum or minimum at these intersection points!

[!TRAP] 4-Stroke vs 2-Stroke Cycle Angle: - 4-Stroke Engine: 1 cycle $= 2 \text{ revolutions} \implies \theta_{\text{cycle}} = 4\pi \text{ rad}$. - 2-Stroke Engine: 1 cycle $= 1 \text{ revolution} \implies \theta_{\text{cycle}} = 2\pi \text{ rad}$. - Forgetting $4\pi$ for 4-stroke engines will halve calculated work per cycle and cause a $50\%$ error in $T_{\text{mean}}$!

Turning Moment Diagram Analysis
│
├── Determine Engine Type
│   ├── 4-Stroke Engine ──► Cycle Angle theta_cycle = 4*pi rad (720°)
│   └── 2-Stroke / Steam Engine ──► Cycle Angle theta_cycle = 2*pi rad (360°)
│
├── Calculate Work Done per Cycle (W_cycle)
│   ├── From Power: W_cycle = Power (W) * t_cycle = (P * 60) / N  (for 2-stroke)
│   │                                              = (P * 120) / N (for 4-stroke)
│   └── From Area: W_cycle = Integral(T d_theta)
│
└── Calculate Mean Torque (T_mean)
    └── T_mean = W_cycle / theta_cycle = Power / omega_mean
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $W_{\text{cycle}} = \int T d\theta$.
  • [ ] 4-stroke cycle $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 4\pi$.
  • [ ] 2-stroke cycle $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 2\pi$.
  • [ ] $T_{\text{mean}} = \frac{P}{\omega_m}$.
  • [ ] Speed extrema occur at $T = T_{\text{mean}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Mean Torque of 4-Stroke Engine - Problem: A 4-stroke IC engine develops $50 \text{ kW}$ power at a mean speed of $600 \text{ rpm}$. Calculate: (a) Mean angular velocity $\omega_m$, (b) Work done per cycle $W_{\text{cycle}}$, (c) Mean resisting torque $T_{\text{mean}}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate mean angular velocity $\omega_m$:

$${\omega_m = \frac{2\pi N_m}{60} = \frac{2\pi \times 600}{60} = 20\pi \approx 62.83 \text{ rad/s}}$$
2. For 4-stroke engine, cycle time $t_{\text{cycle}} = \frac{2 \times 60}{N_m} = \frac{120}{600} = 0.2 \text{ s}$.
$${W_{\text{cycle}} = P \cdot t_{\text{cycle}} = 50000 \times 0.2 = 10000 \text{ J} = 10 \text{ kJ}}$$
3. Calculate mean torque $T_{\text{mean}}$:
$${T_{\text{mean}} = \frac{P}{\omega_m} = \frac{50000}{62.83} \approx 795.77 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
Alternatively: $T_{\text{mean}} = \frac{W_{\text{cycle}}}{\theta_{\text{cycle}}} = \frac{10000}{4\pi} = \frac{2500}{\pi} \approx 795.77 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. - Key Insight: $W_{\text{cycle}} = 10 \text{ kJ}$, $T_{\text{mean}} \approx 795.8 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

3.2 Fluctuation of Energy & Loop Summation Method

1. Concept Pointers
  • Surplus & Deficit Energy Loops:
  • Areas above $T_{\text{mean}}$ line = Surplus Energy (Engine accelerates, flywheel absorbs energy).
  • Areas below $T_{\text{mean}}$ line = Deficit Energy (Engine decelerates, flywheel releases energy).
  • Loop Summation Method for Finding Max Energy Fluctuation $\Delta E$:
  • Let energy of flywheel at initial point $A$ be $E_A$.
  • Move sequentially to points $B, C, D, E...$ adding positive loop areas and subtracting negative loop areas:
    $${E_B = E_A + a_1}$$
    $${E_C = E_B - a_2 = E_A + a_1 - a_2}$$
    $${E_D = E_C + a_3 = E_A + a_1 - a_2 + a_3}$$
    $${...}$$
  • Identify the absolute maximum energy value $E_{\max}$ and absolute minimum energy value $E_{\min}$ among all points.
  • Maximum fluctuation of energy:
    $${\boxed{\Delta E = E_{\max} - E_{\min}}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Energy Loop Method: The maximum fluctuation of energy $\Delta E$ is NOT simply the largest single loop area! It is the difference between the HIGHEST cumulative energy level $E_{\max}$ and the LOWEST cumulative energy level $E_{\min}$ across the entire cycle.

[!TRAP] Single Loop vs Cumulative $\Delta E$: Assuming $\Delta E$ equals the biggest single area loop is a frequent exam mistake! Always perform step-by-step cumulative energy summation to locate absolute $E_{\max}$ and $E_{\min}$.

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Loop Summation Method for Delta E
│
├── Step 1: Assign baseline energy E_A at starting point A
├── Step 2: Tabulate cumulative energy at all intersection points
│   ├── E_B = E_A + Area_1
│   ├── E_C = E_B - Area_2 = E_A + Area_1 - Area_2
│   ├── E_D = E_C + Area_3 = E_A + Area_1 - Area_2 + Area_3
│   └── Continue across full cycle back to starting point (E_final = E_A)
│
├── Step 3: Identify E_max (highest value) and E_min (lowest value)
└── Step 4: Calculate Delta E = E_max - E_min
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Add areas above $T_{\text{mean}}$, subtract areas below $T_{\text{mean}}$.
  • [ ] $E_{\text{final}}$ must equal $E_{\text{initial}}$ for a closed cyclic process.
  • [ ] $\Delta E = E_{\max} - E_{\min}$.
  • [ ] Maximum speed occurs at $E_{\max}$.
  • [ ] Minimum speed occurs at $E_{\min}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Cumulative Loop Energy Summation - Problem: The turning moment diagram for a multi-cylinder engine yields the following loop areas above and below the mean torque line taken in order from start to end of cycle: $+520, -120, +380, -410, +150, -520 \text{ mm}^2$. The scale for turning moment is $1 \text{ mm} = 100 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ and for crank angle is $1 \text{ mm} = 1^\circ$. Calculate the maximum fluctuation of energy $\Delta E$ in Joules. - Solution: 1. Determine energy scale factor:

$${1 \text{ mm}^2 = (100 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}) \times \left(1^\circ \times \frac{\pi}{180} \text{ rad}\right) = 100 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{5\pi}{9} \approx 1.7453 \text{ J}}$$
2. Tabulate cumulative energy at points $A, B, C, D, E, F, G$: - $E_A = E$ - $E_B = E + 520$ - $E_C = E + 520 - 120 = E + 400$ - $E_D = E + 400 + 380 = E + 780 \quad \longleftarrow \text{MAXIMUM } (E_{\max})$ - $E_E = E + 780 - 410 = E + 370$ - $E_F = E + 370 + 150 = E + 520$ - $E_G = E + 520 - 520 = E \quad \longleftarrow \text{MINIMUM } (E_{\min} \text{ at } A \text{ and } G)$ 3. Identify absolute maximum and minimum energies: - $E_{\max} = E + 780 \text{ mm}^2$ (at point $D$) - $E_{\min} = E + 0 \text{ mm}^2$ (at points $A$ and $G$) 4. Calculate max energy fluctuation in $\text{mm}^2$:
$${\Delta E_{\text{area}} = E_{\max} - E_{\min} = (E + 780) - E = 780 \text{ mm}^2}$$
5. Convert to Joules:
$${\Delta E = 780 \times 1.74533 \approx 1361.36 \text{ J}}$$
- Key Insight: $\Delta E \approx 1361.4 \text{ J}$. Maximum speed occurs at point $D$, minimum speed at points $A$ and $G$.

3.3 Coefficient of Fluctuation of Speed & Kinetic Energy Storage

1. Concept Pointers
  • Speed Fluctuation Formulas:
  • Coefficient of fluctuation of speed:
    $${C_s = \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\omega_m} = \frac{N_1 - N_2}{N_m}}$$
  • Coefficient of steadiness:
    $${m = \frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{\omega_m}{\omega_1 - \omega_2}}$$
  • Extreme speed ratio formula:
    $${\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\omega_{\min}} = \frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{2 + C_s}{2 - C_s}}$$
  • Kinetic Energy Storage Formulas:
  • Energy stored at mean speed: $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_m^2$.
  • Maximum Fluctuation of Energy $\Delta E$:
    $${\Delta E = E_{\max} - E_{\min} = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_1^2 - \frac{1}{2} I \omega_2^2 = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2)}$$
  • Factoring difference of squares $(\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2) = (\omega_1 - \omega_2)(\omega_1 + \omega_2)$:
    $${\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1 - \omega_2)(\omega_1 + \omega_2) = I (\omega_1 - \omega_2) \left(\frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2}\right) = I (\omega_1 - \omega_2) \omega_m}$$
  • Multiplying and dividing by $\omega_m$:
    $${\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 \left(\frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\omega_m}\right) \implies \boxed{\Delta E = I \cdot \omega_m^2 \cdot C_s}}$$
  • Substituting $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_m^2 \implies I \omega_m^2 = 2E$:
    $${\boxed{\Delta E = 2 \cdot E \cdot C_s}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Triadic $\Delta E$ Master Relations: 1. $\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 C_s$ (Design formula with inertia). 2. $\Delta E = 2 E C_s$ (Design formula with mean Kinetic Energy $E$). 3. $\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2)$ (Exact energy difference).

[!TRAP] Factor of 2 Trap: - $\Delta E = 2 E C_s$ has a factor of 2 (NOT 1/2)! - $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_m^2$ has a factor of 1/2. - Confusing $\Delta E = 2 E C_s$ with $\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} E C_s$ is one of the most common MCQ errors.

2. ASCII Decision Tree
Flywheel Inertia & Mass Calculation
│
├── Given Speeds (N1, N2) or Cs
│   ├── Calculate mean speed N_m = (N1 + N2) / 2
│   └── Calculate Cs = (N1 - N2) / N_m
│
├── Calculate Required Moment of Inertia (I)
│   ├── From Delta E: I = Delta E / (omega_m^2 * Cs)
│   └── From Exact Speeds: I = 2 * Delta E / (omega_1^2 - omega_2^2)
│
└── Calculate Required Flywheel Mass (m)
    ├── For Solid Disc: I = 0.5 * m * R^2  ──► m = 2 * I / R^2
    └── For Thin Rim:   I = m * R^2        ──► m = I / R^2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $C_s = \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\omega_m}$.
  • [ ] $m = 1/C_s$.
  • [ ] $\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 C_s$.
  • [ ] $\Delta E = 2 E C_s$.
  • [ ] $\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2)$.
  • [ ] $\frac{\omega_1}{\omega_2} = \frac{2 + C_s}{2 - C_s}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Finding Moment of Inertia from Given Speeds - Problem: A flywheel absorbs $400 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ of energy $\Delta E = 400 \text{ J}$ as its speed increases from $\omega_2 = 190 \text{ rad/s}$ to $\omega_1 = 210 \text{ rad/s}$. Calculate: (a) Mean angular velocity $\omega_m$, (b) Coefficient of fluctuation of speed $C_s$, (c) Mass moment of inertia $I$. - Solution: 1. Calculate mean angular velocity $\omega_m$:

$${\omega_m = \frac{\omega_1 + \omega_2}{2} = \frac{210 + 190}{2} = 200 \text{ rad/s}}$$
2. Calculate $C_s$:
$${C_s = \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\omega_m} = \frac{210 - 190}{200} = \frac{20}{200} = 0.10 \quad (10\%)}$$
3. Calculate $I$ using exact kinetic energy formula:
$${\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2) \implies 400 = \frac{1}{2} I (210^2 - 190^2)}$$
$${400 = \frac{1}{2} I (44100 - 36100) = \frac{1}{2} I (8000) = 4000 I}$$
$${I = \frac{400}{4000} = 0.10 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
4. Verify using approximate formula $\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 C_s$:
$${\Delta E = 0.10 \times (200)^2 \times 0.10 = 0.10 \times 40000 \times 0.10 = 400 \text{ J}}$$
(Exact match!). - Key Insight: $I = 0.10 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$.

Worked Example 2: Calculating Speed Fluctuation Band - Problem: A flywheel of moment of inertia $I = 5 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ operates at a mean speed of $N_m = 300 \text{ rpm}$. If the maximum fluctuation of energy is $\Delta E = 750 \text{ J}$, calculate the maximum and minimum speeds $N_1, N_2$ in $\text{rpm}$. - Solution: 1. Calculate mean angular velocity $\omega_m$:

$${\omega_m = \frac{2\pi \times 300}{60} = 10\pi \approx 31.416 \text{ rad/s}}$$
2. Apply $\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 C_s$ to solve for $C_s$:
$${750 = 5 \times (31.416)^2 \times C_s = 5 \times 986.96 \times C_s = 4934.8 \times C_s}$$
$${C_s = \frac{750}{4934.8} \approx 0.0152 \quad (1.52\%)}$$
3. Speed range $N_1 - N_2 = C_s \times N_m$:
$${N_1 - N_2 = 0.0152 \times 300 \approx 4.56 \text{ rpm}}$$
4. Calculate $N_1$ and $N_2$:
$${N_1 = N_m + \frac{N_1 - N_2}{2} = 300 + 2.28 = 302.28 \text{ rpm}}$$
$${N_2 = N_m - \frac{N_1 - N_2}{2} = 300 - 2.28 = 297.72 \text{ rpm}}$$
- Key Insight: $N_1 = 302.28 \text{ rpm}$, $N_2 = 297.72 \text{ rpm}$.

3.4 Solid Disc vs Rim-Type Flywheels & Hoop Stress Derivation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Moment of Inertia Comparison:
  • Solid Disc Flywheel: Mass distributed uniformly from center to outer radius $R$.
    $${I_{\text{disc}} = \frac{1}{2} m R^2 \implies k_{\text{disc}} = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 R}$$
  • Thin Rim Flywheel: Mass concentrated primarily in the outer rim at mean radius $R$.
    $${I_{\text{rim}} \approx m R^2 \implies k_{\text{rim}} \approx R}$$
  • Efficiency Ratio: For the same total mass $m$ and outer radius $R$, a Rim flywheel stores TWICE the energy of a Solid Disc flywheel $I_{\text{rim}} / I_{\text{disc}} = 2.0$!
  • Centrifugal Hoop Stress Derivation $\sigma = \rho v^2$:
  • Consider a thin rim of mean radius $R$, cross-sectional area $A = b \cdot t$, density $\rho$, rotating at angular velocity $\omega$.
  • Mass of a small element subtending angle $d\theta$: $dm = \rho \cdot (A \cdot R \, d\theta)$.
  • Centrifugal force on element: $dF_c = dm \cdot \omega^2 R = \rho A R^2 \omega^2 \, d\theta$.
  • Vertical component balancing tensile hoop force $2P$:
    $${\int_0^\pi dF_c \sin\theta = \rho A R^2 \omega^2 \int_0^\pi \sin\theta \, d\theta = 2 \rho A R^2 \omega^2}$$
  • Tensile force in rim $2P = 2 \sigma A \implies 2 \sigma A = 2 \rho A R^2 \omega^2$.
  • Dividing by $2A$ yields the famous Hoop Stress equation:
    $${\boxed{\sigma = \rho \cdot R^2 \cdot \omega^2 = \rho \cdot v_{\text{rim}}^2}}$$
    where $v_{\text{rim}} = \omega R$ is the rim peripheral velocity.
💡 Key Concept: Area Independence of Hoop Stress: Rim tensile hoop stress $\sigma = \rho v^2$ depends ONLY on material density $\rho$ and peripheral linear velocity $v_{\text{rim}}$. It is completely INDEPENDENT of rim cross-sectional area $A$ or rim width $b$!

[!TRAP] Disc vs Rim Gyration Factor: - Disc: $k = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}} \implies k^2 = 0.5 R^2$. - Rim: $k = R \implies k^2 = R^2$. - Always verify whether the problem specifies a "solid disc" or "rim-type" flywheel before using $I = \frac{1}{2} m R^2$ or $I = m R^2$.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Solid Disc Flywheel Kinetic Energy - Problem: A solid circular disc flywheel of uniform thickness has mass $m = 20 \text{ kg}$, radius $R = 200 \text{ mm} = 0.2 \text{ m}$, and rotates at $N = 600 \text{ rpm}$. Calculate: (a) Moment of inertia $I$, (b) Kinetic energy stored $E$. - Solution: 1. Calculate moment of inertia $I$ for solid disc:

$${I = \frac{1}{2} m R^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times (0.2)^2 = 10 \times 0.04 = 0.40 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
2. Calculate angular velocity $\omega$:
$${\omega = \frac{2\pi \times 600}{60} = 20\pi \approx 62.832 \text{ rad/s}}$$
3. Calculate kinetic energy $E$:
$${E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 0.40 \times (62.832)^2 = 0.20 \times 3947.84 \approx 789.57 \text{ J}}$$
- Key Insight: $I = 0.40 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$, $E \approx 789.6 \text{ J}$.

Worked Example 2: Maximum Safe Rim Velocity & Hoop Stress - Problem: A cast iron flywheel rim has density $\rho = 7200 \text{ kg/m}^3$ and allowable tensile stress $\sigma_{\text{allow}} = 7.2 \text{ MPa} = 7.2 \times 10^6 \text{ N/m}^2$. Calculate: (a) Maximum safe peripheral velocity $v_{\max}$, (b) Maximum allowable speed $N_{\max}$ in $\text{rpm}$ if mean rim diameter is $D = 1.2 \text{ m}$. - Solution: 1. Apply hoop stress equation $\sigma = \rho v^2 \implies v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_{\text{allow}}}{\rho}}$:

$${v_{\max} = \sqrt{\frac{7.2 \times 10^6}{7200}} = \sqrt{1000} \approx 31.62 \text{ m/s}}$$
2. Calculate maximum rotational speed $N_{\max}$:
$${v_{\max} = \frac{\pi D N_{\max}}{60} \implies 31.62 = \frac{\pi \times 1.2 \times N_{\max}}{60} = 0.062832 \cdot N_{\max}}$$
$${N_{\max} = \frac{31.62}{0.062832} \approx 503.2 \text{ rpm}}$$
- Key Insight: $v_{\max} \approx 31.62 \text{ m/s}$, $N_{\max} \approx 503 \text{ rpm}$.

3.5 Punching Press Flywheel Calculations

1. Concept Pointers
  • Operation Cycle Breakdown:
  • Total time for one complete cycle: $t_{\text{cycle}} = \frac{60}{N} \text{ s}$.
  • Actual punching time: $t_{\text{punch}}$ (typically a small fraction of $t_{\text{cycle}}$, e.g. $10\% - 20\%$).
  • Idle time (rest of cycle): $t_{\text{idle}} = t_{\text{cycle}} - t_{\text{punch}}$.
  • Energy Balance Equations:
  • Energy required per punch $E_{\text{punch}}$: For shearing a plate of thickness $t_{\text{sheet}}$, hole diameter $d$, shear strength $\tau$: Maximum shearing force $F_{\max} = \pi \cdot d \cdot t_{\text{sheet}} \cdot \tau$. Assuming force varies linearly from $F_{\max}$ to $0$:
    $${\boxed{E_{\text{punch}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot F_{\max} \cdot t_{\text{sheet}}}}$$
  • Energy supplied by electric motor during cycle $E_{\text{motor}}$: The motor supplies energy continuously at constant power $P$:
    $${E_{\text{motor/cycle}} = P \cdot t_{\text{cycle}} = E_{\text{punch}}}$$
    Motor Power required: $\boxed{P = \frac{E_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}}$
  • Energy supplied by motor DURING punching stroke $E_{\text{motor,punch}}$:
    $${E_{\text{motor,punch}} = P \cdot t_{\text{punch}} = E_{\text{punch}} \cdot \left(\frac{t_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}\right)}$$
  • Net Energy supplied by Flywheel during punching $\Delta E$:
    $${\boxed{\Delta E = E_{\text{punch}} - E_{\text{motor,punch}} = E_{\text{punch}} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{t_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}\right)}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Flywheel Role in Punching Press: The motor size is chosen based on AVERAGE power across the entire cycle $P = E_{\text{punch}} / t_{\text{cycle}}$, NOT peak punching power! The flywheel stores energy during idle time $t_{\text{idle}}$ and supplies the deficit energy $\Delta E$ during the brief punching stroke $t_{\text{punch}}$.
2. ASCII Decision Tree
Punching Press Flywheel Analysis
│
├── Step 1: Calculate Energy per Punch (E_punch)
│   ├── Max Force F_max = pi * d * t_sheet * tau
│   └── Energy E_punch = 0.5 * F_max * t_sheet
│
├── Step 2: Calculate Motor Power (P)
│   ├── Cycle Time t_cycle = 60 / N
│   └── Power P = E_punch / t_cycle
│
├── Step 3: Calculate Flywheel Energy Fluctuation (Delta E)
│   └── Delta E = E_punch * (1 - t_punch / t_cycle)
│
└── Step 4: Size Flywheel Inertia (I)
    └── I = Delta E / (omega_m^2 * Cs)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $F_{\max} = \pi d t \tau$.
  • [ ] $E_{\text{punch}} = \frac{1}{2} F_{\max} t$.
  • [ ] $P = E_{\text{punch}} / t_{\text{cycle}}$.
  • [ ] $\Delta E = E_{\text{punch}} \left(1 - \frac{t_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}\right)$.
  • [ ] Size $I = \Delta E / (\omega_m^2 C_s)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Complete Punching Press Flywheel Sizing - Problem: A punching press punches 30 holes per minute ($N = 30 \text{ rpm}$, $t_{\text{cycle}} = 2 \text{ s}$). Each punch requires $5 \text{ kJ}$ of energy $E_{\text{punch}} = 5000 \text{ J}$. The punching operation takes $t_{\text{punch}} = 0.4 \text{ s}$. Speed of flywheel varies by $\pm 5\%$ $C_s = 0.10$ about a mean speed of $N_m = 250 \text{ rpm}$. Calculate: (a) Motor power required, (b) Maximum fluctuation of energy $\Delta E$, (c) Moment of inertia $I$ of the flywheel. - Solution: 1. Calculate Motor Power $P$:

$${P = \frac{E_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}} = \frac{5000 \text{ J}}{2 \text{ s}} = 2500 \text{ W} = 2.5 \text{ kW}}$$
2. Calculate energy supplied by motor during punching stroke $t_{\text{punch}} = 0.4 \text{ s}$:
$${E_{\text{motor,punch}} = P \cdot t_{\text{punch}} = 2500 \times 0.4 = 1000 \text{ J}}$$
3. Calculate net energy supplied by flywheel $\Delta E$:
$${\Delta E = E_{\text{punch}} - E_{\text{motor,punch}} = 5000 - 1000 = 4000 \text{ J}}$$
4. Calculate mean angular velocity $\omega_m$:
$${\omega_m = \frac{2\pi \times 250}{60} = \frac{25\pi}{3} \approx 26.18 \text{ rad/s}}$$
5. Calculate required moment of inertia $I$:
$${\Delta E = I \omega_m^2 C_s \implies 4000 = I \times (26.18)^2 \times 0.10}$$
$${4000 = I \times 685.39 \times 0.10 = 68.54 I \implies I = \frac{4000}{68.54} \approx 58.36 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2}$$
- Key Insight: Motor Power $= 2.5 \text{ kW}$, $\Delta E = 4000 \text{ J}$, $I \approx 58.36 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$.

3.6 Flywheel vs Governor Comparison

1. Comparative Analysis Table
Feature / Aspect Flywheel Governor
Primary Function Smooths out cyclic speed variations WITHIN a single cycle. Regulates mean speed OVER MULTIPLE CYCLES across load changes.
Cause of Speed Variation Internal cyclic torque fluctuations (surplus/deficit energy). External load changes on the engine/prime mover.
Mode of Operation Purely mechanical passive kinetic energy storage $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega^2$. Active control mechanism regulating fuel/fluid flow valve.
Control Action Continuous, automatic, instantaneous during every stroke. Intermittent, activates only when mean speed strays from setpoint.
Effect on Mean Speed Has NO CONTROL over mean speed if external load changes! Controls mean speed, keeping it within narrow limits.
Effect on Cyclic Torque Directly smooths out cyclic torque variations. Has NO EFFECT on cyclic torque variations within a stroke.
Dependency Essential for engines with high cyclic torque variation (4-stroke IC). Essential for applications with varying load (generators, turbines).

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (20 Core Conceptual Pairs)

Q1: What is the primary function of a flywheel in an engine?
A: To act as a kinetic energy reservoir that absorbs energy during surplus periods and releases energy during deficit periods, thereby controlling speed fluctuations WITHIN a single cycle.

Q2: How does a Flywheel differ from a Governor?
A: A flywheel smooths out cyclic speed variations WITHIN one cycle caused by internal torque fluctuations, whereas a governor regulates mean speed OVER MULTIPLE CYCLES across changing external loads by adjusting fuel supply.

Q3: Define Coefficient of Fluctuation of Speed $C_s$.
A: The ratio of the maximum speed range $\omega_1 - \omega_2$ to the mean angular velocity $\omega_m$, i.e., $C_s = \frac{\omega_1 - \omega_2}{\omega_m}$.

Q4: Define Coefficient of Steadiness $m$.
A: The reciprocal of the coefficient of fluctuation of speed, $m = \frac{1}{C_s} = \frac{\omega_m}{\omega_1 - \omega_2}$.

Q5: State the formula linking Maximum Fluctuation of Energy $\Delta E$ to moment of inertia $I$, mean speed $\omega_m$, and $C_s$.
A: $\Delta E = I \cdot \omega_m^2 \cdot C_s$.

Q6: State the formula linking Maximum Fluctuation of Energy $\Delta E$ to mean kinetic energy $E$ and $C_s$.
A: $\Delta E = 2 \cdot E \cdot C_s$, where $E = \frac{1}{2} I \omega_m^2$.

Q7: Write the exact energy fluctuation formula in terms of maximum $\omega_1$ and minimum $\omega_2$ speeds.
A: $\Delta E = \frac{1}{2} I (\omega_1^2 - \omega_2^2)$.

Q8: What is the angle of one complete cycle $\theta_{\text{cycle}}$ for a 4-stroke IC engine?
A: $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 4\pi \text{ rad}$ ($720^\circ$ or 2 crankshaft revolutions).

Q9: What is the angle of one complete cycle $\theta_{\text{cycle}}$ for a 2-stroke IC engine?
A: $\theta_{\text{cycle}} = 2\pi \text{ rad}$ ($360^\circ$ or 1 crankshaft revolution).

Q10: At what points on a Turning Moment Diagram does the engine reach maximum or minimum speed?
A: At the points where the actual torque curve intersects the mean resisting torque line $T = T_{\text{mean}}$, where acceleration $\alpha = 0$.

Q11: Write the formula for moment of inertia and radius of gyration of a solid circular disc flywheel.
A: $I = \frac{1}{2} m R^2$ and $k = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}}$.

Q12: Write the formula for moment of inertia and radius of gyration of a thin rim-type flywheel.
A: $I = m R^2$ and $k = R$.

Q13: How much more energy does a thin rim flywheel store compared to a solid disc flywheel of identical mass and outer radius?
A: A thin rim flywheel stores TWICE $2\times$ the energy of a solid disc flywheel because $I_{\text{rim}} / I_{\text{disc}} = 2.0$.

Q14: Derive the expression for centrifugal hoop stress $\sigma$ in a thin flywheel rim.
A: $\sigma = \rho v^2$, where $\rho$ is material density and $v = \omega R$ is rim peripheral velocity.

Q15: Does the centrifugal hoop stress in a flywheel rim depend on its cross-sectional area?
A: No. Hoop stress $\sigma = \rho v^2$ depends ONLY on material density $\rho$ and peripheral velocity $v$, independent of rim cross-sectional area.

Q16: What is the maximum safe peripheral velocity for cast iron flywheel rims?
A: Approximately $v_{\max} \approx 30 \text{ m/s}$.

Q17: How is motor power determined for a punching press operated with a flywheel?
A: Motor power is determined from AVERAGE energy required across the entire cycle time: $P = E_{\text{punch}} / t_{\text{cycle}}$.

Q18: Write the net energy formula $\Delta E$ supplied by a flywheel during the punching stroke.
A: $\Delta E = E_{\text{punch}} \left(1 - \frac{t_{\text{punch}}}{t_{\text{cycle}}}\right)$.

Q19: Express extreme speed ratio $\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\omega_{\min}}$ in terms of $C_s$.
A: $\frac{\omega_{\max}}{\omega_{\min}} = \frac{2 + C_s}{2 - C_s}$.

Q20: If speed fluctuates by $\pm 2\%$ about mean speed, what is the value of $C_s$?
A: $C_s = 2 \times 2\% = 4\% = 0.04$.
