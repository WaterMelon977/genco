2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Slider-Crank Mechanism: A 4-bar kinematic chain comprising one sliding pair (piston-cylinder) and three turning pairs (crank-frame, crank-conrod, conrod-piston pin).
  • Connecting Rod Obliquity Ratio $n$: The ratio of connecting rod center-to-center length $l$ to crank radius $r$ $n = l/r$. As $n \to \infty$, piston motion approaches pure Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM).
  • Piston Effort $F_p$: The net effective axial force acting along the line of stroke that propels the piston forward.
  • Crank Effort / Turning Moment $T$: The net torque exerted about the crankshaft rotation axis by the tangential component of connecting rod force.
  • Primary Unbalanced Force $F_P$: The unbalanced reciprocating force acting along the stroke line at engine rotation frequency $\omega$.
  • Secondary Unbalanced Force $F_S$: The unbalanced reciprocating force caused by connecting rod angular obliquity, acting at twice engine rotation frequency $2\omega$ with magnitude $1/n$ times primary force.
  • Partial Balancing $c$: The practice of balancing only a fraction $c$ (typically $50\% \text{ to } 75\%$) of reciprocating mass using revolving counterweights to prevent excessive vertical forces (Hammer Blow).
  • Tractive Effort Variation $\Delta F_T$: The net variation in forward pulling force along the rails in a locomotive due to unbalanced primary reciprocating forces.
  • Swaying Couple $C_{\text{sway}}$: An unbalanced horizontal couple acting about a vertical axis through the center of a locomotive, tending to sway the engine sideways against rail flanges.
  • Hammer Blow: The maximum vertical unbalanced force exerted by revolving overbalance masses on locomotive rails. It increases with speed squared $\omega^2$ and reduces effective wheel-rail contact pressure.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Typical Obliquity Ratio $n = l/r$: $3.5 \text{ to } 5.0$ in internal combustion engines.
  • Standard Reciprocating Balance Fraction $c$: $50\% \text{ to } 66.7\%$ $c = 0.5 \text{ to } 0.67$.
  • Swaying Couple Max Angles: $\theta = 45^\circ \text{ and } 225^\circ$.
  • Tractive Effort Max Angles: $\theta = 135^\circ \text{ and } 315^\circ$.
  • Secondary Force Frequency: $2 \times \text{ Engine Speed}$ $2\omega$.

3.1 Kinematics of Slider-Crank (Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Piston Displacement $x$:
  • Distance moved by piston from Inner Dead Center (IDC / TDC):
    $${x = r (1 - \cos\theta) + l (1 - \cos\phi)}$$
    Since $\sin\phi = \frac{\sin\theta}{n}$ and $\cos\phi = \sqrt{1 - \frac{\sin^2\theta}{n^2}} \approx 1 - \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2 n^2}$:
    $${\boxed{x = r \left[(1 - \cos\theta) + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2n}\right]}}$$
  • Piston Velocity $v_p$:
  • Differentiating displacement with respect to time $v_p = \frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{dx}{d\theta} \cdot \frac{d\theta}{dt}$:
    $${\boxed{v_p = \omega r \left(\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2n}\right)}}$$
  • At IDC $\theta = 0^\circ$ and ODC $\theta = 180^\circ$: $v_p = 0$.
  • Maximum velocity occurs slightly before $\theta = 90^\circ$ (at $\theta \approx 90^\circ - \frac{1}{2n} \text{ rad}$).
  • Piston Acceleration $a_p$:
  • Differentiating velocity with respect to time $a_p = \frac{dv_p}{dt}$:
    $${\boxed{a_p = \omega^2 r \left(\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}\right)}}$$
  • At IDC $\theta = 0^\circ$: $a_p = \omega^2 r \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)$ (Maximum acceleration).
  • At ODC $\theta = 180^\circ$: $a_p = -\omega^2 r \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$ (Maximum deceleration).
  • When $n \to \infty$ (infinite conrod length), $a_p = \omega^2 r \cos\theta$ (pure SHM!).
  • Connecting Rod Motion:
  • Angular velocity: $\omega_{cr} = \frac{d\phi}{dt} = \frac{\omega \cos\theta}{n}$.
  • Angular acceleration: $\alpha_{cr} = \frac{d\omega_{cr}}{dt} = -\frac{\omega^2 \sin\theta}{n}$.
💡 Key Concept: Obliquity Correction Effect: Connecting rod obliquity $n = l/r$ creates a secondary acceleration component $\frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}$. This increases IDC acceleration by $\frac{\omega^2 r}{n}$ and decreases ODC acceleration by $\frac{\omega^2 r}{n}$.

[!TRAP] Zero Acceleration Crank Angle: Piston acceleration $a_p = 0$ when $\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n} = 0$. For $n = 4$, this occurs at $\theta \approx 76^\circ$ (NOT at $\theta = 90^\circ$!).

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Piston Velocity & Acceleration at Given Crank Angle - Problem: A slider-crank mechanism has crank radius $r = 150 \text{ mm} = 0.15 \text{ m}$ and connecting rod length $l = 600 \text{ mm} = 0.6 \text{ m}$ $n = 4$. The crank rotates at $20 \text{ rad/s}$ constant angular velocity. When crank angle $\theta = 60^\circ$ from IDC, calculate: (a) Piston velocity $v_p$, (b) Piston acceleration $a_p$. - Solution: 1. Calculate trigonometric values for $\theta = 60^\circ$:

$${\sin 60^\circ = 0.8660, \quad \cos 60^\circ = 0.5000}$$
$${\sin 120^\circ = 0.8660, \quad \cos 120^\circ = -0.5000}$$
2. Calculate Piston Velocity $v_p$:
$${v_p = \omega r \left(\sin 60^\circ + \frac{\sin 120^\circ}{2n}\right) = (20 \times 0.15) \left(0.8660 + \frac{0.8660}{8}\right)}$$
$${v_p = 3.0 \times (0.8660 + 0.10825) = 3.0 \times 0.97425 \approx 2.923 \text{ m/s}}$$
3. Calculate Piston Acceleration $a_p$:
$${a_p = \omega^2 r \left(\cos 60^\circ + \frac{\cos 120^\circ}{n}\right) = (20^2 \times 0.15) \left(0.5000 + \frac{-0.5000}{4}\right)}$$
$${a_p = (400 \times 0.15) \times (0.5000 - 0.1250) = 60 \times 0.375 = 22.50 \text{ m/s}^2}$$
- Key Insight: $v_p = 2.92 \text{ m/s}$, $a_p = 22.5 \text{ m/s}^2$. Neglecting the secondary term $\frac{\cos 120^\circ}{4}$ would yield an erroneous $a_p = 30 \text{ m/s}^2$ ($33\%$ error!).

Worked Example 2: Dead Center Acceleration & Zero Acceleration Angle - Problem: For the mechanism in Example 1 $r = 0.15 \text{ m}, n = 4, \omega = 20 \text{ rad/s}$, calculate: (a) Acceleration at IDC $\theta = 0^\circ$, (b) Acceleration at ODC $\theta = 180^\circ$, (c) The crank angle $\theta$ where acceleration $a_p = 0$. - Solution: 1. Base term $\omega^2 r = (20)^2 \times 0.15 = 400 \times 0.15 = 60 \text{ m/s}^2$. 2. Acceleration at IDC $\theta = 0^\circ$:

$${a_{p,\text{IDC}} = \omega^2 r \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right) = 60 \times \left(1 + \frac{1}{4}\right) = 60 \times 1.25 = 75.0 \text{ m/s}^2}$$
3. Acceleration at ODC $\theta = 180^\circ$:
$${a_{p,\text{ODC}} = -\omega^2 r \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right) = -60 \times \left(1 - \frac{1}{4}\right) = -60 \times 0.75 = -45.0 \text{ m/s}^2}$$
4. Find zero acceleration angle $\theta$ $\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{4} = 0$: Using identity $\cos 2\theta = 2\cos^2\theta - 1$:
$${\cos\theta + \frac{2\cos^2\theta - 1}{4} = 0 \implies 2\cos^2\theta + 4\cos\theta - 1 = 0}$$
Solving quadratic equation for $\cos\theta$:
$${\cos\theta = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4(2)(-1)}}{4} = \frac{-4 \pm \sqrt{24}}{4} = \frac{-4 + 4.899}{4} = 0.22475}$$
$${\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.22475) \approx 77.01^\circ}$$
- Key Insight: $a_{p,\text{IDC}} = 75 \text{ m/s}^2$, $a_{p,\text{ODC}} = -45 \text{ m/s}^2$, $a_p = 0$ at $\theta = 77.01^\circ$.

3.2 Dynamic Force Analysis (Piston Effort, Conrod Thrust, Turning Moment)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Inertia Force of Reciprocating Masses $F_i$:
  • D'Alembert's principle inertia force opposes acceleration:
    $${\boxed{F_i = m_{\text{rec}} \cdot a_p = m_{\text{rec}} \omega^2 r \left(\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}\right)}}$$
  • During initial half stroke (acceleration phase), $F_i$ REDUCES net driving force on piston.
  • During secondary half stroke (deceleration phase), $F_i$ INCREASES net driving force on piston.
  • Piston Effort $F_p$:
  • Net force acting along line of stroke:
    • Horizontal engine: $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i \mp R_f$.
    • Vertical engine (downward stroke): $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i \mp R_f + m_{\text{rec}} g$.
    • Vertical engine (upward stroke): $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i \mp R_f - m_{\text{rec}} g$.
  • Force Resolution in Mechanism:
  • Connecting Rod Thrust $F_q$: Force transmitted along conrod centerline:
    $${\boxed{F_q = \frac{F_p}{\cos\phi}}}$$
  • Side Thrust on Cylinder Wall $N_s$: Reaction force normal to cylinder bore:
    $${\boxed{N_s = F_p \tan\phi}}$$
  • Tangential Force / Crank Pin Effort $F_t$: Force perpendicular to crank arm:
    $${\boxed{F_t = F_q \sin(\theta + \phi) = F_p \frac{\sin(\theta + \phi)}{\cos\phi}}}$$
  • Radial Force on Crank Bearing $F_b$: Force along crank arm towards main bearings:
    $${\boxed{F_b = F_q \cos(\theta + \phi)}}$$
  • Turning Moment / Crank Effort $T$:
  • Instantaneous torque applied to crankshaft:
    $${T = F_t \cdot r = F_p \cdot r \left[\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2\sqrt{n^2 - \sin^2\theta}}\right]}$$
    $${\boxed{T \approx F_p \cdot r \left(\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2n}\right)}}$$
💡 Key Concept: Piston Effort Sign Rule: - Acceleration phase $0^\circ < \theta < 76^\circ$: Inertia force $F_i$ works AGAINST gas force $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i$. - Deceleration phase $76^\circ < \theta < 180^\circ$: Inertia force $F_i$ AIDS gas force $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} + |F_i|$.

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Crank Effort & Side Thrust Calculation - Problem: A horizontal steam engine has stroke $2 r = 400 \text{ mm}$ $r = 0.2 \text{ m}$ and conrod length $l = 1.0 \text{ m}$ $n = 5$. Reciprocating mass $m = 60 \text{ kg}$. Engine speed $N = 300 \text{ rpm}$ $\omega = 10\pi \approx 31.416 \text{ rad/s}$. At crank angle $\theta = 45^\circ$, gas pressure force $F_{\text{gas}} = 25 \text{ kN} = 25000 \text{ N}$. Calculate: (a) Inertia force $F_i$, (b) Piston effort $F_p$, (c) Side thrust $N_s$, (d) Turning moment $T$. - Solution: 1. Calculate $a_p$ at $\theta = 45^\circ$:

$${\cos 45^\circ = 0.7071, \quad \cos 90^\circ = 0}$$
$${a_p = \omega^2 r \left(\cos 45^\circ + \frac{\cos 90^\circ}{5}\right) = (31.416^2 \times 0.2) \times (0.7071 + 0) = 197.39 \times 0.7071 \approx 139.57 \text{ m/s}^2}$$
2. Calculate Inertia Force $F_i$:
$${F_i = m \cdot a_p = 60 \times 139.57 \approx 8374 \text{ N} = 8.374 \text{ kN}}$$
3. Calculate Piston Effort $F_p$ (neglecting friction):
$${F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i = 25000 - 8374 = 16626 \text{ N} = 16.626 \text{ kN}}$$
4. Calculate obliquity angle $\phi$:
$${\sin\phi = \frac{\sin 45^\circ}{5} = \frac{0.7071}{5} = 0.1414 \implies \phi = \sin^{-1}(0.1414) \approx 8.13^\circ}$$
$${\cos\phi = \cos 8.13^\circ \approx 0.9900, \quad \tan\phi = \tan 8.13^\circ \approx 0.1428}$$
5. Calculate Side Thrust $N_s$:
$${N_s = F_p \tan\phi = 16626 \times 0.1428 \approx 2374 \text{ N}}$$
6. Calculate Turning Moment $T$:
$${T \approx F_p r \left(\sin 45^\circ + \frac{\sin 90^\circ}{2(5)}\right) = (16626 \times 0.2) \times \left(0.7071 + \frac{1}{10}\right)}$$
$${T = 3325.2 \times 0.8071 \approx 2683.8 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
- Key Insight: $F_p = 16.63 \text{ kN}$, $N_s = 2.37 \text{ kN}$, $T = 2684 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

Worked Example 2: Vertical Engine Piston Effort Calculation - Problem: A vertical IC engine has reciprocating mass $m = 80 \text{ kg}$, $r = 0.1 \text{ m}, n = 4, \omega = 30 \text{ rad/s}$. At crank angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ during downward expansion stroke, gas force $F_{\text{gas}} = 30 \text{ kN} = 30000 \text{ N}$. Calculate piston effort $F_p$. - Solution: 1. Gravity weight force $W_r = m g = 80 \times 9.81 = 784.8 \text{ N}$ (acts DOWNWARDS, aiding downward stroke). 2. Calculate $a_p$ at $\theta = 30^\circ$:

$${\cos 30^\circ = 0.8660, \quad \cos 60^\circ = 0.5000}$$
$${a_p = (30^2 \times 0.1) \times \left(0.8660 + \frac{0.5000}{4}\right) = 90 \times (0.8660 + 0.1250) = 90 \times 0.9910 = 89.19 \text{ m/s}^2}$$
3. Calculate Inertia Force $F_i$:
$${F_i = m \cdot a_p = 80 \times 89.19 = 7135.2 \text{ N}}$$
4. Calculate Piston Effort $F_p$ (downward stroke $\implies +m g$):
$${F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i + m g = 30000 - 7135.2 + 784.8 = 23649.6 \text{ N} \approx 23.65 \text{ kN}}$$
- Key Insight: $F_p = 23.65 \text{ kN}$. Including weight $m g$ adds $785 \text{ N}$ to piston effort.

3.3 Dynamically Equivalent Systems & Graphical Constructions

1. Concept Pointers
  • Dynamically Equivalent Two-Mass System:
  • To replace a rigid body of mass $m$, center of gravity $G$, and mass moment of inertia $I$ by two point masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ at distances $l_1$ and $l_2$ from $G$:
    1. Total mass condition: $\boxed{m_1 + m_2 = m}$
    2. Center of gravity condition: $\boxed{m_1 l_1 = m_2 l_2}$
    3. Moment of inertia condition: $\boxed{m_1 l_1^2 + m_2 l_2^2 = I = m k^2}$
  • Multiplying (1) by $l_1 l_2$ and subtracting (3) yields the fundamental relationship:
    $${\boxed{l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2}}$$
    where $k$ is the radius of gyration of the body about $G$.
  • Correction Couple $T_c$ for Non-Equivalent Systems:
  • If mass $m_2$ is placed at arbitrary distance $l_2' \neq l_2$ (e.g., at the big-end pin axis), the system is kinetically incorrect.
  • Correction couple $T_c = m (k^2 - l_1 l_2') \alpha_{cr}$, where $\alpha_{cr}$ is the angular acceleration of the connecting rod.
  • Graphical Velocity & Acceleration Constructions:
  • Klein's Construction: Uses a circle of diameter equal to crank $OM$ and another circle centered at $M$ with radius equal to conrod length $MN$ to quickly draw the acceleration quadrilateral $O-N-K-L$.
  • Ritterhaus Construction: Used when crank angle is near dead centers.
  • Bennett's Construction: Alternative construction for fast determination of piston acceleration.
💡 Key Concept: Dynamically Equivalent Condition: A 2-mass system is dynamically equivalent to a connecting rod ONLY IF $l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$. If $l_1$ is located at the small-end gudgeon pin, $l_2$ MUST be located at distance $l_2 = k^2/l_1$ from CG!
2. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Mass sum: $m_1 + m_2 = m$.
  • [ ] CG balance: $m_1 l_1 = m_2 l_2$.
  • [ ] Dynamic equivalence condition: $l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$.
  • [ ] Mass formula $m_1 = m \frac{l_2}{l_1+l_2}$, $m_2 = m \frac{l_1}{l_1+l_2}$.
  • [ ] Correction couple $T_c = m (k^2 - l_1 l_2') \alpha$.
3. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Equivalent Two-Mass System for Connecting Rod - Problem: A connecting rod has mass $m = 40 \text{ kg}$, length between centers $L = 800 \text{ mm} = 0.8 \text{ m}$, and radius of gyration about CG $k = 300 \text{ mm} = 0.3 \text{ m}$. The CG is located at distance $l_1 = 320 \text{ mm} = 0.32 \text{ m}$ from the small-end center. Calculate: (a) Mass $m_1$ at small end, (b) Distance $l_2$ for dynamically equivalent mass $m_2$, (c) Mass $m_2$. - Solution: 1. Calculate required distance $l_2$ from CG for dynamic equivalence:

$${l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2 \implies 0.32 \cdot l_2 = (0.3)^2 = 0.09}$$
$${l_2 = \frac{0.09}{0.32} = 0.28125 \text{ m} = 281.25 \text{ mm}}$$
2. Calculate total distance between equivalent masses $L_{\text{eq}} = l_1 + l_2$:
$${L_{\text{eq}} = 0.32 + 0.28125 = 0.60125 \text{ m}}$$
3. Calculate equivalent mass $m_1$ at small end:
$${m_1 = m \cdot \frac{l_2}{l_1 + l_2} = 40 \times \frac{0.28125}{0.60125} = 40 \times 0.4678 \approx 18.71 \text{ kg}}$$
4. Calculate equivalent mass $m_2$:
$${m_2 = m - m_1 = 40 - 18.71 = 21.29 \text{ kg}}$$
- Key Insight: $l_2 = 281.25 \text{ mm}$, $m_1 = 18.71 \text{ kg}$, $m_2 = 21.29 \text{ kg}$. Note $l_1 + l_2 = 601.25 \text{ mm} \neq 800 \text{ mm}$, so mass $m_2$ does NOT fall on the big-end pin axis!

Worked Example 2: Correction Couple for Non-Equivalent Mass Placement - Problem: For the connecting rod in Example 1, if mass $m_2'$ is placed at the big-end pin center $l_2' = L - l_1 = 0.8 - 0.32 = 0.48 \text{ m}$, calculate the correction couple $T_c$ when angular acceleration of connecting rod $\alpha_{cr} = 50 \text{ rad/s}^2$. - Solution: 1. Calculate term $(k^2 - l_1 l_2')$:

$${k^2 - l_1 l_2' = (0.3)^2 - (0.32 \times 0.48) = 0.09 - 0.1536 = -0.0636 \text{ m}^2}$$
2. Calculate Correction Couple $T_c$:
$${T_c = m (k^2 - l_1 l_2') \alpha_{cr} = 40 \times (-0.0636) \times 50 = -127.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
- Key Insight: $T_c = -127.2 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Placed at the big-end pin, the 2-mass system creates an artificial inertia torque that must be corrected by applying $T_c$.

3.4 Primary & Secondary Unbalanced Reciprocating Forces

1. Concept Pointers
  • Primary Unbalanced Force $F_P$:
  • Caused by reciprocating mass acceleration at fundamental engine speed $\omega$:
    $${\boxed{F_P = m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2 \cos\theta}}$$
  • Operates along the line of stroke.
  • Reaches maximum value twice per revolution $\theta = 0^\circ \text{ and } 180^\circ$.
  • Secondary Unbalanced Force $F_S$:
  • Caused by obliquity of the connecting rod $n = l/r$:
    $${\boxed{F_S = m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2 \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}}}$$
  • Operates along the line of stroke at TWICE engine rotational frequency $2\omega$.
  • Reaches maximum value four times per revolution $\theta = 0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$.
  • Magnitude is $\frac{1}{n}$ times the maximum primary force.
  • Comparison of Primary vs Secondary Forces:
Feature / Aspect Primary Unbalanced Force $F_P$ Secondary Unbalanced Force $F_S$
Frequency of Action Engine Speed $\omega$ $1\times$. Double Engine Speed $2\omega$ $2\times$.
Maximum Magnitude $m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2$. $\frac{m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2}{n}$.
Cause Reciprocating crank motion. Connecting rod obliquity $n = l/r$.
Ease of Balancing Can be partially/fully balanced by rotating counterweights. Difficult to balance; requires twin counter-rotating shafts at $2\omega$ (Lanchester balancer).
💡 Key Concept: Secondary Force Ratio: Max Secondary Force $= \frac{1}{n} \times \text{Max Primary Force}$. Since $n = 4 \text{ to } 5$, secondary force is $20\% \text{ to } 25\%$ of primary force magnitude, but acts at DOUBLE frequency $2\omega$.

[!TRAP] Secondary Max Angle Trap: - Primary force is MAX at $\theta = 0^\circ, 180^\circ$ and ZERO at $\theta = 90^\circ, 270^\circ$. - Secondary force is MAX at $\theta = 0^\circ, 90^\circ, 180^\circ, 270^\circ$ and ZERO at $\theta = 45^\circ, 135^\circ, 225^\circ, 315^\circ$!

│   │                           ├── Frequency: w (1x engine speed)
│   │                           └── Max Value: m_rec * r * w^2  (at theta = 0, 180 deg)

│   │                            └── Max Value: (m_rec * r * w^2) / n  (at theta = 0, 90, 180, 270 deg)

3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $F_P = m r \omega^2 \cos\theta$.
  • [ ] $F_S = m r \omega^2 \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}$.
  • [ ] Secondary frequency $= 2\omega$.
  • [ ] Max secondary force $= \frac{1}{n} F_{P,\text{max}}$.
  • [ ] Secondary force increases as conrod length $l$ decreases $n \downarrow$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Primary and Secondary Unbalanced Force Calculation - Problem: A single-cylinder engine has reciprocating mass $m = 12 \text{ kg}$, crank radius $r = 100 \text{ mm} = 0.1 \text{ m}$, connecting rod length $l = 400 \text{ mm} = 0.4 \text{ m}$ $n = 4$. The engine rotates at $1200 \text{ rpm}$ $\omega = 40\pi \approx 125.66 \text{ rad/s}$. Calculate: (a) Maximum primary unbalanced force, (b) Maximum secondary unbalanced force, (c) Total unbalanced force at $\theta = 0^\circ$ (TDC). - Solution: 1. Calculate inertia constant $m r \omega^2$:

$${m r \omega^2 = 12 \times 0.1 \times (125.66)^2 = 1.2 \times 15791.4 \approx 18950 \text{ N} = 18.95 \text{ kN}}$$
2. Calculate Maximum Primary Unbalanced Force $F_{P,\text{max}}$:
$${F_{P,\text{max}} = m r \omega^2 = 18.95 \text{ kN}}$$
3. Calculate Maximum Secondary Unbalanced Force $F_{S,\text{max}}$:
$${F_{S,\text{max}} = \frac{m r \omega^2}{n} = \frac{18.95}{4} = 4.738 \text{ kN}}$$
4. Calculate Total Unbalanced Force at $\theta = 0^\circ$:
$${F_{\text{total}} = F_{P,\text{max}} + F_{S,\text{max}} = 18.95 + 4.738 = 23.688 \text{ kN}}$$
- Key Insight: $F_{P,\text{max}} = 18.95 \text{ kN}$, $F_{S,\text{max}} = 4.74 \text{ kN}$, $F_{\text{total}} = 23.69 \text{ kN}$.

Worked Example 2: Secondary Force at 90 Degree Crank Angle - Problem: For the engine in Example 1, calculate the primary, secondary, and net unbalanced forces at crank angle $\theta = 90^\circ$. - Solution: 1. Primary force at $\theta = 90^\circ$:

$${F_P = m r \omega^2 \cos 90^\circ = 18.95 \times 0 = 0 \text{ N}}$$
2. Secondary force at $\theta = 90^\circ$ $\cos 180^\circ = -1$:
$${F_S = \frac{m r \omega^2 \cos 180^\circ}{n} = \frac{18.95 \times (-1)}{4} = -4.738 \text{ kN}}$$
3. Total unbalanced force $F = F_P + F_S = 0 - 4.738 = -4.738 \text{ kN}$. - Key Insight: At $\theta = 90^\circ$, primary force is ZERO, but secondary force is at its maximum magnitude $-4.74 \text{ kN}$ acting towards ODC!

3.5 Locomotive Balancing, Tractive Force, Swaying Couple & Hammer Blow

1. Concept Pointers
  • Partial Balancing of Reciprocating Masses:
  • In locomotives, balancing $100\%$ of reciprocating mass requires large revolving counterweights $B$.
  • These counterweights create an unbalanced vertical force (Hammer Blow) equal to $B b \omega^2$, which causes severe rail damage and potential wheel lift!
  • Therefore, only a fraction $c$ ($50\% \text{ to } 75\%$, typically $c = 2/3$) of reciprocating mass is balanced.
  • Unbalanced reciprocating fraction $= (1 - c)$.
  • Unbalanced Effects in 2-Cylinder Locomotives:
  • For a 2-cylinder uncoupled locomotive with cranks at right angles $\theta_2 = \theta_1 + 90^\circ$:
  • 1. Variation of Tractive Effort $\Delta F_T$:
    • Net unbalanced primary force along rails:
      $${\Delta F_T = (1-c) m r \omega^2 (\cos\theta - \sin\theta)}$$
    • Maximum Variation of Tractive Effort:
      $${\boxed{\Delta F_T = \pm \sqrt{2} (1-c) m r \omega^2}}$$
    • Occurs at crank angles $\theta = 135^\circ \text{ and } 315^\circ$.
  • 2. Swaying Couple $C_{\text{sway}}$:
    • Couple about vertical axis through center of locomotive (cylinder centerlines separated by distance $a$):
      $${C_{\text{sway}} = (1-c) m r \omega^2 \frac{a}{2} (\cos\theta + \sin\theta)}$$
    • Maximum Swaying Couple:
      $${\boxed{C_{\text{sway}} = \pm \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} (1-c) m r \omega^2}}$$
    • Occurs at crank angles $\theta = 45^\circ \text{ and } 225^\circ$.
  • 3. Hammer Blow $H_{\text{blow}}$:
    • Maximum vertical unbalanced force exerted by the revolving balancing mass $B$ at radius $b$:
      $${\boxed{H_{\text{blow}} = B \cdot b \cdot \omega^2}}$$
    • Varies as speed squared $\omega^2$.
    • Net pressure on rail varies between $(P_{\text{static}} - H_{\text{blow}})$ and $(P_{\text{static}} + H_{\text{blow}})$.
    • Wheel Lifting Condition: If $H_{\text{blow}} > P_{\text{static}}$, wheel lifts off the rail!
💡 Key Concept: Locomotive Trade-Off: - Increasing balance fraction $c$ REDUCES Tractive Effort Variation and Swaying Couple, but INCREASES Hammer Blow! - Decreasing balance fraction $c$ REDUCES Hammer Blow, but INCREASES Tractive Effort Variation and Swaying Couple!

  • [ ] Hammer Blow $H_{\text{blow}} = B b \omega^2$.
  • [ ] Hammer blow varies as speed squared $\omega^2$.

  • [ ] Max Tractive Variation $= \sqrt{2} (1-c) m r \omega^2$ at $\theta = 135^\circ, 315^\circ$.
  • [ ] Wheel lift occurs if $H_{\text{blow}} > P_{\text{static}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Locomotive Hammer Blow, Swaying Couple & Tractive Variation - Problem: An inside two-cylinder uncoupled locomotive has cylinder centerlines $a = 0.7 \text{ m}$ apart. Stroke $2 r = 600 \text{ mm}$ $r = 0.3 \text{ m}$. Reciprocating mass per cylinder $m = 300 \text{ kg}$. Revolving masses are fully balanced. Two-thirds $c = 2/3$ of reciprocating masses are balanced by counterweights placed at radius $b = 0.6 \text{ m}$ in wheel planes separated by $1.5 \text{ m}$. Driving wheel diameter $= 1.8 \text{ m}$. Locomotive speed $v = 90 \text{ km/h} = 25 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate: (a) Angular velocity $\omega$, (b) Maximum Hammer Blow, (c) Maximum Swaying Couple, (d) Maximum Variation of Tractive Effort. - Solution: 1. Calculate wheel angular velocity $\omega$:

$${\text{Wheel radius } R_w = \frac{1.8}{2} = 0.9 \text{ m} \implies \omega = \frac{v}{R_w} = \frac{25}{0.9} \approx 27.78 \text{ rad/s}}$$
$${\omega^2 = (27.78)^2 \approx 771.6 \text{ (rad/s)}^2}$$
2. Calculate unbalanced reciprocating mass per cylinder $(1-c)m$:
$${(1 - c) m = \left(1 - \frac{2}{3}\right) \times 300 = 100 \text{ kg}}$$
3. Calculate balancing mass $B$ per wheel for reciprocating parts $c m r = B b$:
$${B \cdot b = c \cdot m \cdot r = \frac{2}{3} \times 300 \times 0.3 = 60 \text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}}$$
$${B = \frac{60}{b} = \frac{60}{0.6} = 100 \text{ kg}}$$
4. Calculate Maximum Hammer Blow $H_{\text{blow}}$:
$${H_{\text{blow}} = B \cdot b \cdot \omega^2 = 60 \times 771.6 \approx 46296 \text{ N} = 46.30 \text{ kN}}$$
5. Calculate Maximum Swaying Couple $C_{\text{sway}}$:
$${C_{\text{sway}} = \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} (1-c) m r \omega^2 = \frac{0.7}{\sqrt{2}} \times 100 \times 0.3 \times 771.6}$$
$${C_{\text{sway}} = 0.4950 \times 30 \times 771.6 \approx 11458 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m} = 11.46 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}}$$
6. Calculate Maximum Variation of Tractive Effort $\Delta F_T$:
$${\Delta F_T = \sqrt{2} (1-c) m r \omega^2 = \sqrt{2} \times 100 \times 0.3 \times 771.6}$$
$${\Delta F_T = 1.4142 \times 23148 \approx 32736 \text{ N} = 32.74 \text{ kN}}$$
- Key Insight: Hammer Blow $= 46.30 \text{ kN}$, Swaying Couple $= 11.46 \text{ kN}\cdot\text{m}$, Tractive Variation $= 32.74 \text{ kN}$.

Worked Example 2: Maximum Speed to Prevent Wheel Lifting - Problem: For the locomotive in Example 1, each driving wheel carries a static dead weight $P_{\text{static}} = 50 \text{ kN} = 50000 \text{ N}$. Determine the maximum permissible linear speed $v_{\text{limit}}$ $\text{km/h}$ above which the wheels will lift off the rails. - Solution: 1. Wheel lift occurs when Hammer Blow $H_{\text{blow}} = P_{\text{static}}$:

$${B \cdot b \cdot \omega_{\text{limit}}^2 = 50000 \implies 60 \cdot \omega_{\text{limit}}^2 = 50000}$$
2. Solve for $\omega_{\text{limit}}^2$ and $\omega_{\text{limit}}$:
$${\omega_{\text{limit}}^2 = \frac{50000}{60} = 833.33 \implies \omega_{\text{limit}} = \sqrt{833.33} \approx 28.868 \text{ rad/s}}$$
3. Calculate linear speed $v_{\text{limit}}$:
$${v_{\text{limit}} = \omega_{\text{limit}} \times R_w = 28.868 \times 0.9 = 25.98 \text{ m/s}}$$
4. Convert to $\text{km/h}$:
$${v_{\text{limit}} = 25.98 \times 3.6 \approx 93.53 \text{ km/h}}$$
- Key Insight: Maximum speed without wheel lifting is $93.53 \text{ km/h}$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (20 Core Conceptual Pairs)

Q1: What is the relation for piston displacement in a slider-crank mechanism?
A: $x = r \left[(1 - \cos\theta) + \frac{\sin^2\theta}{2n}\right]$.

Q2: Write the exact formula for piston velocity $v_p$.
A: $v_p = \omega r \left(\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2n}\right)$.

Q3: Write the exact formula for piston acceleration $a_p$.
A: $a_p = \omega^2 r \left(\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}\right)$.

Q4: What is the piston acceleration at Inner Dead Center $\theta = 0^\circ$?
A: $a_p = \omega^2 r \left(1 + \frac{1}{n}\right)$.

Q5: What is the piston acceleration at Outer Dead Center $\theta = 180^\circ$?
A: $a_p = -\omega^2 r \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)$.

Q6: What happens to piston acceleration as connecting rod length $l \to \infty$?
A: Obliquity ratio $n \to \infty$, and piston acceleration reduces to $a_p = \omega^2 r \cos\theta$ (pure Simple Harmonic Motion).

Q7: Write the expression for inertia force of reciprocating parts.
A: $F_i = m_{\text{rec}} \cdot a_p = m_{\text{rec}} \omega^2 r \left(\cos\theta + \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}\right)$.

Q8: Define Piston Effort $F_p$.
A: The net effective driving force acting along the line of stroke $F_p = F_{\text{gas}} - F_i \mp R_f$.

Q9: Write the formula for cylinder side thrust $N_s$.
A: $N_s = F_p \tan\phi$ (where $\sin\phi = \frac{\sin\theta}{n}$).

Q10: Write the formula for connecting rod axial force $F_q$.
A: $F_q = \frac{F_p}{\cos\phi}$.

Q11: Write the formula for turning moment (crank effort) $T$.
A: $T \approx F_p r \left(\sin\theta + \frac{\sin 2\theta}{2n}\right)$.

Q12: What is the condition for a two-mass system to be dynamically equivalent to a connecting rod?
A: (1) $m_1 + m_2 = m$, (2) $m_1 l_1 = m_2 l_2$, (3) $l_1 \cdot l_2 = k^2$.

Q13: Name the graphical construction used to determine piston acceleration.
A: Klein's Construction (also Ritterhaus Construction and Bennett's Construction).

Q14: What is the primary unbalanced force formula?
A: $F_P = m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2 \cos\theta$ (acting at engine frequency $\omega$).

Q15: What is the secondary unbalanced force formula?
A: $F_S = m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2 \frac{\cos 2\theta}{n}$ (acting at double frequency $2\omega$).

Q16: Why are reciprocating masses in locomotives only partially balanced?
A: Balancing $100\%$ of reciprocating mass produces a large vertical unbalanced force (Hammer Blow) that damages rails and risks lifting wheels.

Q17: Define Hammer Blow in locomotives.
A: The maximum vertical unbalanced force exerted on rails by revolving counterweights, equal to $H_{\text{blow}} = B b \omega^2$.

Q18: How does Hammer Blow vary with locomotive speed?
A: Hammer Blow varies directly as the square of rotational speed $\omega^2$.

Q19: Write the maximum Swaying Couple formula for a 2-cylinder locomotive.
A: $C_{\text{sway}} = \pm \frac{a}{\sqrt{2}} (1-c) m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2$ (occurring at $\theta = 45^\circ \text{ and } 225^\circ$).

Q20: Write the maximum Variation of Tractive Effort formula.
A: $\Delta F_T = \pm \sqrt{2} (1-c) m_{\text{rec}} r \omega^2$ (occurring at $\theta = 135^\circ \text{ and } 315^\circ$).
