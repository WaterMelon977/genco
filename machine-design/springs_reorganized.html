SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Helical Compression & Tension Springs (Stress, Deflection & Geometry)

  • Spring Index $C$:
    $${\boxed{C = \frac{D}{d}}}$$
  • $D$: mean coil diameter $\text{mm}$
  • $d$: wire diameter $\text{mm}$

  • Manufacturing optimum range: $4 \le C \le 12$ (preferred standard $C \approx 6 \text{ to } 8$).

  • Direct Shear Stress Factor $K_s$:

    $${\boxed{K_s = 1 + \frac{1}{2C} = \frac{2C + 1}{2C}}}$$

  • Wahl Stress Factor $K_w$:

    $${\boxed{K_w = \frac{4C - 1}{4C - 4} + \frac{0.615}{C}}}$$

  • Accounts for BOTH direct shear stress and curvature stress concentration.

  • Maximum Torsional Shear Stress $\tau_{\text{max}}$:

    $${\boxed{\tau_{\text{max}} = K_w \cdot \frac{8 \cdot P \cdot D}{\pi \cdot d^3} = K_w \cdot \frac{8 \cdot P \cdot C}{\pi \cdot d^2} \quad (\text{MPa})}}$$

  • $P$: axial load on spring $\text{N}$

  • Occurs on the INNER SURFACE of the coil wire!

  • Axial Deflection $\delta$:

    $${\boxed{\delta = \frac{8 \cdot P \cdot D^3 \cdot n}{G \cdot d^4} = \frac{8 \cdot P \cdot C^3 \cdot n}{G \cdot d} \quad (\text{mm})}}$$

  • $n$: number of active coils

  • $G$: modulus of rigidity / shear modulus $\text{MPa}$

  • Spring Stiffness / Rate $k$:

    $${\boxed{k = \frac{P}{\delta} = \frac{G \cdot d^4}{8 \cdot D^3 \cdot n} = \frac{G \cdot d}{8 \cdot C^3 \cdot n} \quad (\text{N/mm})}}$$

  • Polar Moment of Inertia of Wire $J$:

    $${\boxed{J = \frac{\pi \cdot d^4}{32} \quad (\text{mm}^4)}}$$

  • End Conditions & Active Coils $n$:

  • Plain ends: $\boxed{n = n_t, \quad L_s = n_t \cdot d}$
  • Plain & ground ends: $\boxed{n = n_t - 1, \quad L_s = n_t \cdot d}$
  • Squared ends: $\boxed{n = n_t - 2, \quad L_s = (n_t + 1) \cdot d}$

  • Squared & ground ends: $\boxed{n = n_t - 2, \quad L_s = n_t \cdot d}$

  • Lengths & Pitch:

  • Solid Length: $\boxed{L_s = n_t \cdot d \quad (\text{mm})}$
  • Free Length: $\boxed{L_f = L_s + \delta_{\text{max}} + \text{clash allowance} \quad (\text{mm})}$ (clash allowance $\approx 0.15 \delta_{\text{max}}$)
  • Coil Pitch: $\boxed{p = \frac{L_f - L_s}{n} + d \quad (\text{mm})}$

2. Helical Torsion Springs

  • Wahl Factor for Torsion Spring $K_b$:

    $${\boxed{K_b = \frac{4C - 1}{4C - 4}}}$$

  • Bending Stress $\sigma$:

    $${\boxed{\sigma = K_b \cdot \frac{32 \cdot M}{\pi \cdot d^3} \quad (\text{MPa})}}$$

  • $M$: applied twisting/bending moment $\text{N}\cdot\text{mm}$

  • Angular Deflection $\theta$:

    $${\boxed{\theta = \frac{64 \cdot M \cdot D \cdot n}{E \cdot d^4} \quad (\text{radians})}}$$

  • $E$: Young's modulus $\text{MPa}$

  • Torsional Stiffness $k_\theta$:

    $${\boxed{k_\theta = \frac{M}{\theta} = \frac{E \cdot d^4}{64 \cdot D \cdot n} \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{mm/rad})}}$$

3. Spring Combinations & Cutting Rules

  • Series Combination:
    $${\boxed{\frac{1}{k_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} \implies k_{\text{eq}} = \frac{k_1 \cdot k_2}{k_1 + k_2} \quad (\text{N/mm})}}$$
  • Load is equal on both springs $P_1 = P_2 = P$; deflections add up $\delta = \delta_1 + \delta_2$.

  • Two identical springs $k$ in series $\implies \mathbf{k_{\text{eq}} = k/2}$.

  • Parallel Combination:

    $${\boxed{k_{\text{eq}} = k_1 + k_2 \quad (\text{N/mm})}}$$

  • Deflections are equal $\delta_1 = \delta_2 = \delta$; loads add up $P = P_1 + P_2$.
  • Two identical springs $k$ in parallel $\implies \mathbf{k_{\text{eq}} = 2k}$.

  • Nested springs (one inside another) operate in parallel!

  • Cutting a Spring:

  • Cutting a spring of stiffness $k$ into two equal halves of $n/2$ coils:
    $${\boxed{k' = 2 \cdot k}}$$
  • Connecting the two cut halves in parallel:
    $${\boxed{k_{\text{total}} = 2k + 2k = 4 \cdot k}}$$

4. Strain Energy & Resilience

  • Total Strain Energy Stored $U$:
    $${\boxed{U = \frac{1}{2} \cdot P \cdot \delta = \frac{P^2}{2k} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot k \cdot \delta^2 \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})}}$$
    $${\boxed{U = \frac{\tau^2}{4G} \cdot V}}$$
  • Active wire volume: $V = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 \cdot (\pi D n) = \frac{\pi^2}{4} d^2 D n \quad (\text{mm}^3)$
  • Volumetric strain energy / resilience: $\boxed{\frac{U}{V} = \frac{\tau^2}{4G}}$

5. Leaf Springs & Nipping

  • Maximum Bending Stress (Semi-Elliptic):
    $${\boxed{\sigma = \frac{3 \cdot P \cdot L}{2 \cdot n \cdot b \cdot t^2} \quad (\text{MPa})}}$$

  • $P$: total central load $\text{N}$, $L$: span length $\text{mm}$, $n$: total leaves $n = n_f + n_g$, $b$: leaf width $\text{mm}$, $t$: leaf thickness $\text{mm}$

  • Maximum Deflection (Semi-Elliptic):

    $${\boxed{\delta = \frac{3 \cdot P \cdot L^3}{8 \cdot E \cdot n \cdot b \cdot t^3} \quad (\text{mm})}}$$

  • Un-nipped Leaf Stress Distribution:

  • Load sharing: Graduated leaves carry $P_g = \frac{2}{3} P$; Full-length leaves carry $P_f = \frac{1}{3} P$.

  • Stress ratio: $\boxed{\sigma_f = 1.5 \cdot \sigma_g}$

  • Nipping Condition (Pre-stressed Leaf Spring):

    $${\boxed{\text{Nipping Condition: } \sigma_f = \sigma_g}}$$

  • Extra full-length leaf is given an initial curvature radius larger than graduated leaves so that pre-stress cancels assembly stress, equalizing stress across all leaves under full load.

6. Buckling, Surge & Fluctuating Load Design

  • Buckling Critical Load $P_{\text{cr}}$:
    $${\boxed{P_{\text{cr}} = C_B \cdot \frac{E \cdot d^4 \cdot L_f}{8 \cdot D^3 \cdot n} \quad (\text{N})}}$$

  • Buckling avoided by maintaining low slenderness ratio $L_f / D < 4.0$.

  • Fundamental Natural Frequency $f_n$ & Surge:

    $${\boxed{f_n = \frac{d}{2\pi D^2 n} \sqrt{\frac{G \cdot g}{2\rho}} \quad (\text{Hz})}}$$
    $${\boxed{\omega_n = \frac{d}{2 D^2 n} \sqrt{\frac{G \cdot g}{2\rho}} \quad (\text{rad/s})}}$$

  • Operating frequency must satisfy $f_{\text{operating}} < 0.5 f_n$ or $> 2.0 f_n$ to avoid resonance (surge).

  • Fluctuating Load Parameters:

  • Mean load: $\boxed{P_m = \frac{P_{\text{max}} + P_{\text{min}}}{2}}, \quad \text{Amplitude load: } \boxed{P_a = \frac{P_{\text{max}} - P_{\text{min}}}{2}}$
  • Mean stress: $\boxed{\tau_m = K_w \frac{8 P_m D}{\pi d^3}}, \quad \text{Amplitude stress: } \boxed{\tau_a = K_w \frac{8 P_a D}{\pi d^3}}$
  • Soderberg Criterion: $\boxed{\frac{\tau_a}{\tau_e / N} + \frac{\tau_m}{\tau_y / N} = 1}$
  • Goodman Criterion: $\boxed{\frac{\tau_a}{\tau_e / N} + \frac{\tau_m}{\tau_u / N} = 1}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Spring Index $C$: The ratio of mean coil diameter $D$ to wire diameter $d$ $C = D/d$. Measures coil curvature sharpness.
  • Wahl Stress Factor $K_w$: A combined multiplication factor that corrects for both torsional shear stress non-uniformity and inner coil curvature stress concentration.
  • Active Coils $n$: The coils of a spring that compress or extend freely under load, contributing directly to deflection and strain energy storage.
  • Nipping: The process of giving extra full-length leaves of a leaf spring a larger initial radius of curvature than graduated leaves to equalize bending stresses under load.
  • Concentric Springs: Two or more helical springs nested co-axially inside one another with OPPOSITE winding directions to prevent coil interlocking.
  • Belleville Spring: A cupped, conical disc washer spring that transmits high load capacities within extremely small axial space.
  • Spring Surge: A resonant phenomenon occurring when the frequency of an external fluctuating force matches the natural frequency of the spring coil, causing destructive vibration and coil clashing.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Optimum Spring Index: $C = 6.0 \text{ to } 8.0$ (range $4 \le C \le 12$).
  • Un-nipped Leaf Stress Ratio: $\sigma_f / \sigma_g = 1.5$.
  • Resilience Constant: $1/4$ $U/V = \tau^2 / 4G$.
  • Inactive Coils (Squared & Ground): $2$ coils $n = n_t - 2$.
  • Spring Steel Shear Modulus $G$: $80 \text{ to } 85 \text{ GPa}$.
  • Spring Steel Young's Modulus $E$: $200 \text{ to } 210 \text{ GPa}$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Helical Compression & Tension Springs

1. Concept Pointers
  • Stress Distribution in Spring Wire:
  • Pure torsional shear stress: $\tau_{\text{tor}} = \frac{8 P D}{\pi d^3}$.
  • Direct shear stress factor: $K_s = 1 + \frac{1}{2C}$.
  • Wahl Stress Factor (accounts for direct shear AND inner curvature concentration):
    $${K_w = \frac{4C - 1}{4C - 4} + \frac{0.615}{C}}$$
  • Maximum shear stress occurs at the INNERMOST SURFACE of the coil.
  • Deflection & Stiffness Relations:
  • Deflection $\delta = \frac{8 P D^3 n}{G d^4} = \frac{8 P C^3 n}{G d}$.
  • Stiffness $k = \frac{G d^4}{8 D^3 n} = \frac{G d}{8 C^3 n}$.
  • Stiffness is directly proportional to $d^4$ and inversely proportional to $D^3$ and $n$!
  • End Conditions & Coil Counts:
  • For squared & ground ends: active coils $n = n_t - 2$; solid length $L_s = n_t d$.
  • Free length $L_f = L_s + \delta_{\text{max}} + \text{clash allowance}$ (clash allowance $\approx 15\% \delta_{\text{max}}$).
💡 Key Concept: Wahl Factor & Inner Fiber Stress: The Wahl factor $K_w = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4} + \frac{0.615}{C}$ corrects for BOTH direct shear and coil curvature stress concentration. Peak shear stress ALWAYS occurs at the INNER coil fiber!
⚠️ Exam Trap: Stiffness Proportionality Rules: Stiffness $k \propto \frac{d^4}{D^3 n}$. Doubling wire diameter $d$ increases stiffness by $2^4 = 16$ times! Doubling mean diameter $D$ reduces stiffness to $1/2^3 = 1/8$th!
2. Decision Tree
Helical Spring Calculation Check?
├─ Is question asking for Peak Shear Stress? → Use tau_max = Kw * (8 * P * D) / (pi * d^3)
├─ Is question asking for Deflection delta? → Use delta = (8 * P * D^3 * n) / (G * d^4)
├─ How does stiffness k change if wire diameter d is doubled? → Increases 16x (k proportional to d^4)
└─ Where does maximum shear stress occur? → INNER SURFACE of coil wire
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $C = D/d$.
  • [ ] $K_w = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4} + \frac{0.615}{C}$.
  • [ ] $\tau_{\text{max}} = K_w \frac{8PD}{\pi d^3}$.
  • [ ] $\delta = \frac{8PD^3n}{Gd^4}$.
  • [ ] $k = \frac{Gd^4}{8D^3n}$.
  • [ ] Squared & ground active coils: $n = n_t - 2$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A helical compression spring has a wire diameter $d = 8 \text{ mm}$, mean coil diameter $D = 48 \text{ mm}$, and $n = 10$ active coils. The shear modulus is $G = 80 \text{ GPa} = 80000 \text{ N/mm}^2$. Calculate: (a) Spring index $C$, (b) Wahl factor $K_w$, (c) Spring stiffness $k$. - Solution: - Calculate spring index $C$:

$${C = \frac{D}{d} = \frac{48}{8} = 6.0}$$
- Calculate Wahl factor $K_w$:
$${K_w = \frac{4(6) - 1}{4(6) - 4} + \frac{0.615}{6} = \frac{23}{20} + 0.1025 = 1.15 + 0.1025 = 1.2525}$$
- Calculate spring stiffness $k$:
$${k = \frac{G \cdot d^4}{8 \cdot D^3 \cdot n} = \frac{80000 \times 8^4}{8 \times 48^3 \times 10} = \frac{80000 \times 4096}{8 \times 110592 \times 10} = \frac{327680000}{8847360} \approx 37.04 \text{ N/mm}}$$
- Key Insight: $C = 6.0$, $K_w \approx 1.2525$, $k \approx 37.04 \text{ N/mm}$.

3.2 Helical Torsion Springs

1. Concept Pointers
  • Stress Type:
  • Unlike compression/extension springs (which operate in torsion), helical torsion springs operate in PURE BENDING!
  • Formulas:
  • Bending stress: $\sigma = K_b \frac{32 M}{\pi d^3}$, where $K_b = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4}$.
  • Angular deflection: $\theta = \frac{64 M D n}{E d^4}$ radians.
  • Torsional spring rate: $k_\theta = \frac{E d^4}{64 D n} \text{ N}\cdot\text{mm/rad}$.
💡 Key Concept: Helical Torsion Stress Mode: Helical TORSION springs experience BENDING STRESS $\sigma = K_b \frac{32M}{\pi d^3}$, whereas helical COMPRESSION/EXTENSION springs experience TORSIONAL SHEAR STRESS!
2. Decision Tree
Torsion Spring Check?
├─ Stress mode in Helical Torsion spring? → BENDING STRESS (sigma)
├─ Wahl factor Kb for torsion spring? → Kb = (4C - 1) / (4C - 4)
└─ Angular deflection theta formula? → theta = (64 * M * D * n) / (E * d^4)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Helical torsion spring operates in bending.
  • [ ] $\sigma = K_b \frac{32M}{\pi d^3}$.
  • [ ] $K_b = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4}$.
  • [ ] $\theta = \frac{64 M D n}{E d^4}$.

3.3 Spring Combinations (Series & Parallel) & Cutting Rules

1. Concept Pointers
  • Series Combination:
  • Equivalent stiffness: $\frac{1}{k_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{k_1} + \frac{1}{k_2} \implies k_{\text{eq}} = \frac{k_1 k_2}{k_1 + k_2}$.
  • Softens the spring system $k_{\text{eq}} < \min(k_1, k_2)$.
  • Parallel Combination:
  • Equivalent stiffness: $k_{\text{eq}} = k_1 + k_2$.
  • Stiffens the spring system $k_{\text{eq}} > \max(k_1, k_2)$.
  • Nested concentric springs act in parallel.
  • Cutting a Spring in Half:
  • Original spring: stiffness $k$, active coils $n$.
  • Cut into two equal halves: each half has $n/2$ active coils.
  • Since $k \propto 1/n \implies k_{\text{half}} = 2k$.
  • If two cut halves are then connected in parallel:
    $${k_{\text{parallel}} = k_{\text{half}} + k_{\text{half}} = 2k + 2k = 4k!}$$
💡 Key Concept: Spring Cutting Rule: Cutting a spring into half DOUBLES its stiffness $k' = 2k$. Connecting the two cut halves in parallel quadruples the original stiffness $4k$!
⚠️ Exam Trap: Series vs Parallel Load Sharing: - Series: Both springs carry SAME load $P$. - Parallel: Both springs undergo SAME deflection $\delta$.
2. Decision Tree
Spring Combination / Cut Check?
├─ Are springs connected in Series? → 1/k_eq = 1/k1 + 1/k2  (k_eq is smaller)
├─ Are springs connected in Parallel? → k_eq = k1 + k2  (k_eq is larger)
├─ Is spring cut into TWO equal halves? → Stiffness of EACH half is 2k
└─ Two cut halves connected in parallel? → k_total = 2k + 2k = 4k
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Series: $1/k_{\text{eq}} = 1/k_1 + 1/k_2$.
  • [ ] Parallel: $k_{\text{eq}} = k_1 + k_2$.
  • [ ] Cut in half $\implies k' = 2k$.
  • [ ] 2 halves in parallel $\implies k_{\text{total}} = 4k$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A helical spring has a stiffness $k = 40 \text{ N/mm}$. It is cut into two equal parts. Calculate: (a) Stiffness of each part, (b) Equivalent stiffness if the two parts are connected in parallel. - Solution: - (a) Stiffness of each half:

$${k_{\text{half}} = 2 \cdot k = 2 \times 40 = 80 \text{ N/mm}}$$
- (b) Equivalent stiffness in parallel:
$${k_{\text{parallel}} = k_{\text{half}} + k_{\text{half}} = 80 + 80 = 160 \text{ N/mm}}$$
- Key Insight: $k_{\text{half}} = 80 \text{ N/mm}$, $k_{\text{parallel}} = 160 \text{ N/mm} = 4k$.

3.4 Strain Energy, Resilience & Concentric Springs

1. Concept Pointers
  • Strain Energy in Helical Springs:
  • Total strain energy stored under load $P$:
    $${U = \frac{1}{2} P \delta = \frac{P^2}{2k} = \frac{\tau^2}{4G} \cdot V}$$
  • Volumetric strain energy (resilience): $\frac{U}{V} = \frac{\tau^2}{4G}$.
  • Concentric / Co-axial Springs:
  • Nesting one spring inside another co-axially.
  • Must be wound with OPPOSITE HAND OF HELIX (one right-hand, one left-hand) so that coils do not interlock/bind during compression.
  • Advantages: High load capacity in compact space; fail-safe redundancy.
  • Equal stress design: Both inner and outer springs designed for same maximum shear stress $\tau$.
💡 Key Concept: Concentric Winding Hand: Concentric helical springs MUST be wound in OPPOSITE directions (one LH, one RH) to prevent coil interlocking during operation!
⚠️ Exam Trap: Resilience Denominator: Resilience of a helical spring in torsion is $\frac{\tau^2}{4G}$ (denominator is $4G$, NOT $2G$!).
2. Decision Tree
Concentric / Resilience Check?
├─ Volumetric strain energy (resilience) formula? → U / V = tau^2 / (4 * G)
├─ Winding direction requirement for Concentric Springs? → OPPOSITE HANDS (prevent locking)
└─ Primary advantage of Concentric Springs? → Compact space + Fail-safe redundancy
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Strain energy $U = \frac{1}{2} P \delta = \frac{P^2}{2k}$.
  • [ ] Resilience $= \frac{\tau^2}{4G}$.
  • [ ] Concentric springs: opposite winding hand.
  • [ ] Concentric: equal stress design.

3.5 Leaf Springs, Nipping & Belleville Springs

1. Concept Pointers
  • Leaf Spring Construction:
  • Composed of a master leaf (full length with eye ends) and several graduated leaves clamped by U-bolts.
  • Designed as a cantilever beam or simply supported beam of uniform strength.
  • Camber is provided so that the spring becomes flat under full working load.
  • Un-nipped Leaf Stress Ratio:
  • Without nipping, the full-length leaf experiences higher bending stress than graduated leaves:
    $${\sigma_f = 1.5 \cdot \sigma_g}$$
  • Load sharing: Graduated leaves carry $P_g = \frac{2}{3} P$; Full-length leaves carry $P_f = \frac{1}{3} P$.
  • Nipping Process:
  • Pre-stressing technique: Full-length leaf is given a larger initial curvature radius than graduated leaves.
  • When tightened together with U-bolts, an initial reverse bending stress is induced in the full-length leaf.
  • Purpose of Nipping: Equalizes bending stress across ALL leaves under maximum load $\sigma_f = \sigma_g$.
  • Belleville Springs (Conical Disc Springs):
  • Cupped conical washers stacked in series or parallel.
  • High load capacity in extremely small axial space.
💡 Key Concept: Nipping Purpose: Nipping pre-stresses leaf springs so that bending stress in the master leaf equals bending stress in graduated leaves under full working load $\sigma_f = \sigma_g$.
⚠️ Exam Trap: Un-nipped Stress Ratio: In an un-nipped leaf spring, full-length leaf stress is $1.5\times$ graduated leaf stress $\sigma_f = 1.5 \sigma_g$.
2. Decision Tree
Leaf & Belleville Spring Check?
├─ Un-nipped Leaf Spring stress ratio sigma_f / sigma_g? → 1.5
├─ Primary purpose of Nipping in leaf springs? → EQUALIZE BENDING STRESS in all leaves
├─ Spring type providing high load in small axial space? → BELLEVILLE / CONICAL DISC SPRING
└─ Leaf spring beam model? → BEAM OF UNIFORM STRENGTH
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Un-nipped: $\sigma_f = 1.5 \sigma_g$.
  • [ ] Nipping equalizes stresses $\sigma_f = \sigma_g$.
  • [ ] Belleville: high load, small axial space.
  • [ ] Leaf spring: beam of uniform strength.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: In an un-nipped semi-elliptic leaf spring, the bending stress induced in the graduated leaves is $\sigma_g = 200 \text{ MPa}$. Calculate the bending stress $\sigma_f$ induced in the full-length leaf. - Solution: - Apply un-nipped stress ratio:

$${\sigma_f = 1.5 \cdot \sigma_g = 1.5 \times 200 = 300 \text{ MPa}}$$
- Key Insight: $\sigma_f = 300 \text{ MPa}$. Full-length leaf is overloaded by $50\%$ without nipping.

3.6 Buckling, Surge & Fluctuating Load Design

1. Concept Pointers
  • Buckling of Compression Springs:
  • Critical load $P_{\text{cr}} = C_B \frac{E d^4 L_f}{8 D^3 n}$.
  • Avoided by keeping slenderness ratio $L_f / D < 4.0$.
  • Spring Surge & Natural Frequency:
  • Resonant vibration occurring when external dynamic frequency matches spring natural frequency $f_n = \frac{d}{2\pi D^2 n}\sqrt{\frac{G g}{2\rho}}$.
  • Prevented by maintaining operating frequency $< 0.5 f_n$ or $> 2.0 f_n$, adding dampers, or using variable pitch coils.
  • Fluctuating Load Criteria:
  • Mean load $P_m = \frac{P_{\text{max}} + P_{\text{min}}}{2}$; Amplitude load $P_a = \frac{P_{\text{max}} - P_{\text{min}}}{2}$.
  • Mean stress $\tau_m = K_w \frac{8 P_m D}{\pi d^3}$; Amplitude stress $\tau_a = K_w \frac{8 P_a D}{\pi d^3}$.
  • Soderberg: $\frac{\tau_a}{\tau_e/N} + \frac{\tau_m}{\tau_y/N} = 1$.
  • Goodman: $\frac{\tau_a}{\tau_e/N} + \frac{\tau_m}{\tau_u/N} = 1$.
💡 Key Concept: Fluctuating Stress Separation: Fluctuating loads must be separated into mean load $P_m$ and amplitude load $P_a$ before calculating mean shear stress $\tau_m$ and amplitude shear stress $\tau_a$ for Goodman/Soderberg criteria!
2. Decision Tree
Fluctuating Load / Surge Check?
├─ Is load fluctuating between P_max & P_min? → Split into P_m = (Pmax+Pmin)/2 and P_a = (Pmax-Pmin)/2
├─ Which criterion uses Yield Strength in shear (tau_y)? → SODERBERG CRITERION
├─ Which criterion uses Ultimate Strength in shear (tau_u)? → GOODMAN CRITERION
└─ Cause of Spring Surge? → RESONANCE (f_excitation = f_n)
3. Revision Checklist

  • [ ] Surge $=$ resonance phenomenon $f_n = \frac{d}{2\pi D^2 n}\sqrt{\frac{G g}{2\rho}}$.
  • [ ] Soderberg uses $\tau_y$; Goodman uses $\tau_u$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Write the formula for Wahl Stress Factor $K_w$ and explain what it accounts for.
A: $K_w = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4} + \frac{0.615}{C}$. It accounts for both direct shear stress and curvature stress concentration.

Q2: Where does maximum shear stress occur in a helical spring coil?
A: On the innermost surface of the coil wire.

Q3: How does spring stiffness $k$ scale with wire diameter $d$ and mean coil diameter $D$?
A: $k \propto d^4$ and $k \propto 1/D^3$.

Q4: If a helical spring of stiffness $k$ is cut into two equal halves, what is the stiffness of each half?
A: $2k$ (stiffness doubles).

Q5: What is the equivalent stiffness when two identical springs of stiffness $k$ are connected in series?
A: $k_{\text{eq}} = k/2$.

Q6: What is the equivalent stiffness when two identical springs of stiffness $k$ are connected in parallel?
A: $k_{\text{eq}} = 2k$.

Q7: Write the strain energy per unit volume (resilience) formula for a helical compression spring.
A: $\frac{U}{V} = \frac{\tau^2}{4G}$.

Q8: Why must concentric helical springs be wound with opposite hands of helix?
A: To prevent the coils of inner and outer springs from interlocking during compression.

Q9: What is Nipping in leaf springs?
A: Pre-stressing leaf springs by giving the full-length leaf a larger initial curvature radius than graduated leaves.

Q10: What is the primary purpose of Nipping in leaf springs?
A: To equalize bending stress across all leaves $\sigma_f = \sigma_g$ under full working load.

Q11: What is the ratio of full-length leaf bending stress to graduated leaf bending stress in an UN-NIPPED leaf spring?
A: $\sigma_f / \sigma_g = 1.5$.

Q12: Which type of spring is suitable for transmitting very high loads within a small axial space?
A: Belleville Spring (conical disc spring).

Q13: What is Spring Surge?
A: Resonant vibration of spring coils occurring when the external excitation frequency matches the natural frequency of the spring.

Q14: How can Spring Surge be prevented in valve springs?
A: By keeping natural frequency $f_n > 20\times$ operating frequency, using variable pitch coils, or adding damping springs.

Q15: State the stress mode in a helical torsion spring vs a helical compression spring.
A: Helical torsion springs operate in pure BENDING STRESS $\sigma$, whereas helical compression springs operate in TORSIONAL SHEAR STRESS $\tau$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Helical Springs:
  • $C = D/d$ $4 \le C \le 12$.
  • Wahl Factor $K_w = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4} + \frac{0.615}{C}$.
  • Max shear stress at INNER coil surface: $\tau_{\text{max}} = K_w \frac{8PD}{\pi d^3}$.
  • Deflection $\delta = \frac{8PD^3 n}{Gd^4}$; Stiffness $k = \frac{Gd^4}{8D^3 n}$.
  • Active coils for squared & ground ends: $n = n_t - 2$.
  • $k \propto d^4 / D^3 n$.
  • Helical Torsion Springs:
  • Operates in BENDING STRESS $\sigma = K_b \frac{32M}{\pi d^3}$, $K_b = \frac{4C-1}{4C-4}$.
  • Angular deflection $\theta = \frac{64 M D n}{E d^4}$.
  • Spring Combinations & Cutting:
  • Cut in half $\implies k' = 2k$.
  • Two cut halves in parallel $\implies k_{\text{total}} = 4k$.
  • Series: $1/k_{\text{eq}} = 1/k_1 + 1/k_2$; Parallel: $k_{\text{eq}} = k_1 + k_2$.
  • Strain Energy & Concentric Springs:
  • Resilience $U/V = \tau^2 / 4G$.
  • Concentric springs wound in OPPOSITE HANDS to avoid binding.
  • Leaf Springs & Belleville Springs:
  • Un-nipped stress ratio: $\sigma_f = 1.5 \sigma_g$.
  • Nipping equalizes stresses: $\sigma_f = \sigma_g$.
  • Belleville springs: high load in small axial space.
  • Buckling, Surge & Fatigue:
  • Buckling avoided if $L_f / D < 4.0$.
  • Surge $=$ resonance condition $f_n = \frac{d}{2\pi D^2 n}\sqrt{\frac{G g}{2\rho}}$.
  • Soderberg uses $\tau_y$; Goodman uses $\tau_u$.
