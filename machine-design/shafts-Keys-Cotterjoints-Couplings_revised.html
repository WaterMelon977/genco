Design of Shafts, Keys, Cotter Joints & Couplings

Torsion · Combined Loading · Key Types · Misalignment  ·  APGENCO AE | SSC JE Mechanical

1. Shaft Design — Torsional Stress

A shaft is a rotating member that transmits power. Primary design loading is torsion, but shafts also experience bending moments from transverse loads (pulleys, gears, belts).

Torsional Shear Stress — Solid Shaft

$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{16\,T}{\pi\,d^3}}$$
$T$ — applied torque, N·mm $d$ — shaft diameter, mm $\tau$ — maximum shear stress (outer surface), MPa

Torsional Shear Stress — Hollow Shaft

$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{16\,T\,d_o}{\pi(d_o^4 - d_i^4)} = \frac{16\,T}{\pi\,d_o^3(1-k^4)}}$$
$d_o$ — outer diameter, mm; $d_i$ — inner diameter, mm; $k = d_i/d_o$
💡 Hollow Shaft Advantage: A hollow shaft has a higher polar section modulus per unit mass than a solid shaft of equal mass and material — making it both stronger and lighter!

Angle of Twist

$$\boxed{\theta = \frac{T\,L}{G\,J} = \frac{32\,T\,L}{G\,\pi\,d^4} \quad \text{radians}}$$
$J = \pi d^4/32$ — polar second moment of area, mm⁴

2. Combined Bending and Torsion

When a shaft carries both bending moment $M$ and torque $T$ simultaneously:

Equivalent Twisting Moment $T_e$ — Maximum Shear Stress Theory

$$\boxed{T_e = \sqrt{M^2 + T^2}} \qquad \boxed{\tau_\text{max} = \frac{16\,T_e}{\pi\,d^3}}$$

Equivalent Bending Moment $M_e$ — Maximum Normal Stress Theory

$$\boxed{M_e = \frac{1}{2}\!\left[M + \sqrt{M^2+T^2}\right]} \qquad \boxed{\sigma_\text{max} = \frac{32\,M_e}{\pi\,d^3}}$$
⚠️ Exam Trap: Use $T_e$ for Maximum Shear Stress Theory (Tresca — standard for ductile shafts). Use $M_e$ for Maximum Normal Stress Theory (Rankine).

3. Key Design

A key transmits torque from shaft to hub. It acts as an intentional mechanical fuse to protect expensive shafts and hubs from overload.

Square Key — Shear and Crushing

$$\text{Shear:} \quad \boxed{T = \frac{d}{2}\,(l\,w\,\tau)} \qquad \text{Crushing:} \quad \boxed{T = \frac{d}{2}\!\left(l\,\frac{t}{2}\,\sigma_c\right)}$$
Standard proportions: $w = t = d/4$, $l \ge 1.5d$

For a square key ($w = t$), crushing stress is exactly twice the shear stress:

$$\boxed{\sigma_c = 2\,\tau}$$

4. Cotter Joints

A cotter joint is a temporary rigid fastening connecting two co-axial rods (socket and spigot) under axial tension or compression only (cannot transmit torque). Loaded in double shear.

Cotter thickness $t$$0.31d$ ($\approx 0.3d$ to $0.4d$)$d$ = rod diameter

📌 Quick-Fire Exam Checklist
  • Solid shaft: $\tau = 16T/(\pi d^3)$; max stress at outer surface.
  • Hollow shaft: $\tau = 16T/[\pi d_o^3(1-k^4)]$; $k = d_i/d_o$.
  • Hollow shaft: higher polar section modulus per unit mass — stronger AND lighter.
  • Angle of twist: $\theta = TL/(GJ)$; $J = \pi d^4/32$.
  • $T_e = \sqrt{M^2+T^2}$ — for max shear stress design (Tresca).
  • $M_e = \frac{1}{2}[M + \sqrt{M^2+T^2}]$ — for max normal stress design (Rankine).
  • Square key: $\sigma_c = 2\tau$ always; crushing governs.
  • Standard square key: $w = t = d/4$; $l \ge 1.5d$.
  • Key = mechanical fuse; weakest link by design.
  • Woodruff key: semi-circular, self-aligning, for tapered shafts.
  • Feather key: axial sliding permitted while transmitting torque.
  • Saddle key: friction drive only; light duty.
  • Cotter joint: temporary, axial loads only (no torque); double shear; taper 1:24 to 1:48, one side.
  • $t_\text{cotter} \approx 0.31d$; $b_\text{cotter} \approx 1.3d$–$1.5d$.
  • Oldham's coupling: parallel offset. Hooke's joint: angular misalignment (propeller shafts).
  • Bushed-pin coupling: rubber bushes for shock absorption; slight angular misalignment.
