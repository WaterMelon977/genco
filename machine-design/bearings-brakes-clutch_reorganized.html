2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Rolling Contact Bearings

  • Basic Rating Life $L_{10}$:
    $${\boxed{L_{10} = \left( \frac{C}{P_e} \right)^k \times 10^6 \quad (\text{revolutions})}}$$
  • $C$: basic dynamic load rating $\text{N}$
  • $P_e$: equivalent radial dynamic load $\text{N}$

  • $k = 3.0$ for ball bearings; $k = \frac{10}{3} = 3.33$ for roller bearings

  • Load–Life Proportionality (Ball Bearings):

    $${\boxed{L \propto \frac{1}{P^3} \implies L_1 \cdot P_1^3 = L_2 \cdot P_2^3}}$$

  • Doubling load $2P$ $\implies$ Life drops to $\mathbf{1/8^{\text{th}}}$ of original $L/8$.

  • Halving load $P/2$ $\implies$ Life increases to $\mathbf{8\times}$ original $8L$.

  • Equivalent Radial Load $P_e$:

    $${\boxed{P_e = X \cdot F_r + Y \cdot F_a \quad (\text{N})}}$$

  • $F_r$: radial load component $\text{N}$
  • $F_a$: axial (thrust) load component $\text{N}$

  • $X, Y$: radial and axial load factors

  • Bearing Designation System:

    $${\text{Bearing Number Format: } X X Y Y \implies \text{Bore Diameter } d = Y Y \times 5 \quad (\text{mm})}$$

  • Example: Bearing $408 \implies$ Heavy series $4$, Bore diameter $= 08 \times 5 = 40 \text{ mm}$.

2. Journal / Sliding Bearings

  • Petroff's Equation for Coefficient of Friction $f$:
    $${\boxed{f = 2\pi^2 \cdot \left(\frac{\mu \cdot N}{P}\right) \cdot \left(\frac{r}{c}\right)}}$$
  • $\mu$: dynamic viscosity ($\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{Ns/m}^2$)
  • $N$: shaft speed $\text{rev/s}$
  • $P$: bearing pressure on projected area ($P = \frac{W}{d \cdot \ell}$, $\text{N/m}^2$)

  • $r$: shaft radius $\text{m}$, $c$: radial clearance $\text{m}$

  • Bearing Characteristic Number:

    $${\boxed{\text{Bearing Characteristic Number} = \frac{Z \cdot N}{P}}}$$

  • $Z$: viscosity $\text{cP}$, $N$: speed $\text{rpm}$, $P$: bearing pressure $\text{MPa}$

  • Hydrodynamic lubrication regime requires operating above the critical value $(ZN/P)_{\text{crit}}$.

  • Standard Journal Design Parameters:

  • Radial Clearance: $\boxed{\frac{c}{r} \approx 0.001 \implies 1 \mu\text{m per mm of diameter}}$
  • Length-to-Diameter Ratio: $\boxed{\frac{L}{D} \in [1.0, 2.0]}$

3. Brakes

  • Equivalent Coefficient of Friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$ for Long Shoe Brake $2\theta > 60^\circ$:
    $${\boxed{\mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4 \cdot \mu \cdot \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}}}$$
  • $\mu$: actual coefficient of friction
  • $\theta$: half angle of contact ($\text{radians}$ in denominator!)

  • $2\theta$: total angle of contact ($2\theta > 60^\circ \implies$ long shoe)

  • Braking Torque $T$:

    $${\boxed{T = F_r \cdot R = \mu_{\text{eq}} \cdot R_n \cdot R \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{m})}}$$

  • $F_r$: friction force on drum surface ($F_r = \mu_{\text{eq}} R_n$, $\text{N}$)

  • $R_n$: normal force on shoe $\text{N}$, $R$: drum radius $\text{m}$

  • Block Brake Design Proportion:

    $${\boxed{\text{Shoe Width } b = (0.25 \text{ to } 0.50) \cdot D}}$$

4. Clutches

  • Uniform Wear Theory (Safe Design / Worn Clutch):
    $${\boxed{T = \mu \cdot W \cdot R_m \cdot n \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{m})}}$$
    $${\boxed{R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2} \quad (\text{m})}}$$
  • $W$: total axial clamping force $\text{N}$
  • $r_1, r_2$: outer and inner radii of friction disc $\text{m}$

  • $n$: number of active pairs of contact surfaces

  • Uniform Pressure Theory (New Clutch):

    $${\boxed{T = \frac{2}{3} \cdot \mu \cdot W \left( \frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2} \right) \cdot n \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{m})}}$$

  • Multi-Plate Clutch Contact Pairs $n$:

    $${\boxed{n = n_1 + n_2 - 1}}$$

  • $n_1$: number of discs on driving shaft

  • $n_2$: number of discs on driven shaft

  • Centrifugal Clutch Shoe Engagement:

  • Initial engagement speed $N_1$: $\boxed{F_{\text{cf}} = F_{\text{spring}}}$
  • Running speed $N_2 > N_1$: $\boxed{\text{Net Pressing Force } F_{\text{net}} = F_{\text{cf}} - F_{\text{spring}}}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Basic Rating Life $L_{10}$: The number of revolutions (or hours at constant speed) that $90\%$ of a group of identical bearings will achieve or exceed before the first evidence of fatigue flaking occurs.
  • Hydrodynamic Bearing: A sliding contact bearing in which the load is supported entirely by self-generated fluid pressure within a converging wedge of oil created by shaft rotation.
  • Hydrostatic Bearing: A sliding contact bearing in which highly pressurized lubricant is supplied from an external pump, providing full fluid film support even at zero shaft speed.
  • Petroff's Equation: An analytical formula derived assuming a concentric shaft and uniform clearance, used to determine the coefficient of friction in lightly loaded high-speed journal bearings.
  • Long Shoe Brake: A shoe brake where the total angle of contact $2\theta > 60^\circ$. Pressure distribution is non-uniform, requiring the use of equivalent coefficient of friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$.
  • Internal Expanding Shoe Brake: An automotive drum brake mechanism containing internal shoes expanded outwards against the drum by a cam or wheel cylinder; self-energizing and provides high braking torque.
  • Uniform Wear Theory: Clutch design assumption that wear rate $p \cdot r$ is constant across the friction surface. Preferred for safe engineering design because it yields a lower, conservative torque rating.
  • Uniform Pressure Theory: Clutch design assumption that pressure $p$ is uniform across the entire friction surface; applies strictly to new, unworn clutch plates.
  • Jaw (Positive) Clutch: A mechanical clutch that transmits torque through interlocking metal jaws with zero slip.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Long Shoe Angle Threshold: $2\theta > 60^\circ$ $1.047 \text{ rad}$.
  • Journal Clearance Ratio: $c/d \approx 0.001$ $1 \mu\text{m/mm}$.
  • Journal L/D Ratio: $1.0$ to $2.0$.
  • Block Brake Width Ratio: $b/D = 0.25 - 0.50$.
  • Ball Bearing Life Exponent: $k = 3.0$.
  • Roller Bearing Life Exponent: $k = 10/3 \approx 3.33$.

3.1 Rolling Contact Bearings & Life Calculations

1. Concept Pointers
  • Load-Life Relationship:
  • Ball bearings: $L \propto \frac{1}{P^3}$.
  • Roller bearings: $L \propto \frac{1}{P^{10/3}}$.
  • Doubling load $P_2 = 2P_1$ $\implies L_2 = \frac{L_1}{2^3} = \frac{L_1}{8}$.
  • Halving load $P_2 = 0.5P_1$ $\implies L_2 = \frac{L_1}{(0.5)^3} = 8 L_1$.
  • Equivalent Radial Load $P_e$:
  • Combines radial force $F_r$ and axial force $F_a$ into a single equivalent radial load: $P_e = X F_r + Y F_a$.
  • Bearing Designations:
  • Last two digits $\times 5 =$ Bore diameter in mm.
  • Series codes: $100$ (extra light), $200$ (light), $300$ (medium), $400$ (heavy).
  • Example: Bearing $408 \implies 400$ series (heavy), bore $= 08 \times 5 = 40 \text{ mm}$.
  • Bearing Type Applications:
  • Deep Groove Ball Bearing: Combined radial & thrust load at high speeds.
  • Tapered Roller Bearing: Heavy radial & high axial thrust loads (vehicle wheel hubs).
  • Spherical Roller Bearing: High radial load + self-aligning for angular shaft misalignment.
💡 Key Concept: Load-Life Cube Rule for Ball Bearings: Bearing life is inversely proportional to the CUBE of equivalent load $L \propto 1/P^3$. Doubling the load reduces bearing life to 1/8th of its original value!

[!TRAP] Bearing Designation Bore Code: Last two digits multiplied by 5 gives the bore diameter in mm (e.g., $08 \times 5 = 40 \text{ mm}$). Do not mistake 08 directly for 8 mm or 80 mm!

2. Decision Tree
Rolling Contact Bearing Selection Check?
├─ Is load purely Radial at high speed? → DEEP GROOVE BALL BEARING
├─ Is there heavy Axial Thrust load? → TAPERED ROLLER BEARING
├─ Is there Angular Shaft Misalignment? → SPHERICAL ROLLER BEARING
├─ How does Life change if load is doubled (Ball Bearing)? → Drops to 1/8th (L2 = L1 / 8)
└─ How to find bore from 408 code? → Last two digits (08) * 5 = 40 mm
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Ball bearing life $L \propto 1/P^3$.
  • [ ] Roller bearing life $L \propto 1/P^{10/3}$.
  • [ ] $P_e = X F_r + Y F_a$.
  • [ ] Bore diameter $= \text{last two digits} \times 5$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A ball bearing operating under a radial load of $8 \text{ kN}$ has a rated life of $8000 \text{ hours}$. If the load is increased to $16 \text{ kN}$ while keeping the speed constant, calculate the new expected bearing life. - Solution: - Apply load-life relation for ball bearings:

$${\frac{L_2}{L_1} = \left(\frac{P_1}{P_2}\right)^3}$$
- Substitute values ($P_1 = 8 \text{ kN}$, $P_2 = 16 \text{ kN}$, $L_1 = 8000 \text{ hrs}$):
$${\frac{L_2}{8000} = \left(\frac{8}{16}\right)^3 = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^3 = \frac{1}{8}}$$
$${L_2 = \frac{8000}{8} = 1000 \text{ hours}}$$
- Key Insight: $L_2 = 1000 \text{ hours}$. Doubling load reduces life to $1/8^{\text{th}}$.

3.2 Journal (Sliding Contact) Bearings

1. Concept Pointers
  • Hydrodynamic vs Hydrostatic:
  • Hydrodynamic: Oil film pressure is self-generated by rotation (converging oil wedge).
  • Hydrostatic: External pump supplies pressurized lubricant; supports load even at $N=0$.
  • Bearing Characteristic Number $ZN/P$:
  • Dimensionless group ($Z$: viscosity in cP, $N$: speed in rpm, $P$: pressure in MPa).
  • Stable hydrodynamic operation requires $ZN/P > (ZN/P)_{\text{crit}}$.
  • Clearance & Length Ratios:
  • Radial clearance $c/r \approx 0.001 \implies 1 \mu\text{m per mm of diameter}$.
  • Length to diameter ratio $L/D = 1.0 \text{ to } 2.0$ (best compromise between leakage and load capacity).
  • Petroff's Equation:
  • Gives friction coefficient for lightly loaded, concentric journal bearings:
    $${f = 2\pi^2 \left(\frac{\mu N}{P}\right)\left(\frac{r}{c}\right)}$$
  • Projected Area Pressure:
  • Bearing pressure $P = \frac{W}{d \cdot \ell}$ $\text{N/mm}^2$.
💡 Key Concept: Hydrodynamic vs Hydrostatic Principle: Hydrodynamic bearings generate oil pressure via shaft rotation (wedge action). Hydrostatic bearings rely on an EXTERNAL PUMP to supply pressure regardless of speed.

[!TRAP] Journal Pressure Area: Journal bearing pressure uses projected area $d \cdot \ell$, NOT circular area $\frac{\pi}{4}d^2$!

2. Decision Tree
Journal Bearing Design Check?
├─ Is pressure generated internally by shaft rotation? → HYDRODYNAMIC BEARING
├─ Is pressure supplied by an external pump? → HYDROSTATIC BEARING
├─ What area is used for bearing pressure P = W / Area? → PROJECTED AREA (d * length)
├─ Standard clearance per mm diameter? → 1 micron per mm (c/r = 0.001)
└─ Optimum L/D ratio? → 1.0 to 2.0
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Hydrodynamic: self-generated oil wedge.
  • [ ] Hydrostatic: external pump.
  • [ ] Petroff: $f = 2\pi^2 (\mu N/P)(r/c)$.
  • [ ] Projected area $= d \cdot \ell$.
  • [ ] Clearance $= 1 \mu\text{m/mm}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A hydrodynamic journal bearing of diameter $d = 30 \text{ mm}$ supports a maximum radial load $W = 1.8 \text{ kN}$. If the allowable bearing pressure is $P = 6 \text{ N/mm}^2$, determine the required length $\ell$ of the bearing. - Solution: - Calculate bearing pressure on projected area:

$${P = \frac{W}{d \cdot \ell} \implies 6 = \frac{1800}{30 \times \ell}}$$
$${180 \cdot \ell = 1800 \implies \ell = \frac{1800}{180} = 10 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: $\ell = 10 \text{ mm}$. Use projected area $d \cdot \ell = 30 \times \ell$.

3.3 Shoe & Automotive Brakes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Short Shoe vs Long Shoe Brakes:
  • Short Shoe $2\theta < 60^\circ$: Pressure assumed uniform across contact arc; actual coefficient of friction $\mu$ is used.
  • Long Shoe $2\theta > 60^\circ$: Pressure is non-uniform; equivalent coefficient of friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$ MUST be used:
    $${\mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}}$$
  • Braking Torque:
  • Torque on drum $T = F_r \cdot R = \mu_{\text{eq}} \cdot R_n \cdot R$.
  • Depends on friction force at drum surface $\times$ drum radius.
  • Block Brake Width Ratio:
  • Shoe width $b$ is designed to be $0.25d$ to $0.50d$ ($d =$ drum diameter).
  • Internal Expanding Shoe Brake:
  • Most common brake in motor cars and two-wheelers. Contains internal shoes pushed outwards against the drum by a wheel cylinder/cam; provides self-energizing action.
  • Parking Brake:
  • Operated mechanically for safety reliability.
💡 Key Concept: Long Shoe Brake 60° Rule: When total contact angle $2\theta > 60^\circ$, pressure varies across the arc, requiring equivalent friction coefficient $\mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}$.

[!TRAP] Braking Torque Radius: Braking torque $T = \text{Friction Force } (F_r) \times \text{Drum Radius } (R)$. Do not multiply normal force directly by radius without coefficient of friction!

2. Decision Tree
Brake Design / Type Check?
├─ Is total angle of contact 2*theta > 60 deg? → LONG SHOE (Use equivalent mu_eq)
├─ Is total angle of contact 2*theta < 60 deg? → SHORT SHOE (Use actual mu)
├─ Most common service brake in cars/motorcycles? → INTERNAL EXPANDING SHOE BRAKE
├─ Parking brake operation method? → MECHANICAL
└─ Block brake shoe width ratio b/D? → 0.25 to 0.50
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $2\theta > 60^\circ \implies \mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}$.
  • [ ] Torque $T = \mu_{\text{eq}} R_n R$.
  • [ ] Block brake width $b = (0.25 - 0.50)D$.
  • [ ] Automobile service brake: internal expanding shoe.
  • [ ] Parking brake: mechanical.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A block brake has a drum diameter $D = 300 \text{ mm}$ (radius $R = 150 \text{ mm}$) and a total contact angle $2\theta = 90^\circ$ $\theta = 45^\circ = 0.7854 \text{ rad}$. The actual friction coefficient is $\mu = 0.35$. Calculate the equivalent coefficient of friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$. - Solution: - Convert $\theta = 45^\circ \implies \sin 45^\circ = 0.7071$, $2\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708 \text{ rad}$, $\sin 2\theta = \sin 90^\circ = 1.0$. - Apply equivalent friction formula:

$${\mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta} = \frac{4 \times 0.35 \times 0.7071}{1.5708 + 1.0} = \frac{0.9900}{2.5708} \approx 0.385}$$
- Key Insight: $\mu_{\text{eq}} \approx 0.385$. Since $2\theta = 90^\circ > 60^\circ$, $\mu_{\text{eq}}$ is higher than actual $\mu = 0.35$.

3.4 Friction Clutches: Uniform Wear vs Uniform Pressure

1. Concept Pointers
  • Uniform Wear Theory (Safe Design / Worn Clutch):
  • Assumes wear rate $p \cdot r = \text{constant}$.
  • Pressure is maximum at inner radius $r_2$ and minimum at outer radius $r_1$.
  • Mean radius: $R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$.
  • Torque capacity: $T = \mu W R_m n$.
  • Preferred for safe engineering design because it gives a lower, conservative torque rating.
  • Uniform Pressure Theory (New Clutch):
  • Assumes pressure $p = \text{constant}$ across the disc.
  • Mean radius: $R_m = \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2}\right)$.
  • Torque capacity: $T = \frac{2}{3} \mu W \left(\frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2}\right) n$.
  • Applies ONLY to brand-new, unworn clutch plates.
  • Theory Comparison:
  • Uniform wear theory gives LOWER torque capacity than uniform pressure theory for the same dimensions and axial load $T_{\text{wear}} < T_{\text{pressure}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Clutch Safe Design Preference: Clutches are ALWAYS designed assuming UNIFORM WEAR ($T = \mu W R_m$ with $R_m = \frac{r_1+r_2}{2}$) because worn plates yield a lower, safer torque capacity than new plates.

[!TRAP] Clutch Theory Mean Radius: - Uniform Wear: $R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$. - Uniform Pressure: $R_m = \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2}\right)$. Do not mix up the mean radius formulas!

2. Decision Tree
Clutch Theory Selection Check?
├─ Is question asking for Safe Engineering Design / Used Clutch? → UNIFORM WEAR THEORY (Rm = (r1+r2)/2)
├─ Is question explicitly specifying a Brand New unworn clutch? → UNIFORM PRESSURE THEORY
├─ Which theory gives lower (conservative) torque capacity? → UNIFORM WEAR THEORY
└─ Does torque capacity increase with mean radius Rm? → YES (T is directly proportional to Rm)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Uniform wear: $p \cdot r = \text{const}$, $R_m = \frac{r_1+r_2}{2}$.
  • [ ] Uniform pressure: $p = \text{const}$, $R_m = \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{r_1^3-r_2^3}{r_1^2-r_2^2}\right)$.
  • [ ] Safe design $\implies$ uniform wear.
  • [ ] $T_{\text{wear}} < T_{\text{pressure}}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A single plate clutch with effective friction surfaces on both sides $n = 2$ has an outer radius $r_1 = 15 \text{ cm} = 0.15 \text{ m}$ and inner radius $r_2 = 10 \text{ cm} = 0.10 \text{ m}$. The axial clamping force is $W = 1 \text{ kN} = 1000 \text{ N}$ and coefficient of friction $\mu = 0.5$. Calculate the torque transmitted assuming uniform wear theory. - Solution: - Calculate mean radius $R_m$ under uniform wear:

$${R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2} = \frac{0.15 + 0.10}{2} = 0.125 \text{ m}}$$
- Calculate torque capacity ($n = 2$ for both sides effective):
$${T = \mu \cdot W \cdot R_m \cdot n = 0.5 \times 1000 \times 0.125 \times 2 = 125 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}$$
- Key Insight: $T = 125 \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$. Both sides effective doubles the torque capacity $n=2$.

3.5 Multi-Plate, Centrifugal & Special Clutches

1. Concept Pointers
  • Multi-Plate Clutch Pairs:
  • Number of active contact pairs: $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$.
  • Used in motorcycles/scooters where high torque capacity is required within a small radial space.
  • Wet multi-plate clutches use oil to dissipate heat generated during slipping.
  • Centrifugal Clutch:
  • Operates automatically based on engine speed.
  • Initial engagement speed $N_1$: Centrifugal force equals spring force $F_{\text{cf}} = F_{\text{spring}}$.
  • Running operating speed $N_2 > N_1$: Net force pressing against drum $= F_{\text{cf}} - F_{\text{spring}}$.
  • Vehicle Application Matching:
  • Single Plate Clutch $\implies$ Trucks / Buses (robust, simple heat dissipation).
  • Multi-Plate Clutch $\implies$ Motorcycles / Scooters (compact radial dimensions).
  • Jaw (Positive) Clutch $\implies$ Direct positive drive with zero slip.
  • Cone Clutch $\implies$ Obsolete due to strict coaxiality requirements.
💡 Key Concept: Multi-Plate Contact Surfaces: Number of active friction pairs is $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$ (one less than total plates because end plates contact on one side only).

[!TRAP] Centrifugal Clutch Net Force: In running condition, the force pressing shoes against the drum is $F_{\text{cf}} - F_{\text{spring}}$, NOT $F_{\text{cf}}$ alone!

2. Decision Tree
Clutch Type & Application Check?
├─ What is number of friction pairs for n1 and n2 discs? → n = n1 + n2 - 1
├─ Clutch type for Trucks/Buses? → SINGLE PLATE CLUTCH
├─ Clutch type for Motorcycles/Scooters? → MULTI-PLATE CLUTCH
├─ Clutch for positive drive with zero slip? → JAW (POSITIVE) CLUTCH
└─ Centrifugal clutch running net force? → F_cf - F_spring
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Multi-plate pairs $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$.
  • [ ] Centrifugal engagement: $F_{\text{cf}} = F_{\text{spring}}$.
  • [ ] Centrifugal running force: $F_{\text{cf}} - F_{\text{spring}}$.
  • [ ] Trucks: single plate; Scooters: multi-plate.
  • [ ] Positive drive: jaw clutch.
  • [ ] Cone clutch obsolete due to coaxiality.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A multi-plate clutch has 4 discs on the driving shaft $n_1 = 4$ and 3 discs on the driven shaft $n_2 = 3$. Calculate the number of active friction contact surface pairs. - Solution: - Apply multi-plate contact surface formula:

$${n = n_1 + n_2 - 1 = 4 + 3 - 1 = 6}$$
- Key Insight: $n = 6$ active friction pairs.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: How does bearing life $L_{10}$ scale with equivalent load $P$ for ball bearings?
A: Inversely proportional to the cube of load: $L \propto \frac{1}{P^3}$.

Q2: How does doubling the load affect ball bearing life?
A: Life reduces to $1/8^{\text{th}}$ $1/2^3$ of its original value.

Q3: How is bore diameter determined from bearing designation 309?
A: Bore diameter $= 09 \times 5 = 45 \text{ mm}$ (medium series 300).

Q4: Differentiate between Hydrodynamic and Hydrostatic journal bearings.
A: Hydrodynamic bearings self-generate fluid pressure via shaft rotation. Hydrostatic bearings rely on an external pump for pressure at all speeds.

Q5: What is the typical radial clearance in a hydrodynamic journal bearing?
A: Approximately $1 \mu\text{m}$ per mm of shaft diameter $c/r \approx 0.001$.

Q6: Write Petroff's equation for friction coefficient in journal bearings.
A: $f = 2\pi^2 \left(\frac{\mu N}{P}\right)\left(\frac{r}{c}\right)$.

Q7: When must the equivalent coefficient of friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$ be used in shoe brakes?
A: When the total angle of contact $2\theta > 60^\circ$ (long shoe brake).

Q8: Write the formula for equivalent coefficient of friction $\mu_{\text{eq}}$ for long shoe brakes.
A: $\mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}$.

Q9: What type of service brake is most commonly used in motor cars?
A: Internal expanding shoe brake.

Q10: Why is Uniform Wear Theory preferred over Uniform Pressure Theory for safe clutch design?
A: Because uniform wear theory yields a lower, conservative torque capacity estimate compared to new unworn plates.

Q11: Write the mean radius formula $R_m$ under Uniform Wear Theory.
A: $R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}$.

Q12: Write the mean radius formula $R_m$ under Uniform Pressure Theory.
A: $R_m = \frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2}\right)$.

Q13: How many active pairs of contact surfaces exist in a multi-plate clutch with $n_1$ driving discs and $n_2$ driven discs?
A: $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$.

Q14: What force condition governs initial shoe engagement in a centrifugal clutch?
A: Centrifugal force on the shoes equals the spring restraining force $F_{\text{cf}} = F_{\text{spring}}$.

Q15: Match the clutch types for (a) Trucks, (b) Scooters, (c) Zero-slip drive.
A: (a) Single plate clutch, (b) Multi-plate clutch, (c) Jaw (positive) clutch.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Rolling Contact Bearings:
  • Ball bearing life: $L \propto 1/P^3$. Double load $\implies 1/8^{\text{th}}$ life; Half load $\implies 8\times$ life.
  • Roller bearing life: $L \propto 1/P^{10/3}$.
  • $P_e = X F_r + Y F_a$.
  • Bore diameter $= \text{last two digits} \times 5$ mm.
  • Deep groove ball: combined load at high speed. Tapered roller: heavy axial thrust. Spherical roller: angular misalignment.
  • Journal Bearings:
  • Hydrodynamic: self-generated oil wedge. Hydrostatic: external pump.
  • Petroff: $f = 2\pi^2 (\mu N/P)(r/c)$.
  • Projected area $= d \cdot \ell$.
  • Clearance: $1 \mu\text{m/mm}$ diameter. $L/D = 1.0 - 2.0$.
  • Brakes:
  • Long shoe $2\theta > 60^\circ \implies \mu_{\text{eq}} = \frac{4\mu \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta}$.
  • Short shoe $2\theta < 60^\circ \implies$ actual $\mu$.
  • Torque $T = F_r R = \mu_{\text{eq}} R_n R$.
  • Block brake width $b = (0.25 - 0.50)D$.
  • Automotive service brake: internal expanding shoe. Parking brake: mechanical.
  • Clutches:
  • Safe design / Worn clutch $\implies$ Uniform Wear ($T = \mu W R_m n$, $R_m = \frac{r_1+r_2}{2}$).
  • New clutch $\implies$ Uniform Pressure $T = \frac{2}{3}\mu W \left(\frac{r_1^3-r_2^3}{r_1^2-r_2^2}\right) n$.
  • $T_{\text{wear}} < T_{\text{pressure}}$.
  • Multi-plate pairs $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$.
  • Centrifugal initial engagement: $F_{\text{cf}} = F_{\text{spring}}$; Running net force: $F_{\text{cf}} - F_{\text{spring}}$.
  • Applications: Trucks $\implies$ Single plate; Scooters $\implies$ Multi-plate; Positive drive $\implies$ Jaw clutch; Cone clutch obsolete (coaxiality).
