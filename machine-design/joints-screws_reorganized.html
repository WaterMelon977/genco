2.1 — All Formulas & Quantitative Relations (with symbols & units)

1. Power Screws & Threaded Fasteners

  • Metric Thread Designation:

    $${\text{M } d \times p \implies d = \text{nominal (major) diameter (mm)}, \quad p = \text{pitch (mm)}}$$

  • Self-Locking Condition (Square Thread):

    $${\boxed{\phi \ge \alpha \quad \text{or} \quad \mu \ge \tan\alpha}}$$

  • $\phi$: friction angle ($\text{degrees}$, $\mu = \tan\phi$)
  • $\alpha$: helix angle ($\text{degrees}$, $\tan\alpha = \frac{L}{\pi d_m}$)

  • Self-locking efficiency limit: $\boxed{\eta \le 50\%}$ (if $\eta > 50\% \implies$ Overhauling).

  • Effort & Torque to Raise Load (Square Thread):

    $${\boxed{P = W \cdot \tan(\alpha + \phi) \quad (\text{N})}}$$
    $${\boxed{T = \frac{W \cdot d_m}{2} \cdot \tan(\alpha + \phi) \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})}}$$

  • $W$: axial load $\text{N}$

  • $d_m$: mean diameter of screw threads ($d_m = d - 0.5p$, $\text{mm}$)

  • Effort & Torque to Lower Load (Square Thread):

    $${\boxed{P_{\text{lower}} = W \cdot \tan(\phi - \alpha) \quad (\text{N})}}$$
    $${\boxed{T_{\text{lower}} = \frac{W \cdot d_m}{2} \cdot \tan(\phi - \alpha) \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})}}$$

  • Efficiency of Screw Jack $\eta$:

    $${\boxed{\eta = \frac{\tan\alpha}{\tan(\alpha + \phi)}}}$$

  • Maximum Efficiency of Screw Jack $\eta_{\text{max}}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi}}}$$

  • Occurs at optimum helix angle: $\boxed{\alpha = \frac{\pi}{4} - \frac{\phi}{2} = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}}$

  • V-Thread Equivalent Coefficient of Friction $\mu'$:

    $${\boxed{\mu' = \frac{\mu}{\cos(\beta/2)}}}$$

  • $\beta$: included thread angle ($60^\circ$ for metric threads $\implies \beta/2 = 30^\circ$)

  • Multi-Start Thread Lead $L$:

    $${\boxed{L = n \cdot p}}$$

  • $n$: number of starts, $p$: pitch $\text{mm}$

2. Bolted Joints & Tension Members

  • Core Diameter from Direct Tensile Load $d_c$:
    $${\boxed{d_c = \sqrt{\frac{4P}{\pi \cdot \sigma_t}} \quad (\text{mm})}}$$
  • $P$: axial tensile load $\text{N}$
  • $\sigma_t$: allowable tensile stress $\text{MPa}$

  • Core area: $A_c = \frac{\pi}{4} d_c^2 \approx 0.7854 d_c^2$.

  • Resilience / Strain Energy of Bolt of Uniform Strength:

    $${\boxed{U = \frac{\sigma^2 \cdot V}{2E} \quad (\text{N}\cdot\text{mm})}}$$

  • Shank diameter reduced to core diameter $d_c$ throughout unthreaded length $\implies$ uniform stress distribution $\implies$ maximum shock energy absorption.

3. Welded Joints

  • Throat Thickness of Fillet Weld $t$:
    $${\boxed{t = 0.707 \cdot h = h \cdot \sin 45^\circ \quad (\text{mm})}}$$
  • $h$: leg size of weld $\text{mm}$

  • Throat is the WEAKEST section in a fillet weld!

  • Shear Strength of Double Parallel Fillet Weld:

    $${\boxed{P = 2 \cdot (0.707 \cdot h \cdot \ell) \cdot \tau \quad (\text{N})}}$$

  • $\ell$: effective length of each weld $\text{mm}$

  • $\tau$: allowable shear stress $\text{MPa}$

  • Transverse Fillet Weld Shear Strength Comparison:

  • Transverse fillet weld is $\mathbf{1.17\times}$ stronger in shear than a parallel fillet weld.

  • Tensile Strength of Butt Weld:

    $${\boxed{P = \sigma_t \cdot t \cdot L \cdot \eta \quad (\text{N})}}$$

  • $t$: plate thickness = throat size $\text{mm}$
  • $L$: length of weld $\text{mm}$

4. Riveted Joints

  • Unwin's Formula for Rivet Hole Diameter $d$:
    $${\boxed{d = 6\sqrt{t} \quad (\text{mm})}}$$

  • $t$: plate thickness $\text{mm}$

  • Margin to Prevent Edge Tearing $m$:

    $${\boxed{m = 1.5 \cdot d \quad (\text{mm})}}$$

  • Tearing Efficiency of Plate $\eta_t$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_t = \frac{p - d}{p}}}$$

  • $p$: rivet pitch $\text{mm}$
  • $d$: rivet hole diameter $\text{mm}$

  • If $\eta_t = 75\% \implies \boxed{\frac{d}{p} = 0.25}$

  • Single Riveted Lap Joint Strengths per Pitch:

  • Plate Tearing: $\boxed{P_t = (p - d) \cdot t \cdot \sigma_t}$
  • Rivet Single Shear: $\boxed{P_s = \frac{\pi}{4} d^2 \cdot \tau}$
  • Rivet Crushing: $\boxed{P_c = d \cdot t \cdot \sigma_c}$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Square Thread: Thread profile with $0^\circ$ flank angle. Offers highest efficiency for power transmission in both directions with zero radial bursting pressure.
  • Acme / Trapezoidal Thread: Thread profile with $29^\circ$ included angle ($14.5^\circ$ flank angle). Easier to cut than square threads; used in lathe lead screws and screw jacks.
  • Buttress Thread: Asymmetric thread profile ($45^\circ$ one side, $7^\circ$ other). Transmits high power efficiently in ONE DIRECTION ONLY.
  • Self-Locking Screw: A power screw that does not rotate back under load $\phi \ge \alpha \implies \eta \le 50\%$. Essential for safety in screw jacks and hoists.
  • Overhauling Screw: A power screw where the load lowers itself under gravity $\phi < \alpha \implies \eta > 50\%$.
  • Bolt of Uniform Strength: A bolt whose unthreaded shank diameter is reduced to equal the thread core diameter $d_c$, creating uniform stress along its entire length and maximizing strain energy (shock) absorption.
  • Turnbuckle: A coupler with left-hand threads on one end and right-hand threads on the other, allowing length and tension adjustment without rotating connected rods.
  • Fillet Weld Throat: The shortest distance from the root to the face of a fillet weld $t = 0.707 h$. Represents the failure plane under shear.
  • Caulking & Fullering: Mechanical cold-hammering operations performed on riveted joint edges to press plate margins tightly together, producing LEAK-PROOF joints for boilers and pressure vessels.

2.3 — Standard Values & Physical Constants

  • Acme Thread Angle: $29^\circ$ included angle $\theta = 14.5^\circ$.
  • Metric V-Thread Angle: $60^\circ$ included angle $\beta/2 = 30^\circ$.
  • BSW Thread Angle: $55^\circ$ included angle.
  • Fillet Throat Multiplier: $0.707$ $\sin 45^\circ$.
  • Unwin Multiplier: $6.0$ $d = 6\sqrt{t}$.
  • Margin Multiplier: $1.5$ $m = 1.5d$.
  • Max Self-Locking Efficiency: $50\%$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Thread Profiles & Nomenclature

1. Concept Pointers
  • Thread Classifications:
  • Square Thread: $0^\circ$ flank angle. Highest efficiency for power transmission in BOTH directions. Zero radial force on nut. Difficult to cut with taps/dies.
  • Acme / Trapezoidal Thread: $29^\circ$ included angle ($14.5^\circ$ flank angle). Stronger than square threads, easily cut on lathes, used in lathe lead screws.
  • Buttress Thread: Asymmetric profile $45^\circ/7^\circ$. Transmits high power in ONE DIRECTION ONLY. Used in bench vices, screw presses, cargo hooks.
  • V-Threads (Metric $60^\circ$, BSW $55^\circ$): High friction due to wedging effect $\mu' = \mu/\cos(\beta/2)$. Used for FASTENING ONLY, never for power transmission.
  • Metric Designation:
  • M $d \times p$ (e.g., M$24 \times 2 \implies$ nominal/major diameter $24 \text{ mm}$, pitch $2 \text{ mm}$).
  • Lead vs Pitch:
  • Single-start thread: $\text{Lead} = \text{Pitch}$.
  • Multi-start thread: $\text{Lead} = n \cdot \text{Pitch}$. Multi-start increases lead and efficiency, but reduces mechanical advantage and self-locking tendency.
💡 Key Concept: Thread Application Rule: - Square/Acme $\implies$ Power transmission in both directions. - Buttress $\implies$ Power transmission in ONE direction only. - V-threads $\implies$ Fastening/locking (never power transmission).

[!TRAP] Acme vs Metric Thread Angles: Acme thread included angle is $29^\circ$ (flank angle $14.5^\circ$). Metric V-thread included angle is $60^\circ$ (flank angle $30^\circ$). Do not confuse thread angles in MCQs!

2. Decision Tree
Thread Profile Selection Check?
├─ Power transmission in BOTH directions with maximum efficiency? → SQUARE THREAD
├─ Power transmission in Lead Screws requiring easy cutting/split nut? → ACME THREAD (29 deg)
├─ Power transmission in ONE direction only under heavy load? → BUTTRESS THREAD
├─ Fastening / Locking joints against loosening? → METRIC V-THREAD (60 deg)
└─ Multi-start thread lead calculation? → Lead = n * Pitch
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Metric: M $d \times p$.
  • [ ] Acme: $29^\circ$ included angle.
  • [ ] Square: highest efficiency, both directions.
  • [ ] Buttress: one direction power transmission.
  • [ ] V-thread: fastening, $\mu' = \mu/\cos(\beta/2)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Identify the nominal diameter and pitch for a bolt designated as M$24 \times 2$. - Solution: - Metric thread designation format: M Nominal Diameter $\times$ Pitch. - Nominal (major) diameter $= 24 \text{ mm}$. - Pitch $= 2 \text{ mm}$. - Key Insight: M$24 \times 2$ means $24 \text{ mm}$ major diameter and $2 \text{ mm}$ pitch.

3.2 Power Screws: Effort, Torque, Efficiency & Self-Locking

1. Concept Pointers
  • Self-Locking Mechanics:
  • A screw jack is self-locking if the load does not descend automatically when the lifting effort is removed.
  • Condition: Friction angle $\phi \ge \text{helix angle } \alpha \implies \mu \ge \tan\alpha$.
  • Maximum efficiency of any self-locking screw is ALWAYS $\le 50\%$.
  • If $\phi < \alpha \implies$ Overhauling $\eta > 50\%$.
  • Lifting Effort & Torque (Square Thread):
  • Lifting effort: $P = W \tan(\alpha + \phi)$.
  • Lifting torque: $T = \frac{W d_m}{2} \tan(\alpha + \phi)$.
  • Lowering Effort & Torque:
  • Lowering effort (self-locking): $P_{\text{lower}} = W \tan(\phi - \alpha)$.
  • Screw Jack Efficiency & Maximum Efficiency:
  • Efficiency: $\eta = \frac{\tan\alpha}{\tan(\alpha + \phi)}$.
  • Maximum efficiency: $\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi}$.
  • Occurs at optimum helix angle: $\alpha = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}$.
  • Screw Jack Cup Function:
  • The cup fitted at the top of a screw jack supports the load and prevents the load from rotating along with the screw.
💡 Key Concept: Self-Locking Efficiency Limit: The efficiency of a self-locking power screw can NEVER exceed $50\%$ $\eta \le 50\%$. If efficiency exceeds $50\%$, the screw is overhauling!

[!TRAP] Maximum Efficiency Formula: $\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1-\sin\phi}{1+\sin\phi}$. Do not confuse with $\frac{1-\cos\phi}{1+\cos\phi}$ or $\frac{1-\tan\phi}{1+\tan\phi}$!

2. Decision Tree
Power Screw / Screw Jack Check?
├─ Is friction angle phi >= helix angle alpha? → SELF-LOCKING (Efficiency <= 50%)
├─ Is friction angle phi < helix angle alpha? → OVERHAULING (Efficiency > 50%)
├─ What is effort to RAISE load W? → P = W * tan(alpha + phi)
├─ What is effort to LOWER load W? → P = W * tan(phi - alpha)
├─ Maximum efficiency formula? → eta_max = (1 - sin(phi)) / (1 + sin(phi))
└─ Optimum helix angle for max efficiency? → alpha = 45 deg - phi/2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Self-locking: $\phi \ge \alpha \implies \eta \le 50\%$.
  • [ ] Raising effort: $P = W \tan(\alpha+\phi)$.
  • [ ] Lowering effort: $P = W \tan(\phi-\alpha)$.
  • [ ] Max efficiency: $\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1-\sin\phi}{1+\sin\phi}$.
  • [ ] Cup prevents load rotation.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A screw jack has a friction angle $\phi = 30^\circ$. Calculate the maximum possible efficiency of the screw jack. - Solution: - Apply maximum efficiency formula:

$${\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi}}$$
- Substitute $\phi = 30^\circ \implies \sin 30^\circ = 0.5$:
$${\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1 - 0.5}{1 + 0.5} = \frac{0.5}{1.5} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 33.33\%}$$
- Key Insight: $\eta_{\text{max}} = 33.33\%$. Note that since $\eta_{\text{max}} < 50\%$, the screw operates in the self-locking range.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A square-threaded power screw has a mean diameter $d_m = 50 \text{ mm}$ and pitch $p = 10 \text{ mm}$. The coefficient of friction is $\mu = 0.15$. Determine the effort required at the mean radius to raise a load of $20 \text{ kN}$. - Solution: - Calculate helix angle $\alpha$:

$${\tan\alpha = \frac{p}{\pi d_m} = \frac{10}{\pi \times 50} = \frac{10}{157.08} \approx 0.06366 \implies \alpha \approx 3.64^\circ}$$
- Calculate friction angle $\phi$:
$${\tan\phi = \mu = 0.15 \implies \phi \approx 8.53^\circ}$$
- Calculate $\tan(\alpha + \phi)$:
$${\tan(\alpha + \phi) = \frac{\tan\alpha + \tan\phi}{1 - \tan\alpha \tan\phi} = \frac{0.06366 + 0.15}{1 - (0.06366 \times 0.15)} = \frac{0.21366}{0.99045} \approx 0.2157}$$
- Calculate effort $P$:
$${P = W \cdot \tan(\alpha + \phi) = 20000 \times 0.2157 = 4314 \text{ N} = 4.314 \text{ kN}}$$
- Key Insight: $P \approx 4.314 \text{ kN}$. Use $\tan(\alpha+\phi) = \frac{\tan\alpha+\mu}{1-\mu\tan\alpha}$.

3.3 Bolted Joints: Tensile Design, Uniform Strength & Resilience

1. Concept Pointers
  • Tensile Failure & Core Area:
  • Bolts under pure tension fail at the root of the threads (core diameter $d_c$).
  • Design formula: $P = \frac{\pi}{4} d_c^2 \sigma_t \implies d_c = \sqrt{\frac{4P}{\pi \sigma_t}}$.
  • Bolt of Uniform Strength (Resilience):
  • Standard bolts have a shank diameter $d$ larger than core diameter $d_c$. Under impact loads, high stress concentrates at the threaded portion while shank stress remains low, resulting in low strain energy absorption.
  • A Bolt of Uniform Strength is created by reducing the unthreaded shank diameter to equal the core diameter $d_c$ (or drilling a axial hole through the shank).
  • Stress becomes uniform along the entire length $\implies$ strain energy (resilience $U = \frac{\sigma^2 V}{2E}$) increases drastically, protecting the bolt against shock failure!
  • Washers:
  • A washer under the nut distributes the clamping load over a larger bearing area and prevents surface galling/damage.
  • Tightening Torque Components:
  • Total tightening torque $T = T_{\text{thread friction}} + T_{\text{collar friction}} + T_{\text{preload}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Bolt of Uniform Strength: Reducing the unthreaded shank diameter to equal thread core diameter $d_c$ makes stress uniform throughout the length, maximizing shock energy absorption (resilience).

[!TRAP] Bolt Core Diameter Area: Core diameter $d_c \approx 0.84 d$ (major diameter). Always use core area $A_c = \frac{\pi}{4} d_c^2$ for tensile strength calculations, NOT major diameter area!

2. Decision Tree
Bolted Joint Design Check?
├─ Is design for tensile load P? → Core diameter dc = sqrt(4 * P / (pi * sigma_t))
├─ Purpose of reducing shank diameter to core diameter? → BOLT OF UNIFORM STRENGTH (Max Resilience)
├─ Purpose of Washer under nut? → Distribute load / provide bearing area / protect surface
└─ Tightening torque components? → Thread friction + Collar friction + Preload torque
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Tensile design uses core diameter $d_c$.
  • [ ] Uniform strength bolt: shank diameter $= d_c$.
  • [ ] Uniform strength increases shock resilience $U = \frac{\sigma^2 V}{2E}$.
  • [ ] Washer provides bearing area.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: An eye bolt is subjected to an axial lifting load $P = 70 \text{ kN}$. The allowable tensile stress of the bolt material is $\sigma_t = 100 \text{ MPa}$. Calculate the required core diameter $d_c$ of the bolt. - Solution: - Apply core area tensile formula:

$${P = \frac{\pi}{4} d_c^2 \cdot \sigma_t \implies 70000 = \frac{\pi}{4} d_c^2 \times 100}$$
$${d_c^2 = \frac{70000 \times 4}{100 \pi} = \frac{2800}{\pi} \approx 891.27 \implies d_c = \sqrt{891.27} \approx 29.85 \text{ mm}}$$
- Standard core diameter selection: $d_c \approx 30 \text{ mm}$. - Key Insight: $d_c \approx 30 \text{ mm}$. Tensile calculation strictly uses thread core area.

3.4 Special Threaded Elements

1. Concept Pointers
  • Stud: A cylindrical rod threaded at both ends with no head. One end is permanently screwed into a tapped hole in a body; the other end receives a nut.
  • Set Screw: A headless screw used to prevent relative motion between two machine parts (such as a pulley hub and shaft) by driving its point against the inner part (compressive friction holding).
  • Turnbuckle: A coupling device with a central sleeve/coupler containing a left-hand thread on one end and a right-hand thread on the other end. Rotating the coupler adjusts rod length and tension without turning the connected rods.
  • Eye Bolt: A bolt with a circular loop/eye head used for lifting and transporting heavy machinery.
  • Thread Stripping Failure: Prevented by ensuring adequate engaged thread length $L_e \ge 0.8 d - 1.0 d$ in the nut to withstand direct shear.
💡 Key Concept: Turnbuckle Thread Orientation: A turnbuckle coupler MUST have a left-hand thread on one end and a right-hand thread on the other end so that turning the sleeve pulls both connected rods together or pushes them apart.

[!TRAP] Stud vs Bolt: A stud has NO head! It is threaded on both ends.

2. Decision Tree
Special Threaded Fastener Check?
├─ Fastener threaded at both ends with NO head? → STUD
├─ Headless screw producing compressive holding to prevent relative motion? → SET SCREW
├─ Coupler with Left-Hand & Right-Hand threads for tension adjustment? → TURNBUCKLE
└─ Bolt with circular loop head for lifting heavy loads? → EYE BOLT
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Stud: threaded both ends, no head.
  • [ ] Set screw: headless, compressive holding.
  • [ ] Turnbuckle: left-hand + right-hand threads.
  • [ ] Eye bolt: lifting heavy loads.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Explain why turnbuckles are constructed with opposite-hand threads on their two ends. - Solution: - Having a left-hand thread on one end and a right-hand thread on the other end allows the central coupler to adjust cable/rod tension when rotated in one direction, without needing to rotate the attached long cables or rods. - Key Insight: Opposite thread hands enable pure axial length/tension adjustment upon coupler rotation.

3.5 Welded Joints: Fillet & Butt Welds

1. Concept Pointers
  • Fillet Weld Geometry & Weakest Section:
  • Throat thickness: $t = 0.707 h = h \sin 45^\circ$ ($h$ = leg size).
  • Throat is the WEAKEST SECTION of a fillet weld, failing under shear.
  • Parallel vs Transverse Fillet Welds:
  • Parallel Fillet Weld: Weld axis parallel to load direction. Subjected to pure shear along throat $P = 2 \times 0.707 h \ell \tau$.
  • Transverse Fillet Weld: Weld axis perpendicular to load direction. Subjected to combined tensile and shear stress. Fails in shear at throat plane, but is $1.17\times$ stronger in shear than a parallel fillet weld.
  • Butt Welded Joints:
  • Size of weld = throat thickness = plate thickness $t$.
  • Tensile strength: $P = \sigma_t \cdot t \cdot L \cdot \eta$.
  • Full penetration butt welds have no throat reduction factor $t = \text{plate thickness}$.
  • Backing rings are inserted inside pipes during butt welding to ensure proper root penetration and alignment.
💡 Key Concept: Fillet Weld Throat Rule: Fillet weld throat thickness is $t = 0.707 h$. All fillet welds (parallel or transverse) are designed based on SHEAR FAILURE across this minimum throat area!

[!TRAP] Butt Weld vs Fillet Weld Size: - Fillet weld size $= h$; Throat $= 0.707 h$. - Butt weld size $= \text{Throat} = \text{Plate thickness } t$ (no $0.707$ multiplier!).

2. Decision Tree
Welded Joint Design Check?
├─ Fillet weld throat thickness formula? → t = 0.707 * h
├─ Weakest plane in fillet weld? → THROAT (Fails in Shear)
├─ Double parallel fillet weld shear load P? → P = 2 * (0.707 * h * L) * tau
├─ Transverse vs Parallel shear strength ratio? → Transverse is 1.17x stronger
└─ Butt weld throat thickness? → Equals full plate thickness t
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Fillet throat $t = 0.707 h$.
  • [ ] Weakest section: throat (shear failure).
  • [ ] Double parallel fillet: $P = 1.414 h \ell \tau$.
  • [ ] Transverse fillet $1.17\times$ stronger than parallel.
  • [ ] Butt weld throat $= t$ (plate thickness).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Two steel plates of width $100 \text{ mm}$ and thickness $10 \text{ mm}$ are joined by double parallel fillet welds of leg size $h = 10 \text{ mm}$. If the permissible shear stress is $\tau = 55 \text{ MPa}$, calculate the required length of each weld to transmit a static load of $77 \text{ kN}$. - Solution: - Formula for double parallel fillet weld strength:

$${P = 2 \times (0.707 \cdot h \cdot \ell) \cdot \tau}$$
- Substitute given values ($P = 77000 \text{ N}$, $h = 10 \text{ mm}$, $\tau = 55 \text{ MPa}$):
$${77000 = 2 \times 0.707 \times 10 \times \ell \times 55}$$
$${77000 = 777.7 \cdot \ell \implies \ell = \frac{77000}{777.7} \approx 99.01 \text{ mm} \approx 100 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: $\ell \approx 100 \text{ mm}$. Double parallel weld uses factor of 2 with $0.707 h \ell \tau$.

3.6 Riveted Joints: Design, Failures & Leak-Proofing

1. Concept Pointers
  • Permanent Fastening Classification:
  • Riveted and welded joints are PERMANENT FASTENINGS (cannot be disassembled without destroying rivets or plates). Bolted joints are temporary.
  • Riveted Joint Failure Modes:
  • Tearing of plate at margin (prevented by keeping margin $m \ge 1.5d$).
  • Tearing of plate across rivet row $P_t = (p-d)t\sigma_t$.
  • Shearing of rivets ($P_s = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2 \tau$ single shear, $2 \times \frac{\pi}{4}d^2 \tau$ double shear).
  • Crushing of rivets/plates ($P_c = d t \sigma_c$, hole deforms into oval shape).
  • Unwin's Formula:
  • Used to find rivet hole diameter $d$ from plate thickness $t$: $d = 6\sqrt{t}$ ($d$ and $t$ in mm).
  • Tearing Efficiency $\eta_t$:
    $${\eta_t = \frac{p - d}{p} = 1 - \frac{d}{p}}$$
  • High-yield relation: If $\eta_t = 75\% \implies \frac{d}{p} = 0.25$.
  • Caulking & Fullering:
  • Post-riveting cold-hammering operations using narrow chisel-like tools to pack plate edges and rivet heads tightly against each other.
  • Done exclusively to make riveted joints LEAK-PROOF in boilers, pressure vessels, and tanks.
💡 Key Concept: Unwin's Formula & Leak-Proofing: - Rivet diameter $d = 6\sqrt{t}$ (mm). - Caulking and Fullering are performed ONLY to make riveted boiler joints LEAK-PROOF.

[!TRAP] Tearing Efficiency Ratio: $\eta_t = \frac{p-d}{p}$. If efficiency is $75\%$, then $d/p = 0.25$, NOT $0.75$!

2. Decision Tree
Riveted Joint Design & Failure Check?
├─ Is riveting permanent or temporary? → PERMANENT FASTENING
├─ Rivet hole diameter from plate thickness t? → UNWIN'S FORMULA: d = 6 * sqrt(t)
├─ Margin to prevent edge tearing? → m = 1.5 * d
├─ Tearing efficiency formula? → eta_t = (p - d) / p
├─ If tearing efficiency is 75%, what is d/p? → d/p = 0.25

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A riveted joint has a tearing efficiency of $75\%$. Determine the ratio of rivet hole diameter to pitch $d/p$. - Solution: - Tearing efficiency formula:

$${\eta_t = \frac{p - d}{p} = 1 - \frac{d}{p}}$$
- Substitute $\eta_t = 0.75$:
$${0.75 = 1 - \frac{d}{p} \implies \frac{d}{p} = 1 - 0.75 = 0.25}$$
- Key Insight: $d/p = 0.25$. Pitch $p = 4d$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Calculate the rivet hole diameter for a boiler joint connecting two steel plates of thickness $t = 9 \text{ mm}$ using Unwin's formula. - Solution: - Apply Unwin's formula ($t$ in mm):

$${d = 6\sqrt{t} = 6\sqrt{9} = 6 \times 3 = 18 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: $d = 18 \text{ mm}$. Direct application of $d = 6\sqrt{t}$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is the included thread angle of an Acme thread? Where is it used?
A: $29^\circ$ included angle ($14.5^\circ$ flank angle). Used in lathe lead screws and screw jacks due to ease of cutting and split-nut engagement.

Q2: Which thread form is designed for transmitting heavy power in ONE DIRECTION ONLY?
A: Buttress thread (asymmetric profile: $45^\circ$ one side, $7^\circ$ other).

Q3: State the condition for self-locking in a square-threaded power screw.
A: Friction angle $\phi \ge \text{helix angle } \alpha$ $\mu \ge \tan\alpha$.

Q4: What is the maximum efficiency limit of any self-locking power screw?
A: Maximum efficiency $\eta \le 50\%$.

Q5: Write the formula for maximum efficiency of a screw jack.
A: $\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi}$, occurring at helix angle $\alpha = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}$.

Q6: What is the function of the cup at the top of a screw jack?
A: To support the load and prevent the load from rotating along with the screw.

Q7: How does a Bolt of Uniform Strength increase shock resilience?
A: By reducing the unthreaded shank diameter to equal the thread core diameter $d_c$, distributing stress uniformly along its entire length and maximizing strain energy absorption $U = \frac{\sigma^2 V}{2E}$.

Q8: Describe the thread arrangement in a Turnbuckle coupler.
A: It has a left-hand thread on one end and a right-hand thread on the other end to allow length and tension adjustment without turning the connected rods.

Q9: What is a Stud?
A: A headless cylindrical rod threaded at both ends; one end is screwed into a tapped component hole, and the other receives a nut.

Q10: What is the throat thickness $t$ of a fillet weld of leg size $h$?
A: $t = 0.707 h = h \sin 45^\circ$.

Q11: Which section is the weakest in a fillet weld, and how does it fail?
A: The throat section is the weakest section and fails under shear stress.

Q12: State Unwin's formula for rivet hole diameter.
A: $d = 6\sqrt{t}$ (where $d$ and $t$ are in mm).

Q13: What is the margin $m$ in a riveted joint and what failure does it prevent?
A: $m = 1.5d$. It prevents tearing of the plate at the edge.

Q14: Write the formula for tearing efficiency $\eta_t$ of a riveted joint.
A: $\eta_t = \frac{p - d}{p} = 1 - \frac{d}{p}$.

Q15: What is the primary purpose of Caulking and Fullering in riveted boiler joints?
A: To press plate margins and rivet heads tightly together to make the joint LEAK-PROOF.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES & HIGH-YIELD EXAM TRAPS

  • Thread Profiles:
  • Metric: M $d \times p$ ($60^\circ$ V-thread, fastening only).
  • Square: $0^\circ$ flank angle, max efficiency, power in both directions.
  • Acme: $29^\circ$ included angle ($14.5^\circ$ flank angle), lead screws.
  • Buttress: asymmetric $45^\circ/7^\circ$, power in ONE direction only.
  • Power Screws & Screw Jacks:
  • Self-locking: $\phi \ge \alpha \implies \mu \ge \tan\alpha$. Efficiency $\eta \le 50\%$.
  • Overhauling: $\phi < \alpha \implies \eta > 50\%$.
  • Raising effort: $P = W \tan(\alpha+\phi)$; Torque $T = \frac{W d_m}{2}\tan(\alpha+\phi)$.
  • Lowering effort: $P = W \tan(\phi-\alpha)$.
  • Max efficiency: $\eta_{\text{max}} = \frac{1-\sin\phi}{1+\sin\phi}$ at $\alpha = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}$.
  • Cup prevents load rotation. V-thread equivalent friction $\mu' = \frac{\mu}{\cos(\beta/2)}$.
  • Bolted Joints:
  • Tensile core diameter: $d_c = \sqrt{\frac{4P}{\pi \sigma_t}}$.
  • Uniform strength bolt: shank diameter $= d_c \implies$ uniform stress $\implies$ max shock resilience $U = \frac{\sigma^2 V}{2E}$.
  • Washer provides bearing area. Stud has no head (threaded both ends). Turnbuckle has L-hand & R-hand threads.
  • Welded Joints:
  • Fillet throat thickness $t = 0.707 h$. Weakest section (fails in shear).
  • Double parallel fillet shear strength: $P = 2(0.707 h \ell)\tau = 1.414 h \ell \tau$.
  • Transverse fillet is $1.17\times$ stronger in shear than parallel fillet.
  • Butt weld throat $= t$ (plate thickness). Backing rings used inside pipes for penetration.
  • Riveted Joints: Permanent fastening.
  • Unwin formula: $d = 6\sqrt{t}$ mm. Margin $m = 1.5d$.
  • Tearing efficiency: $\eta_t = \frac{p-d}{p}$. $75\%$ efficiency $\implies \frac{d}{p} = 0.25$.
  • Caulking & Fullering: make joints LEAK-PROOF for boilers.
