Design of Joints & Screws

Power Screws · Bolted Joints · Welded & Riveted Joints  ·  APGENCO AE | SSC JE Mechanical

1. Thread Profiles & Designations

Thread profile determines the thread's suitability for power transmission, fastening, or sealing. The four standard profiles tested in APGENCO/SSC JE are characterised by their flank angle.

Thread TypeFlank angle (half)Primary useKey property
Square$0^\circ$Power transmission (screw jacks, lead screws)Highest efficiency; no radial thrust on nut
Acme$14.5^\circ$ (total $29^\circ$)Lead screws, lathe feed screwsStronger than square; permits split nut disengagement
ButtressAsymmetric ($3^\circ/45^\circ$)One-direction power (breech mechanisms, jacks)High efficiency in one direction only
V-thread (metric)$30^\circ$ (total $60^\circ$)FasteningSelf-locking; high friction; equivalent $\mu' = \mu/\cos 30^\circ$

Metric designation: M$d \times p$ where $d$ is nominal (major) diameter in mm and $p$ is pitch in mm. Example: M20 × 2.5 means 20 mm major diameter, 2.5 mm pitch.

Multi-start threads: Lead $L = n \times p$, where $n$ is the number of starts.

$$\boxed{L = n \cdot p}$$

2. Power Screws & Screw Jacks

A power screw converts rotary motion into linear axial force. Helix angle $\alpha$ relates lead to mean diameter:

$$\tan\alpha = \frac{L}{\pi d_m}$$
$d_m$ — mean thread diameter $= d - 0.5p$, mm

Raising and Lowering Loads — Square Thread

$$\boxed{P_\text{raise} = W\tan(\alpha + \phi)} \qquad \boxed{T_\text{raise} = \frac{W\,d_m}{2}\tan(\alpha+\phi)}$$
$$\boxed{P_\text{lower} = W\tan(\phi - \alpha)} \qquad \boxed{T_\text{lower} = \frac{W\,d_m}{2}\tan(\phi-\alpha)}$$
$W$ — axial load, N $\phi$ — friction angle, $\mu = \tan\phi$ $\alpha$ — helix angle

Self-Locking Condition

A screw is self-locking when the load cannot lower itself without applied torque ($\phi \ge \alpha$):

$$\boxed{\phi \ge \alpha \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \mu \ge \tan\alpha \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \eta \le 50\%}$$
⚠️ Exam Trap: If $\eta > 50\%$, the screw is overhauling — the load will lower itself without applied torque.

Screw Jack Efficiency

$$\boxed{\eta = \frac{\tan\alpha}{\tan(\alpha+\phi)}}$$

Maximum Efficiency of a Screw Jack

$$\boxed{\eta_\text{max} = \frac{1-\sin\phi}{1+\sin\phi}} \qquad \text{at} \quad \alpha = 45^\circ - \frac{\phi}{2}$$

V-Thread Equivalent Friction

$$\boxed{\mu' = \frac{\mu}{\cos(\beta/2)} = \frac{\mu}{\cos 30^\circ} = \frac{\mu}{0.866}}$$

3. Bolted Joints

Bolts loaded in direct tension are designed based on the core (root, minor) diameter $d_c$:

$$\boxed{d_c = \sqrt{\frac{4P}{\pi\,\sigma_t}}}$$

Bolt of Uniform Strength

A standard bolt has shank diameter $d > d_c$. Under impact loads, stress concentrations at thread roots cause failure. A bolt of uniform strength has its shank diameter reduced to core diameter $d_c$ along the unthreaded grip length, maximising resilience:

$$\boxed{U = \frac{\sigma^2 V}{2E}}$$

4. Welded Joints

Two fundamental weld types: fillet welds and butt welds.

Fillet Weld — Throat Thickness

$$\boxed{t = 0.707\,h = h\sin 45^\circ}$$
$h$ — leg size of fillet weld, mm; throat $t$ is always the weakest section

Double Parallel Fillet Weld — Shear Strength

$$\boxed{P = 2\,(0.707\,h\,\ell)\,\tau}$$
💡 Transverse Fillet Weld: A transverse fillet weld is $1.17\times$ stronger in shear than a parallel fillet weld of the same size and length.

Butt Weld — Tensile Strength

$$\boxed{P = \sigma_t \cdot t \cdot L \cdot \eta_w}$$
$t$ — plate thickness = weld throat (full penetration), mm

5. Riveted Joints

Unwin's Formula & Margin

$$\boxed{d = 6\sqrt{t} \quad \text{mm}} \qquad \boxed{m = 1.5\,d}$$

Failure Modes & Strengths per Pitch

Failure modeFormula (per pitch length)Variables
Plate tearing (tension)$P_t = (p-d)\,t\,\sigma_t$$p$ = pitch; $\sigma_t$ = tensile strength
Rivet shearing (single shear)$P_s = \frac{\pi}{4}d^2\,\tau$$\tau$ = shear strength
Rivet crushing (bearing)$P_c = d\,t\,\sigma_c$$\sigma_c$ = crushing strength

Tearing Efficiency of Plate

$$\boxed{\eta_t = \frac{p-d}{p}}$$

If $\eta_t = 75\% \Rightarrow d/p = 0.25$, meaning rivet hole diameter is $25\%$ of pitch.

Leak-proofing in boilers: Caulking and fullering are cold working hammering operations used to make riveted boiler joints pressure-tight.

📌 Quick-Fire Exam Checklist
  • Square thread: $0^\circ$ flank, highest efficiency. Acme: $29^\circ$ total, lead screws. V-thread (metric): $60^\circ$ total, fastening.
  • $L = n \times p$ (multi-start thread).
  • $\tan\alpha = L/(\pi d_m)$; $d_m = d - 0.5p$.
  • Raise: $P = W\tan(\alpha+\phi)$; Lower: $P = W\tan(\phi-\alpha)$.
  • Self-locking: $\phi \ge \alpha$, equivalently $\eta \le 50\%$.
  • Screw jack $\eta = \tan\alpha/\tan(\alpha+\phi)$; $\eta_\text{max} = (1-\sin\phi)/(1+\sin\phi)$.
  • V-thread: $\mu' = \mu/\cos 30^\circ$; higher effective friction than square thread.
  • Bolt core: $d_c = \sqrt{4P/(\pi\sigma_t)}$.
  • Bolt of uniform strength: shank reduced to $d_c$; maximises resilience $U = \sigma^2 V/(2E)$.
  • Fillet weld throat: $t = 0.707h$; throat is the weakest section.
  • Transverse fillet $1.17\times$ stronger in shear than parallel fillet (same size).
  • Butt weld: throat = plate thickness (full penetration).
  • Unwin's formula: $d = 6\sqrt{t}$; margin: $m = 1.5d$.
  • Tearing efficiency: $\eta_t = (p-d)/p$; $\eta_t = 75\% \Rightarrow d/p = 0.25$.
  • Caulking and fullering: leak-proofing for boiler/pressure vessel riveted joints.
