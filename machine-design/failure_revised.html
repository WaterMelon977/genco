Static & Dynamic Loading, Failure Theories & Fatigue

S-N Diagram · Stress Concentration · Endurance Limit · Fluctuating Load Criteria  ·  APGENCO AE | SSC JE Mechanical

1. Stress Concentration

Any geometric discontinuity in a machine member — a hole, fillet, notch, keyway, or groove — produces a localised elevation of stress far above the average stress on the net cross-section. This phenomenon is stress concentration. The theoretical stress concentration factor $K_t$ quantifies this amplification.

$$\boxed{K_t = \frac{\sigma_\text{max}}{\sigma_\text{nom}}}$$
$\sigma_\text{max}$ — peak local stress at the discontinuity, MPa $\sigma_\text{nom}$ — nominal stress on net cross-section, MPa $K_t \ge 1$ always ($K_t = 1$ for smooth uniform bar)

For a small circular hole in a large flat plate under uniaxial tension, the stress concentration factor is exactly $K_t = 3.0$. The maximum stress occurs at the edge of the hole transverse to the load direction.

⚠️ Exam Trap: Under static loading, $K_t$ is ignored for ductile materials (local yielding redistributes stress harmlessly), but MUST be applied for brittle materials. Under fatigue loading, $K_f$ must ALWAYS be applied regardless of material ductility!

2. Static Failure Theories

Static failure theories predict the onset of yielding or fracture in a member subjected to combined multiaxial stresses by comparing an equivalent uniaxial stress to material tensile properties.

💡 Pure Shear Strengths: Von Mises theory predicts pure shear yield strength $S_{sy} = 0.577\,S_{yt}$, whereas Tresca theory predicts $S_{sy} = 0.5\,S_{yt}$.

3. Fatigue & the S-N Diagram

Fatigue failure is fracture under repeated or fluctuating stress below the material's ultimate tensile strength. It proceeds in three stages: crack initiation at a surface defect, stable crack propagation under cyclic loading, and sudden catastrophic final fracture.

The S-N diagram (Wöhler diagram) plots fatigue strength $S_f$ vs number of cycles $N$ to failure on log-log scales:

  • Low-cycle fatigue (LCF): $N < 10^3$ cycles. Stresses exceed yield point; governed by cyclic plastic strain.
  • High-cycle fatigue (HCF): $N \ge 10^3$ cycles. Stresses remain within elastic range.
  • Endurance Limit Asymptote ($S_e'$): For ferrous metals (steels), the S-N curve becomes horizontal at $N = 10^6$ cycles. Non-ferrous metals (aluminium, copper) do NOT exhibit a true endurance limit.
⚠️ Exam Trap: Fatigue cracks almost always initiate on the component surface. Surface finish, scratches, and surface treatments directly govern the endurance limit.

4. Endurance Limit & Modifying Factors

The endurance limit of a standard polished rotating-beam specimen $S_e'$ is approximately half the ultimate strength for steel:

$$\boxed{S_e' \approx 0.5\,S_{ut} \quad \text{(steel, } S_{ut} \le 1400\text{ MPa)}}$$

The actual component endurance limit $S_e$ incorporates Marin modifying factors:

$$\boxed{S_e = k_a \cdot k_b \cdot k_c \cdot k_d \cdot k_e \cdot S_e'}$$
$k_a$ — surface finish factor $k_b$ — size factor ($k_b \downarrow$ as size $\uparrow$) $k_c$ — reliability factor $k_d$ — loading factor ($1.0$ bending; $0.85$ axial; $0.59$ torsion) $k_e$ — miscellaneous (temperature, corrosion)

Notch Sensitivity & Fatigue SCF

$$\boxed{q = \frac{K_f - 1}{K_t - 1}} \qquad \boxed{K_f = 1 + q(K_t - 1)}$$
$q = 0$: notch-insensitive ($K_f = 1$, e.g. grey cast iron) $q = 1$: fully notch-sensitive ($K_f = K_t$, high-strength steels)

Fatigue Life Improvement

Processes that introduce compressive residual stress at the surface delay crack initiation: shot peening, surface polishing, case hardening (carburising/nitriding), and cold rolling of fillets.

5. Design Criteria for Fluctuating Loads

For stresses fluctuating between $\sigma_\text{max}$ and $\sigma_\text{min}$:

$$\boxed{\sigma_m = \frac{\sigma_\text{max} + \sigma_\text{min}}{2}} \qquad \boxed{\sigma_a = \frac{\sigma_\text{max} - \sigma_\text{min}}{2}}$$

Gerber$\frac{\sigma_a}{S_e} + \left(\frac{\sigma_m}{S_{ut}}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{N}$$S_{ut}$ (Parabolic)Least conservative
💡 Completely Reversed Load ($\sigma_m = 0$): All three fluctuating criteria simplify to $\sigma_a = \frac{S_e}{N}$.

6. Griffith Brittle Fracture Theory

Griffith's theory predicts brittle fracture when pre-existing cracks release strain energy equal to the surface energy of new crack faces:

$$\boxed{\sigma_f = \sqrt{\frac{2\,E\,\gamma}{\pi\,c}}}$$
$\sigma_f$ — nominal fracture stress, MPa $E$ — Young's modulus, MPa $\gamma$ — surface energy per unit area, J/m² $c$ — crack half-length, m ($\sigma_f \downarrow$ as $c \uparrow$)
📌 Quick-Fire Exam Checklist
  • $K_t = \sigma_\text{max}/\sigma_\text{nom}$; circular hole in infinite plate: $K_t = 3.0$.
  • Static loading: $K_t$ ignored for ductile (yielding redistributes), applied for brittle materials.
  • Fatigue: $K_f$ always applied regardless of ductility.
  • $K_f = 1 + q(K_t-1)$; $q = 0$: insensitive (cast iron); $q = 1$: fully sensitive (high-strength steel).
  • Steel endurance limit: $S_e' \approx 0.5\,S_{ut}$; occurs at $N = 10^6$ cycles.
  • Non-ferrous metals (Al, Cu): no true endurance limit — S-N keeps declining.
  • Loading factor $k_d$: bending $= 1.0$; axial $= 0.85$; torsion $= 0.59$.
  • $\sigma_m = (\sigma_\text{max}+\sigma_\text{min})/2$; $\sigma_a = (\sigma_\text{max}-\sigma_\text{min})/2$.
  • Soderberg: $\sigma_a/S_e + \sigma_m/S_{yt} = 1/N$ — most conservative.
  • Goodman: $\sigma_a/S_e + \sigma_m/S_{ut} = 1/N$ — standard design criterion.
  • Gerber: parabolic — fits experiment best but non-conservative.
  • Shot peening, surface polishing, case hardening: improve fatigue life via compressive residual stress.
  • Fatigue cracks initiate at surfaces — surface finish critically affects $S_e$.
  • Griffith: $\sigma_f = \sqrt{2E\gamma/\pi c}$; $\sigma_f \downarrow$ as crack length $\uparrow$.
  • Von Mises: $S_{sy} = 0.577\,S_{yt}$; Tresca: $S_{sy} = 0.5\,S_{yt}$.
