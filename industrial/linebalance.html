2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Assembly Line Balancing: Problem of assigning tasks to a series of workstations such that precedence constraints are satisfied and workstation idle time is minimised.
  • Line Balance Condition: A line is balanced when every workstation has (approximately) equal operation time.
  • Pacing Bottleneck: The overall production rate of an unbalanced line is governed by the slowest station (station with maximum time).
  • Cycle Time (CCC): Maximum allowable time spent at each workstation to meet desired output rate (C=T/DC = T/DC=T/D). Must satisfy C≥tmax⁡C \ge t_{\max}C≥tmax​ (longest single task time).
  • Theoretical Minimum Workstations (Nmin⁡N_{\min}Nmin​): Lower bound on required stations, calculated as ⌈∑ti/C⌉\lceil \sum t_i / C \rceil⌈∑ti​/C⌉.
  • Line Efficiency (η\etaη): Ratio of total work content to total available station time (η=∑tinC×100%\eta = \frac{\sum t_i}{n C} \times 100\%η=nC∑ti​​×100%).
  • Balance Delay (Balance Loss): Percentage of idle time on the assembly line (Balance Delay=100%−η\text{Balance Delay} = 100\% - \etaBalance Delay=100%−η).
  • Shop Loading: Function of assigning specific jobs or operations to work centers/machines. Answers “Which machine?” without sequencing.
  • Scheduling: Function of prescribing the exact sequence and start/finish times of jobs on machines. Answers “When?”.
  • Shortest Processing Time (SPT): Priority rule sorting jobs by ascending processing time. Minimises mean flow time and mean WIP inventory.
  • Earliest Due Date (EDD): Priority rule sorting jobs by ascending due date. Minimises maximum tardiness.
  • Flow Time: Total time a job spends in the plant. Flow time is NOT related to due dates.
  • Johnson’s Rule: Algorithmic procedure to find the optimal job sequence for nnn jobs on 2 machines (A→BA \to BA→B) to minimise total makespan.
  • Dispatching: Operational release of authorized work orders to the shop floor (issuing material, drawings, and tools).

3.1 — Assembly Line Balancing

1. Concept Pointers
  • Objective: Assign work elements to workstations to equalize workload, minimize total idle time, maintain required output rate, and reduce WIP inventory.
  • Bottleneck Rule: The overall production rate of an assembly line is dictated by the slowest workstation (station with the maximum cycle time).
  • Precedence Constraints: Task AAA must precede Task BBB if specified in the precedence diagram; tasks assigned to a station must satisfy all prior precedence dependencies.
  • Cycle Time (CCC):
    • C = \frac{\text{Effective Available Production Time (T)}}{\text{Desired Output Quantity (D)}}.
    • Constraint: Cycle time can NEVER be less than the longest individual task time (C≥tmax⁡C \ge t_{\max}C≥tmax​).
  • Theoretical Minimum Workstations (Nmin⁡N_{\min}Nmin​):
    • Nmin⁡=⌈∑tiC⌉N_{\min} = \left\lceil \frac{\sum t_i}{C} \right\rceilNmin​=⌈C∑ti​​⌉. Always use the ceiling function (⌈… ⌉\lceil \dots \rceil⌈…⌉).
    • Actual stations nnn may be greater than Nmin⁡N_{\min}Nmin​ due to task time grouping conflicts and precedence restrictions.
  • Line Efficiency (η\etaη) & Balance Delay:
    • η=∑tin×C×100%\eta = \frac{\sum t_i}{n \times C} \times 100\%η=n×C∑ti​​×100%. Note that the denominator uses actual stations nnn, not Nmin⁡N_{\min}Nmin​.
    • Balance Delay =nC−∑tinC×100%=100%−η= \frac{n C - \sum t_i}{n C} \times 100\% = 100\% - \eta=nCnC−∑ti​​×100%=100%−η.

3.2 — Shop Loading

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition: Shop Loading is the assignment of specific jobs or tasks to work centers or processing machines.
  • Loading vs Scheduling:
    • Loading answers: “WHICH machine processes WHICH job?” (Capacity allocation).
    • Scheduling answers: “WHEN does EACH job start and finish?” (Time sequencing).
  • Objective: Balance workload across machines, avoid over-utilization or under-utilization, and verify plant capacity against demand.
  • Input & Output:
    • Inputs: Process routing plans, machine capacities, batch quantities.
    • Output: Load reports / Load charts displaying assigned workload hours versus available capacity hours.

3.3 — Scheduling & Priority Rules

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition: Scheduling establishes the exact sequence, start time, and completion time of operations on machines.
  • Common Priority Rules:
    • SPT (Shortest Processing Time): Jobs sorted in ascending order of processing time. Minimises Mean Flow Time, mean WIP inventory, and average job lateness.
    • EDD (Earliest Due Date): Jobs sorted in ascending order of due date. Minimises Maximum Lateness and Maximum Tardiness.
    • FCFS (First-Come, First-Served): Jobs processed in arrival order. Fair to customers but poor on performance metrics.
    • LPT (Longest Processing Time): Jobs sorted in descending order of processing time. Keeps large machines busy but inflates WIP.
    • Critical Ratio (CR): CR=Due Date−Current TimeRemaining Processing Time\text{CR} = \frac{\text{Due Date} - \text{Current Time}}{\text{Remaining Processing Time}}CR=Remaining Processing TimeDue Date−Current Time​. (CR<1→\text{CR} < 1 \toCR<1→ Behind schedule; CR=1→\text{CR} = 1 \toCR=1→ On schedule; CR>1→\text{CR} > 1 \toCR>1→ Ahead of schedule).
  • Key Performance Metrics:
    • Flow Time: Total elapsed time from job arrival to completion (Ci−riC_i - r_iCi​−ri​). Flow time is independent of due dates.
    • Lateness: Ci−diC_i - d_iCi​−di​ (can be positive or negative).
    • Tardiness: max⁡(0,Ci−di)\max(0, C_i - d_i)max(0,Ci​−di​) (only positive lateness; zero if completed on or before due date).

3.4 — Johnson’s Rule (nnn Jobs on 2 Machines)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Scope: Used to find the optimal job sequence for nnn jobs passing through 2 machines in a fixed technological order (M1→M2M_1 \to M_2M1​→M2​) to minimise total makespan.
  • Algorithmic Steps:
    1. List all processing times for all remaining jobs on Machine 1 and Machine 2.
    2. Find the smallest processing time among all remaining times.
    3. If the minimum time occurs on Machine 1, place that job in the earliest available slot (left-to-right).
    4. If the minimum time occurs on Machine 2, place that job in the latest available slot (right-to-left).
    5. If a tie occurs between M1M_1M1​ and M2M_2M2​, place the M1M_1M1​ job first and the M2M_2M2​ job last.
    6. Remove the scheduled job and repeat steps 2–5 until all jobs are placed.

3.5 — Dispatching

1. Concept Pointers
  • Definition: Dispatching is the operational release of authorized production orders to the shop floor.
  • Functions of Dispatcher:
    1. Release materials from stores to the first operation.
    2. Issue route sheets, job cards, drawings, and tool requisitions.
    3. Authorize movement of WIP inventory between machine stations.
    4. Record operation start and completion times.
  • PPC Hierarchy Position: Routing →\to→ Loading →\to→ Scheduling →\to→ Dispatching →\to→ Follow-up.
  • Dispatching represents the transition from planning to physical execution.
2. Worked Example

    (A) Routing
    (B) Scheduling

Note on Scheduling Rules & Due Dates

  • SPT minimises Mean Flow Time and average WIP inventory.
  • EDD minimises Maximum Lateness and Tardiness.
  • Flow Time measures total shop time and is completely independent of customer due dates.
