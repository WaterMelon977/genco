2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Statistical Process Control (SPC): Application of statistical techniques (control charts) to monitor process variability and maintain process capability. Introduced by W. A. Shewhart.
  • Chance Causes vs Assignable Causes:
    • Chance (Random) Causes: Inherent background noise/variation unavoidable in normal operations. Process remains in statistical control.
    • Assignable (Special) Causes: Specific external disruptions (tool wear, operator error, bad raw material). Causes process to become out of control.
  • Control Limits vs Specification Limits:
    • Control Limits (UCL,LCL\text{UCL}, \text{LCL}UCL,LCL): Derived from actual process data (μ±3σ\mu \pm 3\sigmaμ±3σ). Set by the process.
    • Specification Limits (USL,LSL\text{USL}, \text{LSL}USL,LSL): Set externally by product design engineers / customers.
  • Xˉ\bar{X}Xˉ-Chart: Variable control chart monitoring process central tendency (mean).
  • RRR-Chart: Variable control chart monitoring process dispersion (range/variability). Always interpreted together with Xˉ\bar{X}Xˉ-chart.
  • ppp-Chart: Attribute control chart for fraction defective (binomial distribution). Sample size nnn may vary.
  • ccc-Chart: Attribute control chart for number of defects per unit (Poisson distribution). Opportunity space / sample size must be constant.
  • Acceptance Sampling: Quality verification procedure where a sample is drawn from a lot to decide whether to accept or reject the entire lot. Used in mass production.
  • AQL (Acceptable Quality Level): Maximum percent defective that can be considered satisfactory as a process average. Represents producer’s preferred quality (Pa≈0.95P_a \approx 0.95Pa​≈0.95).
  • LTPD (Lot Tolerance Percent Defective): Poorest quality that the consumer is willing to accept in an individual lot (Pa≈0.10P_a \approx 0.10Pa​≈0.10). Represents consumer’s protection limit.
  • Producer’s Risk (α\alphaα): Probability of rejecting a good lot (Type I error). Typically α=0.05\alpha = 0.05α=0.05 (5%5\%5%).
  • Consumer’s Risk (β\betaβ): Probability of accepting a bad lot (Type II error). Typically β=0.10\beta = 0.10β=0.10 (10%10\%10%).
  • OC (Operating Characteristic) Curve: Plot of incoming lot quality (ppp) on x-axis versus probability of acceptance (PaP_aPa​) on y-axis.
  • AOQ (Average Outgoing Quality): Expected average quality of outgoing product after inspection and rectification of rejected lots.
  • AOQL: Peak point of the AOQ curve representing the worst possible average outgoing quality.
  • Six Sigma: Business management strategy targeting 3.4 DPMO3.4 \text{ DPMO}3.4 DPMO, corresponding to ±6σ\pm 6\sigma±6σ capability with a 1.5σ1.5\sigma1.5σ process mean shift.
  • W. Edwards Deming: Pioneer of modern Total Quality Management (TQM) and the PDCA cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act).

3.1 — Control Charts for Variables (Xˉ\bar{X}Xˉ and RRR Charts)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Purpose: Monitor processes where quality characteristics are measurable continuous variables (e.g., diameter, weight, tensile strength).
  • Dual Chart Requirement:
    • Xˉ\bar{X}Xˉ-chart tracks process centering / mean.
    • RRR-chart tracks process dispersion / variability.
    • Both charts MUST be evaluated together. A process is in statistical control only if points on BOTH charts lie inside control limits without non-random patterns.
  • Shewhart 3σ3\sigma3σ Control Limits:
    • UCLxˉ=xˉˉ+A2Rˉ,LCLxˉ=xˉˉ−A2Rˉ\text{UCL}_{\bar{x}} = \bar{\bar{x}} + A_2 \bar{R}, \qquad \text{LCL}_{\bar{x}} = \bar{\bar{x}} - A_2 \bar{R}UCLxˉ​=xˉˉ+A2​Rˉ,LCLxˉ​=xˉˉ−A2​Rˉ.
    • UCLR=D4Rˉ,LCLR=D3Rˉ\text{UCL}_R = D_4 \bar{R}, \qquad \text{LCL}_R = D_3 \bar{R}UCLR​=D4​Rˉ,LCLR​=D3​Rˉ.
    • For small sample sizes (n≤6n \le 6n≤6), D3=0  ⟹  LCLR=0D_3 = 0 \implies \text{LCL}_R = 0D3​=0⟹LCLR​=0.
  • Process Standard Deviation Estimate: σ^=Rˉd2\hat{\sigma} = \frac{\bar{R}}{d_2}σ^=d2​Rˉ​.

3.2 — Control Charts for Attributes (p,np,cp, np, cp,np,c Charts)

1. Concept Pointers
  • ppp-Chart (Fraction Defective):
    • Used for attribute data where items are classified as defective or non-defective (Go / No-Go).

3.3 — Acceptance Sampling, OC Curve, AOQ & ATI

1. Concept Pointers
  • Operating Characteristic (OC) Curve:
    • Plots incoming lot quality fraction defective (ppp) on x-axis vs probability of lot acceptance (PaP_aPa​) on y-axis.
    • AQL (Acceptable Quality Level): Good quality level with high acceptance probability (Pa=1−α≈0.95P_a = 1 - \alpha \approx 0.95Pa​=1−α≈0.95).
    • LTPD (Lot Tolerance Percent Defective): Poor quality level with low acceptance probability (Pa=β≈0.10P_a = \beta \approx 0.10Pa​=β≈0.10).
  • Risks & Errors:
    • Producer’s Risk (α\alphaα): Risk of rejecting a good lot of AQL quality (Type I error, 5%5\%5%).
    • Consumer’s Risk (β\betaβ): Risk of accepting a bad lot of LTPD quality (Type II error, 10%10\%10%).
  • Average Outgoing Quality (AOQ):
    • AOQ=Pa×p×N−nN≈Pa×p\text{AOQ} = P_a \times p \times \frac{N-n}{N} \approx P_a \times pAOQ=Pa​×p×NN−n​≈Pa​×p.
    • AOQL: Peak value of the AOQ curve representing the maximum possible average outgoing fraction defective.
  • Average Total Inspection (ATI):
    • Total items inspected per lot considering 100%100\%100% screening of rejected lots: ATI=n+(1−Pa)(N−n)\text{ATI} = n + (1 - P_a)(N - n)ATI=n+(1−Pa​)(N−n).

    • If Cp>1.33  ⟹  C_p > 1.33 \impliesCp​>1.33⟹ Process is capable. If Cpk=Cp  ⟹  C_{pk} = C_p \impliesCpk​=Cp​⟹ Process is perfectly centered.

SECTION 4: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

Note on Variable vs Attribute Control Charts

  • Variables Charts (Xˉ,R\bar{X}, RXˉ,R): Measure continuous variables; require smaller sample sizes (n=4 to 5n = 4 \text{ to } 5n=4 to 5).
  • Attribute Charts (p,cp, cp,c): Count discrete occurrences/defects; require larger sample sizes (n≥50n \ge 50n≥50 for ppp-chart).

Note on Producer’s vs Consumer’s Risk

  • Producer’s Risk (α\alphaα): Rejecting a good lot (PaP_aPa​ at AQL).
  • Consumer’s Risk (β\betaβ): Accepting a bad lot (PaP_aPa​ at LTPD).
