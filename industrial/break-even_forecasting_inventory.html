2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

Break-even Point (BEP): Volume where total revenue = total cost; profit = 0. Recovers both fixed and variable costs.

Contribution Margin: (s−v)(s - v)(s−v) = revenue remaining per unit after covering variable cost; covers fixed costs and generates profit.

P/V Ratio (Profit-Volume Ratio): (s−v)/s(s-v)/s(s−v)/s; measures profitability per unit of sales. Higher P/V ratio → steeper profit increase with volume.

Cost Indifference Point: Volume where total costs of two processes are equal: xindiff=(FX−FY)/(vY−vX)x_{\text{indiff}} = (F_X - F_Y)/(v_Y - v_X)xindiff​=(FX​−FY​)/(vY​−vX​).

ABC Analysis: Classifies inventory by annual consumption value (= annual units × unit cost), using Pareto’s 80-20 rule:

  • A items: 10–20% of items, 70–80% of value → strict control, frequent review, minimal safety stock
  • B items: 20–30% of items, 15–25% of value → moderate control
  • C items: 50–70% of items, 5–10% of value → loose control, bulk ordering, higher safety stock

EOQ: Optimal order quantity that minimizes total annual inventory cost (ordering + holding). At EOQ: ordering cost = holding cost.

VED Analysis: Classifies by criticality — Vital, Essential, Desirable. Used for maintenance spares.

FSN Analysis: Classifies by consumption rate — Fast-moving, Slow-moving, Non-moving. Best for retail.

GOLF Analysis: Classifies by source — Government, Open market, Local, Foreign.

Reorder Point (ROP): Stock level at which a replenishment order is triggered; = lead-time demand + safety stock.

Safety Stock (SS): Buffer stock to protect against demand or lead-time variability. Higher service level → higher SS.

Perpetual Inventory System: Continuous monitoring; reorder triggered when stock reaches minimum level (ROP). Records updated after every transaction.

Cycle Counting: Periodic physical verification of a portion of inventory items (not all at once); frequency based on ABC classification (A items counted most often).

Delphi Technique: Structured, anonymous, iterative expert opinion method with controlled feedback until consensus. Used for long-range/new-product forecasting. NOT brainstorming.

Exponential Smoothing: Weighted average method; gives exponentially decreasing weights to older data. New forecast adjusts by fraction α\alphaα of the most recent error.

Smoothing Constant (α\alphaα): Controls responsiveness. Higher α\alphaα → more responsive/sensitive to recent data. α=0\alpha = 0α=0 → forecast never updates.

MSE (Mean Squared Error): Most commonly cited forecast accuracy measure. Squares errors → penalizes large deviations more than MAD.

MAD (Mean Absolute Deviation): Average of absolute forecast errors; less sensitive to outliers than MSE.

Causal Forecasting Methods: Use cause-effect relationships (regression, econometric models). Not the same as time-series methods.

Non-Causal / Time-Series Methods: Use only past values of the variable itself (moving average, exponential smoothing). Trend-adjusted exponential smoothing is not causal.

Kendall’s Notation (a/b/c:d/e/fa/b/c:d/e/fa/b/c:d/e/f):

  • aaa = arrival process (M=Poisson, D=deterministic, G=general)
  • bbb = service process (M=exponential, D=deterministic, G=general)
  • ccc = number of parallel servers
  • ddd = queue discipline (FCFS, SIRO, LCFS)
  • eee = system capacity (∞ or finite N)
  • fff = calling population (∞ or finite)

Utilization Factor (ρ\rhoρ): Fraction of time server is busy = λ/μ\lambda/\muλ/μ. Must be < 1 for stable (steady-state) queue.

Little’s Law: L=λWL = \lambda WL=λW. Holds for virtually any queueing system in steady state. Converts between length and time measures.

Jockeying: Customer switches between parallel queues hoping for faster service.

Balking: Customer refuses to join a queue (looks too long).

Reneging: Customer leaves queue after having joined (waited too long).

3.1 — Break-even Analysis

(All formulas in Quick Reference §2.1-A)

1. Concept Pointers

  • BEP is the exact production/sales volume where total sales revenue = total cost; profit = 0 (no profit, no loss).
  • At BEP, contribution margin exactly covers all fixed costs. Every unit beyond BEP contributes directly to profit.
  • BEP recovers both fixed costs and variable costs — not fixed only, not variable only.
  • BEP is primarily a short-run CVP (Cost-Volume-Profit) analysis tool; assumes constant prices and costs within capacity.
  • BEP can be expressed in physical units (xBEP=F/(s−v)x_{\text{BEP}} = F/(s-v)xBEP​=F/(s−v)) or monetary sales value (BEP₹ = F⋅s/(s−v)F \cdot s/(s-v)F⋅s/(s−v)).
  • Graphical and algebraic approaches yield identical results.
  • Target-profit output: add desired profit PPP to numerator: xreq=(F+P)/(s−v)x_{\text{req}} = (F + P)/(s-v)xreq​=(F+P)/(s−v).
  • At a stated % of rated capacity: compute actual output = % × rated capacity, then use profit formula.
  • Indifference point between two processes (X: low F, high v; Y: high F, low v) = xindiff=(FX−FY)/(vY−vX)x_{\text{indiff}} = (F_X - F_Y)/(v_Y - v_X)xindiff​=(FX​−FY​)/(vY​−vX​). Choose process with lower total cost on each side.

2. Formulas (detailed)

Break-even Chart Rules:

  • Fixed cost line → horizontal (constant, independent of volume)
  • Total cost line → starts at FFF on y-axis; slope = vvv per unit
  • Sales revenue line → starts at origin (0,0)(0,0)(0,0); slope = sss per unit
  • BEP = intersection of Sales Revenue line and Total Cost line
  • Left of BEP (Sales < Total Cost) → Loss zone
  • Right of BEP (Sales > Total Cost) → Profit zone
  • Increasing FFF shifts total cost line up → BEP moves right (later)
  • Increasing sss steepens revenue line → BEP moves left (sooner)

Indifference Point Decision Rule:

  • At x<xindiffx < x_{\text{indiff}}x<xindiff​ → choose process with lower fixed cost (low F)
  • At x>xindiffx > x_{\text{indiff}}x>xindiff​ → choose process with lower variable cost (low v)

3.2 — Inventory Control

(All formulas in Quick Reference §2.1-B)

1. Concept Pointers

ABC Analysis:

  • Classifies by annual consumption value = annual demand (units) × unit cost (₹)
  • Rank items descending; assign A/B/C using percentage cut-offs
  • A items get strict control, frequent review, and low safety stock — NOT higher safety stock
  • C items: loose control, bulk ordering, higher safety stock acceptable
  • ABC logic: value-based (not criticality=VED, not movement=FSN)

EOQ Model:

  • Minimizes sum of annual ordering cost + annual holding cost
  • Key condition at EOQ: annual ordering cost = annual holding cost
  • Purchase cost excluded (constant regardless of Q)
  • Number of orders/year N=D/EOQN = D/\text{EOQ}N=D/EOQ; time between orders t=EOQ/Dt = \text{EOQ}/Dt=EOQ/D
  • Total cost at EOQ: TC∗=2DCoCh\text{TC}^* = \sqrt{2DC_oC_h}TC∗=2DCo​Ch​​
  • Total cost curve is U-shaped; minimum at EOQ

Holding Cost Components: Storage rent, insurance, obsolescence, capital tied up (opportunity cost)

Ordering Cost Components: Requisition, transportation, receiving, administrative processing per order

Safety Stock and ROP:

  • ROP = lead-time demand + SS
  • SS cushions against demand/lead-time variability; depends on service level desired
  • Higher service level → higher SS
  • SS (statistical) = Z⋅σdLTZ \cdot \sigma_{dLT}Z⋅σdLT​ where ZZZ from service-level table
  • SS (range method) = (Max. weekly usage × Max. LT) − (Avg. weekly usage × Avg. LT)

Perpetual Inventory: Continuous; material checked/reordered when stock reaches minimum value (ROP)

Cycle Counting: Periodic physical record verification; more frequent for A items; provides measure of inventory accuracy

JIT Philosophy: Minimizes inventory levels, especially safety stock

3.3 — Demand Forecasting

(All formulas in Quick Reference §2.1-C)

1. Concept Pointers

Delphi Technique:

  • Structured, anonymous expert consensus through iterative questionnaires with controlled feedback
  • Experts of the same rank participate; anonymity reduces bias and dominance effects
  • Continue rounds until convergence; final forecast = aggregated opinion (mean/median of final round)
  • Best for: long-range forecasting, new-product demand, technology trends — when historical data is scarce
  • NOT brainstorming (which is open discussion), not a quantitative/statistical method

Exponential Smoothing:

  • Weighted moving average giving exponentially decreasing weights to older observations
  • Ft=Ft−1+α(Dt−Ft−1)F_t = F_{t-1} + \alpha(D_t - F_{t-1})Ft​=Ft−1​+α(Dt​−Ft−1​) — adjusts forecast by fraction α\alphaα of last period’s error
  • Higher α\alphaα → more weight to recent data → higher sensitivity, lower smoothing
  • Lower α\alphaα → smoother forecast → slower response → less tracking of actual changes
  • α=0\alpha = 0α=0 → forecast never updates (stays at initial value)
  • α=1\alpha = 1α=1 → forecast = last actual demand (no smoothing at all)
  • Takes a fraction of the forecast error into account for next period — this is how it updates

Simple Moving Average (SMA):

  • Equal weights to most recent nnn observations; drops oldest when new data arrives
  • Longer nnn → lower sensitivity, more smoothing, slower response
  • Shorter nnn → higher sensitivity, better tracks turning points

Weighted Moving Average (WMA):

  • Assigns higher weights to recent periods; weights sum to 1 (or to the divisor used)
  • More responsive than SMA for same window; customizable weighting scheme

Forecast Error Measures:

  • MSE — most commonly cited in PYQs as the primary error measure; squares errors → penalizes outliers heavily
  • MAD — average absolute error; less sensitive to large outliers
  • MAPE — percentage error; good for comparing forecasts across products of different scales

Causal vs Non-Causal Methods:

  • Causal: Regression, econometric models (use independent variable as predictor) — Ŷ = a + bX
  • Non-causal / Time-series: Moving average, exponential smoothing — use only past values of the forecast variable itself
  • Trend-adjusted exponential smoothing is NOT causal
  • Long-range new-product forecasting best done with market surveys / direct survey method
  • Jury of executive opinion and sales force composite are judgmental (not causal)

Time Series Components: Trend, Seasonal, Cyclical, Irregular/Random. Basic smoothing methods assume no strong trend or seasonality.

Worked Example 3 — Method Identification MCQs

Q: Which forecasting method uses anonymous expert iterations?
Answer: Delphi Technique

Q: Which method is best for long-range new-product demand forecasting?
Answer: Market survey / direct survey method (not Delphi, not exponential smoothing)

Q: Trend-adjusted exponential smoothing is a causal method. True/False?
Answer: FALSE — It is a time-series method; it only uses past values of the variable itself.

Q: Increasing α\alphaα from 0.2 to 0.8 will:
Answer: Increase forecast sensitivity / responsiveness to recent data; reduce smoothing.

Key Insight: Exam MCQs frequently swap the effect of α\alphaα (higher α\alphaα = higher sensitivity, NOT lower). Fix this in memory.

3.4 — Queueing Theory

(All formulas in Quick Reference §2.1-D)

1. Concept Pointers

Kendall’s Notation a/b/c : d/e/f:

  • aaa = arrival process (M = Markovian/Poisson, D = deterministic, G = general)
  • bbb = service process (M = exponential, D = deterministic, G = general)
  • ccc = number of parallel servers
  • ddd = queue discipline (FCFS/FIFO, SIRO, LCFS, GD = general)
  • eee = system capacity (∞ or finite N)
  • fff = calling population size (∞ or finite)
  • Most common exam model: M/M/1 : ∞/FCFS (Poisson arrivals, exponential service, single server, infinite capacity, FCFS discipline)

Steady-State Condition (M/M/1):

  • Utilization ρ=λ/μ\rho = \lambda/\muρ=λ/μ must be < 1 (arrival rate < service rate)
  • ρ≥1\rho \ge 1ρ≥1 → queue grows without bound (no steady state)
  • P0=1−ρP_0 = 1 - \rhoP0​=1−ρ = probability server is idle
  • Pn=(1−ρ)ρnP_n = (1-\rho)\rho^nPn​=(1−ρ)ρn = probability of exactly nnn customers in system

Performance Measures:

  • LsL_sLs​ = avg. in system (queue + being served); Ls=ρ/(1−ρ)L_s = \rho/(1-\rho)Ls​=ρ/(1−ρ)
  • LqL_qLq​ = avg. in queue only; Lq=ρ2/(1−ρ)=Ls−ρL_q = \rho^2/(1-\rho) = L_s - \rhoLq​=ρ2/(1−ρ)=Ls​−ρ
  • WsW_sWs​ = avg. time in system; Ws=1/(μ−λ)W_s = 1/(\mu - \lambda)Ws​=1/(μ−λ)
  • WqW_qWq​ = avg. time in queue; Wq=λ/[μ(μ−λ)]W_q = \lambda/[\mu(\mu-\lambda)]Wq​=λ/[μ(μ−λ)]
  • All measures increase sharply as ρ→1\rho \to 1ρ→1

Little’s Law: L=λWL = \lambda WL=λW — holds for any steady-state system. Use it to convert between LLL and WWW without re-deriving.

Queue Disciplines:

  • FCFS / FIFO: First-come, first-served (standard default)
  • SIRO: Service in random order (lottery-style)
  • LCFS: Last-come, first-served
  • Pre-emptive: Higher priority customer interrupts current service (e.g., emergency cases)
  • Non-pre-emptive: Higher priority customer waits for current service to finish

Special Customer Behaviours:

  • Jockeying: Switching between parallel queues hoping for faster service
  • Balking: Refusing to join a queue (looks too long)
  • Reneging: Leaving the queue after having joined (waited too long)

Multi-Server Systems (M/M/c): Single queue feeding ccc parallel servers. Pooling (single queue + multiple servers) is always better than multiple separate queues for same total capacity — reduces mean wait and variance. A slow server in a separate-queue model blocks its entire line; pooling isolates the delay.

Multi-Phase Systems: Service consists of sequential stages; effective overall service rate adjusted accordingly.

Worked Example 2 — Kendall Notation MCQs

Q: In notation M/M/1 : ∞/FCFS, what does the ‘1’ represent?
Answer: Number of parallel servers = 1 (single server)

Q: What is the difference between c and e in Kendall notation?
Answer: ccc = number of servers; eee = system capacity (max. customers allowed in system). Confusing these is the most common MCQ trap.

Q: Pooled queue (single line for multiple servers) vs separate lines — which is better?
Answer: Pooled single queue — always lower average wait and variance; prevents one slow server from blocking its entire line.

Key Insight: Jockeying can sometimes increase overall variance even if individual customers believe it helps. Pooling is structurally superior.

SECTION 4: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

Note on BEP Short-Run Limitation:
Break-even analysis assumes linear costs and revenues valid only within existing capacity. For decisions requiring capacity expansion or major price/technology changes, long-run cost analysis is needed. See §3.1 for BEP details.

Note on EOQ in Practice:
Classic EOQ assumes instantaneous replenishment. In practice, finite production rate model (EPQ) applies when items are produced (not purchased): EPQ=2DCo/[Ch(1−d/p)]\text{EPQ} = \sqrt{2DC_o/[C_h(1-d/p)]}EPQ=2DCo​/[Ch​(1−d/p)]​ where ddd = daily demand rate, ppp = daily production rate. This variant has low PYQ representation but flag for completeness.

Note on ABC + Safety Stock Interaction:
ABC policy directly sets safety stock levels: A items → minimize SS (expensive, high supervision); C items → higher SS acceptable (cheap, loose control). See §3.2 for full ABC coverage.

Note on Exponential Smoothing and Forecasting Errors:
MSE is the primary benchmark error metric in most competitive exam PYQs. MAD is also tested. MAPE appears in some MBA-focused exams. All three computed from error et=Dt−Fte_t = D_t - F_tet​=Dt​−Ft​. See §3.3 for forecasting details.

Note on Queueing: Multi-Phase Repair
In multi-phase service (e.g., 5 sequential repair steps), the effective overall service rate must account for all phases in series. Effective μeff=1/∑(1/μi)\mu_{\text{eff}} = 1/\sum(1/\mu_i)μeff​=1/∑(1/μi​) for series stages. See §3.4 for queueing details.
