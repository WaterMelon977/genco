2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Material Requirement Planning (MRP): Technique for planning procurement of dependent demand items (raw materials, components, sub-assemblies). Ensures availability at right time and quantity.
  • Master Production Schedule (MPS): Complete timetable containing quantities and required delivery dates of all final products only.
  • Bill of Materials (BOM): Hierarchical structure showing how each final product is manufactured (components + sequence of build-up).
  • Inventory Record File: File tracking on-hand inventory, scheduled receipts, and transit inventory.
  • Routing: Determines the sequence of operations, path of work, and machines to be used (“how and where” work is done).
  • Scheduling: Assigns starting/completion time and priority to operations (“when” work is done).
  • Dispatching: Authorises and releases actual production work orders to the shop floor.
  • Follow-up (Expediting): Tracks progress and resolves bottlenecks.
  • Product Layout (Flow Shop): Machines arranged strictly in the sequence of operations for high-volume, low-variety production.
  • Process Layout (Functional): Similar machines grouped by function (e.g., all lathes together) for low-volume, high-variety production (Job Shop).
  • Cellular Layout (Group Technology): Machines grouped into cells to process a family of similar parts.
  • Fixed-Position Layout: Product remains stationary while men, machines, and materials are brought to it (e.g., ships, aircraft).
  • Material Handling: System of moving, storing, and controlling materials. Considered economic waste that cannot be eliminated, only minimised.
  • Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV): Driverless internal transport vehicle guided by magnetic tape, laser, or RFID.
  • Automated Storage/Retrieval System (AS/RS): Computer-controlled high-bay warehouse storage and retrieval system.
  • Group Technology (GT): Manufacturing philosophy grouping similar parts into families to share machines, tooling, and process plans.
  • Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS): Automated group of CNC machines linked by automated material handling (AGVs/conveyors) and central computer control.
  • Direct Numerical Control (DNC): Networked download of G-code from central computer + real-time feedback from multiple CNC machines.
  • ABC Analysis: Inventory classification by annual consumption value (A: high value, tight control; C: low value, loose control).
  • VED Analysis: Inventory classification by functional criticality (Vital, Essential, Desirable).
  • XYZ Analysis: Inventory classification by demand variability (X: stable, Y: moderate, Z: erratic).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 — Production Planning and Control (PPC) Core & MRP

1. Concept Pointers
  • PPC Sequence: Correct chronological sequence is Routing →\to→ Scheduling →\to→ Dispatching →\to→ Follow-up. (See Quick Reference §2.2).
    • Routing: Sequence of operations (“how and where”).
    • Scheduling: Time assignment (“when”).
    • Dispatching: Work authorization & release.
    • Follow-up: Monitoring progress & removing bottlenecks.
  • Routing + Scheduling = Planning functions; Dispatching + Follow-up = Control functions.
  • MRP Inputs: Requires exactly THREE mandatory inputs:
    1. Master Production Schedule (MPS)
    2. Bill of Materials (BOM)
    3. Inventory Record File (Inventory Position)
  • MRP Exclusions: Purchase Orders, Speed Rating, Machine Capacity, and Delivery Details are NOT MRP inputs. (Purchase orders are MRP outputs).
  • Demand Type: MRP is for dependent demand items (raw materials, sub-assemblies, components). Independent demand uses EOQ.
  • MRP Terms: Time phasing, Time bucket, Planned order release. (Speed rating is not an MRP term).
  • MRP Objectives: Inventory reduction, shorter manufacturing/delivery lead times, realistic commitments, higher efficiency.
  • Aggregate Production Planning: Tool to meet weekly fluctuating customer demand. Concerned with quantity & timing of production in the long term (6–18 months). Strategies: Level, Chase, Hybrid.
2. Formulas & Context
  • MRP Explosion & Netting Logic:
    Net Requirement=Gross Requirement−(On-Hand Inventory+Scheduled Receipts)+Safety Stock\text{Net Requirement} = \text{Gross Requirement} - (\text{On-Hand Inventory} + \text{Scheduled Receipts}) + \text{Safety Stock}Net Requirement=Gross Requirement−(On-Hand Inventory+Scheduled Receipts)+Safety Stock

4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1: Identification of MRP Inputs

  • Question: Which of the following combinations of items is required for the determination of Material Requirement Planning (MRP)?
    1. Master Production Schedule
    2. Inventory Position / Record File
    3. Machine Capacity
    4. Details of Delivery
    5. Bill of Materials
  • Solution:
    1. Recall the three canonical inputs of MRP: MPS (1), Inventory Position (2), BOM (5).
    2. Eliminate options containing Machine Capacity (3) or Details of Delivery (4).
  • Answer: 1, 2, and 5 only.
  • Key Insight (Examiner Trap): Purchase orders, machine capacity, and speed rating are frequently placed as traps. Purchase orders are outputs, while capacity is handled by Capacity Requirements Planning (CRP).

Worked Example 2: PPC Sequence Identification

  • Question: Select the correct sequence of operations in Production Planning and Control.
  • Solution:
    1. Path & Machine selection (→\to→ Routing).
    2. Time assignment (→\to→ Scheduling).
    3. Release work to shop floor (→\to→ Dispatching).
    4. Monitor progress (→\to→ Follow-up).
  • Answer: Routing →\to→ Scheduling →\to→ Dispatching →\to→ Follow-up.
  • Key Insight (Examiner Trap): Routing must always come first. Any sequence starting with Scheduling or Dispatching is wrong.

3.2 — Plant Layout & Line Balancing

1. Concept Pointers
  • Primary Objective: Overall simplification, safety, and integration of the production system. (Secondary: economy of space, minimum travel time, convenient shop locations).
  • Product Layout (Flow Shop):
    • Machines arranged strictly in sequence of operations.
    • High volume, low variety, stable demand.
    • Straight material flow, low WIP, high throughput, automated handling.
    • High capital cost, inflexible, breakdown stops entire line, worker monotony.
  • Process Layout (Functional / Job Shop):
    • Similar machines grouped by function (all lathes together, all mills together).
    • Low volume, high variety.
    • High flexibility, high machine utilization, no single-point failure.
    • Complex handling paths, high WIP, long lead times, needs sophisticated control systems.
  • Cellular Layout (Group Technology):
    • Machines grouped into cells processing families of similar parts.
    • Medium volume, medium variety (batch 5–50 units).
    • Combines flow of product layout with flexibility of process layout. Requires GT coding.
  • Fixed-Position Layout:
    • Product remains stationary; men, machines, materials come to it (ships, aircraft, dams).
  • Layout Principles:
    • Primary: Straight-flow (minimise back-tracking), Proximity (related operations close), Gravity (cost-free motion), Safety (isolate hazards).
    • Secondary: Flexibility, Visibility, Accessibility, Ergonomics.
  • Line Balancing: Assigns work elements to stations within a product layout to minimise cycle-time variation and idle time.

3.3 — Material Handling Systems & Equipment Selection

1. Concept Pointers
  • Elements of Material Handling: Motion, Time, and Quantity. (Quality is explicitly excluded).
  • Economic Nature: Material handling is economically waste that cannot be eliminated, only minimised.
  • Handling Principles:
    • Planning: System principle (receive-handle-dispatch integrated), Standardization (pallets/racks), Space utilization (vertical stacking), Ergonomics.
    • Economic: Cost-benefit (payback 2–3 yrs), Utilization (60–80% target), Preventive maintenance.
  • Manual & Non-Powered Handling:
    • Manual: Wheelbarrows, dollies, pallet trucks. (Limits: load < 10 kg, distance < 5 m, volume < 1000 units/day).
    • Mechanical (Non-powered): Gravity rollers, inclined planes, skids. Implements the gravity principle.
  • Powered Continuous Equipment (Conveyors):
    • Belt Conveyor: Bulk solids (limestone in cement plants, coal) or packages; continuous, high-volume.
    • Roller Conveyor: Cartons, packages; gravity slope 3−4∘3-4^\circ3−4∘, powered speed 20−60 m/min20-60\text{ m/min}20−60 m/min.
    • Screw / Auger Conveyor: Powders, grains, plastics; enclosed dust control, incline up to 45∘45^\circ45∘.
    • Bucket Conveyor / Elevator: Bulk materials needing pure vertical lift (20−30 m20-30\text{ m}20−30 m).
    • Pneumatic Conveyor: Fine powders/granules in closed tubes over >100 m> 100\text{ m}>100 m.
    • Chain Conveyor: Heavy parts/assemblies (500 kg−5 t500\text{ kg} - 5\text{ t}500 kg−5 t).
  • Powered Mobile / Intermittent Equipment:
    • Fork-lift Truck: Counterbalance (aisle 3.5−4 m3.5-4\text{ m}3.5−4 m) vs Reach truck (aisle 2.5−3 m2.5-3\text{ m}2.5−3 m).
    • Overhead Cranes: Bridge crane (full workshop length), Gantry crane (outdoor/shipyard), Jib crane (local circular area).
    • Automated Guided Vehicle (AGV): Driverless internal transport, 24/7 flexible software routing.
    • Stacker Crane: Automated high-bay (30 m+30\text{ m}+30 m+) storage retrieval (AS/RS).
  • Unit Load Formation: Standard EUR pallet (1.2 m×1.0 m1.2\text{ m} \times 1.0\text{ m}1.2 m×1.0 m).

3.4 — Computer Integrated Manufacturing (CAD / CAM / CIM / FMS / GT / CNC)

1. Concept Pointers
  • CAD: Computer systems for creation, modification, analysis (FEA/CFD/kinematics), and documentation of designs. Parametric feature-based modelling updates entire assemblies when parameters change. Data formats: STEP, IGES, DXF, STL.
  • CAM: Generates toolpaths and post-processes CNC G/M-codes from CAD geometry. Optimises cycle time, tool life, surface finish.
  • CIM: Total enterprise integration of design, planning, manufacturing, quality, and shipping through computer networks. Data flows seamlessly from order entry to delivery.
  • Group Technology (GT): Groups similar parts into families sharing machines and tooling. Coding systems: Monocode (hierarchical, Opitz) vs Polycode (attribute chains). Enables cellular layouts.
  • Flexible Manufacturing System (FMS):
    • Multiple CNC workstations + automated material handling (AGVs/conveyors/pallet changers) + central computer.
    • Domain: Medium volume (500−5000 parts/yr500-5000\text{ parts/yr}500−5000 parts/yr) and Medium variety (10−5010-5010−50 part types).
    • Economic batch size of 1 (setup <10−15 min< 10-15\text{ min}<10−15 min), lead time 2−52-52−5 days, utilization 60−75%60-75\%60−75%.
  • CNC Systems:
    • G-Codes: G00 (rapid), G01 (linear feed), G02/G03 (circular CW/CCW), G90/G91 (absolute/incremental).
    • M-Codes: M03/M04/M05 (spindle CW/CCW/Stop), M06 (tool change), M08/M09 (coolant ON/OFF), M30 (program end).
  • DNC (Direct/Distributed Numerical Control): Central computer network downloading G-code to multiple CNC machines simultaneously with real-time feedback.

3.5 — Materials Management & Inventory Stock Levels

1. Concept Pointers
  • Procurement Process: Requisition →\to→ Vendor evaluation (RFQ) →\to→ Purchase order →\to→ Goods receipt & inspection →\to→ 3-way match (PO–receipt–invoice) →\to→ Payment.
  • Vendor Strategies: Single sourcing (volume discount, single point failure), Multiple sourcing (backup, higher admin cost), JIT supplier (near-zero inventory, needs proximity & high reliability), Consignment stock (vendor owns inventory at buyer site until consumed), Blanket PO (annual commitment with scheduled releases).
  • Demand Forecasting:
    • Patterns: Deterministic, Seasonal, Trend, Cyclic, Random.
    • Exponential Smoothing: Ft=αDt+(1−α)Ft−1F_t = \alpha D_t + (1 - \alpha) F_{t-1}Ft​=αDt​+(1−α)Ft−1​. Higher α\alphaα reacts faster to recent demand changes; lower α\alphaα smooths noise. (See Quick Reference §2.1).

    • ABC: Annual money value (A: 70−80%70-80\%70−80% value, 10%10\%10% items; C: 5−10%5-10\%5−10% value, 60%60\%60% items).
    • VED: Criticality (Vital, Essential, Desirable).
    • XYZ: Demand variability (X: stable CV<0.2CV<0.2CV<0.2, Y: moderate, Z: erratic CV>0.5CV>0.5CV>0.5).

SECTION 4: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

Note on MRP across PPC & Materials Management

  • MRP is introduced as a PPC core technique (§3.1) and re-examined under Materials Management (§3.5).
  • In PPC, MRP provides the time-phased shop schedule; in Materials Management, it serves as the procurement explosion engine.
  • Master inputs remain strictly: MPS + BOM + Inventory Record File. (See Quick Reference §2.2).

Note on Group Technology & FMS across Layout & CAD/CAM

  • Cellular layout (§3.2) is the physical layout concept of Group Technology.
  • FMS (§3.4) is the automated system executing cellular manufacturing.
  • Both rely on part family coding (Opitz monocode/polycode) to optimize mid-volume, mid-variety batch production.
