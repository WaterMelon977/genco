SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Impact of Jets

  • Fixed Normal Flat Plate:
    $${\boxed{F = \rho A V^2}}$$
  • $F$: normal force on plate $\text{N}$
  • $\rho$: fluid mass density $\text{kg/m}^3$
  • $A$: jet cross-sectional area $\text{m}^2$

  • $V$: jet velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • Moving Normal Flat Plate (velocity $u$):

    $${\boxed{F = \rho A (V - u)^2}}$$
    $${\text{Work Done / sec} = F \cdot u = \rho A (V - u)^2 u}$$

  • Series of Moving Flat Vanes on Wheel:

    $${\boxed{F = \rho A V (V - u)}}$$
    $${\text{Efficiency } \eta = \frac{2 u (V - u)}{V^2} \quad \implies \quad \eta_{\max} = 50\% \quad \text{at } u = \frac{V}{2}}$$

  • Fixed Inclined Flat Plate (angle $\theta$ to plate surface):

    $${\boxed{F_n = \rho A V^2 \sin\theta}}$$

  • Moving Curved Vane (Central Impact):

    $${\boxed{F_x = \rho A (V - u)^2 (1 + \cos\phi)}}$$

  • $\phi$: vane exit deflecting angle

2. Water Hammer & Surge Tank

  • Critical Valve Closure Time:
    $${\boxed{t_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2 L}{C}}}$$
  • Gradual Closure: $t > \frac{2L}{C}$
  • Sudden Closure: $t \le \frac{2L}{C}$
  • $L$: penstock length $\text{m}$
  • $C$: acoustic pressure wave speed $\text{m/s}$

3. Hydraulic Turbines & Specific Speed

  • Turbine Specific Speed $N_s$:
    $${\boxed{N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}}}$$
  • $N$: rotational speed $\text{rpm}$
  • $P$: shaft power output $\text{kW}$

  • $H$: net working head $\text{m}$

  • Specific Speed Ranges (SI/Metric Units):

  • Single-Jet Pelton: $N_s = 8 - 30$
  • Multi-Jet Pelton: $N_s = 30 - 70$
  • Francis Turbine: $N_s = 50 - 300$

  • Kaplan / Propeller Turbine: $N_s = 300 - 1000$

  • Turbine Head & Unit Quantities:

    $${\boxed{N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}, \quad Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}, \quad P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}}}$$

4. Pelton Wheel (Impulse Turbine)

  • Jet Velocity:

    $${\boxed{V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2 g H}}}$$

  • Bucket Speed:

    $${\boxed{u = \phi \sqrt{2 g H}} \quad (\phi \approx 0.43 - 0.48)}$$

  • Maximum Hydraulic Efficiency:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{h,\max} = \frac{1 + \cos\phi}{2} \quad \text{occurring at } u = \frac{V_1}{2}}}$$

  • Number of Buckets (Taygun's Formula):

    $${\boxed{Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2d} = 15 + \frac{m}{2}}}$$

  • $m$: jet ratio $\frac{D}{d} \approx 11 - 15$

5. Francis & Kaplan Turbines (Reaction Turbines)

  • Francis Discharge:

    $${\boxed{Q = \pi D_1 B_1 V_{f1} = \pi D_2 B_2 V_{f2}}}$$

  • Kaplan Discharge:

    $${\boxed{Q = \frac{\pi}{4} (D_o^2 - D_b^2) V_f}}$$

  • $D_o$: runner outer tip diameter $\text{m}$

  • $D_b$: runner hub/boss diameter $\text{m}$

  • Francis Hydraulic Efficiency (Radial Outlet, $V_{w2} = 0$):

    $${\boxed{\eta_h = \frac{V_{w1} u_1}{g H}}}$$

  • Draft Tube Energy Efficiency:

    $${\boxed{\eta_d = \frac{\frac{V_1^2}{2g} - \frac{V_2^2}{2g} - h_f}{\frac{V_1^2}{2g}}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Impulse Turbine: Turbine where the entire available hydraulic head is converted into kinetic energy in a nozzle before entering the runner (e.g. Pelton wheel). Operating pressure remains constant (atmospheric) throughout runner.
  • Reaction Turbine: Turbine where fluid enters runner partly with pressure energy and partly with kinetic energy. Pressure drops continuously across runner blades (e.g. Francis, Kaplan). Requires airtight casing and draft tube.
  • Draft Tube: Diverging outlet conduit fitted below reaction turbine runner that recovers exit kinetic head $\frac{V_1^2}{2g}$ into pressure head, allowing turbine elevation above tailrace.
  • Water Hammer: Severe pressure spike in a closed pipe caused by rapid closure of a control valve, converting kinetic energy into acoustic pressure waves.
  • Surge Tank: Open standpipe or storage chamber installed near penstock bottom to relieve water hammer pressure surges during load reduction and supply water during load acceptance.
  • Thoma's Cavitation Factor $\sigma$: Parameter determining cavitation onset $\sigma = \frac{H_a - H_v - H_s}{H}$.
  • Cavitation: Formation and violent collapse of vapor bubbles at low pressure zones (runner outlet / draft tube inlet), causing blade erosion and noise.

2.3 — Standard Values & Constants

  • Pelton Speed Ratio $\phi$: $\phi \approx 0.45$.
  • Pelton Max Efficiency Bucket Speed: $u = 0.5 V_1$.
  • Pelton Jet Ratio $m = D/d$: Typically $11 - 15$.
  • Kaplan Speed Ratio $\phi$: $\phi \approx 1.8 - 2.2$.
  • Francis Blade Count: $16 - 24$ (fixed).
  • Kaplan Blade Count: $4 - 8$ (adjustable).

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Impact of Jets on Plates & Vanes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Force exerted by a jet equals mass flow rate times net velocity reduction $\mathbf{F} = \dot{m} \Delta \mathbf{V}$.
  • For a fixed normal plate: jet is completely deflected through $90^\circ$, so $F = \rho A V^2$.
  • For a moving normal plate: effective mass flow striking plate per sec is $\rho A (V - u)$, and relative velocity destroyed is $(V - u) \implies F = \rho A (V - u)^2$.
  • For a series of flat vanes mounted on a wheel rim: mass flow passing through jet per sec is $\rho A V$, so $F = \rho A V (V - u)$.
💡 Key Concept: Single Plate vs Series of Vanes: - Single Moving Flat Plate: Force $F = \rho A (V - u)^2$; Max Efficiency $\eta_{\max} = 29.6\%$ at $u = \frac{V}{3}$. - Series of Vanes on Wheel: Force $F = \rho A V (V - u)$; Max Efficiency $\eta_{\max} = 50\%$ at $u = \frac{V}{2}$.

[!TRAP] Inclined Plate Angle Definition: Check whether $\theta$ is given relative to the plate surface or normal to the plate! If $\theta$ is angle between jet and plate surface, normal force $F_n = \rho A V^2 \sin\theta$.

2. Decision Tree
Jet Impact Problem?
├─ Fixed Normal Plate → F = rho * A * V^2
├─ Moving Normal Plate → F = rho * A * (V - u)^2, Work = F * u, Max efficiency at u = V/3
├─ Series of Vanes on Wheel → F = rho * A * V * (V - u), Max efficiency = 50% at u = V/2
└─ Moving Curved Vane → F_x = rho * A * (V - u)^2 * (1 + cos(phi))
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Fixed normal plate $F = \rho A V^2$.
  • [ ] Moving normal plate $F = \rho A (V - u)^2$.
  • [ ] Vane series max efficiency $\eta = 50\%$ at $u = V/2$.
  • [ ] Nozzle joint reaction force $F = \rho Q (V_2 - V_1)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A jet of water $50 \text{ mm}$ in diameter $V = 20 \text{ m/s}$ strikes a series of flat vanes moving at $10 \text{ m/s}$ in the direction of the jet. Calculate the force exerted, work done per second, and efficiency. - Solution:

$${A = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.05)^2 = 0.0019635 \text{ m}^2}$$
$${F = \rho A V (V - u) = 1000 \times 0.0019635 \times 20 \times (20 - 10) = 392.7 \text{ N}}$$
$${\text{Work Done / sec} = F \cdot u = 392.7 \times 10 = 3927 \text{ W} = 3.927 \text{ kW}}$$
$${\text{Jet Kinetic Energy / sec} = \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^3 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times 0.0019635 \times (20)^3 = 7854 \text{ W}}$$
$${\eta = \frac{3927}{7854} = 0.50 = 50\%}$$
- Key Insight: Series of vanes at $u = V/2 = 10 \text{ m/s}$ achieves exact maximum efficiency of $50\%$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A jet of area $0.01 \text{ m}^2$ moving at $30 \text{ m/s}$ strikes a single flat plate moving at $10 \text{ m/s}$ in the same direction. Calculate force on plate and work done per second. - Solution:

$${F = \rho A (V - u)^2 = 1000 \times 0.01 \times (30 - 10)^2 = 10 \times 400 = 4000 \text{ N}}$$
$${\text{Work Done / sec} = F \cdot u = 4000 \times 10 = 40,000 \text{ W} = 40 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: Single moving plate force uses $(V - u)^2$, whereas vane series uses $V(V - u)$.

3.2 Water Hammer & Surge Tank Protection

1. Concept Pointers
  • Water hammer occurs in penstocks when a valve is rapidly closed, converting liquid kinetic energy into acoustic pressure waves.
  • Critical time of closure $t_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2 L}{C}$.
  • Sudden Closure: $t \le \frac{2L}{C}$ (pressure wave returns after valve is closed $\implies \Delta P = \rho C V$).
  • Gradual Closure: $t > \frac{2L}{C}$ (pressure wave returns while valve is closing $\implies \Delta P = \frac{\rho L V}{t}$).
  • Liquid temperature does NOT affect water hammer magnitude.
  • Surge tanks protect the penstock by storing excess water during sudden valve closure and supplying water during sudden load acceptance.
💡 Key Concept: Surge Tank Functions: 1. Protects penstock from water hammer pressure spikes during sudden valve closure. 2. Acts as a small storage reservoir to supply water to turbine during load acceleration. Located as close to the powerhouse as practicable.

[!TRAP] Water Hammer Misconceptions: Water hammer develops in the PENSTOCK, not the draft tube or turbine casing. Liquid temperature has no influence on water hammer pressure.

2. Decision Tree
Water Hammer Problem?
├─ Closure Time Check → t <= 2L/C (Sudden Closure), t > 2L/C (Gradual Closure)
├─ Sudden Pressure Rise → Delta P = rho * C * V
├─ Gradual Pressure Rise → Delta P = rho * L * V / t
└─ Surge Tank Purpose → Protects penstock, absorbs pressure surge, supplies load acceptance water
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Sudden closure: $t \le 2L/C$; Gradual closure: $t > 2L/C$.
  • [ ] Sudden pressure rise $\Delta P = \rho C V$.
  • [ ] Surge tank is installed on penstock near powerhouse.
  • [ ] Temperature of water does not affect water hammer pressure.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Water flows at $2 \text{ m/s}$ in a $1000 \text{ m}$ penstock $C = 1000 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate critical valve closure time and maximum pressure rise for sudden closure. - Solution:

$${t_{\text{cr}} = \frac{2 L}{C} = \frac{2 \times 1000}{1000} = 2.0 \text{ s}}$$
$${\Delta P_{\text{sudden}} = \rho C V = 1000 \times 1000 \times 2 = 2,000,000 \text{ Pa} = 2.0 \text{ MPa}}$$
- Key Insight: Closure time $t \le 2.0 \text{ s}$ is sudden closure, producing $\Delta P = 2.0 \text{ MPa}$.

3.3 Pelton Wheel (High Head Impulse Turbine)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Pelton wheel is an impulse turbine suited for high working heads $H > 250 \text{ m}$ and low flow rates.
  • Runner operates under atmospheric pressure inside casing.
  • Double-cupped buckets split the jet into two equal halves to eliminate axial thrust.
  • Maximum hydraulic efficiency occurs when bucket speed $u = \frac{V_1}{2}$.
💡 Key Concept: Pelton Hydraulic Efficiency: $\eta_h = \frac{2 u (V_1 - u)(1 + k \cos\phi)}{V_1^2}$. For smooth buckets $k = 1$, maximum efficiency occurs at $u = 0.5 V_1$, yielding $\eta_{h,\max} = \frac{1 + \cos\phi}{2}$.

[!TRAP] Specific Speed and Number of Jets: Specific speed of a multi-jet Pelton wheel increases with the number of jets $n$: $N_{s,\text{total}} = \sqrt{n} \cdot N_{s,\text{single jet}}$.

2. Decision Tree
Pelton Turbine Problem?
├─ Jet Velocity → V1 = C_v * sqrt(2*g*H)
├─ Bucket Speed → u = phi * sqrt(2*g*H), phi approx 0.45
├─ Maximum Efficiency Condition → u = V1 / 2, Max Efficiency = (1 + cos(phi)) / 2
└─ Number of Buckets → Z = 15 + D / (2*d) = 15 + m / 2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] High head $> 250 \text{ m}$, low discharge, tangential flow.
  • [ ] Inlet velocity triangle is a straight line $V_{w1} = V_1$.
  • [ ] Maximum efficiency occurs at $u = 0.5 V_1$.
  • [ ] Number of buckets $Z = 15 + D/(2d)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A Pelton wheel operates under a net head of $400 \text{ m}$ at $600 \text{ rpm}$. Jet velocity coefficient $C_v = 0.98$ and bucket deflection angle is $160^\circ$ $\cos 160^\circ = -0.9397$. Find jet velocity and maximum theoretical hydraulic efficiency. - Solution:

$${V_1 = C_v \sqrt{2 g H} = 0.98 \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 400} = 0.98 \times 88.588 = 86.816 \text{ m/s}}$$
$${\phi_{\text{vane}} = 180^\circ - 160^\circ = 20^\circ \implies \cos 20^\circ = 0.9397}$$
$${\eta_{h,\max} = \frac{1 + \cos 20^\circ}{2} = \frac{1 + 0.9397}{2} = \frac{1.9397}{2} = 0.9698 = 96.98\%}$$
- Key Insight: Deflection angle $160^\circ$ means bucket outlet angle $\phi = 180^\circ - 160^\circ = 20^\circ$.

3.4 Francis & Kaplan Turbines (Reaction Turbines)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reaction turbines operate under dropping pressure across runner blades.
  • Francis Turbine: Medium head $30 - 250 \text{ m}$, mixed flow (inward radial inlet, axial outlet). Fixed blades $16 - 24$.
  • Kaplan Turbine: Low head $< 70 \text{ m}$, high discharge, purely axial flow. Adjustable runner blades $4 - 8$, enabling high efficiency across wide load ranges.
💡 Key Concept: Francis vs Kaplan Discharge Formulas: - Francis: $Q = \pi D_1 B_1 V_{f1}$ - Kaplan: $Q = \frac{\pi}{4} (D_o^2 - D_b^2) V_f$ Kaplan discharge accounts for hub blockage diameter $D_b$!

[!TRAP] Specific Speed Ordering:

$${\text{Pelton (Single Jet)} < \text{Pelton (Multi-Jet)} < \text{Francis} < \text{Propeller} < \text{Kaplan}}$$
Kaplan turbine has the HIGHEST specific speed $300 - 1000$.
2. Decision Tree
Reaction Turbine Problem?
├─ Medium Head (30-250m), Mixed Flow → Francis Turbine (Q = pi * D1 * B1 * V_f1)
├─ Low Head (< 70m), High Discharge, Axial Flow → Kaplan Turbine (Q = pi/4 * (Do^2 - Db^2) * V_f)
├─ Radial Outlet Condition → V_w2 = 0 -> Hydraulic Efficiency eta_h = (V_w1 * u1) / (g * H)
└─ Specific Speed -> N_s = (N * sqrt(P)) / H^(5/4)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Francis: Medium head, mixed flow, fixed runner blades $16 - 24$.
  • [ ] Kaplan: Low head, high discharge, axial flow, adjustable runner blades $4 - 8$.
  • [ ] Specific speed formula $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$.
  • [ ] Unit quantities: $N_u = N/\sqrt{H}$, $Q_u = Q/\sqrt{H}$, $P_u = P/H^{3/2}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A Kaplan turbine runner has an outer diameter of $4 \text{ m}$ and a hub diameter of $2 \text{ m}$. Flow velocity is $8 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate the discharge through the turbine. - Solution:

$${Q = \frac{\pi}{4} (D_o^2 - D_b^2) V_f = \frac{\pi}{4} (4^2 - 2^2) \times 8 = \frac{\pi}{4} (16 - 4) \times 8 = \frac{\pi}{4} (12) \times 8 = 24 \pi \approx 75.4 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
- Key Insight: Net flow area for Kaplan runner is $\frac{\pi}{4} (D_o^2 - D_b^2)$.

3.5 Draft Tube & Cavitation

1. Concept Pointers
  • Draft tube is used ONLY in reaction turbines (never in Pelton impulse wheels).
  • Functions of Draft Tube:
  • Permits placing turbine runner above tailrace level without loss of net head.
  • Recovers residual kinetic energy $\frac{V_1^2}{2g}$ at runner outlet into static pressure.
  • Maximum draft tube divergent cone angle is limited to $7^\circ - 8^\circ$ to prevent boundary layer separation.
  • Cavitation in reaction turbines occurs at the runner exit or draft tube inlet where pressure drops to minimum $P \le P_{vop}$.
💡 Key Concept: Draft Tube Efficiency: $\eta_d = \frac{\frac{V_1^2}{2g} - \frac{V_2^2}{2g} - h_f}{\frac{V_1^2}{2g}}$. Higher inlet velocity $V_1$ and lower exit velocity $V_2$ increase kinetic head recovery.

[!TRAP] Draft Tube Purpose: Draft tube converts kinetic energy to pressure energy $V \downarrow \implies P \uparrow$. NEVER confuse this with nozzles which convert pressure to kinetic energy!

2. Decision Tree
Draft Tube / Cavitation Problem?
├─ Draft Tube Function → Recovers kinetic head V1^2/2g into pressure head; enables runner elevation above tailrace
├─ Max Cone Angle → 7-8 degrees (prevents boundary layer separation)
├─ Draft Tube Efficiency → eta_d = [(V1^2/2g - V2^2/2g - h_f) / (V1^2/2g)]
└─ Cavitation Location → Runner outlet / Draft tube inlet (lowest pressure zone)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Draft tube is used ONLY on reaction turbines (Francis, Kaplan).
  • [ ] Divergent cone angle $< 8^\circ$ prevents flow separation.
  • [ ] Cavitation occurs at lowest pressure zone (runner outlet / draft tube inlet).
  • [ ] Stainless steel blades and smooth surface delay cavitation.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Water enters a draft tube at $6 \text{ m/s}$ and exits at $1.5 \text{ m/s}$. Head loss in the tube is $0.4 \text{ m}$. Calculate draft tube efficiency $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$. - Solution:

$${\frac{V_1^2}{2g} = \frac{6^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{36}{19.62} = 1.835 \text{ m}}$$
$${\frac{V_2^2}{2g} = \frac{1.5^2}{2 \times 9.81} = \frac{2.25}{19.62} = 0.115 \text{ m}}$$
$${\eta_d = \frac{1.835 - 0.115 - 0.4}{1.835} = \frac{1.320}{1.835} = 0.7193 = 71.93\%}$$
- Key Insight: Draft tube efficiency is $\eta_d = \frac{H_{k1} - H_{k2} - h_f}{H_{k1}} = 71.93\%$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is the force exerted by a jet of area $A$ and velocity $V$ striking a fixed normal plate?
A: $F = \rho A V^2$.

Q2: What is the maximum efficiency of a series of flat vanes mounted on a wheel struck by a jet?
A: Maximum efficiency is $50\%$, occurring when vane speed $u = \frac{V}{2}$.

Q3: What causes water hammer in penstocks?
A: Rapid valve closure converting fluid kinetic energy into acoustic pressure waves.

Q4: Where is a surge tank located and what is its main function?
A: Located on the penstock near the powerhouse to absorb pressure spikes during valve closure and supply water during load acceptance.

Q5: What is the specific speed formula for hydraulic turbines?
A: $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$.

Q6: Compare the specific speed ranges of Pelton, Francis, and Kaplan turbines.
A: Pelton single-jet $8 - 30$ < Pelton multi-jet $30 - 70$ < Francis $50 - 300$ < Kaplan $300 - 1000$.

Q7: Why does a Pelton wheel use double-cupped buckets?
A: To split the jet into two equal side streams, eliminating axial thrust on the wheel shaft.

Q8: What bucket speed $u$ maximizes the hydraulic efficiency of a Pelton wheel?
A: Bucket speed $u = \frac{V_1}{2}$ (half the jet velocity).

Q9: What formula gives the recommended number of buckets on a Pelton wheel?
A: Taygun's formula: $Z = 15 + \frac{D}{2d} = 15 + \frac{m}{2}$.

Q10: What is the difference between Francis and Kaplan turbine flow directions?
A: Francis is a mixed-flow turbine (inward radial inlet, axial outlet); Kaplan is a purely axial-flow turbine.

Q11: Why does a Kaplan turbine maintain high efficiency across part-load conditions?
A: Because its runner blades are adjustable, optimizing the flow angle at different load demands.

Q12: What is the primary function of a draft tube in reaction turbines?
A: Converts residual kinetic energy $\frac{V_1^2}{2g}$ at runner outlet into static pressure and permits runner placement above tailrace level.

Q13: Why is the divergent angle of a draft tube restricted to $7^\circ - 8^\circ$?
A: To prevent boundary layer flow separation and eddy losses inside the tube.

Q14: Where does cavitation most frequently occur in a reaction turbine installation?
A: At the runner exit and draft tube inlet, where local static pressure drops to its minimum.

Q15: How do turbine speed and power scale with net head $H$?
A: Speed scales as $N \propto \sqrt{H}$; power scales as $P \propto H^{3/2}$.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

  • Note on Governing vs Flywheel:
  • Flywheel controls short-term cyclic speed fluctuations within a single revolution due to torque variation.
  • Governor controls long-term average rotational speed under varying external electrical loads by regulating water supply $Q$.
  • Note on Unit Quantities:
  • Unit Speed $N_u = \frac{N}{\sqrt{H}}$
  • Unit Discharge $Q_u = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{H}}$
  • Unit Power $P_u = \frac{P}{H^{3/2}}$
