Hydraulic Pumps, Ram, Accumulator & Intensifier — Comprehensive Reorganized Study Guide

SECTION 1: TOPIC OVERVIEW

This master guide covers Hydraulic Pumps (Reciprocating Pumps, Centrifugal Pumps, Specific Speed, NPSH & Cavitation, Series/Parallel Multistage Systems) and Special Hydraulic Machinery (Hydraulic Ram, Accumulator, Intensifier) for AP GenCo AE, GATE, and SSC JE examinations. High-yield PYQ areas include reciprocating pump discharge $Q_{th} = \frac{A L N}{60}$ and slip, air vessel function (power saving + uniform flow), centrifugal pump manometric head $H_m$ and manometric efficiency $\eta_{man} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}$, pump specific speed $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$, pump affinity laws ($Q \propto N$, $H \propto N^2$, $P \propto N^3$), NPSH formula, and operational starting procedures (priming and starting with delivery valve closed). All raw file content has been consolidated without losing any theory Q&As or worked examples.

SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Reciprocating Pumps

  • Theoretical Discharge:
  • Single-acting:
    $${\boxed{Q_{th} = \frac{A L N}{60}}}$$
  • Double-acting:
    $${\boxed{Q_{th} = \frac{2 A L N}{60}}}$$
  • $A$: piston cross-sectional area $\text{m}^2$
  • $L$: stroke length $\text{m}$

  • $N$: rotational speed $\text{rpm}$

  • Percentage Slip & Coefficient of Discharge $C_d$:

    $${\boxed{\%\text{Slip} = \frac{Q_{th} - Q_{act}}{Q_{th}} \times 100 = (1 - C_d) \times 100}}$$

  • Negative Slip occurs when $Q_{act} > Q_{th}$ (due to short delivery pipe and high suction acceleration head).

  • Theoretical Power Required:

    $${\boxed{P = \frac{\rho g Q_{th} (h_s + h_d)}{1000} \quad (\text{kW})}}$$

  • $h_s$: suction lift $\text{m}$
  • $h_d$: delivery head $\text{m}$

2. Centrifugal Pumps

  • Manometric Head $H_m$:
    $${\boxed{H_m = h_s + h_d + h_{fs} + h_{fd} + \frac{V_d^2}{2g}}}$$
  • $h_{fs}, h_{fd}$: friction head losses in suction and delivery pipes $\text{m}$

  • $V_d$: velocity in delivery pipe $\text{m/s}$

  • Manometric Efficiency $\eta_{man}$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_{man} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}}}$$

  • $V_{w2}$: whirl velocity at impeller outlet $\text{m/s}$

  • $u_2$: peripheral impeller velocity at outlet $\frac{\pi D_2 N}{60}$ $\text{m/s}$

  • Impeller Discharge:

    $${\boxed{Q = \pi D_1 b_1 V_{f1} = \pi D_2 b_2 V_{f2}}}$$

  • $D$: impeller diameter $\text{m}$
  • $b$: impeller width $\text{m}$

  • $V_f$: radial flow velocity $\text{m/s}$

  • Overall Efficiency $\eta_o$:

    $${\boxed{\eta_o = \frac{\text{Water Power}}{\text{Shaft Power}} = \frac{\rho g Q H_m}{1000 \cdot P_{\text{shaft}}}}}$$

3. Specific Speed & Affinity Laws (Pumps)

  • Pump Specific Speed $N_s$:
    $${\boxed{N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H_m^{3/4}}}}$$
  • $N$: rotational speed $\text{rpm}$
  • $Q$: discharge $\text{m}^3/\text{s}$

  • $H_m$: head per stage $\text{m}$

  • Affinity Laws (Variable Speed $N$):

    $${\boxed{\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \frac{N_1}{N_2}, \quad \frac{H_1}{H_2} = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^2, \quad \frac{P_1}{P_2} = \left(\frac{N_1}{N_2}\right)^3}}$$

  • Affinity Laws (Variable Impeller Diameter $D$):

    $${\boxed{\frac{Q_1}{Q_2} = \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^3, \quad \frac{H_1}{H_2} = \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2, \quad \frac{P_1}{P_2} = \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^5}}$$

4. NPSH & Cavitation

  • Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH Available):
    $${\boxed{\text{NPSH}A = \frac{P{atm}}{\rho g} - \frac{P_v}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs}}}$$
  • $P_{atm}$: atmospheric pressure $\text{Pa}$

  • $P_v$: liquid vapour pressure $\text{Pa}$

  • Thoma's Cavitation Factor for Pumps:

    $${\boxed{\sigma = \frac{\text{NPSH}}{H_m}}}$$

5. Special Hydraulic Machinery

  • Hydraulic Ram Efficiency:
  • D'Aubuisson's Efficiency: $\eta = \frac{q h}{Q H}$
  • Rankine's Efficiency: $\eta = \frac{q h}{(Q - q) H}$

  • $Q$: supply water rate; $q$: raised water rate; $H$: supply head; $h$: delivery height above supply reservoir.

  • Hydraulic Intensifier:

    $${\boxed{p_2 A_2 = p_1 A_1 \implies p_2 = p_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Positive-Displacement Pump: Pump where liquid is physically trapped in a chamber and forced out by a reciprocating piston or rotary screw/gear (e.g. reciprocating, gear, screw pumps).
  • Roto-Dynamic Pump: Pump where dynamic kinetic energy is continuously imparted to liquid by a rotating impeller and converted to pressure in a casing (e.g. centrifugal, axial pumps).
  • Air Vessel: Cast iron vessel containing compressed air attached to suction and delivery lines of a reciprocating pump to smooth flow pulsations, reduce friction losses, and save power.
  • Negative Slip: Condition in reciprocating pumps where actual discharge exceeds theoretical discharge $Q_{act} > Q_{th}$ due to inertia effects in short delivery lines.
  • Priming: Operation of filling the suction pipe, casing, and pump cavity with liquid to displace trapped air before starting a centrifugal pump.
  • Manometric Head $H_m$: Total head against which a centrifugal pump must work, equal to total static lift plus friction and exit velocity heads.
  • Hydraulic Ram: Automatic device that uses the inertia energy of a large quantity of falling water (water hammer) to lift a small fraction of water to a much higher elevation without external power.
  • Hydraulic Accumulator: Device that stores energy in the form of high-pressure fluid using a weighted ram.
  • Hydraulic Intensifier: Device used to increase the intensity of fluid pressure using differential piston areas.

2.3 — Standard Values & Constants

  • Starting Delivery Valve Position (Centrifugal): Fully CLOSED (to minimize starting torque).
  • Starting Delivery Valve Position (Reciprocating): Fully OPEN.
  • Vane Exit Angle $\phi$: $20^\circ - 25^\circ$ (backward curved blades for maximum stability).
  • Pump vs Turbine Specific Speed Exponents: Pump uses $H^{3/4}$ and $\sqrt{Q}$; Turbine uses $H^{5/4}$ and $\sqrt{P}$.

  • Specific Speed: Pump formula $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$ contrasted with Turbine formula $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{P}}{H^{5/4}}$ in 5_reorganized.md.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Reciprocating Pumps & Air Vessels

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reciprocating pumps are positive-displacement machines ideal for high pressure and low flow rates.
  • Theoretical discharge for single-acting pump is $Q_{th} = \frac{A L N}{60}$; double-acting is $2 \times \frac{A L N}{60}$.
  • Slip $= Q_{th} - Q_{act}$. Percentage slip $= \frac{Q_{th} - Q_{act}}{Q_{th}} \times 100$.
  • Negative slip $Q_{act} > Q_{th}$ occurs when delivery pipe is short, suction pipe is long, and speed is high, causing momentum to force liquid past the delivery valve before the stroke completes.
  • Air vessels fitted close to the cylinder on both suction and delivery sides convert pulsating flow into steady uniform flow, saving up to $84.8\%$ of friction work in single-acting pumps.
💡 Key Concept: Power Saved by Air Vessels: - Single-acting reciprocating pump: Air vessel saves $84.8\%$ of work done against friction. - Double-acting reciprocating pump: Air vessel saves $39.2\%$ of work done against friction.

[!TRAP] Reciprocating vs Centrifugal Starting Procedure: - Centrifugal Pump: Start with delivery valve FULLY CLOSED. - Reciprocating Pump: Start with delivery valve FULLY OPEN. (Starting a reciprocating pump with closed delivery valve causes severe over-pressurization!)

2. Decision Tree
Reciprocating Pump Problem?
├─ Theoretical Discharge → Q_th = (A * L * N) / 60 (Single), 2*A*L*N / 60 (Double)
├─ Slip Evaluation → Slip% = [(Q_th - Q_act) / Q_th] * 100
├─ Negative Slip Condition → Q_act > Q_th (Short delivery pipe + High suction accel head)
└─ Air Vessel Function → Saves friction work (84.8% single, 39.2% double) + ensures uniform flow
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Single-acting $Q_{th} = A L N / 60$; Double-acting $Q_{th} = 2 A L N / 60$.
  • [ ] Slip $\% = (1 - C_d) \times 100$.
  • [ ] Negative slip occurs when $Q_{act} > Q_{th}$.
  • [ ] Air vessel saves $84.8\%$ friction power in single-acting pump.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A single-acting reciprocating pump has a cylinder diameter of $150 \text{ mm}$ $A = 0.01767 \text{ m}^2$ and stroke length of $300 \text{ mm}$. It runs at $50 \text{ rpm}$ and delivers $0.004 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. Calculate percentage slip. - Solution:

$${Q_{th} = \frac{A L N}{60} = \frac{0.01767 \times 0.30 \times 50}{60} = \frac{0.26505}{60} = 0.004418 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
$${\%\text{Slip} = \frac{Q_{th} - Q_{act}}{Q_{th}} \times 100 = \frac{0.004418 - 0.0040}{0.004418} \times 100 = \frac{0.000418}{0.004418} \times 100 = 9.46\%}$$
- Key Insight: Always verify units ($N$ in rpm divided by 60 gives stroke frequency per second).

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A double-acting reciprocating pump has piston area $0.02 \text{ m}^2$ and stroke $0.4 \text{ m}$, running at $60 \text{ rpm}$. Calculate theoretical discharge in L/s. - Solution:

$${Q_{th} = \frac{2 A L N}{60} = \frac{2 \times 0.02 \times 0.4 \times 60}{60} = 0.016 \text{ m}^3/\text{s} = 16 \text{ L/s}}$$
- Key Insight: Double-acting pump delivers twice the discharge of a single-acting pump $2 A L N / 60$.

3.2 Centrifugal Pumps: Head, Efficiency & Casings

1. Concept Pointers
  • Liquid enters the impeller axially at the eye $V_{w1} = 0$ and is discharged radially outward into a casing.
  • Manometric Head $H_m = h_s + h_d + h_{fs} + h_{fd} + \frac{V_d^2}{2g}$.
  • Backward curved vanes ($\phi < 90^\circ$, typically $20^\circ - 25^\circ$) are universally used in centrifugal pumps because they provide a stable head-capacity curve and high efficiency.
  • Casings convert kinetic head exiting impeller into static pressure:
  • Volute Casing: Spiral chamber of gradually increasing area.
  • Vortex Casing: Circular chamber inserted between impeller and volute to reduce eddy losses.
  • Diffuser (Guide Vane) Casing: Ring of fixed guide vanes surrounding impeller; provides highest pressure recovery.
💡 Key Concept: Manometric Efficiency: $\eta_{man} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2}$. For backward curved vanes with radial entry $V_{w1} = 0$, $V_{w2} = u_2 - V_{f2} \cot\phi$.

[!TRAP] Priming Requirement: Centrifugal pumps CANNOT pump air because the pressure generated $\Delta P = \rho g H_m$ is proportional to fluid density. Air density is $\approx 1/800$ of water density, so unprimed centrifugal pumps generate negligible pressure.

2. Decision Tree
Centrifugal Pump Problem?
├─ Starting Condition → Delivery valve FULLY CLOSED, Casing fully PRIMED
├─ Manometric Head → H_m = h_s + h_d + h_fs + h_fd + V_d^2/(2*g)
├─ Manometric Efficiency → eta_man = (g * H_m) / (V_w2 * u_2)
└─ Casing Selection → Volute (Spiral) vs Vortex (Circular chamber) vs Diffuser (Guide vanes)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Fluid enters at impeller eye $V_{w1} = 0$.
  • [ ] Vanes curved backward $\phi \approx 20^\circ - 25^\circ$.
  • [ ] Delivery valve fully CLOSED at start.
  • [ ] Priming removes air from casing and suction line.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A centrifugal pump impeller has an outer diameter of $300 \text{ mm}$ and runs at $1000 \text{ rpm}$. Manometric head is $25 \text{ m}$ and outlet whirl velocity $V_{w2} = 18 \text{ m/s}$. Find peripheral velocity $u_2$ and manometric efficiency $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$. - Solution:

$${u_2 = \frac{\pi D_2 N}{60} = \frac{\pi \times 0.30 \times 1000}{60} = 5 \pi \approx 15.708 \text{ m/s}}$$
$${\eta_{man} = \frac{g H_m}{V_{w2} u_2} = \frac{9.81 \times 25}{18 \times 15.708} = \frac{245.25}{282.74} = 0.8674 = 86.74\%}$$
- Key Insight: Manometric efficiency evaluates actual work delivered vs Euler head $V_{w2} u_2 / g$.

3.3 Specific Speed, Affinity Laws & Multistage Pumps

1. Concept Pointers
  • Pump Specific Speed $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H_m^{3/4}}$ classifies impeller shape:
  • Low $N_s$ $10 - 30$: Radial flow impeller (high head, low discharge).
  • Medium $N_s$ $30 - 80$: Mixed flow impeller.
  • High $N_s$ $80 - 300+$: Axial flow / propeller pump (low head, high discharge).
  • Multistage Pumps:
  • Impellers in Series: Increases total head $H_{\text{total}} = n \times H_{\text{single}}$ at constant discharge.
  • Impellers in Parallel: Increases total discharge $Q_{\text{total}} = n \times Q_{\text{single}}$ at constant head.
💡 Key Concept: Affinity Scaling Rules: - Discharge: $Q \propto N \cdot D^3$ - Head: $H \propto N^2 \cdot D^2$ - Power: $P \propto N^3 \cdot D^5$ Doubling pump speed $N \implies Q \times 2, H \times 4, P \times 8$!

[!TRAP] Multistage Pump Specific Speed: In $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H^{3/4}}$, $H$ represents head PER STAGE $H = H_{\text{total}} / n$, NOT total head!

2. Decision Tree
Pump Scaling / Multistage Problem?
├─ Speed Change (Affinity) → Q2/Q1 = N2/N1, H2/H1 = (N2/N1)^2, P2/P1 = (N2/N1)^3
├─ Multistage High Head → Impellers in Series (H_total = n * H_stage)
├─ Multistage High Discharge → Impellers in Parallel (Q_total = n * Q_stage)
└─ Specific Speed Formula → N_s = (N * sqrt(Q)) / H_stage^(3/4)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Specific speed $N_s = N \sqrt{Q} / H_m^{3/4}$ (head per stage).
  • [ ] Series impellers $\implies$ high head; Parallel impellers $\implies$ high discharge.
  • [ ] Speed scaling: $Q \propto N$, $H \propto N^2$, $P \propto N^3$.
  • [ ] Diameter scaling: $Q \propto D^3$, $H \propto D^2$, $P \propto D^5$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A centrifugal pump delivers $0.04 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ against a head of $20 \text{ m}$ at $1450 \text{ rpm}$ requiring $10 \text{ kW}$. If speed is increased to $2900 \text{ rpm}$, calculate the new discharge, head, and power required. - Solution:

$${\frac{N_2}{N_1} = \frac{2900}{1450} = 2}$$
$${Q_2 = Q_1 \times 2 = 0.04 \times 2 = 0.08 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
$${H_2 = H_1 \times (2)^2 = 20 \times 4 = 80 \text{ m}}$$
$${P_2 = P_1 \times (2)^3 = 10 \times 8 = 80 \text{ kW}}$$
- Key Insight: Doubling pump speed increases power consumption by a factor of 8 $2^3$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A 4-stage centrifugal pump runs at $1000 \text{ rpm}$ delivering $0.1 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$ against a total head of $80 \text{ m}$. Calculate its specific speed. - Solution:

$${H_{\text{stage}} = \frac{80}{4} = 20 \text{ m}}$$
$${N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H_{\text{stage}}^{3/4}} = \frac{1000 \times \sqrt{0.1}}{(20)^{3/4}} = \frac{1000 \times 0.3162}{9.457} \approx 33.43}$$
- Key Insight: Use head per stage $H_{\text{stage}} = 20 \text{ m}$ in specific speed calculation.

3.4 Special Hydraulic Machinery: Ram, Accumulator & Intensifier

1. Concept Pointers
  • Hydraulic Ram: Uses water hammer inertia to lift water without external energy. Contains drive pipe, waste valve, delivery valve, and air vessel.
  • Hydraulic Accumulator: Stores high-pressure water under a heavy weight/ram to supply sudden peak demands (equalizes pump load).
  • Hydraulic Intensifier: Increases fluid pressure intensity for hydraulic presses using stepped cylinders $p_2 = p_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2}$.
💡 Key Concept: Hydraulic Intensifier Pressure Multiplication: $p_2 A_2 = p_1 A_1 \implies p_2 = p_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2$. High pressure output is inversely proportional to ratio of piston areas.
2. Decision Tree
Hydraulic Auxiliary Device Problem?
├─ Hydraulic Ram → Uses water hammer energy to raise small Q to high elevation without external power
├─ Hydraulic Accumulator → Stores high-pressure fluid energy under a weighted ram to supply peak demand
└─ Hydraulic Intensifier → Increases pressure intensity: p2 = p1 * (D1/D2)^2
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Hydraulic Ram uses water hammer force.
  • [ ] Accumulator stores energy to smooth out pump load fluctuations.
  • [ ] Intensifier multiplies pressure: $p_2 = p_1 (A_1 / A_2)$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A hydraulic intensifier receives water at $3 \text{ MPa}$ $30 \text{ bar}$. Low-pressure ram diameter is $250 \text{ mm}$ and high-pressure ram diameter is $50 \text{ mm}$. Calculate the intensified pressure output. - Solution:

$${p_2 = p_1 \left(\frac{D_1}{D_2}\right)^2 = 3 \times \left(\frac{250}{50}\right)^2 = 3 \times (5)^2 = 3 \times 25 = 75 \text{ MPa}}$$
- Key Insight: Pressure multiplies by the square of the diameter ratio $(250/50)^2 = 25$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is the theoretical discharge of a single-acting reciprocating pump?
A: $Q_{th} = \frac{A L N}{60}$.

Q2: What causes negative slip in a reciprocating pump?
A: High speed, long suction pipe, and short delivery pipe causing liquid momentum to force the delivery valve open before stroke completion $Q_{act} > Q_{th}$.

Q3: What is the main function of an air vessel in a reciprocating pump?
A: To provide continuous uniform flow and reduce friction/acceleration head losses, saving up to $84.8\%$ of friction work in single-acting pumps.

Q4: Why must a centrifugal pump be primed before starting?
A: Because air has extremely low density ($\approx 1/800$ of water), resulting in negligible pressure rise $\Delta P = \rho g H_m$ if unprimed.

Q5: What is the starting position of the delivery valve for a centrifugal pump vs a reciprocating pump?
A: Centrifugal pump: FULLY CLOSED (minimizes starting torque). Reciprocating pump: FULLY OPEN.

Q6: Why are backward curved vanes $\phi \approx 20^\circ - 25^\circ$ preferred in centrifugal pumps?
A: They produce a stable head-capacity characteristic curve and provide high manometric efficiency.

Q7: Define manometric head $H_m$ of a centrifugal pump.
A: Total head generated by the impeller, equal to static lift plus pipe friction losses and exit velocity head $H_m = h_s + h_d + h_{fs} + h_{fd} + \frac{V_d^2}{2g}$.

Q8: What is the specific speed formula for a pump?
A: $N_s = \frac{N \sqrt{Q}}{H_m^{3/4}}$.

Q9: How do discharge, head, and power scale with rotational speed $N$ for a centrifugal pump?
A: Discharge $Q \propto N$, Head $H \propto N^2$, Power $P \propto N^3$.

Q10: What is the difference between connecting impellers in series vs parallel in multistage pumps?
A: Series connection increases total head $H_{total} = n H_{\text{stage}}$; parallel connection increases total discharge $Q_{total} = n Q_{\text{stage}}$.

Q11: What is Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH)?
A: Total suction head available at the impeller inlet above the liquid's vapour pressure head $\text{NPSH}A = \frac{P{atm}-P_v}{\rho g} - h_s - h_{fs}$.

Q12: Where does cavitation occur in a centrifugal pump?
A: At the impeller eye (inlet), where local static pressure drops to its minimum level $P \le P_v$.

Q13: How does a hydraulic ram lift water without an external power source?
A: It utilizes the water hammer pressure impulse generated by the sudden closure of its waste valve.

Q14: What is the primary function of a hydraulic accumulator?
A: Stores fluid energy under high pressure using a weighted ram to supply peak demand requirements and equalize pump load.

Q15: How does a hydraulic intensifier increase fluid pressure?
A: By transferring force from a large-diameter low-pressure piston to a small-diameter high-pressure piston $p_2 = p_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2}$.
