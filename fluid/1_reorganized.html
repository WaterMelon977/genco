2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Basic Fluid Properties & Compressibility

  • Newton's Law of Viscosity:
    $${\boxed{\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}}}$$
  • $\tau$: shear stress ($\text{N/m}^2$ or $\text{Pa}$)
  • $\mu$: dynamic (absolute) viscosity ($\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ or $\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}^2$; $1 \text{ poise} = 0.1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$)

  • $\frac{du}{dy}$: velocity gradient or rate of shear strain $\text{s}^{-1}$

  • Kinematic Viscosity:

    $${\boxed{\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}}}$$

  • $\nu$: kinematic viscosity ($\text{m}^2/\text{s}$; $1 \text{ stoke} = 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2/\text{s} = 1 \text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$)

  • $\rho$: fluid mass density $\text{kg/m}^3$

  • Specific Gravity:

    $${\boxed{S = \frac{\rho_{\text{fluid}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}}}}$$

  • $S$: specific gravity (dimensionless; $S_{\text{water}} = 1.0$, $S_{\text{Hg}} = 13.6$)

  • Specific Weight:

    $${\boxed{\gamma = \rho g}}$$

  • $\gamma$: specific weight ($\text{N/m}^3$; $\gamma_{\text{water}} = 9810 \text{ N/m}^3$)

  • Capillary Rise / Fall:

    $${\boxed{h = \frac{4 \sigma \cos\theta}{\rho g d} = \frac{2 \sigma \cos\theta}{\rho g R}}}$$

  • $h$: capillary rise $+$ or depression $-$ $\text{m}$
  • $\sigma$: surface tension $\text{N/m}$
  • $\theta$: contact angle ($0^\circ$ for water-glass, $\approx 130^\circ - 140^\circ$ for mercury-glass)

  • $d$: tube diameter $\text{m}$

  • Excess Pressure in Liquid Drop:

    $${\boxed{\Delta P = \frac{4 \sigma}{d} = \frac{2 \sigma}{R}}}$$

  • Excess Pressure in Soap Bubble:

    $${\boxed{\Delta P = \frac{8 \sigma}{d} = \frac{4 \sigma}{R}}}$$

  • Excess Pressure in Liquid Jet:

    $${\boxed{\Delta P = \frac{2 \sigma}{d} = \frac{\sigma}{R}}}$$

  • Bulk Modulus of Elasticity:

    $${\boxed{K = -\frac{\Delta P}{\Delta V / V} = \frac{\Delta P}{\Delta \rho / \rho}}}$$

  • $K$: bulk modulus ($\text{N/m}^2$ or $\text{Pa}$)

  • $\frac{\Delta V}{V}$: volumetric strain (dimensionless)

  • Compressibility:

    $${\boxed{\beta = \frac{1}{K}}}$$

  • $\beta$: compressibility $\text{Pa}^{-1}$

  • Speed of Sound / Acoustic Pressure Wave:

    $${\boxed{c = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\gamma R T}}}$$

  • $c$: acoustic wave velocity $\text{m/s}$

2. Fluid Statics, Hydrostatic Forces & Buoyancy

  • Absolute & Gauge Pressure Relation:

    $${\boxed{P_{\text{abs}} = P_{\text{atm}} + P_{\text{gauge}}}}$$
    $${\boxed{P_{\text{vac}} = P_{\text{atm}} - P_{\text{abs}}}}$$

  • Hydrostatic Pressure Intensity:

    $${\boxed{p = \rho g h = \gamma h}}$$

  • $p$: static pressure at vertical depth $h$ $\text{Pa}$

  • Differential U-Tube Manometer (Heavier Liquid $\rho_m$):

    $${\boxed{\Delta P = P_A - P_B = (\rho_m - \rho) g x = \rho g h_w}}$$

  • $x$: manometer liquid column deflection $\text{m}$

  • $h_w$: differential pressure head in meters of water $h_w = x \left(\frac{S_m}{S_f} - 1\right)$

  • Differential U-Tube Manometer (Lighter Liquid $\rho_m$):

    $${\boxed{h_w = x \left(1 - \frac{S_m}{S_f}\right)}}$$

  • Hydrostatic Force on Submerged Plane Surface:

    $${\boxed{F = \rho g \bar{h} A}}$$

  • $F$: total hydrostatic force $\text{N}$
  • $\bar{h}$: vertical depth of area centroid from free surface $\text{m}$

  • $A$: area of submerged plane surface $\text{m}^2$

  • Centre of Pressure $h^*$ for Submerged Vertical Plane:

    $${\boxed{h^* = \bar{h} + \frac{I_G}{A \bar{h}}}}$$

  • $I_G$: area moment of inertia about horizontal centroidal axis $\text{m}^4$

  • Centre of Pressure $h^*$ for Inclined Plane (Angle $\theta$ to free surface):

    $${\boxed{h^* = \bar{h} + \frac{I_G \sin^2\theta}{A \bar{h}}}}$$

  • Hydrostatic Force on Curved Surface:

    $${\boxed{F = \sqrt{F_H^2 + F_V^2}, \quad \tan\theta = \frac{F_V}{F_H}}}$$

  • $F_H$: force on vertical projection of curved surface $\rho g \bar{h} A_v$

  • $F_V$: weight of fluid volume vertically above curved surface to free surface

  • Buoyant Force (Archimedes Principle):

    $${\boxed{F_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{displaced}} g}}$$

  • $F_B$: upward buoyant force acting through Centre of Buoyancy $B$ $\text{N}$

  • Apparent Weight in Fluid:

    $${\boxed{W_{\text{app}} = W_{\text{true}} - F_B}}$$

  • Metacentric Height $GM$ for Floating Body:

    $${\boxed{GM = BM - BG \quad \text{where } BM = \frac{I}{V_{\text{displaced}}}}}$$

  • $GM$: metacentric height $\text{m}$
  • $BM$: metacentric radius $\text{m}$
  • $I$: second moment of area of waterplane about longitudinal tilting axis $\text{m}^4$
  • $BG$: distance between centre of buoyancy $B$ and centre of gravity $G$ $\text{m}$

3. Fluid Kinematics

  • 3D Continuity Equation (Incompressible):

    $${\boxed{\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0}}$$

  • 1D Continuity Equation (Incompressible):

    $${\boxed{A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = Q = \text{constant}}}$$

  • Stream Function $\psi$ Relations (2D Incompressible):

    $${\boxed{u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}}}$$

  • Velocity Potential $\phi$ Relations (2D Irrotational):

    $${\boxed{u = -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}, \quad v = -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}}}$$

  • Cauchy-Riemann Grid Orthogonality:

    $${\boxed{\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}, \quad \frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}}}$$

  • Laplace Equation for Irrotational Flow:

    $${\boxed{\nabla^2 \phi = \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi}{\partial y^2} = 0}}$$

  • Total Acceleration Components:

    $${\boxed{a_x = \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial u}{\partial z}}}$$
    $${\boxed{a_y = \frac{\partial v}{\partial t} + u \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + w \frac{\partial v}{\partial z}}}$$

  • Local (Temporal) Acceleration: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}$

  • Convective (Spatial) Acceleration: $u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$

  • Vorticity $\boldsymbol{\xi}$ & Angular Velocity $\boldsymbol{\omega}$:

    $${\boxed{\omega_z = \frac{1}{2} \left( \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} \right)}}$$
    $${\boxed{\xi_z = 2 \omega_z = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}}}$$

  • Vortex Flow Velocity Distributions:

  • Free Vortex (Irrotational): $\boxed{v_\theta \cdot r = \text{constant} \implies v_\theta \propto \frac{1}{r}}$
  • Forced Vortex (Rotational): $\boxed{v_\theta = \omega r \implies v_\theta \propto r}$

4. Fluid Dynamics & Flow Measurement

  • Euler's Equation along a Streamline:

    $${\boxed{\frac{dp}{\rho} + V dV + g dz = 0}}$$

  • Bernoulli's Equation (Head Form):

    $${\boxed{\frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g} + z = \text{constant}}}$$

  • $\frac{p}{\rho g}$: pressure head $\text{m}$
  • $\frac{V^2}{2g}$: velocity (kinetic) head $\text{m}$

  • $z$: datum (potential) head $\text{m}$

  • Bernoulli's Equation with Head Loss & Machinery:

    $${\boxed{\frac{p_1}{\rho g} + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 = \frac{p_2}{\rho g} + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2 + h_L \pm H_{\text{machine}}}}$$

  • Venturimeter Discharge:

    $${\boxed{Q = C_d \frac{A_1 A_2}{\sqrt{A_1^2 - A_2^2}} \sqrt{2 g h}}}$$

  • $C_d$: coefficient of discharge $0.95 - 0.99$

  • $h$: differential head in meters of water $h = x \left(\frac{S_m}{S_f} - 1\right)$

  • Orifice Meter Discharge:

    $${\boxed{Q = C_d A_o \sqrt{2 g H}, \quad C_d = C_c \times C_v \approx 0.62 - 0.65}}$$

  • Pitot Tube Stagnation Velocity:

    $${\boxed{V = C_v \sqrt{2 g h}}}$$

  • Impulse-Momentum Force Equation:

    $${\boxed{\sum \mathbf{F} = \dot{m} (\mathbf{V}{\text{out}} - \mathbf{V}{\text{in}}) = \rho Q (\mathbf{V}{\text{out}} - \mathbf{V}{\text{in}})}}$$

  • Kinetic Energy & Momentum Correction Factors (Laminar Pipe):

    $${\boxed{\alpha = 2.0, \quad \beta = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Ideal Fluid: Hypothetical fluid with zero viscosity $\mu = 0$, complete incompressibility $\rho = \text{const}$, zero surface tension, and irrotational flow.
  • Newtonian Fluid: Fluid obeying Newton's law of viscosity where shear stress is directly proportional to velocity gradient $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$.
  • Pseudoplastic (Shear-thinning): Non-Newtonian fluid whose apparent viscosity decreases as shear rate increases (e.g. blood, milk, paint, gelatin).
  • Dilatant (Shear-thickening): Non-Newtonian fluid whose apparent viscosity increases as shear rate increases (e.g. cornstarch in water, quicksand).
  • Bingham Plastic: Thixotropic fluid requiring a critical yield stress $\tau_0$ before flow initiates linearly (e.g. toothpaste, sewage sludge, drilling mud).
  • Surface Tension $\sigma$: Tensile force per unit length existing along liquid-gas interfaces ($\text{N/m}$ or $\text{J/m}^2$), dimension $\text{MT}^{-2}$.
  • Capillarity: Phenomenon of rise or fall of liquid column in a small diameter tube due to relative strength of adhesive forces (liquid-glass) vs cohesive forces (liquid-liquid).
  • Bulk Modulus $K$: Measure of resistance to volumetric compression $\text{N/m}^2$; $K = \frac{1}{\beta}$.
  • Pascal's Law: Pressure intensity at any point in a static fluid is isotropic (equal in all directions).
  • Hydrostatic Paradox: Hydrostatic force on the base of a vessel depends only on fluid depth and base area, independent of container shape or total liquid volume.
  • Centre of Pressure $h^*$: Point of application of the total resultant hydrostatic force on a submerged surface; always lies deeper than the centroid $\bar{h}$ for inclined/vertical plane surfaces.
  • Archimedes Principle: A submerged body experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced by the body.
  • Metacentre $M$: Point of intersection of the buoyant force line of action with the original vertical symmetry axis when a floating body undergoes a small heel angle.
  • Metacentric Height $GM$: Distance between metacentre $M$ and centre of gravity $G$. $GM > 0 \implies$ stable equilibrium.
  • Streamline: Instantaneous continuous curve whose tangent at any point gives the direction of the velocity vector.
  • Pathline: Actual path traced by an individual fluid particle over a time interval.
  • Streakline: Locus of all fluid particles that have passed sequentially through a fixed spatial point.
  • Irrotational Flow: Flow field in which fluid elements undergo no net angular rotation about their own axes $\boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$. Velocity potential $\phi$ exists ONLY for irrotational flow.
  • Stream Function $\psi$: Scalar function existing for any 2D incompressible flow; lines of constant $\psi$ define streamlines.
  • Stagnation Point: Spatial point where local velocity drops to zero $V = 0$ and kinetic energy converts into pressure head $H_{\text{stag}} = \frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
  • Free Vortex: Fluid rotation driven without external torque $v_\theta \cdot r = \text{const}$, rendering the flow irrotational.
  • Forced Vortex: Fluid rotation driven by an external torque at constant angular velocity $v_\theta = \omega r$, rendering the flow rotational.

3.1 Basic Fluid Properties & Rheology

1. Concept Pointers
  • Fluids (liquids and gases) offer zero static resistance to shear stress; they deform continuously under any applied shear force.
  • Dynamic viscosity $\mu$ represents internal friction caused by intermolecular cohesion in liquids and molecular momentum transfer in gases.
  • Temperature dependence:
  • Liquids: Viscosity decreases with temperature ($\mu \downarrow$ as $T \uparrow$) because cohesive forces weaken.
  • Gases: Viscosity increases with temperature ($\mu \uparrow$ as $T \uparrow$) because molecular collision frequency rises $\mu \propto \sqrt{T}$.
  • Surface tension $\sigma$ results from unbalanced cohesive forces on surface molecules, causing liquid surfaces to act like stretched elastic membranes.
  • Capillarity occurs in small tubes due to the balance between adhesive forces (liquid-glass) and cohesive forces (liquid-liquid). Water wets glass ($\theta < 90^\circ$, rise); mercury depresses ($\theta > 90^\circ$, fall).
💡 Key Concept: Newton's Law of Viscosity: $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$. Dynamic viscosity unit is $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ $\text{N}\cdot\text{s/m}^2$. Kinematic viscosity $\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}$ has unit $\text{m}^2/\text{s}$ (stokes). $1 \text{ poise} = 0.1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$, $1 \text{ stoke} = 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.

[!TRAP] Temperature Effects on Viscosity: Liquid viscosity DECREASES with temperature, whereas gas viscosity INCREASES with temperature. Do not mix up liquids and gases in exam questions!

[!FOCUS] Non-Newtonian Fluid Classification: - Newtonian: $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$ (water, air, gasoline, kerosene, mercury). - Pseudoplastic (Shear-thinning): Viscosity decreases with shear rate (blood, milk, paint, ink). - Dilatant (Shear-thickening): Viscosity increases with shear rate (starch solution, quicksand). - Bingham Plastic: Yield stress $\tau_0$ required before linear flow (toothpaste, mud, sewage sludge).

2. Decision Tree
Fluid Rheology Problem?
├─ Linear plot through origin (tau = mu * du/dy) → Newtonian
├─ Requires initial yield stress tau_0 → Bingham Plastic (toothpaste)
├─ Slope decreases with rate of shear → Pseudoplastic (paint, blood)
└─ Slope increases with rate of shear → Dilatant (quicksand, starch)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Memorize Viscosity Units: $1 \text{ poise} = 0.1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$; $1 \text{ stoke} = 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.
  • [ ] Memorize Capillary Rise: $h = \frac{4 \sigma \cos\theta}{\rho g d}$ ($\theta = 0^\circ$ for water, $\theta = 130^\circ$ for mercury).
  • [ ] Memorize Droplet Excess Pressure: Drop $= \frac{4 \sigma}{d}$, Soap Bubble $= \frac{8 \sigma}{d}$, Jet $= \frac{2 \sigma}{d}$.
  • [ ] Memorize Bulk Modulus & Acoustic Velocity: $K = \rho \frac{dP}{d\rho}$, $c = \sqrt{K/\rho}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A flat plate of area $0.1 \text{ m}^2$ is pulled at $0.3 \text{ m/s}$ over an oil film of thickness $0.1 \text{ mm}$ $\mu = 0.001 \text{ N}\cdot\text{s/m}^2$. Calculate the shear stress, pulling force, and power required. - Solution:

$${\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{0.3}{0.0001} = 3000 \text{ s}^{-1}}$$
$${\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy} = 0.001 \times 3000 = 3 \text{ N/m}^2 \text{ (Pa)}}$$
$${F = \tau \times A = 3 \times 0.1 = 0.3 \text{ N}}$$
$${P = F \times V = 0.3 \times 0.3 = 0.09 \text{ W}}$$
- Key Insight: Always convert film thickness from mm to m $0.1 \text{ mm} = 10^{-4} \text{ m}$. Power is $F \cdot V$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A soap bubble of diameter $50 \text{ mm}$ has an internal pressure of $4 \text{ N/m}^2$ above atmospheric. Calculate the surface tension of the soap solution. - Solution:

$${\Delta P = \frac{8 \sigma}{d} \implies 4 = \frac{8 \sigma}{0.05} \implies \sigma = \frac{4 \times 0.05}{8} = 0.025 \text{ N/m}}$$
- Key Insight: Soap bubbles have TWO liquid-gas interfaces, so excess pressure is $\Delta P = \frac{8 \sigma}{d}$, whereas liquid droplets have $\Delta P = \frac{4 \sigma}{d}$.

3.2 Fluid Statics: Pressure Measurement & Hydrostatic Forces

1. Concept Pointers
  • Hydrostatic pressure increases linearly with vertical depth $p = \rho g h$.
  • Atmospheric pressure decreases with altitude; absolute pressure $P_{\text{abs}} = P_{\text{atm}} + P_{\text{gauge}}$.
  • Manometers measure pressure differences by balancing liquid column heights.
  • Total hydrostatic force on a plane surface equals pressure at centroid times surface area $F = \rho g \bar{h} A$.
  • Centre of pressure $h^*$ is ALWAYS deeper than centroid $\bar{h}$ for submerged inclined or vertical plane surfaces.
💡 Key Concept: Pascal's Law: Pressure at any point in a static fluid is isotropic (equal in all directions). Hydrostatic pressure intensity depends strictly on vertical depth $h$, not container geometry (hydrostatic paradox).

[!TRAP] Centre of Pressure Location: $h^ = \bar{h} + \frac{I_G \sin^2\theta}{A \bar{h}}$. Notice that $h^$ is ALWAYS below $\bar{h}$. As depth increases $\bar{h} \to \infty$, the gap $\frac{I_G \sin^2\theta}{A \bar{h}} \to 0$, so $h^*$ approaches $\bar{h}$ at great depths.

[!FOCUS] Barometer Fluid Choice: Mercury is selected for barometers due to its extremely low vapor pressure, high density $S = 13.6$, and non-wetting meniscus.

2. Decision Tree
Hydrostatic Pressure Problem?
├─ Depth given → p = rho * g * h
├─ U-tube Manometer → Delta P = (rho_m - rho) * g * x
├─ Force on Plane Surface → F = rho * g * h_bar * A
└─ Centre of Pressure → h_star = h_bar + (I_G * sin^2(theta)) / (A * h_bar)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Absolute = Gauge + Atmospheric; Vacuum = Atmospheric - Absolute.
  • [ ] Barometer measures atmospheric pressure $760 \text{ mm Hg} = 10.33 \text{ m H}_2\text{O}$.
  • [ ] Hydrostatic force magnitude depends on centroid depth $\bar{h}$.
  • [ ] Centre of pressure location is independent of fluid density.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A vertical square gate $2 \text{ m} \times 2 \text{ m}$ is submerged in water with its top edge $1 \text{ m}$ below the free surface. Find the total hydrostatic force and location of centre of pressure. - Solution:

$${A = 2 \times 2 = 4 \text{ m}^2}$$
$${\bar{h} = 1 + \frac{2}{2} = 2 \text{ m}}$$
$${F = \rho g \bar{h} A = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 2 \times 4 = 78.48 \text{ kN}}$$
$${I_G = \frac{b h^3}{12} = \frac{2 \times 2^3}{12} = \frac{16}{12} = 1.333 \text{ m}^4}$$
$${h^ = \bar{h} + \frac{I_G}{A \bar{h}} = 2 + \frac{1.333}{4 \times 2} = 2 + 0.1667 = 2.167 \text{ m}}$$
- Key Insight: Centroid depth $\bar{h}$ is measured from the free surface to the centroid of the area $1 + 1 = 2 \text{ m}$. $h^ = 2.167 \text{ m}$ lies below $2 \text{ m}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Water lake maximum depth is $100 \text{ m}$, $P_{\text{atm}} = 101 \text{ kPa}$. Calculate absolute pressure at the bottom $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$. - Solution:

$${P_{\text{gauge}} = \rho g h = 1000 \times 9.81 \times 100 = 981,000 \text{ Pa} = 981 \text{ kPa}}$$
$${P_{\text{abs}} = P_{\text{atm}} + P_{\text{gauge}} = 101 + 981 = 1082 \text{ kPa}}$$
- Key Insight: Always add atmospheric pressure when absolute pressure is requested.

3.3 Buoyancy & Flotation Stability

1. Concept Pointers
  • Buoyant force $F_B$ acts vertically upward through the Centre of Buoyancy $B$, which is the centroid of the displaced fluid volume.
  • Apparent weight of a submerged body = True Weight in air $- F_B$.
  • Floating body equilibrium: Weight of body $W$ = Buoyant force $F_B$ (body displaces its own weight of liquid).
  • Stability of floating bodies is determined by Metacentric Height $GM = BM - BG$.
💡 Key Concept: Archimedes Principle: A body submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced by the body $F_B = \rho_{\text{fluid}} V_{\text{displaced}} g$.

[!TRAP] Submerged vs Floating Stability Criteria: - Submerged Body: Stable if $B$ is ABOVE $G$ $BG > 0$. - Floating Body: Stable if Metacentre $M$ is ABOVE $G$ $GM > 0$, even if $B$ is below $G$!

[!FOCUS] Metacentric Height $GM$ Summary: - $GM > 0$ ($M$ above $G$): Stable Equilibrium (restoring couple). - $GM = 0$ ($M$ coincides with $G$): Neutral Equilibrium. - $GM < 0$ ($M$ below $G$): Unstable Equilibrium (overturning couple).

2. Decision Tree
Body in Fluid Problem?
├─ Completely Submerged → F_B = rho_f * V_body * g
│  └─ Stability → B above G (Stable), B below G (Unstable)
└─ Floating Body → F_B = W_body
   └─ Stability → M above G (GM > 0 Stable), M below G (GM < 0 Unstable)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid only.
  • [ ] Centre of buoyancy = centroid of displaced volume.
  • [ ] $BM = I / V_{\text{displaced}}$, where $I$ is waterplane area moment of inertia.
  • [ ] Metacentric height $GM = BM - BG$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A stone weighs $400 \text{ N}$ in air and $225 \text{ N}$ when completely submerged in water. Calculate the volume of the stone and its specific gravity $g = 9.81 \text{ m/s}^2$. - Solution:

$${F_B = W_{\text{air}} - W_{\text{water}} = 400 - 225 = 175 \text{ N}}$$
$${F_B = \rho_{\text{water}} g V \implies 175 = 1000 \times 9.81 \times V \implies V = \frac{175}{9810} = 0.01784 \text{ m}^3}$$
$${S = \frac{W_{\text{air}}}{F_B} = \frac{400}{175} \approx 2.286}$$
- Key Insight: Specific gravity of a solid fully submerged in water is given by $S = \frac{W_{\text{air}}}{W_{\text{air}} - W_{\text{water}}}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: A solid wooden block of specific gravity $0.6$ floats in water. Calculate the percentage of volume submerged. - Solution:

$${W_{\text{body}} = F_B \implies \rho_{\text{body}} V_{\text{total}} g = \rho_{\text{water}} V_{\text{submerged}} g}$$
$${\frac{V_{\text{submerged}}}{V_{\text{total}}} = \frac{\rho_{\text{body}}}{\rho_{\text{water}}} = S_{\text{body}} = 0.60 = 60\%}$$
- Key Insight: For a floating body in water, percentage submerged volume equals its specific gravity $\times 100\%$.

3.4 Fluid Kinematics

1. Concept Pointers
  • Kinematics analyzes fluid motion without considering forces.
  • Flow Classifications:
  • Steady vs Unsteady: $\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial t} = 0$ vs $\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial t} \neq 0$.
  • Uniform vs Non-uniform: $\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial s} = 0$ vs $\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial s} \neq 0$.
  • Laminar $Re < 2300$ vs Turbulent $Re > 4000$.
  • Streamlines, Pathlines, and Streaklines coincide in steady flow.
  • Velocity potential $\phi$ exists ONLY for irrotational flow $\boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$.
  • Stream function $\psi$ exists for ANY 2D incompressible flow (rotational or irrotational).
💡 Key Concept: Stream Function $\psi$ & Velocity Potential $\phi$: - $u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y}$, $v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}$ - $u = -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}$, $v = -\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial y}$ - Lines of constant $\psi$ (streamlines) and constant $\phi$ (equipotential lines) intersect orthogonally everywhere.

[!TRAP] Vorticity vs Angular Velocity: Vorticity $\boldsymbol{\xi} = \nabla \times \mathbf{V} = 2 \boldsymbol{\omega}$. Vorticity is TWICE the angular velocity vector, not equal to it.

[!FOCUS] Acceleration Components: - Local (Temporal) Acceleration: $\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial t}$ (present in unsteady flow). - Convective (Spatial) Acceleration: $u \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}$ (present in non-uniform flow).

2. Decision Tree
Velocity Field Given?
├─ Check Continuity → du/dx + dv/dy = 0 → Possible 2D incompressible flow
├─ Check Irrotationality → dv/dx - du/dy = 0 → Irrotational (phi exists)
├─ Stream Function psi → u = d(psi)/dy, v = -d(psi)/dx
└─ Acceleration → Local (du/dt) + Convective (u*du/dx + v*du/dy)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] 2D Continuity: $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0$.
  • [ ] $\psi$ existence $\implies$ continuity satisfied.
  • [ ] $\phi$ existence $\implies$ irrotational flow $\nabla^2 \phi = 0$.
  • [ ] Free vortex: $v_\theta r = \text{const}$ (irrotational); Forced vortex: $v_\theta = \omega r$ (rotational).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Given velocity field $u = 3 x^2$, $v = -6 x y$. Verify if this 2D flow is physically possible for an incompressible fluid and check if it is irrotational. - Solution: 1. Continuity check:

$${\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}(3 x^2) = 6 x}$$
$${\frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial}{\partial y}(-6 x y) = -6 x}$$
$${\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 6 x - 6 x = 0 \quad \text{(Continuity satisfied } \implies \text{Possible flow)}}$$
2. Irrotationality check:
$${\omega_z = \frac{1}{2} \left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right) = \frac{1}{2} (-6 y - 0) = -3 y \neq 0 \quad \text{(Rotational flow)}}$$
- Key Insight: A flow can satisfy continuity (physically possible) while still being rotational!

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Given stream function $\psi = 3 x^2 y - y^3$, find velocity components $u$ and $v$ at point $(1, 2)$. - Solution:

$${u = \frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y} = 3 x^2 - 3 y^2 = 3(1)^2 - 3(2)^2 = 3 - 12 = -9 \text{ units}}$$
$${v = -\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x}(3 x^2 y - y^3) = -6 x y = -6(1)(2) = -12 \text{ units}}$$
$${V = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2} = \sqrt{(-9)^2 + (-12)^2} = \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15 \text{ units}}$$
- Key Insight: Velocity magnitude at a point is $V = \sqrt{u^2 + v^2}$.

3.5 Fluid Dynamics: Euler, Bernoulli & Flow Measurement

1. Concept Pointers
  • Euler's equation assumes inviscid, steady flow along a streamline (pressure + gravity forces only).
  • Integrating Euler's equation yields Bernoulli's equation for incompressible flow.
  • Bernoulli's equation represents energy conservation per unit weight ($\text{m}$ of fluid).
  • Stagnation point: velocity $V = 0$; all kinetic head converts to pressure head $H_{\text{stag}} = \frac{P_{\text{stat}}}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
  • Flow Meters:
  • Venturimeter: Least head loss, highest $C_d$ $0.95 - 0.99$. Divergent cone angle is small $5^\circ - 7^\circ$ to avoid flow separation.
  • Orifice Meter: Highest head loss, lower $C_d$ $0.62 - 0.65$.
  • Pitot Tube: Measures stagnation pressure to compute local velocity $V = C_v \sqrt{2 g h}$.
💡 Key Concept: Bernoulli's Equation Assumptions: 1. Steady flow 2. Incompressible fluid $\rho = \text{const}$ 3. Inviscid fluid $\mu = 0$ 4. Flow along a single streamline (or irrotational entire field) 5. No shaft work added or extracted (no pumps/turbines)

[!TRAP] Total Energy Line (TEL) vs Hydraulic Gradient Line (HGL): - $\text{TEL} = \frac{p}{\rho g} + \frac{V^2}{2g} + z$ - $\text{HGL} = \frac{p}{\rho g} + z$ - The vertical distance between TEL and HGL is ALWAYS velocity head $\frac{V^2}{2g}$. TEL is ALWAYS above HGL.

[!FOCUS] Venturimeter Design Rule: Divergent section is longer with a smaller cone angle $5^\circ - 7^\circ$ than the convergent section $20^\circ - 22^\circ$ strictly to prevent flow boundary-layer separation and eddy formation.

2. Decision Tree
Flow Dynamics Problem?
├─ Ideal Flow Energy → Bernoulli (p/rho*g + V^2/2g + z = const)
├─ Venturimeter Flow → Q = C_d * [A1*A2 / sqrt(A1^2 - A2^2)] * sqrt(2*g*h)
├─ Pitot Tube Velocity → V = C_v * sqrt(2*g*h)
└─ Jet Impact Force → F_x = rho * Q * (V_2x - V_1x)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Bernoulli units = energy per unit weight (metres).
  • [ ] Venturimeter $C_d \approx 0.98$; Orifice meter $C_d \approx 0.62$.
  • [ ] Kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha = 2.0$ (laminar).
  • [ ] Momentum correction factor $\beta = 4/3$ (laminar).
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A horizontal venturimeter with inlet diameter $200 \text{ mm}$ and throat diameter $100 \text{ mm}$ measures water flow. Differential mercury manometer reading is $200 \text{ mm}$. Calculate flow rate $Q$ ($C_d = 0.98$, $S_{\text{Hg}} = 13.6$). - Solution:

$${A_1 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.2)^2 = 0.0314 \text{ m}^2, \quad A_2 = \frac{\pi}{4}(0.1)^2 = 0.00785 \text{ m}^2}$$
$${h = x \left(\frac{S_{\text{Hg}}}{S_{\text{water}}} - 1\right) = 0.20 \times (13.6 - 1) = 0.20 \times 12.6 = 2.52 \text{ m of water}}$$
$${\sqrt{A_1^2 - A_2^2} = \sqrt{(0.0314)^2 - (0.00785)^2} = \sqrt{0.000986 - 0.0000616} = 0.0304 \text{ m}^2}$$
$${Q = 0.98 \times \frac{0.0314 \times 0.00785}{0.0304} \times \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 2.52} = 0.98 \times 0.00811 \times 7.03 = 0.0559 \text{ m}^3/\text{s} \text{ (55.9 L/s)}}$$
- Key Insight: Differential head $h$ in terms of water column must be converted from manometer deflection using $h = x \left(\frac{S_m}{S_f} - 1\right)$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: What is Pascal’s law?
A: Intensity of pressure at any point in a fluid at rest is isotropic (equal in all directions).

Q2: What is the relation between absolute and gauge pressure?
A: Absolute pressure = Atmospheric pressure + Gauge pressure. Gauge pressure can be positive or negative (vacuum).

Q3: Why is mercury commonly used in barometers?
A: Mercury has an extremely low vapour pressure, high density $S = 13.6$, and non-wetting capillary behavior.

Q4: Where is the centre of pressure located for a submerged plane surface?
A: Always deeper than the area centroid $h^* > \bar{h}$. The vertical distance decreases as submergence depth increases.

Q5: What is Archimedes principle?
A: An immersed body experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced by the body, acting through the centre of buoyancy.

Q6: What are the stability criteria for floating bodies?
A: Stable if metacentre $M$ lies above centre of gravity $G$ $GM > 0$; neutral if $M$ coincides with $G$; unstable if $M$ lies below $G$.

Q7: Define a streamline.
A: An instantaneous curve drawn such that the tangent at every point gives the direction of velocity vector at that point.

Q8: When do streamlines, pathlines, and streaklines coincide?
A: In steady flow (all three lines are identical).

Q9: What condition must be met for a velocity potential function $\phi$ to exist?
A: The flow must be irrotational $\boldsymbol{\omega} = 0$.

Q10: What does the existence of a stream function $\psi$ guarantee?
A: The 2D incompressible continuity equation is automatically satisfied.

Q11: What is the physical difference between local and convective acceleration?
A: Local acceleration is the velocity change with time at a fixed point (unsteady flow); convective acceleration is the velocity change due to motion in space (non-uniform flow).

Q12: State Bernoulli's equation assumptions.
A: Steady flow, incompressible fluid, inviscid fluid, flow along a single streamline, and no shaft work added/extracted.

Q13: Why is the divergent cone of a venturimeter longer than its convergent cone?
A: To prevent boundary layer separation and minimize head loss ($5^\circ - 7^\circ$ divergent vs $20^\circ - 22^\circ$ convergent).

Q14: What is a stagnation point?
A: A point where fluid velocity drops to zero and kinetic head is completely converted into static pressure head.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

  • Note on Viscosity across Fluid Statics & Kinematics:
  • In Statics, fluid is at rest so velocity gradient $\frac{du}{dy} = 0 \implies$ shear stress $\tau = 0$ everywhere regardless of viscosity value.
  • In Kinematics & Dynamics, real viscous fluids create boundary layers and friction head losses $h_f$. Ideal fluids $\mu = 0$ are assumed for basic Euler and Bernoulli equations.
  • Note on Stagnation Pressure in Pitot vs Venturi:
  • Pitot tube directly creates a stagnation point $V = 0$ at its impact opening to measure total head.
  • Venturimeter does not stop the flow; it accelerates flow at the throat to create a static pressure drop measured by differential manometer.
