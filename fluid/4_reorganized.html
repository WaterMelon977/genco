SECTION 2: QUICK REFERENCE SHEET

2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Compressible Flow & Mach Number

  • Mach Number:
    $${\boxed{M = \frac{V}{c} = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Inertia force}}{\text{Elastic force}}}}}$$
  • Subsonic: $M < 1$
  • Sonic: $M = 1$
  • Supersonic: $M > 1$

  • Hypersonic: $M > 5$

  • Sonic Velocity (Speed of Sound):

    $${\boxed{c = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho}}}}$$

  • $\gamma$: ratio of specific heats ($C_p/C_v \approx 1.4$ for air)
  • $R$: specific gas constant ($287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ for air)
  • $T$: absolute temperature $\text{K}$

  • $K$: bulk modulus of elasticity $\text{Pa}$

  • Incompressible Approximation Criterion:

    $${\boxed{M < 0.3} \quad (\text{Density changes } < 5\%)}$$

  • 1D Steady Compressible Continuity:

    $${\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} + \frac{dV}{V} + \frac{d\rho}{\rho} = 0} \quad \implies \quad \rho A V = \text{constant}}$$

  • Adiabatic Compressible Bernoulli Equation:

    $${\boxed{\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1} \frac{P}{\rho} + \frac{V^2}{2} + g z = \text{constant}}}$$

  • Isothermal Compressible Bernoulli Equation:

    $${\boxed{\frac{P}{\rho} \ln P + \frac{V^2}{2} + g z = \text{constant}}}$$

  • Area-Velocity Relation for Variable Area Ducts:

    $${\boxed{\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V}}}$$

  • Subsonic $M < 1$: $dA < 0 \implies dV > 0$ (Convergent duct accelerates flow)
  • Supersonic $M > 1$: $dA > 0 \implies dV > 0$ (Divergent duct accelerates flow)
  • Throat $M = 1$: $dA = 0$ (Minimum area occurs at sonic state)

2. External Flow: Drag, Lift & Stokes Law

  • Total Drag Force:
    $${\boxed{F_D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^2}}$$
  • $F_D$: total drag force $\text{N}$
  • $C_D$: drag coefficient (dimensionless)

  • $A$: projected frontal area $\text{m}^2$

  • Lift Force:

    $${\boxed{F_L = C_L \cdot \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^2}}$$

  • $C_L$: lift coefficient (dimensionless)

  • Stokes Law for Low Reynolds Number Sphere ($Re < 0.2$ or $Re < 1.0$):

    $${\boxed{F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V = 6 \pi \mu r V}}$$

  • Friction drag $= 2 \pi \mu d V$ ($2/3$ of total)
  • Pressure drag $= \pi \mu d V$ ($1/3$ of total)
  • Drag coefficient $C_D = \frac{24}{Re}$

3. Dimensional & Model Analysis

  • Buckingham $\Pi$ Theorem:
  • Number of dimensionless $\Pi$-terms $= n - m$
  • $n$: total physical variables

  • $m$: number of fundamental dimensions $M, L, T \implies m = 3$

  • Model Law Scaling Factors:

  • Reynolds Model Law $\frac{\rho V L}{\mu} = \text{const}$: Viscous forces dominate (pipe flow, submerged submarines).
  • Froude Model Law $\frac{V}{\sqrt{g L}} = \text{const}$: Gravity forces dominate (ships, open channel flow, spillways).
  • Mach Model Law $\frac{V}{c} = \text{const}$: Compressibility forces dominate (supersonic aircraft, missiles).
  • Weber Model Law $\frac{\rho V^2 L}{\sigma} = \text{const}$: Surface tension dominates (droplets, capillary waves).

  • Euler Model Law $\frac{V}{\sqrt{\Delta P / \rho}} = \text{const}$: Pressure forces dominate (cavitation, enclosed fluid machinery).

  • Froude Scale Ratios:

  • Length ratio: $L_r = \frac{L_p}{L_m}$
  • Velocity ratio: $V_r = \sqrt{L_r}$
  • Time ratio: $T_r = \sqrt{L_r}$
  • Discharge ratio: $Q_r = L_r^{5/2}$
  • Force ratio: $F_r = \rho_r L_r^3$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Mach Number $M$: Ratio of flow velocity to local speed of sound; square root of ratio of inertia force to elastic force.
  • Normal Shock Wave: Extremely thin, irreversible pressure discontinuity across which supersonic flow $M_1 > 1$ abruptly slows down to subsonic flow $M_2 < 1$. Static pressure, temperature, and density jump upward, while stagnation pressure decreases $\Delta s > 0$.
  • Stagnation State: State attained when a gas stream is brought to rest reversibly and adiabatically $V \to 0$.
  • Streamlined Body: Body shaped such that its surface contours closely align with flow streamlines, delaying boundary layer separation and minimizing form (pressure) drag.
  • Bluff Body: Body whose surface geometry causes early boundary layer separation, creating a large turbulent wake and high pressure drag (e.g. flat plate, cylinder, sphere).
  • Stall: Sudden drop in lift force and sharp increase in drag on an airfoil when angle of attack exceeds a critical threshold $\alpha > \alpha_{cr}$.
  • Froude Number $Fr$: Ratio of inertial force to gravity force $Fr = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g L}}$.
  • Reynolds Model Law: Applied when viscous forces dominate flow dynamics $Re_m = Re_p$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Constants

  • Speed of Sound in Standard Air $15^\circ\text{C}$: $c \approx 340 \text{ m/s}$.
  • Air Specific Heat Ratio $\gamma$: $\gamma = 1.40$.
  • Air Gas Constant $R$: $R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$.
  • Incompressible Limit: $M < 0.3$.
  • Stokes Sphere Drag Coefficients: $C_D = \frac{24}{Re}$, Total Drag $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V$.

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Speed of Sound & Compressible Flow Regimes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Speed of sound $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ depends strictly on absolute temperature $T$ in Kelvin, NOT pressure.
  • Compressibility effects alter fluid density when $M > 0.3$. Below $M = 0.3$, density change is $< 5\%$, allowing incompressible flow assumptions.
  • Mach number is the square root of the ratio of inertia force to elastic force $M = \sqrt{\frac{\text{Inertia}}{\text{Elastic}}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Sonic Speed Formula: $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{\frac{K}{\rho}}$. For air $\gamma = 1.4, R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$, $c \approx 20.05 \sqrt{T(\text{K})} \text{ m/s}$. Double absolute temperature $\implies c$ increases by $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$ times.

[!TRAP] Celsius vs Kelvin in Sonic Speed: ALWAYS convert temperature to Kelvin $T(\text{K}) = T(^\circ\text{C}) + 273.15$. Using Celsius directly in $\sqrt{\gamma R T}$ is a major calculation error!

2. Decision Tree
Compressible Flow Regime?
├─ M < 0.3 → Incompressible approximation valid (density variation < 5%)
├─ 0.3 <= M < 1.0 → Subsonic compressible flow
├─ M = 1.0 → Sonic flow (occurs at throat)
├─ 1.0 < M < 5.0 → Supersonic flow
└─ M > 5.0 → Hypersonic flow
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ depends on absolute temperature $T(\text{K})$.
  • [ ] Mach number $M = V / c$.
  • [ ] Inertia / Elastic force ratio $= M^2$.
  • [ ] Incompressible limit $M < 0.3$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Calculate the speed of sound in air at $27^\circ\text{C}$ $\gamma = 1.4, R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. - Solution:

$${T = 27 + 273 = 300 \text{ K}}$$
$${c = \sqrt{\gamma R T} = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 300} = \sqrt{120540} \approx 347.18 \text{ m/s}}$$
- Key Insight: Always convert $27^\circ\text{C}$ to $300 \text{ K}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: An aircraft flies at $250 \text{ m/s}$ in air at $-23^\circ\text{C}$ $T = 250 \text{ K}$. Calculate its Mach number $\gamma = 1.4, R = 287 \text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$. - Solution:

$${c = \sqrt{1.4 \times 287 \times 250} = \sqrt{100450} \approx 316.93 \text{ m/s}}$$
$${M = \frac{V}{c} = \frac{250}{316.93} \approx 0.789 \quad (\text{Subsonic})}$$
- Key Insight: Mach number $M = V / c = 0.789$.

3.2 Normal Shocks & Nozzle Dynamics

1. Concept Pointers
  • A normal shock wave occurs ONLY when upstream flow is supersonic $M_1 > 1$.
  • Across a normal shock:
  • Downstream Mach number becomes subsonic $M_2 < 1$.
  • Static pressure $P_2 > P_1$, static temperature $T_2 > T_1$, and density $\rho_2 > \rho_1$ jump upward sharply.
  • Flow velocity drops $V_2 < V_1$.
  • Total stagnation pressure decreases $P_{02} < P_{01}$ due to irreversible entropy increase $\Delta s > 0$.
  • Total stagnation temperature remains constant $T_{02} = T_{01}$ for adiabatic shock.
💡 Key Concept: Area-Velocity Relation: $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V}$. - Subsonic Flow $M < 1$: $(M^2 - 1) < 0 \implies$ Decreasing area $dA < 0$ causes acceleration $dV > 0$. (Convergent Nozzle) - Supersonic Flow $M > 1$: $(M^2 - 1) > 0 \implies$ Increasing area $dA > 0$ causes acceleration $dV > 0$. (Divergent Nozzle) - Throat $M = 1$: $dA = 0$.

[!TRAP] Normal Shock Changes: Static temperature RISES across a normal shock $T_2 > T_1$, but total stagnation temperature remains CONSTANT $T_{02} = T_{01}$. Do not confuse static and stagnation quantities!

2. Decision Tree
Duct / Shock Wave Problem?
├─ Convergent Duct Subsonic (M < 1) → Area decreases (dA < 0) -> Velocity increases (dV > 0)
├─ Divergent Duct Supersonic (M > 1) → Area increases (dA > 0) -> Velocity increases (dV > 0)
├─ Minimum Area Throat → M = 1 (Sonic state)
└─ Normal Shock → M1 > 1 -> M2 < 1. P, T, rho jump UP; V, M, P_0 drop DOWN; T_0 CONSTANT
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V}$.
  • [ ] Convergent duct accelerates subsonic flow, decelerates supersonic flow.
  • [ ] Normal shock: $M_1 > 1 \to M_2 < 1$.
  • [ ] Across normal shock: $P_2 > P_1$, $T_2 > T_1$, $V_2 < V_1$, $P_{02} < P_{01}$, $T_{02} = T_{01}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air enters a normal shock at $M_1 = 2.0$, static pressure $P_1 = 50 \text{ kPa}$, static temperature $T_1 = 300 \text{ K}$. Downstream Mach number $M_2 = 0.577$. Calculate the static pressure jump across the shock if $P_2 / P_1 = 4.50$. - Solution:

$${P_2 = 4.50 \times P_1 = 4.50 \times 50 = 225 \text{ kPa}}$$
$${\Delta P = P_2 - P_1 = 225 - 50 = 175 \text{ kPa}}$$
- Key Insight: Static pressure jumps sharply across a normal shock wave $P_2 > P_1$.

3.3 External Flow: Streamlined Bodies, Drag, Lift & Stokes Law

1. Concept Pointers
  • Total Drag = Friction Drag (viscous shear) + Pressure Drag (form drag from wake).
  • Streamlined body: Thin wake, small pressure drag; friction drag dominates.
  • Bluff body: Broad wake, large pressure drag; pressure drag dominates.
  • Stokes Law applies to creeping flow past a sphere ($Re < 0.2$ or $Re < 1.0$):
  • Total Drag $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V$.
  • Friction Drag $= 2 \pi \mu d V$ ($66.7\%$ of total).
  • Pressure Drag $= \pi \mu d V$ ($33.3\%$ of total).
  • Drag Coefficient $C_D = \frac{24}{Re}$.
💡 Key Concept: Stokes Law Breakdown: Total sphere drag $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V = 6 \pi \mu r V$. Friction drag contributes $\frac{2}{3}$ of total drag, while pressure drag contributes $\frac{1}{3}$.

[!TRAP] Airfoil Stall: Stall is caused by boundary layer separation on the upper (suction) surface of the airfoil at high angles of attack, resulting in a sudden DROP in lift and STEEP RISE in drag.

2. Decision Tree
External Drag / Lift Problem?
├─ Body Geometry → Streamlined (Friction Drag Dominates) vs Bluff (Pressure Drag Dominates)
├─ Creeping Sphere Flow (Re < 0.2) → Stokes Law: F_D = 3*pi*mu*d*V, C_D = 24/Re
├─ Flat Plate Normal to Flow → Pure Pressure Drag (Friction Drag = 0)
└─ Airfoil Stall → Angle of attack > alpha_cr -> Separation -> Lift drops, Drag surges
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Total Drag $F_D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^2$.
  • [ ] Lift Force $F_L = C_L \cdot \frac{1}{2} \rho A V^2$.
  • [ ] Stokes sphere drag $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V = 6 \pi \mu r V$.
  • [ ] Stokes drag breakdown: Friction $= 2/3$, Pressure $= 1/3$, $C_D = 24/Re$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A tiny sphere of diameter $2 \text{ mm}$ falls through oil $\mu = 0.5 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ at a terminal velocity of $0.01 \text{ m/s}$. Calculate the total drag force on the sphere. - Solution:

$${F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V = 3 \times \pi \times 0.5 \times (0.002) \times 0.01 = 3 \pi \times 10^{-5} \approx 9.425 \times 10^{-5} \text{ N} = 94.25 \ \mu\text{N}}$$
- Key Insight: Use Stokes Law formula $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V$ for very low velocity flows past tiny spheres.

3.4 Dimensional Analysis & Model Testing Laws

1. Concept Pointers
  • Buckingham $\Pi$ Theorem: If a physical process involves $n$ variables expressed in $m$ fundamental dimensions $M, L, T$, the relationship can be expressed with $n - m$ independent dimensionless $\Pi$-terms.
  • Model Similarity requires Geometric, Kinematic, and Dynamic similarity.
  • Model Laws:
  • Reynolds Law $Re_m = Re_p$: Pipe flow, submarine, fully submerged bodies.
  • Froude Law $Fr_m = Fr_p$: Open channel flow, ships, spillways, wave action.
  • Mach Law $M_m = M_p$: High-speed aircraft, missiles.
💡 Key Concept: Froude Model Scaling Relations $Fr_m = Fr_p \implies \frac{V_m}{\sqrt{g L_m}} = \frac{V_p}{\sqrt{g L_p}}$: - Length ratio: $L_r = \frac{L_p}{L_m}$ - Velocity ratio: $V_r = \sqrt{L_r}$ - Time ratio: $T_r = \sqrt{L_r}$ - Discharge ratio: $Q_r = A_r V_r = L_r^2 \cdot L_r^{1/2} = L_r^{5/2}$ - Force ratio: $F_r = \rho_r L_r^3$

[!TRAP] Selecting Model Laws: - Viscous forces dominate $\implies$ Reynolds Law $Re$. - Gravity / free surface dominates $\implies$ Froude Law $Fr$. - Surface tension dominates $\implies$ Weber Law $We$. - Compressibility dominates $\implies$ Mach Law $M$.

2. Decision Tree
Model Law Selection?
├─ Open channel / Ships / Spillways → Froude Law (Fr = V / sqrt(g*L)). Q_r = L_r^(5/2), V_r = sqrt(L_r)
├─ Submarines / Pipe Flow / Aircraft Low Speed → Reynolds Law (Re = rho*V*L / mu)
├─ Supersonic Aircraft / Missiles → Mach Law (M = V / c)
└─ Capillary / Droplets → Weber Law (We = rho*V^2*L / sigma)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Buckingham $\Pi$ terms $= n - m$.
  • [ ] Froude Law $V_r = \sqrt{L_r}$, $Q_r = L_r^{5/2}$.
  • [ ] Reynolds Law $V_r = \nu_r / L_r$.
  • [ ] Geometric (shape), Kinematic (velocity field), Dynamic (force field) similarity.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: A $1:25$ scale model of a spillway is tested. If the prototype discharge is $2500 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}$, calculate the required model discharge using Froude Model Law. - Solution:

$${L_r = 25}$$
$${Q_r = \frac{Q_p}{Q_m} = L_r^{5/2} = (25)^{5/2} = (5^2)^{5/2} = 5^5 = 3125}$$
$${Q_m = \frac{Q_p}{3125} = \frac{2500}{3125} = 0.80 \text{ m}^3/\text{s}}$$
- Key Insight: Under Froude Law, discharge scales as $Q_r = L_r^{5/2}$.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1. How is Mach number defined?
A: Ratio of local flow velocity to local speed of sound $M = V / c$. It is the square root of the ratio of inertia force to elastic force.

Q2. At what Mach number can compressibility effects be safely neglected?
A: Below $M = 0.3$, where fluid density variations remain under $5\%$.

Q3. What is the formula for the speed of sound in an ideal gas under isentropic conditions?
A: $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$, where $T$ is absolute temperature in Kelvin.

Q4. What type of flow is required upstream for a normal shock wave to occur?
A: Upstream flow MUST be supersonic $M_1 > 1$.

Q5. How do static pressure and Mach number change across a normal shock wave?
A: Static pressure jumps upward $P_2 > P_1$, while Mach number drops below unity ($M_2 < 1$, subsonic).

Q6. What happens to stagnation pressure across a normal shock wave?
A: Stagnation pressure decreases $P_{02} < P_{01}$ due to irreversible entropy generation across the shock.

Q7. What is the area-velocity relation for variable-area compressible duct flow?
A: $\frac{dA}{A} = (M^2 - 1) \frac{dV}{V}$.

Q8. How does a convergent duct affect subsonic vs supersonic flow?
A: A convergent duct accelerates subsonic flow $M < 1$, but decelerates supersonic flow $M > 1$.

Q9. Where does sonic condition $M = 1$ occur in a converging-diverging nozzle?
A: At the minimum cross-sectional area (the throat).

Q10. What is a streamlined body?
A: A body whose geometric shape conforms closely to flow streamlines, suppressing boundary layer separation and minimizing form (pressure) drag.

Q11. What is a bluff body?
A: A body whose geometry causes early boundary layer separation, creating a large turbulent wake and high pressure drag.

Q12. State Stokes Law for low Reynolds number creeping flow past a sphere.
A: Total drag $F_D = 3 \pi \mu d V = 6 \pi \mu r V$, where friction drag is $2/3$ and pressure drag is $1/3$ of the total.

Q13. What causes airfoil stall?
A: Severe boundary layer separation on the upper suction surface when angle of attack exceeds the critical angle $\alpha_{cr}$.

Q14. State Buckingham's $\Pi$ theorem.
A: If a physical relationship involves $n$ variables expressed in $m$ fundamental dimensions, it can be grouped into $n - m$ independent dimensionless $\Pi$-terms.

Q15. Which model law governs open channel and ship model testing?
A: Froude Model Law $Fr_m = Fr_p$, where gravity forces dominate free-surface flow.

SECTION 5: CONSOLIDATION NOTES

  • Note on Speed of Sound & Temperature:
  • $c = \sqrt{\gamma R T}$ depends strictly on absolute temperature $T$ (Kelvin). Density and pressure changes do not alter $c$ for an ideal gas at constant temperature.
  • Note on Shock Waves:
  • Normal shocks ONLY occur in supersonic flow $M_1 > 1$. Downstream flow is always subsonic $M_2 < 1$. Static pressure, static temperature, and density INCREASE; total stagnation pressure DECREASES.
