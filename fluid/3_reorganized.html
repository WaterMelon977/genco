2.1 — All Formulas (with symbols & units)

1. Flow Regimes & Critical Reynolds Numbers

  • Pipe Flow:
    $${\boxed{Re = \frac{\rho V D}{\mu} = \frac{V D}{\nu}}}$$
  • Laminar: $Re < 2000$
  • Transitional: $2000 \le Re \le 4000$

  • Turbulent: $Re > 4000$

  • Flat Plate Boundary Layer:

    $${\boxed{Re_x = \frac{U_\infty x}{\nu}}}$$

  • Laminar: $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$

  • Turbulent: $Re_x > 5 \times 10^5$

  • Open Channel Flow:

    $${\boxed{Re = \frac{V R_h}{\nu}}}$$

  • Laminar: $Re < 500$
  • Turbulent: $Re > 1000$

2. Circular Pipe vs Parallel Plates Laminar Flow

  • Circular Pipe (Hagen-Poiseuille):

    $${\boxed{u(r) = u_{\max}\left(1 - \left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^2\right), \quad u_{\max} = 2 V_{\text{avg}}}}$$
    $${\boxed{\Delta P = \frac{32 \mu V L}{D^2} = \frac{128 \mu Q L}{\pi D^4}}}$$
    $${\boxed{\tau(r) = \frac{r}{2} \left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right), \quad \tau_w = \frac{\Delta P \cdot D}{4L}}}$$

  • Flow Between Fixed Parallel Plates (Gap $h$, Width $w$):

    $${\boxed{u(y) = \frac{1}{2\mu} \left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) (h y - y^2)}}$$
    $${\boxed{u_{\max} = \frac{3}{2} V_{\text{avg}} = 1.5 V_{\text{avg}}}}$$
    $${\boxed{\Delta P = \frac{12 \mu V L}{h^2}, \quad Q = \frac{w h^3 \Delta P}{12 \mu L}}}$$
    $${\boxed{\tau_w = \frac{\Delta P \cdot h}{2L}}}$$

3. Correction Factors

  • Laminar Pipe Flow:
    $${\boxed{\alpha = 2.0, \quad \beta = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33}}$$
  • Turbulent Pipe Flow:
    $${\boxed{\alpha \approx 1.03 - 1.10, \quad \beta \approx 1.01 - 1.05}}$$

4. Boundary Layer Thickness Integral Definitions

  • Displacement Thickness:

    $${\boxed{\delta^* = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy}}$$

  • Momentum Thickness:

    $${\boxed{\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy}}$$

  • Energy Thickness:

    $${\boxed{\delta_e = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} \left(1 - \left(\frac{u}{U_\infty}\right)^2\right) dy}}$$

  • Shape Factor:

    $${\boxed{H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta}}}$$

2.2 — Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Reynolds Number $Re$: Ratio of inertial forces to viscous forces $\frac{\rho V D}{\mu}$.
  • Critical Velocity: Velocity at which flow transitions from laminar to turbulent for a given fluid and geometry.
  • No-Slip Condition: Viscous fluid molecules adhering to a solid wall have zero velocity relative to the wall $u(0) = 0$.
  • Displacement Thickness $\delta^*$: Mass flow deficit represented as an equivalent physical outward displacement of the free stream.
  • Momentum Thickness $\theta$: Momentum flux deficit represented as an equivalent physical thickness.
  • Boundary Layer Separation: Detachment of boundary layer from a surface driven by an adverse pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$, resulting in zero wall shear stress $\tau_w = 0$ and a turbulent wake downstream.
  • Hydraulically Smooth Surface: Surface whose roughness projections $k$ are completely submerged within the laminar sublayer $\frac{k}{\delta'} < 0.25$.

2.3 — Standard Values & Constants

  • Pipe Flow Critical $Re$: $2000$.
  • Flat Plate Transition $Re_x$: $5 \times 10^5$.
  • Open Channel Critical $Re$: $500$.
  • Parallel Plate Velocity Ratio: $\frac{u_{\max}}{V_{\text{avg}}} = 1.5$.
  • Circular Pipe Velocity Ratio: $\frac{u_{\max}}{V_{\text{avg}}} = 2.0$.
  • Blasius Boundary Layer Constants (Flat Plate):
  • $\frac{\delta}{x} = \frac{5.0}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$
  • Local skin friction $C_{fx} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$
  • Total drag coefficient $C_D = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{Re_L}}$

SECTION 3: MAIN BODY

3.1 Flow Regimes & Reynolds Number Criteria

1. Concept Pointers
  • Reynolds number quantifies the ratio of inertial force to viscous force $Re = \frac{\text{Inertia}}{\text{Viscous}}$.
  • High viscosity or low velocity promotes laminar flow; low viscosity or high velocity promotes turbulent flow.
  • Transition depends strictly on geometry-specific critical values of $Re$, not on pipe length.
💡 Key Concept: Geometry Critical Reynolds Numbers: - Circular Pipe: $Re < 2000$ (Laminar), $Re > 4000$ (Turbulent). - Flat Plate: $Re_x < 5 \times 10^5$ (Laminar), $Re_x > 5 \times 10^5$ (Turbulent). - Open Channel: $Re < 500$ (Laminar), $Re > 1000$ (Turbulent).

[!TRAP] Open Channel vs Pipe Re: Open channel flow uses hydraulic radius $R_h$ as the length scale, leading to a critical $Re \approx 500$. NEVER confuse open channel $Re_{cr} = 500$ with pipe $Re_{cr} = 2000$!

2. Decision Tree
Flow Regime Check?
├─ Pipe Geometry → Re < 2000 (Laminar), 2000-4000 (Transitional), > 4000 (Turbulent)
├─ Flat Plate → Re_x < 5x10^5 (Laminar), > 5x10^5 (Turbulent)
└─ Open Channel → Re < 500 (Laminar), > 1000 (Turbulent)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Transition $Re$ depends on geometry and velocity/viscosity, not pipe length.
  • [ ] Critical velocity is the velocity at which transition occurs for a given fluid and temperature.
  • [ ] Capillary flow is always laminar due to small diameter $D$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Water $\nu = 10^{-6} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ flows over a flat plate at $2 \text{ m/s}$. Find the distance $x$ from the leading edge where transition to turbulent flow begins $Re_{x,cr} = 5 \times 10^5$. - Solution:

$${Re_x = \frac{U_\infty x}{\nu} \implies 5 \times 10^5 = \frac{2 \times x}{10^{-6}}}$$
$${x = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \times 10^{-6}}{2} = \frac{0.5}{2} = 0.25 \text{ m} = 250 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: Transition distance on a flat plate is $x = \frac{Re_{x,cr} \nu}{U_\infty}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: Oil $\nu = 10^{-4} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$ flows through a $100 \text{ mm}$ pipe at $1.5 \text{ m/s}$. Determine the flow regime. - Solution:

$${Re = \frac{V D}{\nu} = \frac{1.5 \times 0.1}{10^{-4}} = \frac{0.15}{0.0001} = 1500}$$
$${\text{Since } Re = 1500 < 2000 \implies \text{Flow is Laminar}}$$
- Key Insight: Always evaluate $Re$ using consistent SI units.

3.2 Circular Pipe vs Parallel Plates Laminar Flow

1. Concept Pointers
  • For circular pipes, the cross-section is 2D radial; for parallel plates, flow is 1D between two flat surfaces separated by gap $h$.
  • Pressure drop is inversely proportional to $D^4$ for circular pipes $\Delta P \propto \frac{1}{D^4}$, and inversely proportional to $h^3$ for parallel plates ($Q \propto h^3 \implies \Delta P \propto \frac{1}{h^3}$ for fixed $Q$).
  • Velocity profile is parabolic for both geometries, but centerline ratios differ:
  • Circular Pipe: $u_{\max} = 2.0 V_{\text{avg}}$.
  • Parallel Plates: $u_{\max} = 1.5 V_{\text{avg}}$.
💡 Key Concept: Velocity Ratios Comparison: - Circular Pipe: $V_{\text{avg}} = 0.50 u_{\max}$ - Parallel Plates: $V_{\text{avg}} = 0.67 u_{\max}$ - Turbulent Pipe: $V_{\text{avg}} \approx 0.82 u_{\max}$

[!TRAP] Pressure Drop Exponent: - Circular Pipe: $\Delta P = \frac{128 \mu Q L}{\pi D^4} \implies \Delta P \propto D^{-4}$ for fixed $Q$. - Parallel Plates: $\Delta P = \frac{12 \mu V L}{h^2} = \frac{12 \mu Q L}{w h^3} \implies \Delta P \propto h^{-3}$ for fixed $Q$.

2. Decision Tree
Laminar Geometry Problem?
├─ Circular Pipe → u_max = 2 * V_avg, Delta P = (32 * mu * V * L) / D^2, f = 64 / Re
└─ Parallel Plates → u_max = 1.5 * V_avg, Delta P = (12 * mu * V * L) / h^2, Q = (w * h^3 * Delta P) / (12 * mu * L)
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Circular Pipe: $u(r) = u_{\max}(1 - (r/R)^2)$. Mean velocity at $r = 0.707 R$.
  • [ ] Parallel Plates: $u(y) = \frac{1}{2\mu}(-\frac{dp}{dx})(hy - y^2)$. Mean velocity at $y = 0.211 h$ from wall.
  • [ ] Wall shear stress: Pipe $\tau_w = \frac{\Delta P \cdot D}{4L}$; Plates $\tau_w = \frac{\Delta P \cdot h}{2L}$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Oil $\mu = 0.1 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ flows between two fixed parallel plates $10 \text{ mm}$ apart at a mean velocity of $1 \text{ m/s}$. Plate length is $2 \text{ m}$ and width is $0.5 \text{ m}$. Calculate maximum velocity and total pressure drop. - Solution:

$${u_{\max} = 1.5 V_{\text{avg}} = 1.5 \times 1 = 1.5 \text{ m/s}}$$
$${\Delta P = \frac{12 \mu V L}{h^2} = \frac{12 \times 0.1 \times 1 \times 2}{(0.01)^2} = \frac{2.4}{0.0001} = 24,000 \text{ Pa} = 24 \text{ kPa}}$$
- Key Insight: Use factor 12 for parallel plates $\Delta P = \frac{12 \mu V L}{h^2}$ instead of 32 used for circular pipes.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: For laminar flow between parallel plates separated by $h = 6 \text{ mm}$, the pressure gradient is $-100 \text{ kPa/m}$ $\mu = 0.05 \text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$. Find wall shear stress and maximum velocity. - Solution:

$${\tau_w = \frac{h}{2} \left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{0.006}{2} \times 100,000 = 300 \text{ Pa}}$$
$${u_{\max} = \frac{h^2}{8\mu} \left(-\frac{dp}{dx}\right) = \frac{(0.006)^2}{8 \times 0.05} \times 100,000 = \frac{0.000036 \times 100000}{0.40} = 9 \text{ m/s}}$$
- Key Insight: Maximum velocity occurs at midpoint $y = h/2$, giving $u_{\max} = \frac{h^2}{8\mu}(-\frac{dp}{dx})$.

3.3 Boundary Layer Thicknesses & Integral Methods

1. Concept Pointers
  • Displacement thickness $\delta^$ accounts for mass flow deficit: $\delta^ = \int_0^\delta \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$.
  • Momentum thickness $\theta$ accounts for momentum deficit: $\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_\infty}\right) dy$.
  • Energy thickness $\delta_e$ accounts for kinetic energy deficit.
  • Shape factor $H = \frac{\delta^*}{\theta}$ indicates velocity profile curvature and flow stability.
💡 Key Concept: Physical Order of Boundary Layer Thicknesses: For any standard attached velocity profile:
$${\theta < \delta^* < \delta_e < \delta}$$

[!TRAP] Displacement vs Momentum Thickness Ratios: - Linear Profile $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \frac{y}{\delta}$: $\frac{\delta^}{\delta} = \frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{\theta}{\delta} = \frac{1}{6}$, $H = 3.0$. - Parabolic Profile $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = 2\eta - \eta^2$: $\frac{\delta^}{\delta} = \frac{1}{3}$, $\frac{\theta}{\delta} = \frac{2}{15}$, $H = 2.5$. Memorize these ratios for quick MCQ calculations!

2. Decision Tree
Boundary Layer Integration Problem?
├─ Linear Profile (u/U = y/delta) → delta* = delta / 2, theta = delta / 6, H = 3.0
├─ Parabolic Profile (u/U = 2eta - eta^2) → delta* = delta / 3, theta = 2*delta / 15, H = 2.5
├─ Cubic Profile (u/U = 1.5eta - 0.5eta^3) → delta* = 3*delta / 8, theta = 39*delta / 280, H = 2.69
└─ Sinusoidal Profile (u/U = sin(pi*y / 2*delta)) → delta* = 0.363*delta, theta = 0.137*delta, H = 2.65
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] $\delta^* = \int_0^\delta (1 - u/U_\infty) dy$.
  • [ ] $\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U_\infty} (1 - u/U_\infty) dy$.
  • [ ] Shape factor $H = \delta^* / \theta$.
  • [ ] $\theta < \delta^* < \delta_e < \delta$ holds for all conventional attached profiles.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Given a parabolic velocity profile $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = 2\left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right) - \left(\frac{y}{\delta}\right)^2$ in a boundary layer of thickness $\delta = 15 \text{ mm}$, find $\delta^$ and $\theta$. - Solution:

$${\delta^ = \delta \int_0^1 (1 - 2\eta + \eta^2) d\eta = \delta \left[1 - 1 + \frac{1}{3}\right] = \frac{\delta}{3} = \frac{15}{3} = 5 \text{ mm}}$$
$${\theta = \delta \int_0^1 (2\eta - \eta^2)(1 - 2\eta + \eta^2) d\eta = \delta \int_0^1 (2\eta - 5\eta^2 + 4\eta^3 - \eta^4) d\eta}$$
$${\theta = \delta \left[1 - \frac{5}{3} + 1 - \frac{1}{5}\right] = \delta \left[2 - \frac{28}{15}\right] = \frac{2}{15} \delta = \frac{2}{15} \times 15 = 2 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: Parabolic profile gives $\delta^* = \frac{\delta}{3} = 5 \text{ mm}$ and $\theta = \frac{2}{15}\delta = 2 \text{ mm}$.

Worked Example 2 - Problem: For a linear velocity profile $\frac{u}{U_\infty} = \frac{y}{\delta}$, calculate the shape factor $H$. - Solution:

$${\delta^ = \frac{\delta}{2}, \quad \theta = \frac{\delta}{6}}$$
$${H = \frac{\delta^}{\theta} = \frac{\delta/2}{\delta/6} = \frac{6}{2} = 3.0}$$
- Key Insight: Linear profile shape factor is $3.0$.

3.4 Boundary Layer Growth & Separation Criteria

1. Concept Pointers
  • On a flat plate with zero pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx} = 0$, laminar boundary layer thickness grows as $\delta \propto x^{1/2}$ $\frac{\delta}{x} = \frac{5}{\sqrt{Re_x}}$.
  • Turbulent boundary layer grows faster as $\delta \propto x^{4/5}$ $\frac{\delta}{x} = \frac{0.37}{Re_x^{1/5}}$.
  • Separation occurs when near-wall fluid loses kinetic energy against an adverse pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$.
  • At the separation point: wall shear stress $\tau_w = 0 \implies \left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} = 0$.
💡 Key Concept: Summary of Separation Criteria: - Attached Flow: $\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|{y=0} > 0$ under $\frac{dp}{dx} \le 0$ - Point of Separation: $\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|{y=0} = 0$ under $\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$ - Detached / Reverse Flow: $\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} < 0$ under $\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$ (Wake region)

[!TRAP] Adverse vs Favorable Pressure Gradient: - Favorable Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} < 0$, pressure decreases in flow direction $\implies$ flow accelerates): Delays or prevents separation. - Adverse Gradient ($\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$, pressure increases in flow direction $\implies$ flow decelerates): Causes separation.

2. Decision Tree
Boundary Layer Separation Check?
├─ dp/dx <= 0 (Favorable / Zero Gradient) → Attached Flow (No separation)
├─ dp/dx > 0 and du/dy|_wall > 0 → Decelerating attached flow
├─ dp/dx > 0 and du/dy|_wall = 0 → Point of Separation (tau_w = 0)
└─ dp/dx > 0 and du/dy|_wall < 0 → Reverse Flow / Wake Region
3. Revision Checklist
  • [ ] Blasius solution: $\delta / x = 5 / \sqrt{Re_x}$.
  • [ ] Laminar BL thickness $\delta \propto x^{1/2}$; Turbulent BL thickness $\delta \propto x^{4/5}$.
  • [ ] Nikuradse smooth wall criterion: $k / \delta' < 0.25$.
  • [ ] Separation requires adverse pressure gradient $dp/dx > 0$.
4. Worked Examples

Worked Example 1 - Problem: Air flows over a flat plate at $10 \text{ m/s}$ $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m}^2/\text{s}$. Calculate the laminar boundary layer thickness $\delta$ at a distance $x = 0.5 \text{ m}$ from the leading edge. - Solution:

$${Re_x = \frac{U_\infty x}{\nu} = \frac{10 \times 0.5}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = \frac{5}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 333,333 \quad (< 5 \times 10^5 \implies \text{Laminar})}$$
$${\delta = \frac{5 x}{\sqrt{Re_x}} = \frac{5 \times 0.5}{\sqrt{333333}} = \frac{2.5}{577.35} = 0.00433 \text{ m} = 4.33 \text{ mm}}$$
- Key Insight: Use Blasius formula $\delta = 5 x / \sqrt{Re_x}$ for laminar boundary layer.

SECTION 4: THEORY Q&A (Core Conceptual)

Q1: Why is flow in a capillary tube always laminar?
A: Tube diameter $D$ is extremely small, resulting in low Reynolds number $Re < 2000$ even at high velocities.

Q2: At what radial position does average velocity occur in laminar pipe flow?
A: At $r = \frac{R}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.707 R$ from the pipe centerline.

Q3: How does head loss vary with flow velocity in laminar vs turbulent pipe flow?
A: In laminar flow, head loss is linearly proportional to velocity $h_f \propto V$; in turbulent flow, head loss is proportional to velocity squared $h_f \propto V^2$.

Q4: What is the Darcy friction factor formula for laminar pipe flow?
A: $f = \frac{64}{Re}$ (or Fanning friction factor $f' = \frac{16}{Re}$).

Q5: What is the distinguishing feature of turbulent flow?
A: Velocity and pressure at a fixed spatial point display continuous, high-frequency random fluctuations.

Q6: How does laminar boundary layer thickness grow with distance $x$ from the leading edge?
A: $\delta \propto \sqrt{x}$ (or $x^{1/2}$).

Q7: For fully rough turbulent pipe flow at high $Re$, what does the friction factor depend on?
A: Relative roughness $\epsilon/D$ only (independent of $Re$).

Q8: What are the kinetic energy and momentum correction factors for laminar pipe flow?
A: Kinetic energy correction factor $\alpha = 2.0$, momentum correction factor $\beta = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.33$.

Q9: Which forces dominate laminar flow between parallel plates?
A: Pressure forces and viscous forces (inertia forces are negligible).

Q10: When is a boundary wall considered hydrodynamically smooth?
A: When average roughness projection height $k$ is less than $0.25$ times the laminar sublayer thickness $\frac{k}{\delta'} < 0.25$.

Q11: If pipe diameter is halved at constant discharge $Q$ in turbulent flow, by what factor does friction loss increase?
A: 32 times $h_f \propto D^{-5} \implies 2^5 = 32$.

Q12: What causes boundary layer separation?
A: An adverse pressure gradient $\frac{dp}{dx} > 0$ decelerating near-wall fluid until wall shear stress drops to zero.

Q13: What condition marks the exact point of boundary layer separation?
A: Wall shear stress $\tau_w = 0$ (i.e. $\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0} = 0$).

Q14: Define nominal boundary layer thickness $\delta$.
A: The perpendicular distance from a solid wall to the location where local velocity $u$ reaches $99\%$ of free-stream velocity $U_\infty$.

Q15: What does displacement thickness $\delta^*$ physically represent?
A: The distance by which the external inviscid stream is displaced outward due to mass flow rate deficit within the boundary layer.
