1. Electrical Discharge Machining (EDM)

Principle & Working

  • EDM is an electro-thermal machining process.
  • Material removal occurs by spark erosion between tool and workpiece.
  • Spark energy causes localized melting and vaporization.
  • Both tool and workpiece must be electrically conductive.
  • No mechanical cutting force acts on the workpiece.

Spark Gap & Servo Mechanism

  • A small inter-electrode gap is maintained between tool and workpiece.
  • Servo mechanism maintains the spark gap automatically.
  • Proper gap prevents short circuiting and unstable sparking.

Dielectric Fluid

Common Dielectric: Kerosene-based oil is the most commonly used dielectric. Wire EDM commonly uses deionized water.

Functions of Dielectric:

  • Acts as an insulator before breakdown.
  • Controls spark discharge.
  • Helps spark formation after ionization.
  • Cools tool and workpiece.
  • Flushes away eroded particles.
MCQ Trap: If insulation, cooling, flushing, and spark control are listed together → All are correct.

Tool Materials

Common EDM electrode materials: Copper, Graphite, Brass, Tellurium copper.

Most common exam answer: Copper.

Temperature

  • Spark temperature ≈ 10,000°C.
  • Thermal energy is responsible for material removal.

Surface Finish & Pulse Energy

Surface quality depends on pulse energy:

$$\mathbf{E_p = V \cdot I \cdot t_{\text{on}}}$$
  • Better Surface Finish: High frequency, low discharge current, low pulse energy, small crater size.
  • Poor Surface Finish: Low frequency, high current, large pulse energy, deep craters.

Frequency Relation

$$\mathbf{f = \frac{1}{t_{\text{on}} + t_{\text{off}}}}$$

If $t_{\text{on}} \ll t_{\text{off}} \implies \mathbf{f \approx \frac{1}{t_{\text{off}}}}$

Effect of Current

  • Current ↑ → Crater size ↑ → Surface roughness ↑.
  • Finishing operation uses low current.

Material Removal Rate (MRR)

  • Maximum MRR ≈ 5000 mm³/min.
  • EDM MRR is lower than ECM.

Applications, Advantages & Limitations

Applications: Hardened die steels, tool steels, carbides, intricate cavities, thin delicate conductive components.

Advantages: Machines very hard conductive materials, good dimensional accuracy, complex cavity generation, zero cutting force.

Limitations: Low MRR, tool wear, heat affected zone (HAZ), recast layer, conductive materials only.

Important EDM MCQ Traps:
  • EDM can machine thin sections without distortion.
  • Copper is the most common electrode.
  • Kerosene is the common dielectric.
  • Servo mechanism maintains spark gap, NOT dielectric flow rate.

2. Electrochemical Machining (ECM)

Principle & Arrangement

  • Material removal occurs by anodic dissolution at the atomic level.
  • Based on Faraday's laws of electrolysis.
  • Workpiece = Anode (+) • Tool = Cathode (−).

Electrolytes & Inter-Electrode Gap

  • Common electrolytes: NaCl, NaNO₃.
  • Preferred electrolyte for Copper alloys: Sodium nitrate (NaNO₃).
  • Inter-electrode gap: 0.1–0.6 mm (Common exam value: 0.5 mm).

Characteristics

  • Surface Finish: Excellent, mirror finish, zero thermal damage, zero residual stress.
  • Tool Wear: Negligible.
  • Cutting Forces & HAZ: Negligible / Absent.
  • MRR: Highest among common non-traditional machining processes.

Faraday's Law Formulas (Most Important)

Mass Removed ($m$):

$$\mathbf{m = \frac{M \cdot I \cdot t}{z \cdot F}}$$

Volumetric MRR ($Q$):

$$\mathbf{Q = \frac{M \cdot I}{z \cdot F \cdot \rho}}$$

Required Current ($I$):

$$\mathbf{I = \frac{Q \cdot z \cdot F \cdot \rho}{M} = \frac{m \cdot z \cdot F}{M \cdot t}}$$

Where: $M$ = atomic weight, $z$ = valency, $F = 96500\text{ C/mol}$, $\rho$ = density.

Current Calculation Problem Hint (for Iron):
Given $M = 56, z = 2, F = 96500\text{ C/mol} \implies$ Always use $\mathbf{I = \frac{m \cdot z \cdot F}{M \cdot t}}$.

Advantages & Limitations

Advantages: Very high MRR, mirror finish, zero tool wear, no thermal damage, no burrs, no residual stress, suitable for superalloys (Nimonic, Inconel).

Limitations: High capital cost, electrolyte handling/corrosion problems, stray machining overcut, conductive materials only.

Sharp Corner Limitation: Current spreads in electrolyte causing lateral dissolution → Internal sharp corners are difficult to produce (True in MCQs).

ECG (Electrochemical Grinding): Combination of ECM + grinding; electrolyte acts as both electrolyte & coolant.

3. Ultrasonic Machining (USM)

Principle & Operating Parameters

  • Material removal occurs by abrasive impact and brittle fracture.
  • Tool vibrates at high frequency and low amplitude while abrasive slurry flows in the gap.
ParameterTypical Value
Frequency20–30 kHz
Amplitude0.01–0.06 mm

Tool & Work Materials

Tool Material: Must be ductile and tough (Mild steel, Brass, Copper, Stainless steel).

MCQ Trap: Carbides and ceramics are brittle and NOT used as USM tools.

Abrasives: Boron carbide ($B_4C$) — most common, Silicon carbide (SiC), $Al_2O_3$.

Best Work Materials: Glass, ceramics, quartz, sapphire, diamond, carbides (hard & brittle non-conductors).

Parameter Effects on USM MRR

  • Grain Size Effect: Grain size ↑ → MRR ↑ initially up to optimum size; larger particles reduce active participation → MRR ↓.
  • Amplitude Effect: Amplitude ↑ → MRR ↑.
  • Frequency Effect: Maximum MRR occurs near resonance frequency.
  • Material Property Effect: Toughness ↓ → MRR ↑ • Brittleness ↑ → MRR ↑ • Ductility ↑ → MRR ↓.
Important USM MCQ Traps:
  • USM uses abrasive slurry.
  • Tool is ductile (mild steel/brass).
  • Work material is preferably hard & brittle.
  • Frequency is 20–30 kHz.
