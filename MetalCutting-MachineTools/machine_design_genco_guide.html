MODULE 1: FAILURE THEORIES & STATIC / DYNAMIC LOADING

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers theoretical and actual stress concentration in static and dynamic loading, fracture mechanics fundamentals (Griffith equation), fatigue failure mechanisms, S-N diagrams, endurance limit and its modifying factors ($k_a, k_b, k_c, k_d, k_e$), fluctuating load design criteria (Goodman, Soderberg, Gerber, Langer), and notch sensitivity ($q$).

Exam Relevance
Extremely high for AP Genco AE / AP PSC AEE. Heavy focus on endurance limit calculations, reversed loading, notch effects, and conservative criteria selection.

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Factor of Safety (Static & Fatigue)
$$\boxed{\text{FOS} = \frac{S_{yt}}{\sigma_{\text{working}}}} \quad \text{(Ductile material, static)}$$
$$\boxed{\text{FOS} = \frac{S_e}{\sigma_a}} \quad \text{(Completely reversed fatigue loading)}$$
Theoretical Stress Concentration Factor ($K_t$)
$$\boxed{K_t = \frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{\text{nom}}}}$$
• Circular hole in infinite plate under tension: $K_t = 3$
• Transverse elliptical hole ($2a \perp$ load, $2b \parallel$ load): $$\boxed{K_t = 1 + 2\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)}$$
Griffith Tensile Fracture Strength (Brittle, Crack Length $2c$)
$$\boxed{\sigma_f = \sqrt{\frac{2E\gamma}{\pi c}}}$$

Endurance Limit Modifying Factors (Marin Equation)

$$\boxed{S_e = k_a \cdot k_b \cdot k_c \cdot k_d \cdot k_e \cdot S_e'}$$
Factor Property Represented Typical Trend / Rule
$k_a$Surface finish factor$k_a = 1.0$ for ground; decreases for machined, hot-rolled, forged
$k_b$Size factor$k_b = 1.0$ for $d \le 8\text{ mm}$; decreases as diameter $d$ increases
$k_c$Reliability factor$k_c = 1.0$ ($50\%$), $0.868$ ($90\%$), $0.753$ ($99.9\%$)
$k_d$Temperature factor$k_d = 1.0$ for $T \le 450^\circ\text{C}$
$k_e$Load factor$k_e = 1.0$ (bending), $0.85$ (axial), $0.577$ (torsion)
$S_e'$Standard specimen limit$S_e' \approx 0.5 S_{ut}$ (steels, $S_{ut} \le 1400\text{ MPa}$)

Fluctuating Load Failure Criteria (Factor of Safety = $N$)

Soderberg Line (Most Conservative, based on Yield Strength $S_{yt}$)
$$\boxed{\frac{\sigma_a}{S_e} + \frac{\sigma_m}{S_{yt}} = \frac{1}{N}}$$
Goodman Line (Standard Engineering Design, based on Ultimate $S_{ut}$)
$$\boxed{\frac{\sigma_a}{S_e} + \frac{\sigma_m}{S_{ut}} = \frac{1}{N}}$$
Gerber Parabola (Fits Average Test Data)
$$\boxed{\frac{\sigma_a}{S_e} + \left( \frac{\sigma_m}{S_{ut}} \right)^2 = \frac{1}{N}}$$
Notch Sensitivity ($q$) & Fatigue Stress Factor ($K_f$)
$$\boxed{q = \frac{K_f - 1}{K_t - 1} \quad (0 \le q \le 1)} \implies \boxed{K_f = 1 + q(K_t - 1)}$$

Part B: Deep Dive by Concept

1. Static Stress Concentration & Fracture Mechanics

Stress concentration arises from abrupt geometry discontinuities (holes, fillets, keyways, grooves).

  • Static Load Behavior: In ductile materials, local yielding redistributes stress peaks under static load, so $K_t \approx 1$. In brittle materials, no plastic yielding occurs, so $K_t$ must be applied even under static loading.
  • $K_t$ depends purely on geometry and loading mode, independent of material properties.
Worked Example (APPSC AEE 2016)
Question: A wide plate containing a small circular hole is subjected to remote uniaxial tension stress $f$. Calculate the maximum tensile stress occurring in the plate.
Solution: $K_t = 3 \implies \sigma_{\max} = K_t \times f = 3f$.
Answer: $3f$

3. S-N Diagram and Fatigue Strength

Fatigue failure occurs under cyclic loading at stress levels well below ultimate tensile strength $S_{ut}$. Low Cycle Fatigue (LCF) occurs for $N \le 10^3$ cycles, while High Cycle Fatigue (HCF) occurs for $N > 10^3$ cycles. Endurance limit $S_e \approx 0.5 S_{ut}$ for steels with $S_{ut} \le 1400\text{ MPa}$.

4. Design for Fluctuating Loads

Fluctuating stress cycle consists of mean stress $\sigma_m = \frac{\sigma_{\max} + \sigma_{\min}}{2}$ and stress amplitude $\sigma_a = \frac{\sigma_{\max} - \sigma_{\min}}{2}$.

Worked Example (APPSC AEE 2012)
Question: Steel rod under completely reversed axial load. $S_{ut} = 600\text{ MPa}$, $S_{yt} = 400\text{ MPa}$, $S_e = 200\text{ MPa}$. Find max allowable stress amplitude $\sigma_a$ for infinite life with $\text{FOS} = 2$.
Solution: For reversed load, $\sigma_m = 0 \implies \sigma_a = S_e / \text{FOS} = 200 / 2 = 100\text{ MPa}$.
Answer: $100\text{ MPa}$

MODULE 2: FASTENERS & JOINTS

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers threaded fasteners, power screw mechanics (torque, effort, efficiency, self-locking), bolted joint stiffness & tightening, special threaded elements, welded joints (fillet and butt welds), riveted joints (failure modes, pitch, efficiency, boiler joints), and cotter joints.

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Power Screw Torque & Effort
$$\boxed{P = W \tan(\alpha \pm \phi)} \quad \text{(+ for lifting, - for lowering)}$$
$$\boxed{\text{Torque } T = \frac{W d_m}{2} \tan(\alpha + \phi)}$$
• Self-locking condition: Friction angle $\phi \ge$ Helix angle $\alpha$ ($\mu \ge \tan\alpha$).
• Maximum Efficiency (Square Threads): $$\boxed{\eta_{\max} = \frac{1 - \sin\phi}{1 + \sin\phi} < 50\%}$$
Welded Joints (Fillet & Butt)
• Throat thickness of $45^\circ$ fillet weld: $\boxed{t = h \sin 45^\circ = 0.707 h}$
• Transverse fillet load capacity: $\boxed{P = 0.707 h \cdot l \cdot \sigma_t}$
• Double parallel fillet shear capacity: $\boxed{P = 1.414 h \cdot l \cdot \tau}$
Riveted Joint Efficiency ($\eta$)
$$\boxed{\eta = \frac{\min(P_t, P_s, P_c)}{p \cdot t \cdot \sigma_t} \times 100\%}$$
Cotter Joint Key Parameters
• Connects two co-axial rods under axial tension/compression.
• Cotter taper: 1 in 24 to 1 in 48 (provided on one side only).
• Standard thickness: $t \approx 0.31d$ to $0.4d$.

Part B: Deep Dive by Concept

1. Power Screws — Self-Locking & Efficiency

  • Square Threads: Highest efficiency ($\eta$), zero radial bursting force.
  • Acme / Metric Trapezoidal Threads: $2\beta = 29^\circ / 30^\circ$, easier to cut, splitting action on nuts.
  • Buttress Threads: Transmits power in one direction only with square thread efficiency.
  • Self-locking Condition: $\phi \ge \alpha$. Maximum efficiency is always $< 50\%$.
Worked Example (APPSC AEE 2016)
Question: A square thread power screw has a friction angle $\phi = 15^\circ$. What is the maximum helix angle $\alpha$ for self-locking?
Solution: $\alpha_{\max} = \phi = 15^\circ$.
Answer: $15^\circ$

2. Bolted Joints — Resilience

A shock-absorbing resilient bolt is made by reducing its shank diameter equal to the root diameter ($d_s = d_c$) or drilling a central axial hole through the shank up to the threaded section.

3. Fillet & Butt Welded Joints

Fillet welds are always designed based on shear failure occurring across the minimum throat cross-section ($t = 0.707 h$).

Worked Example (ESE 2017)
Question: Plate width $100\text{ mm}$, thickness $10\text{ mm}$, single transverse fillet weld leg size $7\text{ mm}$, allowable tensile stress $100\text{ MPa}$. Find load capacity.
Solution: $t = 0.707 \times 7 = 4.949\text{ mm} \implies P = 4.949 \times 100 \times 100 = 49,490\text{ N} = 49.49\text{ kN}$.
Answer: $49.49\text{ kN}$

MODULE 3: SHAFTS, KEYS & COUPLINGS

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers solid and hollow transmission shafts under torsion and combined loading (bending + torsion), equivalent twisting moment $T_e$, equivalent bending moment $M_e$, key types (square, Woodruff, feather, Kennedy, saddle, tangent), key stress relations ($\sigma_c = 2\tau$), and shaft couplings (rigid vs flexible, Oldham's, Hooke's joint).

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Torsional Shear Stress in Shafts
$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{16 T}{\pi d^3}} \quad \text{(Solid shaft)}$$
$$\boxed{\tau = \frac{16 T}{\pi d_o^3 (1 - k^4)}} \quad \text{(Hollow shaft, } k = d_i/d_o\text{)}$$
Combined Bending ($M$) and Torsion ($T$)
• Equivalent Twisting Moment: $$\boxed{T_e = \sqrt{M^2 + T^2}} \implies \boxed{\tau_{\max} = \frac{16 T_e}{\pi d^3}}$$
• Equivalent Bending Moment: $$\boxed{M_e = \frac{1}{2}\left[ M + \sqrt{M^2 + T^2} \right]} \implies \boxed{\sigma_{\max} = \frac{32 M_e}{\pi d^3}}$$
Square Key Stress Relation
For square key ($w = t = d/4$): $$\boxed{\sigma_c = 2\tau}$$ (Crushing stress is twice shear stress)

Coupling Selection Matrix

Alignment Condition Recommended Coupling Type Key Feature
Perfectly aligned shaftsMuff / Sleeve, Flange CouplingRigid, simple, positive drive
Small radial & angular offset + shocksBushed-Pin Flexible CouplingRubber bushes cushion shocks
Parallel shafts with small lateral offsetOldham's CouplingIntermediate sliding disc
Intersecting shafts with angular offsetHooke's / Universal JointPropeller shafts, steering drives

Part B: Deep Dive by Concept

1. Solid vs Hollow Shafts

For the same material and weight, a hollow shaft is stronger and stiffer in torsion than a solid shaft because its material is located farther from the neutral axis.

Worked Example (ESE 2018)
Question: Shaft subjected to $M = 3.46\text{ kNm}$ and $T = 11.5\text{ kNm}$. Find $T_e$.
Solution: $T_e = \sqrt{(3.46)^2 + (11.5)^2} = \sqrt{11.97 + 132.25} = 12.0\text{ kNm}$.
Answer: $12.0\text{ kNm}$

2. Keys — Types & Applications

  • Square Key: Equal strength in shear and crushing when $\sigma_c = 2\tau$.
  • Woodruff Key: Semi-circular, self-aligning; ideal for tapered shafts.
  • Feather Key: Permits axial sliding motion of hub on shaft.
  • Kennedy Key: Two square keys at $90^\circ$ for heavy-duty industrial drives.
  • Saddle Key: Power transmitted purely by friction (no keyway in shaft).

MODULE 4: BEARINGS

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers rolling contact bearings (deep groove, roller, tapered, spherical), dynamic rating load $C$, bearing life $L_{10}$, equivalent radial load $P_e$, ISO bearing designation, and journal bearings (hydrodynamic vs hydrostatic, Sommerfeld number, Petroff's formula, clearance ratio).

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Rolling Contact Bearings (Life & Load)
$$\boxed{L_{10} = \left(\frac{C}{P_e}\right)^p}$$
• $p = 3$ (for Ball Bearings)
• $p = \frac{10}{3} = 3.33$ (for Roller Bearings)
• Equivalent Radial Load: $\boxed{P_e = X \cdot F_r + Y \cdot F_a}$
Journal Bearings (Hydrodynamic & Hydrostatic)
• Petroff's Equation: $$\boxed{f = 2\pi^2 \left(\frac{\mu N'}{P}\right)\left(\frac{r}{c}\right)}$$
• Sommerfeld Number ($S$): $$\boxed{S = \left(\frac{r}{c}\right)^2 \left(\frac{\mu N'}{P}\right)}$$
• Diametral clearance ratio: $\frac{c_d}{d} \approx 0.001$ ($1\text{ }\mu\text{m per mm diameter}$).

Part B: Deep Dive by Concept

1. Rolling Contact Bearings

  • Deep Groove Ball Bearing: High speeds, combined radial and axial load.
  • Tapered Roller Bearing: Designed for heavy radial and heavy axial thrust loads.
  • ISO Bearing Designation (e.g., 6208): Last two digits $\times 5 =$ Bore diameter ($08 \implies 40\text{ mm}$).
Worked Example (APPSC AEE 2016)
Question: Ball bearing radial load $4\text{ kN}$, life $8000\text{ hrs}$. If load is doubled to $8\text{ kN}$, find new life.
Solution: $L \propto 1/P^3 \implies L_2 = 8000 \times (1/2)^3 = 8000/8 = 1000\text{ hrs}$.
Answer: $1000\text{ hours}$

2. Journal Bearings

Hydrodynamic: Self-acting wedge action during rotation. Hydrostatic: High-pressure oil supplied by external pump before rotation (zero starting friction).

MODULE 6: BRAKES & CLUTCHES

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers mechanical brakes (block/shoe brakes, long shoe equivalent friction $\mu_{eq}$, band brakes, internal expanding shoe brakes, disk brakes) and friction clutches (uniform wear theory vs uniform pressure theory, multi-plate clutches, centrifugal clutches).

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Friction Clutches
• Uniform Wear Theory (Safe Design): $$\boxed{T = \mu \cdot W \cdot R_m \quad \left(R_m = \frac{r_1 + r_2}{2}\right)}$$
• Uniform Pressure Theory (New Clutch): $$\boxed{T = \frac{2}{3} \mu \cdot W \left( \frac{r_1^3 - r_2^3}{r_1^2 - r_2^2} \right)}$$
• Multi-Plate Contact Pairs: $$\boxed{n = n_1 + n_2 - 1} \implies \boxed{T = n \cdot \mu \cdot W \cdot R_m}$$
Brakes
• Long Shoe Block Brake ($2\theta > 60^\circ$): Use equivalent friction $$\boxed{\mu_{eq} = \mu \left[ \frac{4 \sin\theta}{2\theta + \sin 2\theta} \right]}$$

Part B: Deep Dive by Concept

1. Friction Clutches

Uniform Wear Theory assumes $p \cdot r = C$ and max pressure at inner radius $r_2$; gives lower torque capacity $\implies$ conservative for design. Single plate clutches are used in trucks, while multi-plate clutches are used in scooters.

Worked Example (SJVN ET 2019)
Question: Single plate clutch, both sides effective ($n=2$), $W = 1\text{ kN}$, $r_1 = 150\text{ mm}$, $r_2 = 100\text{ mm}$, $\mu = 0.5$. Calculate torque capacity (Uniform Wear).
Solution: $R_m = (150+100)/2 = 125\text{ mm} = 0.125\text{ m} \implies T = 2 \times 0.5 \times 1000 \times 0.125 = 125\text{ Nm}$.
Answer: $125\text{ Nm}$

MCQ Primer — Brakes & Clutches

Concept Key Point / Formula Frequency
Safe clutch designAlways use Uniform Wear Theory: $T = \mu W R_m$Very High
Multi-plate surfacesActive pairs $n = n_1 + n_2 - 1$Very High
Clutch applicationsTrucks $\to$ Single plate; Scooters $\to$ Multi-plateHigh

MODULE 7: POWER SCREWS

Stage 1 — Topic Overview

This module covers thread profiles for power screws, torque required to raise and lower loads, screw jack mechanics, efficiency, and self-locking conditions.

Stage 2 — Theory Briefing

Part A: Quick-Reference Card

Effort & Torque in Power Screws
• Effort to raise load: $\boxed{P_{\text{raise}} = W \tan(\alpha + \phi)}$
• Effort to lower load: $\boxed{P_{\text{lower}} = W \tan(\phi - \alpha)}$
• Torque: $$\boxed{T = \frac{W d_m}{2} \tan(\alpha + \phi)}$$
• Self-locking condition: $\phi \ge \alpha$ ($\mu \ge \tan\alpha$).
