JIGS & FIXTURES
Ultra-High-Yield Revision Sheet • APGENCO AE / SSC JE / GATE

PART 1 — 📋 JIGS vs FIXTURES

⚙️ Basic Definitions

  • Jig = device that holds + locates workpiece + guides cutting tool.
  • Fixture = device that holds + locates workpiece during manufacturing/inspection; does not guide the cutting tool.
  • Jigs are mainly associated with drilling, reaming, tapping, boring and similar tool-guiding operations.
  • Turning generally does not use a jig; tool is guided by the lathe/tool post.
  • Both jigs and fixtures improve accuracy, repeatability, interchangeability, productivity and safety.

🎯 MCQ Traps (Must Memorize):
  • “Jig is only a locating device” → FALSE; it also holds and guides the tool.
  • “Fixture guides the cutting tool” → FALSE.
  • “Turning uses a jig” → FALSE in standard machining terminology.
  • “Jigs/fixtures do not improve production rate” → FALSE.
  • “Higher cutting speeds cannot be used with jigs/fixtures” → FALSE.

PART 2 — 📍 FUNCTIONS & ADVANTAGES

⚙️ Major Functions

  • Location → establishes the correct position of the workpiece.
  • Clamping → firmly holds workpiece against locating surfaces.
  • Tool guidance → function of jigs through bushes/guide elements.
  • Repeatability → same position for every workpiece.
  • Accuracy → reduces operator-dependent positioning errors.
  • Safety → reduces manual handling and holding of workpieces.
  • Productivity → rapid loading/unloading and reduced setup time.

🔑 Advantages

  • Rapid production due to quick and repeatable setup.
  • Higher production rate permits increased machine utilization.
  • Higher speeds/feeds can often be used because the work is rigidly located and clamped.
  • Consistent dimensions and improved interchangeability.
  • Reduced manufacturing cost in mass/batch production.
  • Automatic/positive location reduces operator skill requirement.
  • Improved operator safety.
🚨 Important Exam Questions (Identify FALSE statements):
• NOT true for jigs & fixtures: “Fast production speed is not possible” → FALSE statement
• NOT true: “Higher speeds cannot be used” → FALSE statement

PART 3 — 📍 WORKPIECE LOCATION

⚙️ 3-2-1 Locating Principle

  • 3-2-1 principle uses 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 locating points.
  • Primary datum plane → 3 points.
  • Secondary datum plane → 2 points.
  • Tertiary datum plane → 1 point.
  • Purpose = obtain stable, repeatable and deterministic location of a rectangular workpiece.

📌 What does 3, 2, 1 mean?

DatumLocating pointsMain role
Primary3Establishes base/support plane
Secondary2Establishes second reference
Tertiary1Establishes final reference

3 in “3-2-1” = number of locating positions/points on the primary face (does not mean number of clamps or setups).

⚠️ 6 DOF vs 9 Movements — VERY IMPORTANT EXAM DISTINCTION:
  • A rigid body in 3D space has 6 Degrees of Freedom (DOF): 3 translational + 3 rotational.
  • In jig/fixture MCQs, movement is often counted as 12 possible positive/negative direction movements ($+X, -X, +Y, -Y, +Z, -Z$ + 6 rotations).
  • 3-2-1 locators are commonly stated to arrest 9 movements.
  • The remaining 3 movements are resisted by clamping.
  • Therefore, exam convention: 6 locating points → 9 movements arrested by locators → 3 by clamps → 12 total movements.
🎯 Exam Rule Summary:
• Asked “DOF of rigid body” → 6
• Asked “movements restricted by 3-2-1 locating system” → 9
• Asked “number of locating points in 3-2-1” → 6
• Asked “total positive/negative movements” → 12

PART 4 — 📍 LOCATING DEVICES

⚙️ V-Block

  • V-block is used to locate/support cylindrical jobs.
  • Provides stable line/edge contact with the cylindrical surface.
  • Common application = round/cylindrical workpieces.

🔑 Typical Matching

Workpiece / RequirementLocator
Cylindrical jobV-block
Two-hole location with toleranceRound pin + diamond pin
Flat rectangular job3-2-1 locating system

⚙️ Diamond Locating Pin

  • Diamond pin is primarily a locating device, not a clamping device.
  • Used with a round locating pin when two holes are used for location.
  • Main purpose = accommodate variation in centre distance between two holes.
  • Prevents binding/over-constraint caused by hole-spacing tolerances.
  • Diamond shape occupies less constraint in one direction while maintaining location.
🎯 MCQ Trap:
Diamond pin used because “diamond is hard/wear-resistant” → NOT the primary reason.
Correct reason = accommodates tolerance on centre distance between two holes.

PART 5 — 🔒 CLAMPING DEVICES

⚙️ Common Clamping Devices

  • Strap clamp
  • Toggle clamp
  • Swing latch / swing clamp
  • Quick-acting nut
  • C-clamp
  • Equaliser clamp

🔑 Strap Clamp

  • Strap clamp = simplest and low-cost clamp used in jigs/fixtures.
  • Basic construction → strap + bolt + support.
  • Used where simple, economical clamping is required.

PART 6 — 🧭 LOCATION vs CLAMPING

  • Location determines where the workpiece is.
  • Clamping maintains the workpiece against the locators.
  • Clamps should not disturb the established location.
  • Proper design sequence → Locate first → Clamp second.
  • Excessive locating points may cause redundancy, binding and inaccuracy.
🔑 Common Trap:
• Clamping device ≠ locating device.
• A strap clamp is not a locator.
• A diamond pin is not a clamp.

PART 7 — 🔄 FLOATING FIXTURE IN ROTARY TRANSFER

  • Floating holding fixture permits small relative movement/self-alignment.
  • Used to compensate for small locating/indexing errors.
  • Main purpose in competitive exam MCQs = improve accuracy of location.
  • Floating arrangement helps prevent rigid misalignment between fixture and locating elements.
🎯 MCQ Answer: Floating fixture → improves location accuracy

PART 8 — 🤖 ROBOT AXIS MOVEMENT

  • Robot axis movement may involve elbow rotation.
  • Wrist rotation is another axis movement.
  • X-Y coordinate motion represents Cartesian movement.
  • Hence, when all are listed together, the appropriate answer is elbow + wrist + X-Y coordinate motion.

PART 9 — 📐 TURNING SURFACE ROUGHNESS

⚙️ Round-Nose Tool Formula

For turning with Feed = $f$ and Nose radius = $r$, theoretical peak-to-valley surface roughness is given by:

$$\mathbf{h = \frac{f^2}{8r}}$$

Where:
$h$ = peak-to-valley roughness height
$f$ = feed per revolution
$r$ = tool nose radius

  • Roughness increases strongly with feed → ($h \propto f^2$).
  • Roughness decreases with larger nose radius → ($h \propto 1/r$).

🔑 Related Formula (Arithmetic Average)

$$\mathbf{R_a \approx \frac{f^2}{32r}}$$
🚨 MCQ Trap:
• $h = \frac{f^2}{8r}$ → Theoretical maximum peak-to-valley roughness.
• $R_a = \frac{f^2}{32r}$ → Approximate arithmetic average roughness.

PART 11 — 🚨 MOST IMPORTANT EXAM TRAPS

  • Jig ≠ fixture: jig guides tool; fixture does not.
  • V-block ≠ jig: V-block is a locating/supporting device for cylindrical work.
  • Diamond pin ≠ clamp: diamond pin is a locator.
  • Strap clamp ≠ locator: strap clamp is a clamping device.
  • 6 DOF ≠ 9 movements: 6 = rigid-body DOF; 9 = commonly used 3-2-1 locator movement count.
  • 3-2-1 has 6 locating points, not 9.
  • 9 movements + 3 clamping movements = 12 total movements in conventional fixture-design treatment.
  • Turning generally does not use a jig because tool guidance comes from the lathe.
  • Diamond pin is selected for tolerance accommodation, not primarily because of hardness.
  • Floating fixture improves locating accuracy, not primarily production speed.
