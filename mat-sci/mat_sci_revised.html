1. Atomic Bonding in Solids

Atomic bonding dictates mechanical ductility, electrical/thermal conductivity, optical transparency, and melting point in engineering materials.

Bond TypeMechanismTypical MaterialsKey Properties
Metallic Delocalised electron "sea" surrounding positive ion cores Metals, alloys (Fe, Cu, Al) Ductile, malleable, high thermal/electrical conductivity, opaque
Covalent Directional shared electron pairs between specific valence shells Diamond, Si, SiC, polymers Extremely hard, brittle, high MP, electrical insulator (usually)
Ionic Electrostatic attraction between cation (+) and anion (-) NaCl, MgO, Al₂O₃ ceramics Hard, brittle, high MP, insulator when solid, conductor when molten
Van der Waals Secondary weak fluctuating or permanent dipole attractions Inter-chain polymer bonds, paraffin Weakest bond, low MP, flexible, low strength
💡 Bond Energy Order: Covalent ≈ Ionic > Metallic >> Van der Waals.
Melting point directly scales with bond energy: Diamond (covalent) > MgO (ionic) > Iron (metallic) > Polymers (Van der Waals).

2. Crystal Structures & Allotropy

BCC (Body-Centred Cubic)
Atoms/cell ($N$): 2
APF: 0.68
Coordination No.: 8
Lattice Relation: $4r = a\sqrt{3}$
Metals: $\alpha$-Fe (<912°C), W, Cr, Mo, V
Note: Less dense; no close-packed planes → higher yield strength, lower ductility than FCC
FCC (Face-Centred Cubic)
Atoms/cell ($N$): 4
APF: 0.74 (maximum)
Coordination No.: 12
Lattice Relation: $4r = a\sqrt{2}$
Metals: $\gamma$-Fe (912–1394°C), Al, Cu, Ni, Pb, Au
Note: Maximum packing density; {111} close-packed planes → most ductile
HCP (Hexagonal Close-Packed)
Atoms/cell ($N$): 6
APF: 0.74
Coordination No.: 12
Ideal $c/a$ ratio: 1.633
Metals: Mg, Zn, Ti, Be, Co
Note: Same APF as FCC, but fewer slip systems → lower room temp ductility
Unit Cell Atom Calculation Formula
$$N = \frac{N_c}{8} + \frac{N_f}{2} + \frac{N_e}{4} + N_i$$

Where $N_c$ = corner atoms (shared by 8 cells), $N_f$ = face-centre atoms (shared by 2 cells), $N_e$ = edge-centre atoms (shared by 4 cells), $N_i$ = interior atoms (100% inside).

⚠️ Exam Trap: FCC Austenite dissolves nearly 100 times more carbon (2.14%) than BCC Ferrite (0.022%) because FCC has larger interstitial octahedral voids (0.414r vs 0.291r in BCC). This dramatic solubility collapse during cooling forms the base for martensitic hardening!

3. Crystal Imperfections & Strengthening

Point Defects (0D)

DefectDescriptionEffect on Properties
VacancyMissing atom at lattice siteEnables solid-state atomic diffusion; equilibrium concentration increases exponentially with temp
InterstitialExtra small atom crammed in voidCauses severe local lattice distortion; hardens matrix (e.g., C in Fe)
SubstitutionalHost atom replaced by solute atomForms solid solution; strength depends on atomic size mismatch
Schottky DefectCation-anion vacancy pair in ionic crystalMaintains charge neutrality; lowers bulk density
Frenkel DefectIon moves to interstitial site, leaving vacancyCation displacement in ionic crystal; density unchanged

Line Defects (1D Dislocations)

Dislocation TypeBurgers Vector ($\mathbf{b}$) vs Line ($\mathbf{t}$)Motion DirectionDeformation Mechanism
Edge Dislocation Perpendicular ($\mathbf{b} \perp \mathbf{t}$) Parallel to Burgers vector Extra half-plane of atoms inserted in lattice
Screw Dislocation Parallel ($\mathbf{b} \parallel \mathbf{t}$) Perpendicular to Burgers vector Helical spiral ramp structure around dislocation line
Hall-Petch Relation — Grain Size Strengthening
$$\sigma_y = \sigma_0 + k_y \cdot d^{-1/2}$$

$\sigma_y$ = yield strength, $\sigma_0$ = friction stress, $k_y$ = locking parameter, $d$ = average grain diameter.
Grain refinement is the ONLY strengthening mechanism that simultaneously increases both yield strength AND impact toughness!

Classification of Steels & Cast Irons
  • Hypoeutectoid Steel (0.008% – 0.80% C): Microstructure = Proeutectoid Ferrite + Pearlite
  • Eutectoid Steel (0.80% C): Microstructure = 100% Pearlite
  • Hypereutectoid Steel (0.80% – 2.14% C): Microstructure = Pearlite + Proeutectoid Cementite network
  • Cast Irons (2.14% – 6.67% C): Commercial range 2.5% – 4.0% C
Lever Rule Formula (Phase Fraction)
$$W_\alpha = \frac{C_\beta - C_0}{C_\beta - C_\alpha} \times 100\%$$

$C_0$ = overall alloy composition, $C_\alpha, C_\beta$ = phase boundaries of tie-line at given temperature.

6. Manufacture of Iron & Steel

Ironmaking — Blast Furnace

Iron ore (Hematite $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$), Coke (fuel & reductant), and Limestone ($\text{CaCO}_3$ flux) are charged at the top. Hot air blast is injected through tuyeres.

Blast Furnace Reaction Chemistry
  • Combustion & Gas Generation: $\text{C} + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow \text{CO}_2 \quad (\Delta H < 0)$; $\text{CO}_2 + \text{C} \rightarrow 2\text{CO}$
  • Reduction of Ore: $3\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{CO} \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + \text{CO}_2 \rightarrow 6\text{FeO} \rightarrow 6\text{Fe} + 6\text{CO}_2$
  • Fluxing & Slag Formation: $\text{CaCO}_3 \rightarrow \text{CaO} + \text{CO}_2$; $\text{CaO} + \text{SiO}_2 \rightarrow \text{CaSiO}_3\text{ (Slag)}$

Product = Pig Iron (~4% C, High Si, Mn, P, S). Very brittle, unforgeable.

Steelmaking Processes

Process RoutePrimary Raw MaterialRefining ActionKey Features
Basic Oxygen Furnace (BOF / LD) 75% Molten Pig Iron + 25% Scrap Supersonic $O_2$ lance burns out C, Si, Mn Dominant global steelmaking process; fast tap time (40 min)
Electric Arc Furnace (EAF) 100% Cold Steel Scrap / DRI Graphite electrodes melt charge via high-current arc Used for high-alloy, stainless, and tool steels
Deoxidation Types Killed Steel ($\text{Al}/\text{Si}$ added) Semi-killed Steel / Rimmed Steel ($\text{FeMnO}$) Killed steel has zero gas porosity, uniform properties

7. Heat Treatment of Steel

Annealing
Heat $30\text{--}50^\circ\text{C}$ above $A_3$ (hypo) or $A_1$ (hyper). Slow furnace cool. Result: Maximum softness, stress relief, coarse pearlite.
Normalising
Heat $30\text{--}50^\circ\text{C}$ above $A_3$ or $A_{cm}$. Air cool in still air. Result: Grain refinement, fine pearlite, higher yield strength than annealing.
Hardening (Quenching)
Heat above $A_3$/$A_1$, hold to austenitise, rapid quench in water/oil below $M_s$. Result: 100% Martensite (Maximum hardness, brittle).
Tempering
Reheat hardened steel ($150\text{--}650^\circ\text{C}$), air cool. Result: Relieves internal quenching stress, restores toughness, sacrifices slight hardness.
Austempering
Quench into salt bath at $250\text{--}450^\circ\text{C}$, hold isothermally until transformed. Result: 100% Bainite. High toughness, zero temper brittleness.
Martempering
Quench to just above $M_s$, hold to equalise temperature, air cool. Result: Martensite with minimal thermal distortion/cracking.
⚠️ Quenching Media Severity Ranking (Cooling Rate):
$$\text{Brine (Salt Water)} > \text{Plain Water} > \text{Oil} > \text{Air}$$ Brine cools fastest because salt prevents stable vapor blanket formation. Oil reduces thermal shock & distortion in alloy steels.

10. Principles of Powder Metallurgy

Mandatory 4-Step Production Sequence

Powder Production  →  Blending & Mixing  →  Compacting (Green Compact)  →  Sintering

PM StepProcess MechanismKey Operational Parameters
1. Powder Production Atomisation (liquid metal stream sprayed by air/water gas jet), Oxide Reduction, Electrolysis Yields controlled particle size distribution ($1\text{--}100\ \mu\text{m}$)
2. Blending & Mixing Homogenising elemental powders + solid lubricants (zinc stearate, wax) Reduces die wall friction and ejection pressure
3. Compacting Uniaxial pressing in rigid die at $100\text{--}800\ \text{MPa}$ Produces Green Compact (interlocking & cold welding). Weight = Powder Weight!
4. Sintering Heating in controlled reducing atmosphere at $0.7\text{--}0.9\ T_m$ Solid-state atomic diffusion seals pores; particles bond without bulk melting!
💡 Secondary PM Operations:
• Impregnation: Pores filled with lubricating oil (10–30% by vol) → Self-lubricating porous bronze bearings.
• Infiltration: Pores filled with lower-melting liquid metal (e.g. molten Cu into Fe compact) → Eliminates porosity, boosts strength.
• Primary PM Application: Tungsten Carbide ($\text{WC-Co}$) cutting tools. WC grains (~90%) bonded with Cobalt binder (~10%).

12. Key Points & Exam Traps

Exam Summary Checklist

  • APF Values: SC = 0.52, BCC = 0.68, FCC = HCP = 0.74. FCC and HCP possess maximum close packing.
  • Coordination Numbers: SC = 6, BCC = 8, FCC = HCP = 12.
  • Fe-C Invariant Temps: Eutectoid = 727°C (0.80% C), Eutectic = 1147°C (4.30% C), Peritectic = 1493°C (0.17% C).
  • Hardest Microstructure: Martensite (BCT structure). Softest/Ductile: Ferrite ($\alpha$). Maximum Machinability: Spheroidite.
  • Quenching Severity: Brine > Water > Oil > Air.
  • Stainless Steel: Minimum 12% Cr required for self-healing passive $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$ oxide layer. 18/8 (304) is non-magnetic austenitic.
  • Hot Hardness Element: Tungsten (W) and Cobalt (Co) preserve cutting tool hardness up to 600°C.
  • German Silver: Contains ZERO Silver! Composition: 60% Cu, 20% Ni, 20% Zn.
  • Babbitt Microstructure: Hard $\text{Sb}_3\text{Sn}$ cuboids embedded in soft tin/lead matrix to accommodate shaft misalignment and absorb grit particles.
  • Powder Metallurgy Rule: Sintering occurs BELOW the bulk melting point ($0.7\text{--}0.9\ T_m$) via solid-state atomic diffusion!
