Powder Metallurgy: Process Sequence, Sintering Mechanics, Secondary Operations & Applications

1. Topic Overview

This guide covers Powder Metallurgy (P/M) for competitive engineering exams (AP Genco AE, SSC JE, RRB JE). Key areas tested in PYQs include the strict 4-step process sequence (Powder Production $\rightarrow$ Blending $\rightarrow$ Compacting $\rightarrow$ Sintering), green compact properties, solid-state diffusion during sintering (below melting point), secondary finishing operations (Impregnation vs Infiltration), and primary industrial applications (tungsten carbide cutting inserts and self-lubricating bearings). All definitions, formulas, flowcharts, and worked examples are consolidated below.

2. Quick Reference Sheet

2.1 All Formulas

Green Compact Mass Balance

$$\boxed{m_{\text{powder}} = m_{\text{green}}}$$

Symbol Meaning Unit
$m_{\text{powder}}$ mass of blended metal powder mixture g / kg
$m_{\text{green}}$ mass of unsintered (green) compact g / kg

Sintering Temperature Criterion

$$\boxed{T_{\text{sintering}} \approx $0.7 \text{ to } 0.8$ \cdot T_m}$$

Symbol Meaning Unit
$T_{\text{sintering}}$ sintering temperature K or $^\circ\text{C}$
$T_m$ melting point of major constituent metal K or $^\circ\text{C}$

Note: Sintering occurs in the solid state below the melting temperature $T_{\text{sintering}} < T_m$; particles bond via atomic diffusion without bulk melting.

2.2 Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Powder Metallurgy (P/M): Manufacturing process that produces dense metal components by pressing metal powders into shape in dies and heating the compact below its melting point.
  • Blending / Mixing: Mixing pure metal powders with alloying elements, solid lubricants (e.g., graphite, stearic acid), or binders to achieve uniform chemical composition and flow properties.
  • Compacting: Cold pressing of blended powder inside a die cavity under high pressure $100\text{--}800\text{ MPa}$ to form a shaped green compact.
  • Green Compact: The unsintered powder press specimen; holds its shape solely by mechanical interlocking of powder particles.
  • Sintering: Thermal treatment of green compact in a controlled atmosphere furnace below the major constituent's melting point to create metallurgical bonds via atomic diffusion.
  • Impregnation: Secondary finishing operation where open pores of a sintered part are filled with oil or liquid lubricant under vacuum (used for self-lubricating bearings).
  • Infiltration: Secondary finishing operation where open pores of a sintered compact are filled with a molten lower-melting-point metal (e.g., copper into iron) to increase density and strength.
  • Coining / Sizing: Post-sintering press operation used to improve dimensional accuracy, surface finish, and mechanical strength.

2.3 Standard Values & Constants

  • Process Sequence Order: Powder Production $\rightarrow$ Blending $\rightarrow$ Compacting $\rightarrow$ Sintering (STRICT ORDER - Never change).
  • Sintering Temperature Range: $70\text{--}80\%$ of absolute melting point $T_m$.
  • Compacting Pressure Range: $100 \text{ to } 800 \text{ MPa}$ $1 \text{ to } 8 \text{ t/cm}^2$.
  • Mass Relation: Weight of raw powder = Weight of green compact = Weight of sintered part.
  • Primary Application Material: Tungsten Carbide (WC) cutting inserts & tool bits.

3. Main Body

3.1 Sequence of Operations & Compacting

1. Concept Pointers
  • The manufacturing sequence is fixed: 1. Powder Production $\rightarrow$ 2. Blending $\rightarrow$ 3. Compacting $\rightarrow$ 4. Sintering.
  • Compacting is performed after blending raw metal powders with lubricants (like graphite or zinc stearate) to reduce die friction.
  • Compacting gives the loose powder its geometrical shape and green strength so it can be handled safely.
  • Mass is strictly conserved: Mass of loose powder = Mass of green compact = Mass of final sintered component.

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: An exam question lists four steps in powder metallurgy: Compacting, Powder Production, Sintering, Blending.
Find: Correct chronological order of operations.

Solution: 1. Step 1: Raw powder is manufactured (Powder Production). 2. Step 2: Powders and lubricants are mixed uniformly (Blending). 3. Step 3: Mixed powder is pressed into die shape (Compacting). 4. Step 4: Green compact is heated to bond particles (Sintering). 5. Answer: Powder Production $\rightarrow$ Blending $\rightarrow$ Compacting $\rightarrow$ Sintering.

Key Insight: Any option starting with compacting or placing sintering before compacting is incorrect.

3.2 Sintering Mechanism & Objectives

1. Concept Pointers
  • Sintering heats the green compact in a protective/reducing furnace atmosphere (hydrogen, dissociated ammonia) below the major constituent's melting point.
  • Particles DO NOT melt: Metallurgical bonding occurs strictly through solid-state atomic diffusion (surface diffusion, volume diffusion, grain boundary diffusion).
  • Objectives of sintering:
  • Establish metallic/crystalline bonding between powder particles.
  • Increase density, strength, and hardness of the part.
  • Reduce internal porosity and brittleness.
  • Remove surface oxide films via reducing furnace atmosphere.
  • Densification during sintering is driven by diffusion mechanisms, NOT by grain growth.
  • Shrinkage during sintering depends on compacting pressure, particle size, sintering temperature, and furnace hold time.

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: Statement: "During sintering of a metal powder compact, powder particles melt completely to fuse the joint."
Find: Whether this statement is True or False.

Solution: 1. Sintering is conducted at $70\text{--}80\%$ of absolute melting temperature $T_m$. 2. Bulk melting does NOT occur; bonding is governed by solid-state atomic diffusion across particle contact necks. 3. Answer: False (Particles do NOT melt).

Key Insight: Sintering is a solid-state diffusion process; complete melting is a common distracter in MCQs.

3.3 Secondary Finishing Operations (Impregnation vs Infiltration)

1. Concept Pointers
  • Sintered parts inherent contain interconnected micro-porosity $5\text{--}25\%$.
  • Impregnation: Filling pores with oil or liquid lubricant under vacuum. Used specifically to produce self-lubricating bearings (e.g., sintered bronze bushing).
  • Infiltration: Filling open pores with a lower-melting-point molten metal (e.g., liquid copper into an iron compact) by capillary force to eliminate porosity, increase density, and improve mechanical strength.
  • Sintering is a primary process; Impregnation, Infiltration, Coining, and Heat Treatment are secondary finishing processes.
2. Formulas

For quick lookup, see Quick Reference Section 2.2.

$$\boxed{\text{Infiltration} = \text{Pores + Molten Lower-Melting Metal (Densification)}}$$

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: A sintered porous metal bearing is immersed in hot oil under vacuum to allow oil absorption into its pores.
Find: Name of this secondary operation.

Solution: 1. Filling pores with oil/lubricant for self-lubrication is called impregnation. 2. Filling pores with molten metal for density/strength is infiltration. 3. Answer: Impregnation.

Key Insight: Remember: Oil = Impregnation (Self-lubricating); Metal = Infiltration (Strength/Density).

3.4 Industrial Applications & Material Selection

1. Concept Pointers
  • Powder Metallurgy is uniquely suited for materials with extremely high melting points or materials that are immiscible in liquid state.
  • Primary Application: Tungsten Carbide (WC) cutting tools and inserts (cobalt binder + WC powder).
  • Self-lubricating bearings: Sintered bronze/iron bushings impregnated with oil (used in automotive motors, home appliances).
  • Porous metal filters: Sintered bronze/stainless steel filters for chemical and fuel filtration.
  • Complex small components: Gears, cams, structural lock parts made with minimal material waste and zero machining (near-net-shape manufacturing).

4. Consolidation Notes

Note on Impregnation vs Infiltration: Do not confuse these two secondary operations in MCQs: - Impregnation uses non-metallic fluids (oil/grease/wax) to create self-lubricating components. - Infiltration uses molten metal (like copper in iron) to fill pores, raising density, hardness, and tensile strength. See Section 3.3.

Note on Sintering vs Fusion Welding/Casting: Unlike casting or fusion welding, sintering is a solid-state process. Solid-state diffusion forms necks between particles without bulk liquid phase formation. See Section 3.2.

Note on Process Priority: Sintering is a primary step required to establish strength. Impregnation, infiltration, coining, and sizing are optional secondary finishing steps performed after sintering. See Section 3.1 & 3.3.

  • Memorize sequence: Powder Production $\rightarrow$ Blending $\rightarrow$ Compacting $\rightarrow$ Sintering.
  • Fix green compact mass relation: Mass of raw powder = Mass of green compact.
  • Remember sintering definition: Heating green compact below melting point $T_{\text{sintering}} < T_m$.
  • Fix bonding mechanism: Solid-state atomic diffusion; particles do NOT melt.
  • Distinguish secondary operations: Impregnation = Oil filling (self-lubrication); Infiltration = Metal filling (densification).
  • Fix primary application: Tungsten carbide tool inserts & self-lubricating bearings.
