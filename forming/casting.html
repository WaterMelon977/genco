Casting

1. Topic Overview

This guide covers metal casting for AP Genco AE-style preparation, focusing on pattern allowances, moulding sand properties, cores and chaplets, gating and risering, Chvorinov solidification, shrinkage compensation, and special casting processes. The most frequent PYQ themes are allowance identification, sand-property recall, gating-riser-cast flow, and solidification/shrinkage numericals. The content below consolidates repeated facts, keeps the worked examples, and presents the topic in a single exam-friendly structure.

2. Quick Reference Sheet

2.1 All Formulas

Shrinkage allowance

$$\boxed{\text{Shrinkage allowance} = \text{liquid shrinkage} + \text{solid shrinkage}}$$

Symbol Meaning
liquid shrinkage contraction during solidification while metal is liquid
solid shrinkage contraction after solidification as temperature falls

Chvorinov's rule

$$\boxed{T_s = k\left$\frac{V}{SA}\right$^2}$$

Symbol Meaning Unit
$T_s$ solidification time time
$k$ mould-casting constant depends on conditions
$V$ volume of casting volume
$SA$ surface area of casting area

Choke area / mass basis

$$\boxed{A_m = \frac{m}{\rho \cdot t \cdot C\sqrt{2gH}}}$$

Symbol Meaning Unit
$A_m$ choke area area
$m$ mass of casting kg
$\rho$ density of molten metal kg/m$^3$
$t$ pouring time s
$C$ efficiency factor dimensionless
$H$ effective metal head m
$g$ acceleration due to gravity m/s$^2$

Time to fill mould

$$\boxed{t_f = \frac{V}{A_g\sqrt{2gh_t}}}$$

Symbol Meaning Unit
$t_f$ filling time time
$V$ mould volume volume
$A_g$ gate area area
$h_t$ head above mould m

Riser relation used in PYQ notes

$$\boxed{V_{\text{riser}} = 3 \times $\text{shrinkage volume}$}$$

Optimum cylindrical riser

$$\boxed{d = \left$\frac{6V_{\text{riser}}}{\pi}\right$^{1/3}}$$

Simplified ratio note

$$\boxed{\text{Gating ratio} = \text{Sprue} : \text{Runner} : \text{In-gate}}$$

2.2 Key Definitions & Master Concepts

  • Pattern allowance: Extra or reduced material added to a pattern to compensate for casting-related effects.
  • Shrinkage allowance: Extra size added to compensate for liquid and solid shrinkage.
  • Draft allowance: Taper provided on vertical faces to permit clean pattern withdrawal.
  • Shake allowance: Reduction in pattern size to compensate for rapping during withdrawal.
  • Machining allowance: Extra stock provided for later machining.
  • Distortion allowance: Intentional pattern correction to counter warping in the casting.
  • Permeability: Ability of moulding sand to allow gases and steam to escape.
  • Collapsibility: Ability of moulding sand to yield after solidification so the casting can contract freely.
  • Refractoriness: Ability of sand to withstand high temperature without fusing.
  • Cohesiveness: Property by which sand grains stick to one another.
  • Adhesiveness: Property by which sand sticks to the mould box walls.
  • Green strength: Strength of moist sand under pressure.
  • Core prints: Projections that locate, support, and hold a core in position.
  • Chaplets: Metallic supports used to hold a core against buoyancy or shifting.
  • Chills: Metallic inserts used to increase heat extraction and promote directional solidification.
  • Riser: Reservoir of molten metal that feeds the casting during solidification.
  • Gate: Passage that carries molten metal into the mould cavity.
  • Sprue: Vertical passage that carries molten metal from the pouring basin to the runner.
  • Runner: Horizontal channel that distributes molten metal.
  • Choke: Smallest cross-section controlling the rate of molten-metal flow.
  • Investment casting: Casting process in which the pattern is melted out to form complex shapes.
  • Shell moulding: Casting process using resin-coated sand to obtain good finish and accuracy.
  • Centrifugal casting: Casting process for hollow axisymmetric parts using centrifugal force.
  • Cold-chamber die casting: Die casting for high-melting-point alloys.
  • Hot-chamber die casting: Die casting for low-melting-point alloys.

2.3 Standard Values / Constants

  • Draft allowance: usually 1� to 3�, or about 5 to 10 mm per metre per side.
  • Shrinkage allowance examples: aluminium about 10 to 12 mm/m; steel about 15 to 20 mm/m.
  • Green sand mix: about 70% sand and 30% clay in the commonly cited rough composition.
  • Chvorinov exponent: 2.
  • Shell moulding accuracy: about 0.003 to 0.005 mm/mm.
  • Broader special-casting note: centrifugal casting is commonly used for pipes and tubes.
  • Typical riser rule in notes: optimum cylindrical riser often has height equal to diameter.

3. Main Body

3.1 Pattern Design and Allowances

1. Concept Pointers
  • Shrinkage allowance compensates for contraction, so the pattern is made larger.
  • Draft allowance is provided on vertical faces to permit easy withdrawal without damaging the mould.
  • Shake allowance makes the pattern slightly smaller to account for rapping during extraction.
  • Machining allowance provides extra stock for later finishing.
  • Distortion allowance counters warping in long or thin castings.
  • Elimination of details is used when fine features cannot be reproduced in sand.
  • Unfinished surfaces may be painted black on the pattern.
  • A sweep pattern is used for bell shapes or other symmetrical forms.
2. Formulas

For quick lookup, see Quick Reference Section 2.1.

$$\boxed{\text{Shrinkage allowance} = \text{liquid shrinkage} + \text{solid shrinkage}}$$

Conditions and usage - Draft is used mainly on vertical faces. - Shake allowance reduces the pattern size slightly. - Shrinkage allowance increases the pattern size.

3. Diagram

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: An aluminium cube of side 100 mm, shrinkage allowance 10 mm/m.
Find: Pattern side.

Solution: 1. Shrinkage allowance = 10 mm per metre = 10/1000 per mm. 2. Extra side = $(10/1000) \times 100 = 1$ mm. 3. Pattern side = 100 + 1 = 101 mm. 4. Answer: 101 mm.

Key Insight: Shrinkage allowance makes the pattern larger, while shake allowance makes it smaller.

Worked Example 2

Given: A question states that due to shake allowance the pattern is made larger.
Find: Whether the statement is correct.

Solution: 1. Shake/rapping allowance compensates for tapping during withdrawal. 2. It makes the pattern slightly smaller, not larger. 3. Answer: Incorrect.

Key Insight: Shake and shrinkage allowances act in opposite directions.

3.2 Moulding Sand Properties and Mould Construction

1. Concept Pointers
  • Permeability allows gases and steam to escape from the mould.
  • Lower clay content generally improves permeability.
  • Collapsibility helps the casting contract freely after solidification.
  • Refractoriness is the ability to resist fusion at pouring temperature.
  • Higher grain size generally improves refractoriness in the PYQ framing.
  • Adhesiveness makes sand stick to the mould box walls.
  • Cohesiveness keeps sand grains together.
  • Uniform ramming improves dimensional stability.
  • Green sand is commonly described as about 70% sand and 30% clay.
2. Formulas

No direct numerical equation is standard here, but the PYQ relationships are:

$$\boxed{\downarrow \text{clay} \Rightarrow \uparrow \text{permeability}}$$

$$\boxed{\uparrow \text{grain size} \Rightarrow \uparrow \text{refractoriness}}$$

$$\boxed{\text{Uniform ramming} \Rightarrow \uparrow \text{dimensional stability}}$$

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: The property of moulding sand that does not provide resistance to contraction of the solidified casting.
Find: The property name.

Solution: 1. The sand must yield after the metal solidifies. 2. That property is collapsibility. 3. Answer: Collapsibility.

Key Insight: Collapsibility prevents restraint during contraction and helps avoid hot tears.

3.3 Cores, Core Prints, Chaplets, and Chills

1. Concept Pointers
  • Core prints locate, support, and hold the core in the correct position.
  • Chaplets support the core against buoyancy and shifting.
  • When metal is poured, the core experiences buoyancy because the core is lighter than the liquid metal.
  • Chaplets melt and become part of the casting.
  • Chills are metallic inserts used to extract heat rapidly.
  • Chills promote directional solidification; they do not support the core.

Given: The main purpose of chaplets in foundry practice.
Find: Correct answer.

Solution: 1. Chaplets are used to support the core. 2. They prevent shifting or sagging under buoyancy. 3. Answer: To support the core.

Key Insight: Chaplets support the core, while chills control solidification direction.

3.4 Gating System, Runner, Riser, Gate, and Sprue

1. Concept Pointers
  • Pouring basin reduces turbulence and maintains head.
  • Sprue carries molten metal downward and is tapered to avoid aspiration.
  • Runner distributes metal horizontally.
  • Gate controls entry into the cavity and may act as a choke.
  • Riser feeds the casting during solidification and must solidify after the casting.
  • Gating ratio is given as sprue : runner : in-gate.
  • Choke is the smallest flow-controlling section.
  • Goals of a gating system are to minimise turbulence, fill the mould before solidification starts, and trap inclusions.
2. Formulas

For quick lookup, see Quick Reference Section 2.1.

$$\boxed{A_m = \frac{m}{\rho \cdot t \cdot C\sqrt{2gH}}}$$

$$\boxed{t_f = \frac{V}{A_g\sqrt{2gh_t}}}$$

Conditions and usage - Use the choke-area relation in mass-based flow questions. - The fill-time formula is used for simple mould-filling estimates. - Riser should solidify after the casting, not before.

3. Diagram

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: The gating ratio is asked in an MCQ.
Find: The correct order.

Solution: 1. Gating ratio is defined as sprue : runner : in-gate. 2. Answer: Sprue : Runner : In-gate.

Key Insight: The order is tested directly and should be memorised exactly.

Worked Example 2

Given: A question asks what the riser does.
Find: Correct role.

Solution: 1. Riser acts as a reservoir of molten metal. 2. It feeds the casting during solidification to compensate for shrinkage. 3. Answer: It feeds shrinkage metal and solidifies last.

Key Insight: Riser is not a runner or sprue; it is the feeder.

3.5 Solidification, Chvorinov's Rule, and Shrinkage

1. Concept Pointers
  • Solidification time depends on the volume-to-surface-area ratio.
  • Thicker sections solidify more slowly.
  • Chvorinov's rule is used for comparing solidification times under the same mould and metal conditions.
  • The riser compensates liquid shrinkage and solidification shrinkage.
  • Solid-state shrinkage is handled by pattern allowance.
  • An optimum cylindrical riser is often taken with height equal to diameter in exam notes.
2. Formulas

For quick lookup, see Quick Reference Section 2.1.

$$\boxed{T_s = k\left$\frac{V}{SA}\right$^2}$$

$$\boxed{V_{\text{riser}} = 3 \times $\text{shrinkage volume}$}$$

$$\boxed{d = \left$\frac{6V_{\text{riser}}}{\pi}\right$^{1/3}}$$

Conditions and usage - The exponent in Chvorinov's rule is 2. - A lower volume-to-surface-area ratio means faster solidification. - The riser feeds only liquid and solidification shrinkage.

3. Diagram

No dedicated Chvorinov diagram found; thick sections solidify slower, so risers are placed on the thickest and highest sections.

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: A 100 mm cube solidifies in 20 min. Under the same conditions, a 100 � 100 � 50 mm slab is asked for solidification time.
Find: Approximate solidification time.

Solution: 1. Use Chvorinov's rule, comparing $(V/SA)^2$. 2. The slab has a lower volume-to-surface-area ratio than the cube. 3. The PYQ note gives the result as about 11.3 min. 4. Answer: 11.3 min.

Key Insight: Thinner sections solidify faster because their volume-to-surface-area ratio is smaller.

Worked Example 2

Given: A casting undergoes 3% liquid shrinkage and 4% solidification shrinkage.
Find: Volume compensated from the riser.

Solution: 1. Riser compensates liquid and solidification shrinkage only. 2. Total = 3% + 4% = 7%. 3. Answer: 7%.

Key Insight: Do not include solid-state shrinkage in the riser calculation; that is handled by pattern allowance.

3.6 Special Casting Processes

1. Concept Pointers
  • Investment casting uses a wax or plastic pattern that is melted out.
  • Shell moulding uses resin-coated sand and gives very good finish and accuracy.
  • Cold-chamber die casting is used for high-melting-point alloys.
  • Hot-chamber die casting is used for low-melting-point alloys.
  • Centrifugal casting is used for hollow axisymmetric parts like pipes and tubes.
  • CO$_2$ moulding uses sodium silicate and carbon dioxide for instant hardening.
  • Blow moulding is used for hollow thin-walled plastic parts.
2. Formulas

No universal equation is used; process choice is based on part shape, metal melting point, and required accuracy.

4. Worked Example

Worked Example 1

Given: A casting process is required for brass or aluminium.
Find: Suitable die-casting type.

Solution: 1. Brass and aluminium are high-melting-point alloys. 2. Cold-chamber die casting is used for such alloys. 3. Answer: Cold-chamber die casting.

Key Insight: Hot chamber is for low-melting alloys like zinc, lead, and tin.

Key Insight: Centrifugal casting is a standard answer for hollow pipes and tubes.

4. Consolidation Notes

Note on pattern allowances: Shrinkage and shake allowances act in opposite directions, and they are often confused in MCQs. Shrinkage makes the pattern larger, while shake makes it smaller. See Section 3.1.

Note on riser, sprue, runner, and gate: The gating system has a strict flow order and each part has a separate role. The riser is a feeder, not a flow channel. See Section 3.4.

Note on cores, chaplets, and chills: Chaplets support cores; chills control solidification. They are not interchangeable. See Section 3.3.

Note on solidification and pattern allowance: Riser compensates liquid and solidification shrinkage, but solid-state shrinkage is handled by making the pattern larger. See Section 3.5.

5. Final Quick-Fire Checklist

  • Memorize the allowance types: shrinkage, draft, shake, machining, and distortion.
  • Keep the sand properties straight: permeability, collapsibility, refractoriness, adhesiveness, cohesiveness, and green strength.
  • Remember the roles: core print locates the core, chaplets support it, chills extract heat.
  • Fix the gating order: sprue : runner : in-gate.
  • Memorize Chvorinov's rule: $T_s = k(V/SA)^2$.
  • Remember that the riser feeds liquid and solidification shrinkage only.
  • Keep special casting choices straight: cold chamber for high-melting alloys, hot chamber for low-melting alloys.

6. MCQ-Focused Enhanced Revision Guide

6.1 Core Concepts & Definitions (High-Yield MCQ Points)

  • Casting: Manufacturing process in which molten metal is poured into a mould cavity and allowed to solidify into the required shape.
    👉 MCQ focus: Net shape process, liquid metal solidification
  • Pattern: Replica of the casting used to form the mould cavity, with allowances added.
    👉 MCQ focus: Made of wood, metal, plastic; used to create mould cavity
  • Mould: Refractory cavity that receives molten metal and defines external shape.
    👉 MCQ focus: Made of sand in sand casting
  • Core: Sand insert used to create internal cavities, holes, and hollow sections.
    👉 MCQ focus: Made using core sand + binder; supported by core prints and chaplets
  • Gating System: Network of channels that delivers molten metal into mould cavity.
    👉 MCQ focus: Includes sprue, runner, ingate, pouring basin
  • Riser (Feeder): Reservoir of molten metal that compensates for shrinkage during solidification.
    👉 MCQ focus: Feeds casting, solidifies last
  • Chill: Metal insert used to increase cooling rate locally.
    👉 MCQ focus: Promotes directional solidification

6.2 Essential Formulas (Expanded MCQ Coverage)

Basic Geometry & Shrinkage

$$\text{Casting Volume: } V = l \times b \times h \quad \text{or} \quad V = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \times H$$ $$\text{Shrinkage Volume: } V_s = \text{shrinkage fraction} \times V_c$$ $$\text{Pattern Shrinkage Allowance: } \text{Allowance} = \frac{\Delta L}{L} \times 1000 \text{ mm/m}$$

👉 MCQ focus: Shrinkage order Steel > Aluminium > Cast Iron; Pattern is made larger.

Riser Design & Solidification

$$\text{Riser Volume: } V_r = k \times V_s = \frac{\pi D^2}{4} \times H$$ $$\text{Optimum Cylindrical Riser Condition: } H = D$$ $$\text{Chvorinov's Rule: } t_s = C\left(\frac{V}{A}\right)^n, \quad n \approx 2$$ $$\text{Cooling Rate } \propto \frac{1}{t_s} \quad (\text{Faster cooling } \rightarrow \text{ Fine grains, Slower cooling } \rightarrow \text{ Coarse grains})$$

👉 MCQ focus: Riser must have minimum surface area to volume ratio and solidify after casting.

Fluid Flow & Nucleation

$$\text{Flow Rate: } Q = A \cdot v \quad (\text{Higher velocity } \rightarrow \text{ More turbulence})$$ $$\text{Critical Nucleation Radius: } r^* = \frac{-2\gamma}{\Delta G_V} \quad (\text{Smaller radius } \rightarrow \text{ Easier nucleation})$$

Gating System Ratios

General Form: Sprue : Runner : Ingate

6.5 Key MCQ Concepts (Highly Tested)

Solidification & Structure:
  • Solidification starts at mould wall $\rightarrow$ inward.
  • Directional solidification $\rightarrow$ shrinkage toward riser.
  • Faster cooling $\rightarrow$ fine grain structure.
  • Slower cooling $\rightarrow$ coarse grain structure.
Shrinkage Types & Handling:
  • Liquid shrinkage: handled by gating system & riser.
  • Solidification shrinkage: handled by riser.
  • Solid-state shrinkage: handled by pattern allowance (pattern made larger).
Die Casting & Special Processes:
  • Hot chamber die casting: low melting point alloys (Zinc, Magnesium, Lead, Tin).
  • Cold chamber die casting: high melting point alloys (Aluminium, Copper, Brass).
  • Shell moulding: resin-coated sand pattern shell.
  • CO₂ moulding: sodium silicate binder + CO₂ gas instant hardening.
  • Investment casting: wax pattern + ceramic shell.

6.6 High-Frequency MCQ Question Patterns

  • Identify defect from description (e.g. blow holes vs cold shut vs hot tears).
  • Match process with product (e.g. centrifugal casting $\rightarrow$ hollow pipes).
  • Select correct gating ratio (pressurized vs unpressurized).
  • Identify correct riser function and optimum dimension ratio ($H = D$).
  • Calculate shrinkage allowance and apply Chvorinov’s rule ($t_s \propto (V/A)^2$).
  • Determine cooling rate effect on grain size.
